# 11 · Extended Stay Packaging and Rate Strategies

> **Part:** [[A - Revenue & Pricing Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Assess the revenue contribution and occupancy benefits of extended-stay guests, enabling you to refine packages and pricing structures that encourage longer visits.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical reservation data indicating length of stay.
- Extended‐stay package details (discounted rates, included amenities or services).
- Occupancy and revenue metrics segmented by stay length (e.g., 7+ nights, 14+ nights).
- Guest feedback or satisfaction ratings from extended‐stay guests.
- Competitive set data on extended‐stay pricing (if available).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Segment past reservations by length of stay. Identify booking patterns for stays of 5+ nights, 7+ nights, etc., based on the property’s definition of “extended stay.”
2. Determine the overall contribution of extended‐stay guests. Calculate occupancy, total room nights, and revenue share from these segments.
3. Review current extended stay packages or discounts. Document the rate structure, included perks, and any marketing channels used to promote them.
4. Compare extended‐stay ADR with standard ADR. Assess whether extended‐stay rates are delivering acceptable margins or if they are underpriced.
5. Analyze guest satisfaction and retention. Determine if extended‐stay guests are more loyal, leave better reviews, or show higher ancillary spend.
6. Benchmark against competitors. If possible, compare extended‐stay offerings and pricing with similar properties or specialized extended‐stay brands.
7. Identify areas for optimization. Look for potential rate adjustments, added amenities, or cross‐promotions that could boost profitability and guest appeal.


## 📤 Output format
- A table showing length‐of‐stay segments, number of bookings, total nights, ADR, and total revenue.
- Charts or graphs highlighting trends in extended‐stay occupancy and revenue over time.

- Narrative assessment of the effectiveness of existing extended‐stay packages and suggested improvements.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A higher volume of extended‐stay bookings can stabilize occupancy, though rates may be lower.
- Strong ancillary spend or higher loyalty from extended‐stay guests can justify discounted rates.
- Negative guest feedback may highlight gaps in extended‐stay amenities (kitchenettes, laundry facilities, workspace, etc.).


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Develop tiered extended‐stay packages with added amenities or services at different length thresholds.
2. Offer tailored perks (e.g., laundry credits, meal plans) to enhance the value proposition for long‐stay guests.
3. Adjust marketing efforts to highlight extended‐stay benefits on direct channels.
4. Refine pricing to balance occupancy gains with satisfactory margins, evaluating competitor rates as a reference.
