# 5 · Stripping Ratio and Waste-to-Ore Ratio Evaluation

> **Part:** [[A - Resource and Reserve Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Metals & Mining Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to assess the ratio of waste material to ore extracted in mining operations. Evaluating the stripping ratio and waste-to-ore ratio is critical for understanding operational efficiency, economic viability, and potential environmental impact.


## 📊 Data required
- Total volume or weight of waste material removed (e.g., in cubic meters or tons).
- Total volume or weight of ore extracted (e.g., in tons).
- Historical stripping ratios and waste-to-ore ratios for benchmarking.
- Cost data for waste removal and ore extraction.
- Geotechnical and mine design data for assessing overburden thickness and ore body geometry.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Gather Data on Waste and Ore Quantities:** ○ Collect operational data for a specific time period, recording the quantities of waste removed and ore extracted.
2. **Calculate Stripping Ratio (for open-pit mining):** ○ Use the formula:
`Stripping Ratio = (Volume or Weight of Waste Removed / Volume or Weight of Ore Extracted) ○ This ratio helps determine the feasibility of an open-pit mine and its economic cutoff.`
3. **Calculate Waste-to-Ore Ratio (for general mining):** ○ Use the formula:
`Waste-to-Ore Ratio = (Weight of Waste Removed / Weight of Ore Extracted) ○ This ratio is similar to the stripping ratio but can be applied to underground mining operations.`
4. **Evaluate Trends and Variations:** ○ Compare current stripping and waste-to-ore ratios with historical trends and industry benchmarks. ○ Identify areas where the ratios deviate significantly, signaling potential inefficiencies.
5. Analyze Cost Implications:
17 ○ Estimate the cost of waste removal and its impact on overall mining profitability using the formula:
`Cost Impact = (Waste Quantity x Cost per Unit of Waste Removal).`
6. **Assess Environmental and Operational Impacts:** ○ Evaluate the environmental impact of waste removal, such as land disturbance or tailings management. ○ Identify operational challenges, such as excessive overburden or difficult ore access.
7. **Segment Analysis by Mining Area:** ○ Break down stripping ratios and waste-to-ore ratios by mining area or ore deposit to pinpoint specific issues.
8. **Perform Scenario Analysis:** ○ Test different scenarios (e.g., higher ore grades, lower waste volumes) to assess how operational changes affect the ratios and profitability.


## 📤 Output format
- Stripping Ratio and Waste-to-Ore Ratio Table showing data for individual mining areas and overall averages.
- Cost Impact Summary detailing waste removal costs and profitability implications.
- Trend Analysis Chart comparing current and historical ratios.
- Environmental Impact Report summarizing potential risks and mitigation strategies.
- Narrative Report highlighting findings, insights, and actionable recommendations.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Low Stripping or Waste-to-Ore Ratios:** Indicates efficient operations with minimal waste removal relative to ore extraction.
- **High Ratios:** Suggest higher costs, lower efficiency, or challenging mining conditions.
- **Improving Ratios Over Time:** Reflects operational improvements or access to more favorable ore deposits.
- **Static or Deteriorating Ratios:** May indicate the need for design optimization or access to higher-grade ore bodies.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Optimize mine design and pit geometry to reduce waste removal.

2. Use selective mining techniques to minimize waste extraction while maximizing ore recovery.
3. Invest in advanced geotechnical analysis to identify efficient access points for ore bodies.
4. Reevaluate cut-off grades to focus on higher-grade ore, reducing waste-to-ore ratios.
5. Explore technological solutions like in-pit crushing and conveying (IPCC) to reduce waste handling costs.
6. Develop waste management plans to minimize environmental impact and enhance land reclamation efforts.
