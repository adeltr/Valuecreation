# 3 · Annual Contract Value (ACV) Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Finance & Revenue]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Annual Contract Value (ACV) analysis is to determine the average annual revenue generated per customer contract. ACV helps SaaS companies understand the average worth of a customer contract over a year and is particularly useful for businesses with multi-year agreements or annual subscriptions. It allows for more accurate revenue forecasting and pricing strategy development.


## 📊 Data required
1. Total contract value for each customer over the contract duration
2. Length of each customer contract (in years)
3. Number of customers or contracts being analyzed


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Determine the time period for analysis:** Choose a period for the analysis, typically a quarter or year. This will help align ACV with other key metrics such as revenue and customer growth.
2. **Collect contract values:** For each customer, gather the total contract value (TCV). This includes any upfront fees, recurring subscription charges, and any additional revenue generated from the customer over the contract term.
3. **Determine contract length:** Identify the length of each contract, typically expressed in years. For example, a 3-year contract or a 1-year agreement. For monthly contracts, convert the term to years (e.g., a 12- month contract = 1 year).
4. **Calculate ACV for each contract:** Use the following formula to calculate ACV for each customer contract:
`ACV = Total Contract Value / Contract Length (in years)`
For example, if a customer signs a 2-year contract worth $60,000, the ACV would be:
`ACV = $60,000 / 2 = $30,000 per year`
5. **Calculate the overall ACV:** If analyzing multiple contracts, calculate the average ACV across all contracts by summing up the ACVs and dividing by the number of customers or contracts. Use this formula:
11 Average ACV = (Sum of ACVs for all customers) / (Number of customers)
For instance, if you have 5 contracts with individual ACVs of $30,000, $40,000, $50,000, $60,000, and $70,000, the average ACV would be:
`Average ACV = ($30,000 + $40,000 + $50,000 + $60,000 + $70,000) / 5 = $50,000`


## 📤 Output format
The output is typically a dollar ﬁgure representing the average annual contract value. For a single customer, the output is the ACV for that contract, and for multiple customers, it is the average ACV across all contracts. In the example above, the output would be:
Average Annual Contract Value (ACV): $50,000


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   Higher ACV: A higher ACV indicates that the company is securing larger contracts, which could mean customers are willing to commit to higher-priced plans or more comprehensive service offerings. This is beneﬁcial for predictable revenue and cash ﬂow.
•   Lower ACV: A lower ACV may suggest that customers are opting for smaller or shorter-term contracts, which could indicate pricing sensitivity or that the product’s perceived value is lower. SaaS companies with a lower ACV may need to focus on upselling or improving the perceived value of their offerings.
ACV is particularly important in forecasting future revenue and understanding customer segmentation. Companies may analyze ACV by customer size, industry, or geography to identify patterns and develop targeted strategies.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Upsell and cross-sell opportunities:** Increase ACV by offering additional features, services, or complementary products to existing customers. Tailor these offers to speciﬁc customer segments to maximize value.
2. **Move customers to higher-priced plans:** Encourage customers to adopt higher-tier pricing plans by offering enhanced features, better support, or additional services that provide added value.
3. **OKer longer contract terms:** Incentivize customers to commit to multi-year contracts by offering discounts or added beneﬁts for longer-term agreements. This stabilizes revenue and increases ACV over time.

5. **Bundle services:** Create bundled offerings where customers receive a package of services or features at a higher price, which provides more value and increases the likelihood of higher ACV.
