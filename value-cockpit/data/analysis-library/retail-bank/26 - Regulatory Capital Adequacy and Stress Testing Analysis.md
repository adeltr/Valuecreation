# 26 · Regulatory Capital Adequacy and Stress Testing Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Operational Efficiency and Risk Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Retail Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the bank’s capital adequacy in meeting regulatory requirements and assess its ability to withstand adverse economic scenarios through stress testing. This analysis ensures financial stability and regulatory compliance while identifying vulnerabilities in capital structure.


## 📊 Data required
1. Current capital structure (Tier 1, Tier 2, and total regulatory capital).
2. Risk-weighted assets (RWAs) by category (e.g., credit, market, operational risk).
3. Historical stress testing results and scenario parameters.
4. Economic indicators for stress scenarios (e.g., GDP decline, unemployment, market shocks).
5. Loan and investment portfolio details (e.g., exposures, default rates).
6. Regulatory requirements for capital adequacy (e.g., Basel III guidelines).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Capital Adequacy Ratios:** Compute the Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1), Tier 1, and Total Capital Ratios using the formulas:
- CET1 Ratio = (CET1 Capital / Risk-Weighted Assets) x 100
- Tier 1 Capital Ratio = (Tier 1 Capital / Risk-Weighted Assets) x 100
- Total Capital Ratio = (Total Regulatory Capital / Risk-Weighted Assets) x 100 Compare these ratios with regulatory minimums and internal targets.
2. Segment Risk-Weighted Assets (RWAs) ○ Analyze RWAs by category (e.g., credit risk, market risk, operational risk). ○ Identify categories contributing the most to RWAs and assess their impact on capital ratios.
3. **Develop Stress Testing Scenarios:** Define baseline and adverse scenarios based on macroeconomic indicators (e.g., GDP contraction, rising interest rates).
Examples of scenarios:
76 ○ Severe economic downturn with a 20% rise in defaults. ○ Market crash reducing portfolio values by 30%.
4. Simulate Impact on Capital Ratios ○ Project changes in RWAs, income, and capital levels under each scenario. ○ Calculate post-stress capital adequacy ratios for each scenario:
`Post-Stress Capital Ratio = (Post-Stress Capital / Post-Stress RWAs) x 100`
5. Evaluate Results Against Regulatory Thresholds ○ Compare post-stress ratios with regulatory minimums (e.g., 4.5% CET1, 8% Total Capital). ○ Highlight scenarios where ratios fall below thresholds, indicating potential vulnerabilities.
6. Assess Risk Mitigation Measures ○ Identify existing measures (e.g., capital buffers, portfolio diversification) and their effectiveness in mitigating risks. ○ Recommend adjustments to maintain capital adequacy under stress conditions.
7. Monitor Trends in Capital Adequacy ○ Track historical trends in capital ratios and stress testing results to evaluate the effectiveness of previous strategies.
8. Benchmark Against Industry Peers ○ Compare capital adequacy and stress testing results with similar institutions to assess relative performance.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing capital adequacy ratios, RWAs by category, and stress testing results.
- Charts showing trends in capital ratios over time and under different stress scenarios.
- Scenario analysis results with projected changes in capital levels and ratios.
- A report highlighting key vulnerabilities and recommendations for capital optimization.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Capital ratios above regulatory minimums under all scenarios indicate strong financial stability.
2. Ratios falling below thresholds in adverse scenarios suggest vulnerabilities requiring mitigation.

3. High contributions from specific RWA categories highlight areas for risk reduction.
4. Benchmark comparisons provide insights into the bank’s relative capital resilience.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Build capital buffers by retaining earnings or issuing additional equity.
2. Diversify the loan and investment portfolios to reduce risk concentration.
3. Enhance risk management practices to lower RWAs, particularly in high-risk categories.
4. Conduct regular stress testing to proactively identify and address vulnerabilities.
5. Align internal capital targets with evolving regulatory standards and market conditions.
