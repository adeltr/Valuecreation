# 23 · Deposit Base Structure and Cost of Funds Review

> **Part:** [[D - Capital, Liquidity, and Funding]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Commercial Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate the composition of the bank’s deposit base and the associated funding costs to ensure efficient liquidity management and competitive pricing.


## 📊 Data required
- Breakdowns of deposit types (e.g., demand deposits, savings, time deposits)
- Historical and current interest rates paid on each deposit category
- Deposit volumes and balances over time
- Cost of funds data and peer or industry benchmarks
- Customer segmentation and deposit concentration details
- Regulatory requirements or guidelines related to liquidity and funding


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Gather data on all deposit categories, including the volumes, balances, and interest rates for each type (e.g., demand, savings, time), ensuring accuracy in segmentation across different customer groups.
Step 2: Calculate the overall cost of funds by aggregating interest expenses and other funding costs (e.g., fees, promotional rates), then expressing these as a percentage of total deposits or relevant funding sources.
Step 3: Identify the concentration of deposits by customer segment or account type, noting any large or potentially volatile depositors that could influence liquidity risk.
Step 4: Compare the bank’s average deposit rates and cost of funds to market benchmarks or peer banks, determining competitiveness and alignment with the bank’s liquidity strategy.
Step 5: Evaluate how changes in interest rate environments (for example, rising or falling interest rates) may impact the deposit base structure and overall cost of funds, using historical data as a reference.
50 Step 6: Document findings in a comprehensive report, highlighting strengths in deposit stability, areas where funding costs might be reduced, and strategies for improving deposit mix and liquidity management.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables showing deposit breakdowns by type, customer segment, and corresponding interest rates
- Charts illustrating trends in deposit balances, cost of funds, and comparison to industry benchmarks
- Narrative summary with key insights, risks, and recommended actions for optimizing the deposit base


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A high proportion of low-cost transactional accounts typically indicates a stable funding source and lower interest expenses.
- Excessive reliance on high-interest deposits or a few large depositors can raise liquidity risk and drive up overall funding costs.
- Favorable comparisons against peers suggest that the bank’s deposit strategy is cost-competitive and robust, while higher costs may signal opportunities for repricing or deposit product innovation.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Diversify the deposit base by targeting different customer segments and products that encourage stable balances.
2. Adjust pricing strategies to attract more low-cost deposits, considering market conditions and competitor rates.
3. Implement strong retention programs and relationship-building initiatives to reduce the likelihood of large outflows.
4. Use data analytics to identify deposit trends and potential for cross-selling or bundling deposit products.
5. Continuously monitor interest rate movements and revisit deposit pricing strategies to maintain cost efficiency while meeting liquidity needs.
