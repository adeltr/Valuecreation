# 17 · Biosimilars Threat Assessment

> **Part:** [[C - Operations & Supply Chain]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Pharmaceutical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Biosimilars Threat Assessment is to evaluate the potential competitive threat that biosimilar products pose to a pharmaceutical company’s biologic drugs. As patents and exclusivity periods for biologics expire, biosimilar manufacturers can enter the market with lower-cost alternatives that may erode market share, reduce revenue, and impact pricing strategies. This analysis helps the company assess the risk posed by biosimilars, understand the competitive landscape, and develop strategies to protect market share, maintain profitability, and optimize lifecycle management for its biologic portfolio.


## 📊 Data required
- List of biologic drugs nearing patent or exclusivity expiration.
- Information on biosimilar competitors in the pipeline, including their stage of development (preclinical, clinical, or approved).
- Regulatory approvals for biosimilars in key markets (e.g., U.S., Europe, Japan).
- Price differentials between biologics and biosimilars.
- Sales and market share data for the company’s biologics.
- Historical data on market erosion rates for biologics after biosimilar entry.
- Analysis of payer and healthcare provider preferences for biosimilars versus branded biologics.
- Company’s ability to defend patents or extend exclusivity (e.g., patent litigation, secondary patents, reformulations, or combination therapies).
- Regulatory strategies in place to maintain market exclusivity (e.g., pediatric extensions, orphan drug exclusivity).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Data Collection:** ○ Collect a list of the company’s biologic drugs that are approaching patent or exclusivity expiration. Identify the expiration dates for key markets (e.g., the U.S., EU, Japan). ○ Gather data on biosimilars that are currently in development or have been approved in key markets. This includes clinical trial results, regulatory submissions, and anticipated market entry dates.
2. **Assess Market Erosion Risk:** ○ Analyze the potential market erosion that could occur once biosimilars enter the market. Historical data on market erosion for similar biologics can help estimate the impact. Use the following formula to calculate potential revenue loss:
`Market Erosion = (Market Share of Biosimilars / Total Market Share) * 100`
○ For example, if biosimilars capture 30% of the market for a specific biologic, calculate the revenue loss based on the current sales of the biologic.
65
3. **Evaluate Regulatory Approvals and Pipeline for Biosimilars:** ○ Review biosimilar approvals in key markets, particularly the U.S. (FDA), EU (EMA), and other high-value regions. Pay close attention to biosimilars that have gained regulatory approval and those in advanced clinical development stages. ○ Investigate how quickly biosimilars are expected to enter the market following the expiration of patents or exclusivity periods.
4. **Analyze Price Differentials Between Biologics and Biosimilars:** ○ Examine the pricing strategies for both branded biologics and biosimilars. Biosimilars are typically priced 15-30% lower than their reference biologics, depending on the market and regulatory environment. ○ Calculate the potential impact of biosimilar pricing on overall revenue by estimating the price reductions required to remain competitive in a biosimilar-dominated market.
5. **Assess Payer and Healthcare Provider Preferences:** ○ Review payer preferences for biosimilars versus branded biologics. In many cases, payers prefer biosimilars due to their lower costs and may restrict coverage for branded biologics once biosimilars are available. ○ Analyze healthcare provider attitudes toward biosimilars, including prescribing habits and concerns about interchangeability or therapeutic equivalence. Identify any regional differences in biosimilar adoption rates.
6. **Review Patent Defense and Lifecycle Management Strategies:** ○ Evaluate the company’s ability to defend its biologics through patent litigation or extend market exclusivity through secondary patents (e.g., formulation patents or method-of-use patents). ○ Assess the feasibility of reformulating biologics or developing combination therapies to maintain market exclusivity beyond the original patent expiration. These strategies can extend product life cycles and delay the impact of biosimilar competition. ○ Explore regulatory strategies to extend exclusivity, such as pediatric exclusivity extensions or orphan drug designations.
7. **Analyze Historical Data on Biosimilar Impact:** ○ Review the market impact of biosimilars on other biologics in the same therapeutic area. Identify how quickly biosimilars have captured market share and how branded biologics responded to competition (e.g., through pricing strategies or innovation). ○ Compare the company’s biologic drugs to other biologics that have faced biosimilar competition to predict the likely impact on market share and revenue.
8. **Risk Mitigation and Competitive Strategy:** ○ Identify potential strategies to mitigate the impact of biosimilars. This may include reducing prices to remain competitive, increasing patient and provider education about the unique benefits of the biologic, or improving patient support programs to maintain brand loyalty. ○ Consider partnerships or licensing agreements with biosimilar manufacturers to 66 maintain a presence in the biosimilar market while minimizing revenue losses.


## 📤 Output format
The output of the Biosimilars Threat Assessment typically includes a narrative summarizing the risk posed by biosimilars, key findings on market erosion potential, and strategies to protect market share. For example:
- **Biosimilar Competition for Leading Oncology Biologic:** The company’s top-selling oncology biologic faces biosimilar competition beginning in 2025, when its market exclusivity expires. Two biosimilar candidates are in late-stage clinical trials, with regulatory submissions expected in early 2025. These biosimilars are expected to enter the market with a 20-25% price reduction, capturing approximately 30% of the market by
2026. The company could lose an estimated $500M in revenue during the first year of biosimilar competition.
- **Price Differentials and Payer Preferences:** Payers in the U.S. and EU are expected to favor biosimilars due to lower costs, with some payers restricting reimbursement for branded biologics unless the biosimilar is proven inferior. In response, the company may need to consider a 15-20% price reduction to retain coverage and maintain market share.
- **Patent Defense and Lifecycle Management:** The company is pursuing patent litigation to extend exclusivity on its formulation patent for the oncology biologic. Additionally, a combination therapy involving the biologic is in development, which could extend market protection by an additional two years.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Risk of Market Erosion:** If biosimilar competitors are in advanced stages of development, with expected market entry soon after patent expiration, the company faces a high risk of significant market erosion. Rapid market share loss and revenue declines are likely unless defensive strategies are implemented.
- **Moderate Risk of Erosion:** If biosimilars are in earlier stages of development or face regulatory or manufacturing challenges, the risk is moderate. The company may have more time to prepare defenses, such as patent litigation or pricing adjustments.
- **Low Risk of Erosion:** If the company’s biologics have extended patents or biosimilars face regulatory hurdles, the immediate risk of market erosion is low. The company can focus on long-term lifecycle management strategies to protect market share.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Strengthen Patent Protection and Defense:** Pursue patent litigation or develop secondary patents that cover formulation improvements, delivery mechanisms, or new indications. This can extend market exclusivity beyond the original patent expiration.
2. **Implement Competitive Pricing Strategies:** Develop competitive pricing strategies that balance revenue preservation with the need to compete with lower-cost biosimilars.
67 Offering discounts, rebates, or pricing agreements with payers can help retain market share.
3.   **Enhance Brand Loyalty Through Patient and Provider Education:** Invest in patient education programs that emphasize the clinical benefits, safety, and efficacy of the branded biologic compared to biosimilars. Educate healthcare providers on the differences between the original biologic and biosimilars, focusing on therapeutic equivalence and long-term safety data.
4.   **Expand Patient Support Programs:** Strengthen patient support programs to improve patient adherence and loyalty to the branded biologic. These programs may include financial assistance, access to patient education, and personalized treatment support.
5.   **Develop New Indications or Reformulations:** Invest in developing new indications for existing biologics or reformulating biologics to improve efficacy, convenience, or safety. New formulations or delivery mechanisms can extend the product lifecycle and delay biosimilar competition.
6.   **Monitor Biosimilar Market Entry and Adjust Strategy:** Continuously monitor the progress of biosimilars in development, including clinical trial results and regulatory submissions. Adjust the company’s competitive strategy as needed, including preparing for early market entry of biosimilars by securing payer agreements and adjusting pricing.
