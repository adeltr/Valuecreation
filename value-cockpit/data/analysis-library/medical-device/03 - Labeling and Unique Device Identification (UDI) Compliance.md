# 3 · Labeling and Unique Device Identification (UDI) Compliance Assessment

> **Part:** [[A - Regulatory & Compliance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Medical Device Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Ensure that all labeling meets applicable regulatory requirements, and confirm that Unique Device Identification (UDI) systems are properly implemented for each device. This includes verifying that device labels are accurate, compliant, and traceable across different markets.


## 📊 Data required
- Current labeling artwork, product packaging, and instructions for use (IFU)
- Applicable regulatory guidelines (for example, FDA’s UDI Rule, EU MDR requirements)
- List of device product codes and device identifiers (DI)
- Label control procedures and records of label revisions
- UDI assignment records and database submission details (for example, GUDID in the US)
- Any known complaints or post-market feedback related to labeling issues


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify relevant UDI and labeling regulations. Determine the specific requirements for each target market, including UDI standards, language requirements, and required symbols/warnings.
2. Gather all current device labels and IFUs. Collect product labels, packaging inserts, and electronic labeling (if applicable) to ensure comprehensive assessment.
3. Check label content against regulations. Verify that mandatory information (such as device name, intended use, manufacturer details, symbols, and warnings) is clearly presented and compliant.
4. Assess the UDI markings. Confirm that UDI codes are correctly assigned, formatted, and placed on labels or direct parts where required, following standards like GS1 or HIBCC.
5. Review UDI database submissions. Evaluate whether required information has been accurately submitted to regulatory databases (for example, FDA GUDID, EUDAMED) and is kept current.
6. Check version control and labeling SOPs. Confirm that labeling processes include procedures for updating and controlling changes, with documented revision histories.

7. Interview or survey stakeholders. Speak with packaging and regulatory teams to gauge their understanding of UDI and labeling requirements.
8. Identify any labeling gaps or inconsistencies. Note issues such as missing elements, unclear language, or noncompliant symbols.
9. Prepare a compliance action plan. Summarize findings, recommend corrective actions, and establish timelines and responsibilities.


## 📤 Output format
- A written assessment report with a list of identified noncompliance or improvement areas
- A gap analysis chart mapping current labeling content vs. regulatory requirements
- UDI compliance checklist indicating status of labeling and database submissions
- An action plan outlining corrective steps, responsible owners, and completion dates


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Labels that fully meet regulatory criteria, including correct UDI markings, indicate strong compliance.
- Missing or incorrect labeling elements, inadequate language translations, or incomplete UDI records signal compliance risk.
- Repeated labeling or UDI errors could reflect broader document control or quality system weaknesses.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
- Establish a clear labeling SOP that addresses all regulatory requirements and includes a robust change control process.
- Provide regular training to ensure teams understand evolving UDI and labeling regulations across global markets.
- Implement software solutions for label design, version control, and UDI database submissions to reduce manual errors.
- Conduct periodic internal audits to verify the accuracy and compliance of labeling and UDI submissions.
- Collaborate with cross-functional teams (for example, regulatory, manufacturing, logistics) to maintain synchronized updates.
