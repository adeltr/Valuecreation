# 15 · Material Handling and Internal Logistics Efficiency

> **Part:** [[A - Operations]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Manufacturing Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Material Handling and Internal Logistics Efficiency analysis is to evaluate the movement, storage, and flow of materials within a manufacturing facility to ensure that they are handled in the most efficient, cost-effective, and safe manner. Optimizing material handling and internal logistics can lead to reduced cycle times, lower labor costs, and improved productivity by minimizing unnecessary movements and streamlining the flow of materials from receiving to production and dispatch.


## 📊 Data required
- **Material Flow Data:** Information on how materials move from receiving to production, storage, and final dispatch.
- **Cycle Times:** The time it takes to move materials through various stages of the production process.
- **Distance Data:** The distance materials travel within the facility between different workstations, storage areas, and production lines.
- **Labor and Equipment Utilization:** Data on the usage of labor and material handling equipment, such as forklifts, conveyors, and carts.
- **Storage Utilization:** Information on the layout and usage of storage areas for raw materials, WIP (work-in-progress), and finished goods.
- **Material Handling Costs:** Costs associated with moving materials, including labor, equipment maintenance, and transportation within the facility.
- **Handling Times and Delays:** Time taken for material handling activities and any delays or inefficiencies in the process.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Map Material Flow:** ○ Create a detailed map showing how materials move throughout the facility. This should include routes from receiving, through production, storage, and dispatch. Identify all material handling equipment used at each stage (e.g., forklifts, conveyors, manual handling).
2. **Measure Cycle Times and Handling Times:** ○ Record the time it takes for materials to move between stages of the production process, including loading, transport, and unloading times. This will help identify bottlenecks or delays in the material handling process.
3. Analyze Material Movement Efficiency:
55 ○ Evaluate the distance materials travel within the facility. Long travel distances or unnecessary movements between storage and production areas can increase handling time and costs. Look for opportunities to shorten routes or consolidate material handling tasks.
4. **Assess Labor and Equipment Utilization:** ○ Determine how efficiently labor and material handling equipment are being used. High utilization may indicate overuse, while low utilization may suggest inefficiencies or idle equipment. Track how often equipment is in use and whether labor is allocated effectively to material handling tasks.
5. **Examine Storage Utilization:** ○ Review the layout and organization of storage areas to ensure optimal usage. Materials should be stored in locations that minimize handling time and allow for easy access to production lines. Ensure that space is being used efficiently, and consider reorganizing or reducing storage areas if they are contributing to excessive movement or delays.
6. **Identify Bottlenecks and Delays:** ○ Identify any points in the material handling process where delays occur. Common bottlenecks include waiting for available equipment, inefficient routes, or poor coordination between departments. Quantify the impact of these delays on overall production time and material flow.
7. **Evaluate Safety and Ergonomics:** ○ Assess whether materials are being handled safely and in compliance with ergonomic standards. Poor material handling practices can lead to worker injuries and lost time, so ensuring that equipment and procedures minimize risks is critical.
8. **Calculate Material Handling Costs:** ○ Calculate the total cost of material handling activities, including labor, equipment, and transportation. Break down these costs by activity (e.g., receiving, transport to production, moving WIP, final dispatch) to identify areas for cost reduction.
9. **Implement Process Improvements:** ○ Based on the analysis, recommend improvements to material handling and internal logistics processes. This could include reorganizing storage areas, using automated material handling systems, optimizing equipment allocation, or introducing lean logistics practices to reduce waste and improve efficiency.


## 📤 Output format
56
- **Material Flow Map:** A visual map showing the movement of materials throughout the facility, including key routes, storage areas, and equipment used.
- **Cycle Time and Handling Time Report:** A table showing the time taken for each material handling task, from receiving to production and dispatch.
- **Labor and Equipment Utilization Chart:** A chart showing how often material handling equipment and labor are used, including idle time and overutilization.
- **Storage Utilization Analysis:** A report summarizing how effectively storage space is being used, with recommendations for reorganization or optimization.
- **Cost Breakdown:** A financial report detailing material handling costs by activity, including labor, equipment, and transportation.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Long Material Travel Distances:** Long travel distances between storage areas and production lines indicate inefficiencies in the layout or handling process. These should be reduced to streamline material flow and reduce handling times.
- **High Handling Times:** Long handling times suggest that materials are being moved inefficiently or that bottlenecks exist in the handling process. Improvements in routes, equipment, or process coordination can help reduce these times.
- **Underutilized or Overutilized Equipment:** Underutilized equipment may indicate that material handling resources are not being used effectively. Conversely, overutilized equipment may suggest a need for additional resources or better scheduling.
- **Excessive Handling Costs:** High material handling costs point to inefficiencies in the internal logistics system, such as excessive labor use, unnecessary transportation, or outdated equipment. Reducing handling costs can improve overall production efficiency.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Reorganize Facility Layout:** ○ Optimize the layout of the facility to reduce travel distances for materials. Place storage areas closer to production lines and ensure that materials flow logically through the facility without unnecessary backtracking.
2. **Implement Automated Material Handling Systems:** ○ Use automation to improve material flow and reduce reliance on manual handling. Automated guided vehicles (AGVs), conveyors, and robotic systems can increase efficiency and reduce handling times.
3. Introduce Lean Logistics Practices:
57 ○ Apply lean logistics principles, such as just-in-time (JIT) inventory management, to reduce the amount of material being handled and minimize the time spent moving materials between stages of production.
4. **Improve Equipment Allocation and Scheduling:** ○ Ensure that material handling equipment is allocated based on demand. Use scheduling systems to optimize the use of forklifts, conveyors, and other equipment to avoid idle time or overuse.
5. **Optimize Storage Organization:** ○ Reorganize storage areas to improve accessibility and reduce the time spent moving materials between storage and production. Consider implementing racking systems or using vertical storage solutions to maximize space utilization.
6. **Train Employees on Efficient Material Handling:** ○ Provide training for employees on best practices for material handling, including safe lifting techniques, efficient use of equipment, and proper storage practices. This can help reduce delays and improve handling times.
7. **Monitor and Track Performance:** ○ Continuously monitor material handling performance metrics, such as cycle times and equipment utilization. Use this data to identify areas for ongoing improvement and ensure that material handling processes remain efficient as production demands change.
