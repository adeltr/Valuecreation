# 25 · Vehicle Repair Cost Inflation & Parts Availability Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Claims & Loss Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate how escalating repair costs and fluctuating parts availability affect claim severity, turnaround times, and overall profitability, and determine strategies to mitigate these impacts.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical vehicle repair cost data (labor rates, parts prices, average repair times)
- Parts availability metrics (backorder rates, delivery times)
- Claims data (frequency, severity, incurred losses)
- Supplier or vendor agreements detailing pricing and service-level expectations
- Industry or competitor benchmarks on repair costs and supply chain trends


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather historical repair cost data over the past several years, including labor rates and parts pricing. Look for trends in average repair times, especially for common repair types (e.g., collision, glass, bodywork).
2. Analyze parts availability metrics such as backorder rates or average delivery times. Identify any specific vehicle makes, models, or part categories that cause frequent delays.
3. Segment your claims data by repair type, severity, and vehicle category (e.g., economy, luxury). Compare claim severity over time to see if inflationary pressures and parts delays correlate with rising costs.
4. Review current supplier agreements and vendor performance to determine if contractual obligations are being met (e.g., guaranteed delivery windows, price caps). Flag any consistent breaches or areas for renegotiation.
5. Benchmark your results against industry or competitor data if available, focusing on average repair costs, turnaround times, and supply chain resilience. Look for significant variances that indicate potential inefficiencies.
6. Investigate alternative parts sourcing or repair methodologies, such as aftermarket components or in-house repair facilities. Estimate potential cost savings and impacts on repair quality and claim satisfaction.
7. Assess the impact of inflation on both labor and parts, noting how much of the price increase is passed on to your organization vs. policyholders. Use this data to forecast future claim costs and premium adjustments.
8. Document findings and propose actionable steps, such as renegotiating vendor contracts, exploring alternative parts suppliers, or adjusting underwriting 55 guidelines. Present these to operations, claims, and product teams for implementation.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts comparing historical and current repair costs, parts delivery times, and claim severity
- Written summary highlighting major cost drivers, supply chain challenges, and relevant trends
- Recommendations for sourcing strategies, vendor management improvements, and underwriting/pricing updates


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Consistent increases in repair costs and extended parts delays can drive higher claim severity and longer settlement times, requiring premium adjustments or new sourcing strategies.
- Significant variance in parts availability among certain vehicle makes or models may indicate a need for specialized underwriting or vendor relationships.
- Benchmark comparisons revealing disproportionate cost inflation suggest opportunities to renegotiate vendor agreements or enhance repair process efficiencies.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Diversify supplier networks and cultivate strategic partnerships to reduce dependency on a single vendor or region.
2. Explore alternative solutions like certified aftermarket parts and mobile repair services to lower costs and speed up turnaround times.
3. Continuously monitor repair cost indices and global supply chain trends to anticipate and plan for inflationary pressures.
4. Revisit underwriting and rating strategies for vehicle models or segments known to have ongoing parts supply issues.
