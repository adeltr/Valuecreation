# KPI — Restaurant

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Travel & Hospitality]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Hospitality Company - Analyse]]

49 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Alcohol License Utilization Rate | For restaurants that have invested in an alcohol license, this KPI measures the percentage of alcohol sales out of total sales to assess if the investment in the license is being fully utilized. |
| Average Check (Ticket) Size | This is calculated by dividing total sales by the number of customers served. It's an important KPI as it can indicate menu pricing power and the customers' willingness to spend more per visit. |
| Average Kitchen Space Utilization | This measures how effectively a restaurant uses its kitchen space. Low utilization may indicate inefficiencies, while high utilization may limit the ability to handle peak demand periods. |
| Capacity Utilization Rate | This is the percentage of a restaurant’s total seat capacity that is being used at a given time. High capacity utilization can indicate strong demand, while consistently low capacity utilization might indicate issues with location, pricing, or quality. |
| Catering Sales as a Percentage of Total Sales | For restaurants that offer catering services, tracking this KPI can show the impact of those services on the overall business. |
| Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) as a Percentage of Food Sales | This is a more specific variation of food and beverage costs as a percentage of revenue, focusing specifically on the costs associated with preparing the food sold. |
| Cover Rate | The number of meals served (covers) over a specific time period. This can provide insights into restaurant demand and be used for staffing and inventory planning. |
| Cross-Selling Success Rate | The rate at which staff members are able to sell complementary items (like appetizers with mains, or desserts and coffees after a meal). This can provide insights into staff selling skills and the appeal of additional menu items. |
| Dine-In vs. Take-Out Sales | Especially relevant in the COVID-19 era and beyond, this KPI tracks the proportion of sales generated from dine-in customers versus take-out or delivery. It can inform operational and marketing strategies in response to changing consumer behavior. |
| Drive-Thru Sales as a Percentage of Total Sales | For fast-food or quick-service restaurants with drive-thru capabilities, this KPI measures the contribution of drive-thru operations to total sales. |
| Food and Beverage Costs as a Percentage of Revenue | Restaurants must control food and beverage costs to maintain profitability. This KPI is the cost of these items divided by total restaurant sales. A rising percentage may signal increasing cost pressures or inefficiencies. |
| Food Cost Variance | The difference between the standard (expected) food costs and the actual food costs. This KPI can help identify issues with portion control, theft, waste, or supplier pricing. |
| Franchise Operating Margin | For franchised restaurants, this measures the franchise's operational profitability after deducting costs associated with food, labor, and other direct expenses. |
| Gift Card Redemption Rate | For restaurants that offer gift cards, tracking the redemption rate can provide insights into customer engagement and potential future sales. |
| Gluten-Free Menu Sales as a Percentage of Total Sales | With increasing dietary awareness, some restaurants offer gluten-free options. This KPI measures the performance of these menu items. |
| Gross Profit per Employee | This measures the profitability of a restaurant in relation to its staff size. It can help in assessing staff productivity and the effectiveness of restaurant operations. |
| Liquor Sales as a Percentage of Total Sales | For restaurants that serve alcohol, this KPI can be critical as liquor sales often come with higher margins than food sales. An increasing percentage can signal a profitable revenue stream. |
| Loyalty Program Participation Rate | The percentage of sales linked to a restaurant's loyalty program. A high rate can indicate effective customer retention strategies. |
| Menu Item Performance | This KPI tracks the sales of each menu item. High-performing items may drive sales and profitability, while underperforming items might need to be reevaluated. Menu item performance can influence menu design, pricing, and promotional strategies. |
| Menu Item Profitability | This KPI measures the profit margin of each item on the menu, helping to identify which dishes are driving profitability and which may need to be reevaluated or repriced. |
| Mobile App Sales as a Percentage of Total Sales | For restaurants with mobile ordering capabilities, this KPI measures the contribution of mobile app sales to total sales and can indicate the success of a restaurant's mobile strategy. |
| Number of Loyalty Program Members | This can provide insights into the potential for repeat business and the effectiveness of customer loyalty initiatives. |
| Off-Peak Sales as a Percentage of Total Sales | For restaurants with distinct peak times, it's useful to know what percentage of their sales comes from off-peak hours. This can help management optimize staffing and operational decisions. |
| Peak Waiting Time | The maximum time customers wait for a table during peak hours. Long wait times could deter customers and negatively affect the dining experience, while short wait times could indicate underutilized capacity. |
| Percentage of Bookings from Online Platforms | For restaurants that accept online reservations, this KPI measures the importance of online platforms to the business and can help inform digital strategies. |
| Percentage of Locally-Sourced Ingredients | For restaurants that emphasize sustainability or farm-to-table concepts, this KPI can be crucial. A higher percentage can signify a stronger commitment to local sourcing. |
| Percentage of Recyclable or Compostable Waste | For restaurants with sustainability goals, this KPI measures the proportion of waste that is either recyclable or compostable. Increasing this percentage could be a part of a restaurant's environmental initiatives. |
| Percentage of Sustainable or Fair Trade Products | For restaurants emphasizing ethical sourcing, this KPI measures the proportion of food items sourced from sustainable or fair trade sources. |
| Prep Time | The average time taken to prepare a meal after an order is placed. Long prep times can slow down service and lead to customer dissatisfaction, particularly in fast food or quick service segments. |
| Rate of New Menu Item Adoption | When a restaurant introduces new menu items, this KPI tracks the rate at which those items are being ordered by customers. It can inform decisions about menu changes and promotions. |
| Ratio of Corporate to Franchise Locations | For restaurant chains, this KPI gives insight into the business model and strategy. A higher ratio of franchise locations may indicate a focus on growth through franchising. |
| Ratio of Front of House to Back of House Staff | This KPI gives insight into how resources are allocated between customer-facing roles and kitchen roles. The optimal ratio can vary depending on the restaurant's service model. |
| Ratio of Indoor to Outdoor Seating | Particularly relevant for restaurants in climates with seasonal weather, this KPI can inform decisions about space utilization and capacity planning. |
| Ratio of Repeat to New Customers | A measure of customer loyalty. A higher ratio indicates that a restaurant retains its customers effectively, which is typically more cost-efficient than acquiring new customers. |
| Reservation No-Shows as a Percentage of Total Reservations | For higher-end restaurants where reservations are common, tracking the percentage of no-shows can help manage table availability and improve operational efficiency. |
| Restaurant Atmosphere Rating | This can be evaluated through customer surveys or online reviews. A pleasant atmosphere can contribute to customer satisfaction and repeat business. |
| Restaurant-Level Operating Margin | This is a profitability measure that considers costs directly tied to restaurant operations, like food and beverage costs, labor, and rent. It can indicate a restaurant's efficiency and is particularly useful in the quick-service restaurant (QSR) and casual dining sectors. |
| Revenue per Available Seat Hour (RevPASH) | This KPI measures the revenue generated per available seat per hour. It's a way to gauge the efficiency of a restaurant in generating revenue considering both its capacity and operating hours. |
| Sales by Daypart | In the restaurant industry, the day is often divided into sections like breakfast, lunch, dinner, and late-night. Analyzing sales by these "dayparts" can help optimize menu offerings, staffing, and promotional efforts. |
| Same-Store Sales Growth (SSSG) | This is a measure of sales growth from a company's existing restaurants over a certain period, typically one year. This metric helps analysts understand how well the restaurant's core locations are performing by eliminating the impact of new restaurant openings or closings. |
| Seasonal Sales Fluctuation | Some restaurants may have higher sales in certain seasons due to their menu offerings or location. Understanding these fluctuations can help in inventory management and staffing decisions. |
| Seating Efficiency | This KPI refers to the number of guests per total seats in a restaurant over a given period. High seating efficiency implies a good balance between demand and seating capacity. |
| Self-Order Kiosk Sales as a Percentage of Total Sales | As technology becomes more integrated into the restaurant industry, monitoring the percentage of sales made through self-ordering kiosks can help evaluate customer acceptance of this technology and its impact on operational efficiency. |
| Table Turnover Rate | This is the number of times a restaurant can seat new guests at a table over a certain period. It's a good measure of operational efficiency. A high rate might indicate good service and food, but it could also imply that the dining experience is rushed. |
| Time Spent per Dine-In Customer | This KPI indicates the average amount of time a customer spends dining in the restaurant. Longer times could indicate customer satisfaction and enjoyment, but could also slow table turnover rates. |
| Time to Serve | This measures the average time it takes from when a customer places an order to when the food is served. This KPI is critical for customer satisfaction, especially in quick-service restaurants. |
| Tip Percentage | Particularly in countries where tipping is customary, tracking the average tip percentage can provide insights into customer satisfaction and server performance. |
| Upselling Success Rate | This is the rate at which staff members are able to sell add-ons, more expensive items, or specials to guests. This KPI can give insight into staff selling skills and the appeal of additional offerings. |
| Vegan/Vegetarian Menu Sales as Percentage of Total Sales | For restaurants offering these options, tracking this KPI can provide insights into customer preferences and the performance of these offerings. |
