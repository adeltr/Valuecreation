# 4 · Loan Portfolio Quality and Concentration Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Lending and Credit Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Commercial Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
Assess the overall health of the loan portfolio by evaluating credit quality indicators and identifying potential concentration risks that could impact the bank’s financial stability and performance.


## 📊 Data required
- Total loan balances by segment (e.g., SME, corporate, commercial real estate)
- Loan classifications (e.g., performing, past due, non-performing)
- Delinquency rates and Non-Performing Loans (NPLs)
- Loan loss reserves/provisions data
- Industry/sector exposures and associated limits
- Collateral details (optional but helpful for deeper risk assessment)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Aggregate loan data by major segments or industry sectors. Identify the outstanding balances and the relative percentage of each segment in the total loan portfolio.
Step 2: Determine the distribution of loans according to their credit quality status (e.g., current, past due, non-performing). Calculate Non-Performing Loan (NPL) Ratio = (Total Non-Performing Loans / Total Gross Loans) x 100 Step 3: Compare the NPL ratio for each segment or sector to identify pockets of higher risk. Track changes over time to assess whether credit quality is improving or deteriorating.
Step 4: Review concentrations by industry, geography, or borrower type. This helps to pinpoint potential vulnerabilities if a particular sector experiences economic stress.
Step 5: Evaluate loan loss provisioning and coverage levels. Coverage Ratio = (Loan Loss Reserves / Total Non-Performing Loans) x 100 This shows how well the bank’s reserves cover anticipated loan losses.
12 Step 6: Benchmark findings against regulatory guidelines, internal risk appetite limits, and peer banks to determine if the bank’s concentration or credit risk profile is within acceptable thresholds.


## 📤 Output format
- Tabular breakdown of the loan portfolio by segments, risk classification, and industry exposures
- Trend charts showing NPL and delinquency rates over time
- Heat map or chart depicting concentration by industry or geography


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A rising NPL ratio or spike in specific segments indicates potential weaknesses in credit underwriting or external economic pressures.
- Excessive concentration in certain industries or borrower types heightens vulnerability to sector-specific downturns.
- A strong coverage ratio suggests the bank is adequately reserving for future losses, while a low coverage ratio may indicate insufficient provisioning.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Strengthen credit underwriting policies for high-risk or overexposed segments.
2. Diversify the loan portfolio to reduce dependence on any single industry or geography.
3. Implement rigorous portfolio monitoring and early warning systems to identify emerging credit risks.
4. Revisit loan pricing strategies to ensure adequate returns for higher-risk segments.
5. Review and adjust loan loss provisioning practices to remain aligned with evolving credit risk profiles.
