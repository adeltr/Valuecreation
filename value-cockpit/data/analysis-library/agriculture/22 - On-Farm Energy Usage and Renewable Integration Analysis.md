# 22 · On-Farm Energy Usage and Renewable Integration Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Environmental Sustainability & Resource]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Agriculture Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate current energy consumption patterns, identify opportunities for efficiency improvements, and assess the feasibility and potential benefits of integrating renewable energy systems on the farm.


## 📊 Data required
- Records of on-farm energy usage (electricity, diesel, propane, etc.)
- Equipment and machinery inventory (age, horsepower, typical operation hours)
- Utility bills or invoices detailing costs and usage patterns
- Local climate data (sunlight hours, wind speed) for renewable potential
- Any existing on-farm renewable energy systems and their performance data
- Regulatory or incentive programs (tax credits, grants) relevant to renewable installations


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Gather energy consumption data. Collect past utility bills and records of fuel consumption for machinery, vehicles, and heating. Organize this information by month or season to identify usage trends.
Step 2: Identify major energy loads. List equipment and processes that consume the most energy (e.g., irrigation pumps, dairy refrigeration units, grain dryers) and note their operational schedules.
Step 3: Evaluate energy efficiency measures. Check equipment age and maintenance history. Determine whether upgrading to more efficient models or optimizing usage schedules could reduce overall consumption.
Step 4: Assess renewable energy potential. Investigate site-specific conditions for solar (roof or ground-mount), wind, or biogas. Factor in local regulations, net metering rules, and possible funding mechanisms.
Step 5: Conduct a cost–benefit analysis. Compare upfront investment (equipment, installation) and operating costs for renewables or efficiency upgrades against projected energy savings and incentives.
49 Step 6: Evaluate implementation logistics. Consider system sizing, technical requirements, and potential disruptions during installation. Review maintenance needs and required skill sets for ongoing operations.
Step 7: Summarize findings and draft an action plan. Combine insights from energy audits and renewable assessments. Propose a phased approach that addresses the most impactful energy efficiency measures and outlines clear steps for renewable integration.


## 📤 Output format
- A tabular summary of current energy usage, costs, and major load areas
- A concise report comparing different renewable options (solar, wind, biogas) and their financial viability
- Recommendations for both short-term efficiency improvements and long-term renewable adoption strategies


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High energy usage in specific areas (e.g., irrigation pumping) signals prime targets for efficiency improvements or partial load offset with renewables.
- Strong ROI and attractive payback periods indicate the viability of solar panels, wind turbines, or biogas systems.
- Continuous monitoring of energy data can confirm whether newly installed systems achieve projected savings.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Install energy meters on key equipment to track consumption and pinpoint efficiency gaps.
2. Update or retrofit legacy machinery with more efficient motors or controls.
3. Explore solar or wind power installations for partial or full farm load offsets.
4. Investigate biogas from manure or organic waste if livestock operations produce sufficient feedstock.
5. Leverage government grants, loans, or tax incentives to reduce upfront costs of renewable systems.
6. Train staff on energy-saving practices, such as operating machinery during off-peak hours.
