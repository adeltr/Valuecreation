# 17 · Product Texture and Sensory Evaluation

> **Part:** [[A - Product and Research & Development (R&D)]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Cosmetics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Product Texture and Sensory Evaluation is to assess the sensory characteristics of a cosmetic product, including its texture, feel during application, absorption, and overall user experience. This analysis ensures that the product delivers a pleasant and consistent sensory experience, aligning with consumer preferences and expectations. Sensory evaluation helps to identify areas for improvement in terms of product feel, spreadability, and absorption while enhancing overall user satisfaction.


## 📊 Data required
- Product formulation details, particularly ingredients affecting texture (e.g., emulsifiers, thickeners, oils).
- Initial texture analysis data (e.g., viscosity, smoothness, spreadability).
- Consumer feedback and sensory perception studies.
- Competitor product benchmarks for texture and sensory characteristics.
- Temperature and humidity stability data (e.g., how texture holds up under different conditions).
- Rheology data (e.g., flow properties, consistency).
- Absorption rate testing results.
- Skin type-specific sensory feedback (e.g., oily, dry, sensitive skin).
- Long-term texture stability data (e.g., does the texture change over time or after repeated use?).
- Fragrance and overall sensory appeal (if applicable).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Review product formulation:** a. Analyze the ingredients in the product that contribute to its texture and sensory experience (e.g., emulsifiers for smoothness, thickeners for consistency, silicones for slip). b. Ensure that the formulation is designed to deliver the desired texture (e.g., lightweight gel, rich cream, smooth serum).
2. **Conduct initial texture analysis:** a. Measure the viscosity and flow properties of the product using a viscometer or rheometer to assess its thickness and spreadability. b. Record texture characteristics such as smoothness, stickiness, oiliness, and overall consistency.
3. **Perform sensory evaluation with a consumer panel:** a. Recruit a panel of consumers with diverse skin types (e.g., dry, oily, sensitive) to evaluate the sensory attributes of the product. b. Ask panelists to apply the product and provide feedback on how it feels during and after application, focusing on factors like smoothness, absorption, and greasiness.
58 c. Measure consumer preferences using a Likert scale or other rating systems to quantify sensory perceptions (e.g., 1-10 scale for texture satisfaction).
4. **Evaluate absorption rate:** a. Test the product’s absorption time on different skin types by applying a set amount and measuring the time it takes to fully absorb. b. Document how the product feels on the skin after absorption (e.g., lightweight, greasy, matte, dewy).
5. **Assess product stability under different conditions:** a. Store product samples under various environmental conditions (e.g., room temperature, high heat, high humidity) to assess whether the texture changes over time. b. Document any changes in texture consistency, such as thickening, separation, or melting, under extreme conditions.
6. **Benchmark against competitor products:** a. Compare the texture and sensory characteristics of the product to similar products from competitors. b. Identify whether the product offers a superior sensory experience in terms of spreadability, smoothness, absorption, and overall feel.
7. **Measure long-term texture stability:** a. Test the product over time (e.g., after 1 month, 3 months) to ensure that its texture remains consistent and pleasant over its intended shelf life. b. Monitor for changes in texture or sensory characteristics, particularly if the product has been exposed to air or light (e.g., if it becomes too thick, runny, or grainy).
8. **Collect feedback on skin feel after use:** a. Ask consumers to evaluate how their skin feels immediately after application, as well as after several hours (e.g., moisturized, sticky, oily). b. Evaluate the skin’s appearance post-application, such as whether it appears hydrated, matte, or shiny.
9. **Analyze fragrance and sensory appeal (if applicable):** a. Evaluate how the product’s fragrance contributes to the overall sensory experience. b. Ensure that the fragrance aligns with consumer preferences and enhances the perceived efficacy of the product.
10. **Prepare a sensory evaluation report:** a. Compile the results from the consumer panel and texture analysis into a comprehensive report. b. Include recommendations for improving the product’s texture or sensory appeal based on feedback.


## 📤 Output format
- A table summarizing consumer feedback on texture characteristics (e.g., smoothness, absorption, greasiness).
- A sensory evaluation report highlighting key findings from the consumer panel, including overall satisfaction scores.
- A comparison chart benchmarking the product’s sensory characteristics against competitors.

- A table showing viscosity and absorption rate data for different skin types.
- A report on product texture stability under various environmental conditions, including any changes in consistency or feel.
- A summary of any improvements needed based on the evaluation (e.g., texture refinement, fragrance adjustments).


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Positive feedback on texture and sensory appeal:** If consumers rate the product highly for texture smoothness, absorption, and overall feel, the product is well-positioned to meet market demand for sensory satisfaction.
- **Issues with absorption or feel:** If consumers report that the product feels too greasy, sticky, or slow to absorb, this may indicate a need for reformulation to improve user experience.
- **Stability concerns:** If the product’s texture changes over time or under certain conditions (e.g., thickening, separation), it may require reformulation or improved packaging to ensure long-term consistency.
- **Competitor benchmarking:** If competitor products offer a superior texture or sensory experience, adjustments may be needed to keep the product competitive in the market.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Reformulate for better texture and absorption:** If consumers report issues with absorption, greasiness, or stickiness, consider adjusting the formulation to include lighter emollients, fast-absorbing oils, or better emulsifiers.
2. **Enhance packaging to preserve texture:** If texture changes are due to exposure to air or light, improve the packaging (e.g., air-tight, UV-protected containers) to better preserve the product’s consistency over time.
3. **Adjust fragrance to enhance sensory appeal:** If fragrance plays a significant role in consumer experience, consider fine-tuning the scent to be more appealing or match the product’s intended use (e.g., a fresh, energizing scent for daytime use).
4. **Improve skin type-specific performance:** If certain skin types (e.g., oily skin) report issues with the product’s feel, develop variations of the product that cater specifically to different skin needs, such as a matte version for oily skin or a richer version for dry skin.
5. **Benchmark and refine based on competitors:** Regularly benchmark the product against top competitors to ensure that the texture and sensory characteristics remain competitive in the market. Use insights from competitor analysis to drive future formulation improvements.
6. **Educate consumers on proper use:** Provide usage instructions that maximize the product’s texture benefits (e.g., how to layer the product, recommended amounts to use, best times for application) to improve overall user experience.
