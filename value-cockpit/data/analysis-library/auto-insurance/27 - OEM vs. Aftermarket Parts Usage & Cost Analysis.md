# 27 · OEM vs. Aftermarket Parts Usage & Cost Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Claims & Loss Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine the cost impact, quality implications, and customer satisfaction effects of using Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) parts versus aftermarket parts in auto repairs, and align parts usage strategies with claim severity and service objectives.


## 📊 Data required
- Claims records specifying whether OEM or aftermarket parts were used
- Parts cost data (unit prices, shipping costs, discounts) broken down by OEM and aftermarket categories
- Repair duration metrics (turnaround times, rework rates) for both parts types
- Customer satisfaction or feedback related to repair outcomes
- Repair vendor contracts and labor rate agreements


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify relevant claim files that include a clear record of parts usage (OEM or aftermarket). Compile total repair costs, parts costs, and any associated rework or warranty data.
2. Calculate average parts cost by dividing total parts expenses by the number of repairs for OEM vs. aftermarket usage. Compare the difference in costs across vehicle make, model, and severity of damage.
3. Assess claim severity and repair duration by correlating parts usage with average repair times and total incurred claim amounts. Look for significant gaps where OEM parts lead to faster or slower repairs.
4. Evaluate rework rates by calculating Rework Rate = (Number of Repairs Requiring Follow-up / Total Repairs in Category) x 100. Determine if aftermarket parts show higher rework or warranty issues than OEM parts.
5. Review customer satisfaction data to see if policyholders receiving OEM parts report different satisfaction scores versus those with aftermarket replacements. Note recurring complaints about part fit, quality, or durability.
6. Benchmark costs and usage patterns against industry or competitor data if available. Identify any notable deviations in OEM vs. aftermarket adoption, cost advantages, or quality concerns.
7. Analyze potential cost savings by estimating how shifting parts usage could affect overall claim severity, repair times, and rework costs. Weigh these against any potential declines in satisfaction or vehicle resale value.

8. Document findings and propose strategic adjustments to parts usage guidelines, supplier relationships, or underwriting coverage rules. Present this analysis to claims, product, and executive teams for review and decision-making.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts contrasting OEM and aftermarket parts costs, repair times, and rework rates
- Written summary detailing customer satisfaction findings and any relevant regional or vehicle-specific trends
- Financial impact estimates illustrating how a change in parts usage could influence claim severity and overall profitability


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A consistently higher cost for OEM parts may be offset by fewer reworks, better fit, and potentially higher customer satisfaction.
- Significant savings with aftermarket parts could be undermined if rework rates or dissatisfaction lead to increased claim costs.
- Notable vehicle-model variations in cost or fit issues suggest the need for nuanced parts usage strategies rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Establish clear guidelines for when OEM parts are mandatory (e.g., safety components) versus when aftermarket parts are permissible.
2. Negotiate volume discounts or service-level agreements with both OEM and reputable aftermarket suppliers.
3. Incorporate customer preference data and offer tiered coverage options, allowing policyholders to choose OEM vs. aftermarket parts.
4. Monitor technological advancements (e.g., 3D-printed parts) that may further expand cost-effective and high-quality repair options.
