# KPI — Consumer Packaged Goods

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Consumer & Retail]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Consumer Packaged Goods Company - Analyse]]

50 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| After-Sales Service Costs | Particularly important for the consumer electronics segment, this KPI tracks the costs associated with servicing products after sale, which can indicate product quality and impact on profitability. |
| Age Demographic Sales Breakdown | This KPI measures the sales distribution across different age demographics. It can provide insights into which age groups are more attracted to a company's products and help in product positioning and marketing. |
| Average Consumer Spend | This measures the average amount of money a consumer spends on a company's products in a given period. It can help understand the company's pricing strategy and consumers' buying behavior. |
| Average Stock-Out Duration | This KPI measures the average length of time products are out of stock. Long durations can lead to lost sales and reduced customer loyalty. |
| Brand Loyalty | Typically assessed via repeat purchase rate or a Net Promoter Score (NPS), it reflects customers' preference for a brand over competitors. High brand loyalty signifies strong customer retention and can lead to predictable revenue streams. |
| Channel Mix | This KPI measures the percentage of sales from each channel (e.g., physical retail, online, direct-to-consumer, wholesales). Changes in channel mix can indicate shifts in consumer buying behavior or the company's strategic focus. |
| Commodity Price Sensitivity | This KPI gauges the impact of commodity price changes (such as wheat, oil, or aluminum) on the company's cost of goods sold (COGS). Firms with high commodity price sensitivity are more exposed to price volatility. |
| Cost Per Impression (CPI) | This KPI is often used in the digital marketing space to measure the cost-effectiveness of a campaign. It shows how much it costs to earn each advertisement impression. |
| Cross-Selling Index | For companies with a wide range of products, this KPI measures the effectiveness of selling related products to existing customers, which can enhance customer value and increase sales. |
| Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) in Digital Sales | This is a specific indicator of the efficiency of a company's online marketing efforts. It measures the resources that a company must spend (on average) to acquire an additional customer through digital channels. |
| Customer Concentration Ratio | This measures the proportion of the company's revenue generated from its top customers. High customer concentration represents a risk if these customers reduce orders or change suppliers. |
| Customer Lifetime Value (CLTV) | In subscription-based or direct-to-consumer models, this KPI estimates the total revenue a company can reasonably expect from a single customer account throughout the relationship's duration. |
| Customer Retention Rate in Subscription Services | For CPG companies offering subscription services (like regular delivery of personal care or food items), the customer retention rate indicates the quality of service and customer satisfaction. |
| Days of Supply (DOS) | A measure of inventory efficiency, DOS calculates the number of days it would take to sell the current inventory at the current sales rate. High DOS could indicate overstocking or slow-moving goods, while low DOS might suggest stock-out risks. |
| Direct Store Delivery (DSD) Penetration | DSD is a method where products are delivered directly to stores, bypassing retailer distribution centers. Higher DSD penetration can imply superior freshness and availability but may also suggest higher distribution costs. |
| Frequency of Store Visits | This KPI measures how often customers are visiting the store to purchase the company's products. It can be used in conjunction with average basket size to understand customer buying behavior. |
| Ingredient Transparency Index | A metric relevant for food & beverage or personal care segments. It measures the extent of disclosure about the ingredients used in the product, reflecting the company's commitment to transparency. |
| Innovation Index | This KPI could be the proportion of revenue from products launched in the past X years. A high innovation index suggests that the company is effectively inventing and launching new products. |
| Lost Sales | This KPI measures the estimated revenue lost due to factors such as stock-outs or underproduction. It can provide insights into supply chain efficiency and demand forecasting accuracy. |
| Market Penetration | This KPI measures the percentage of the total available market that the company has captured. It indicates the popularity and acceptance of a company's products among potential customers. |
| Market Share Growth | A segment-specific KPI, it measures the growth of a company's market share within specific product categories. It's an indicator of competitiveness and acceptance of the company's products in the marketplace. |
| New Product Success Rate | This metric tracks the percentage of new products that meet or exceed their initial sales targets. It provides insight into a company's innovation capabilities and market acceptance of new products. |
| Nutritional Value Score | For food and beverage companies, this KPI measures the average nutritional value of their product portfolio. With consumers becoming more health-conscious, this KPI has gained significance in recent years. |
| Online Review Scores | In the era of e-commerce, this KPI measures the average rating a product receives on online retail platforms. It provides insights into product quality and customer satisfaction. |
| Out-of-Stock Rate | This rate measures the frequency at which a product is not available for purchase when a customer tries to buy it. A high out-of-stock rate could indicate demand forecasting errors, supply chain inefficiencies, or a popular product. |
| Packaging Innovation Efficiency | Measured by the success rate of new packaging introductions in driving sales or reducing costs. Packaging plays a critical role in the CPG industry in influencing customer buying decisions and improving sustainability. |
| Percentage of Ethically Sourced Products | With growing consumer awareness about ethical sourcing and fair trade, this KPI measures the proportion of a company's products sourced from certified ethical/fair-trade sources. |
| Price Per Unit/Weight/Volume | This KPI measures the average price of a product per unit/weight/volume, and can give insights into the pricing strategy of the company in comparison to competitors. |
| Private Label Share | This metric indicates the percentage of a company's sales coming from its private (or house) brand versus branded goods. It provides insight into the company's pricing power and competition level within the industry. |
| Product Cannibalization Rate | This KPI measures the impact of a new product introduction on the sales of existing products. A high rate could indicate that the new product is eating into the sales of the existing product, which could affect overall profitability. |
| Product Lifecycle Stage | This KPI classifies products into stages such as introduction, growth, maturity, and decline based on their sales trend. This helps in managing product portfolios and strategic planning. |
| Product Placement Effectiveness | This KPI measures the impact of product placement in media (movies, TV shows, video games) on brand awareness and sales. It's particularly relevant for consumer brands targeting younger demographics. |
| Product Recalls | The frequency and severity of product recalls can provide insights into the quality control measures of a company. Frequent or significant recalls might indicate serious operational issues. |
| Promotional Elasticity | This KPI measures the sales response to promotional activities such as discounts, coupons, or "buy one get one free" offers. A high promotional elasticity means the company's sales are significantly affected by promotions, which could impact profitability. |
| Rate of Sale (ROS) | This KPI measures how quickly a product is selling in a specific retail outlet. It helps understand the popularity of a product and can guide decisions around production and inventory management. |
| Retail Execution Compliance Rate | This KPI measures how well in-store promotions, displays, and other retail executions adhere to planned guidelines. It can indicate the effectiveness of a company's retail strategy and its relationship with retailers. |
| Retailer Dependence | This KPI measures the percentage of sales generated through one specific retailer or a group of retailers. High retailer dependence could increase the risk of sales decline if the relationship with the retailer sours or if the retailer faces difficulties. |
| Return Rate | In the consumer packaged goods industry, a lower return rate can indicate higher product quality and customer satisfaction. High return rates might signify issues with product quality, packaging, or customer expectations. |
| Route-to-Market Efficiency | For companies with direct distribution, this KPI assesses the efficiency of their distribution routes in terms of cost, time, and coverage. It can highlight potential areas for logistics improvement. |
| Sales Per Employee | This is a measure of productivity in the CPG industry, indicating how much revenue each employee generates. It can highlight operational efficiency when compared to industry peers. |
| Sales Through Organic Channels | This measures the percentage of sales generated through organic or natural product lines, which can be crucial for companies in the food and beverage or personal care sectors, given the growing consumer trend towards organic products. |
| Seasonality Index | This metric indicates how much a company's sales fluctuate based on seasons or certain times of the year. This is particularly important for categories such as beverages, ice cream, or holiday-related items. |
| Shelf Space Allocation | The amount of retail shelf space that a product occupies can provide insights into its market acceptance, brand power, and negotiation ability with retailers. More shelf space generally indicates a higher sales volume. |
| SKU Count | SKU or Stock Keeping Unit count refers to the distinct types of items a company sells. An increase in SKUs could imply product diversification and potential revenue growth, while a decrease could mean the company is streamlining its product portfolio to improve efficiency. |
| Social Media Engagement Rate | Given the importance of social media for brand visibility and consumer engagement, this KPI measures the effectiveness of a company's social media strategy. |
| Sponsorship Effectiveness | For CPG companies sponsoring events or teams, this KPI measures the return on sponsorship investment in terms of brand awareness, favorability, or sales increases. |
| Supply Chain Efficiency | Measured through metrics like order cycle time or fill rate, it indicates how well the company manages its supply chain to avoid stockouts or overstocks, which directly affect customer satisfaction and operational cost, respectively. |
| Sustainability Index | As consumers become more environmentally conscious, the sustainability index, which could measure things like the percentage of recycled packaging used or carbon footprint of manufacturing processes, becomes increasingly important for a company's brand image and long-term viability. |
| Sustainability Score of Suppliers | This KPI assesses the environmental and social governance practices of suppliers. It can impact the company's overall sustainability score and is increasingly important due to consumers' growing awareness of these issues. |
| Trade Spend Efficiency | This KPI assesses the effectiveness of promotional spending with retailers to boost product visibility and sales. It is crucial in the CPG industry due to the significant amount of resources dedicated to trade promotions. |
