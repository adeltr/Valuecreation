# 33 · Emerging Mobility Coverage Analysis

> **Part:** [[E - Technology, Mobility & Operational Efficiency]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Identify and evaluate new mobility trends—such as car-sharing platforms, micro-mobility solutions (e-scooters, e-bikes), and subscription-based vehicle services—and assess whether current coverage offerings and underwriting practices adequately address these evolving risks.


## 📊 Data required
- Policies or endorsements currently covering emerging mobility risks (car-sharing, e-scooter, e-bike, etc.)
- Claims data (frequency, severity, total incurred losses) linked to emerging mobility exposures
- Usage data and demographic information for policyholders engaging in new mobility services
- Competitor or industry benchmarks on emerging mobility coverage offerings, claim rates, and pricing
- Relevant local or national regulations impacting coverage requirements for alternative mobility options


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify all policies or endorsements that cover emerging mobility scenarios, such as short-term rentals, car-sharing, or micro-mobility. Gather incurred losses, claim frequency, premium, and exposure details for these policies.
2. Calculate claim frequency and severity for these emerging mobility endorsements using Claim Frequency = (Number of Claims / Number of Exposures) x 100 and Severity = (Total Incurred Losses / Number of Claims). Compare results to traditional auto coverage.
3. Segment analysis by mobility type (e.g., car-sharing, e-scooters, e-bikes) to isolate distinct patterns in claims experience and usage. Investigate whether certain segments (urban, suburban, specific age groups) exhibit higher or lower loss trends.
4. Assess current coverage provisions to determine if they address unique risks—such as vehicle ownership complexities, multiple drivers, liability transfers, or wear-and-tear responsibilities. Identify coverage gaps that may lead to policyholder disputes or uncovered losses.
72
5. Review competitor offerings and industry benchmarks on emerging mobility coverage. Note differences in pricing, available endorsements, and exclusions to gauge market positioning and potential opportunities.
6. Analyze regulatory requirements that may govern insurance for shared or subscription-based vehicles, ensuring compliance with any newly introduced mandates or guidelines.
7. Determine necessary underwriting criteria (e.g., driver verification, usage limits, technology requirements) to mitigate risk. Pinpoint areas needing additional caution or surcharges, such as high-frequency e-scooter usage or specialized vehicles.
8. Document findings and propose product enhancements (e.g., specialized endorsements, dynamic pricing, telematics-based coverage). Present a final report to underwriting, product, and strategy teams for potential inclusion in overall risk management plans.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts illustrating claim frequency and severity for each category of emerging mobility
- Written summary documenting existing coverage gaps, regulatory considerations, and competitive market intelligence
- Recommendations to adjust underwriting guidelines, pricing, or product structure to capture these evolving mobility risks


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High claim frequency in certain micro-mobility segments may signal an urgent need for refined underwriting or added coverage limitations.
- Lower-than-expected severity in car-sharing or subscription-based services could suggest that flexible insurance models are sustainable, provided adequate risk controls are in place.
- Benchmark variances with competitors can highlight missed market opportunities or signal a need to adjust product offerings to remain relevant.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Expand coverage options tailored to specific mobility modes, such as daily or hourly car-sharing, e-scooter use, or ride subscriptions.
2. Collaborate with mobility providers to integrate telematics or real-time usage data into underwriting decisions.

3. Update policy language to address complex ownership and liability scenarios commonly arising in shared mobility models.
4. Track and adapt quickly to new regulations or consumer demands in the rapidly evolving mobility landscape.
