# 8 · Material Recovery Facility (MRF) Throughput and Efficiency Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Recycling & Materials Recovery]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Waste Management Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the operational performance of a Material Recovery Facility (MRF), measure throughput and recovery efficiency, and identify opportunities to optimize processing, reduce contamination, and increase revenue from recovered materials.


## 📊 Data required
- Incoming material tonnages (daily or weekly)
- Processed tonnages by material type (paper, plastics, metals, etc.)
- Facility operating hours and staffing levels
- Contamination rates and residue disposal records
- Equipment specifications (throughput capacity, downtime records)
- Commodity market prices for recovered materials
- Operational costs (labor, energy, maintenance)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Collect inbound and outbound tonnage data Record the quantity of recyclables arriving at the MRF and track how much of each material type is sorted and baled for sale.
2. Calculate throughput rate Determine the average processing rate based on total tonnage processed over a given period divided by the facility’s actual operating hours.
Equation:
`Throughput (tons/hour) = (Total Tons Processed in a Period) / (Total Operating Hours in the Same Period)`
3. Assess recovery efficiency and contamination Compare the tonnage of recovered marketable materials to total inbound recyclables, noting residue or contamination disposal rates.
24 Equation:
`Recovery Rate (%) = ((Marketable Recovered Materials) / (Inbound Recyclables)) x 100`
4. Evaluate equipment performance and downtime Review maintenance logs, breakdown events, and unplanned outages. Calculate downtime percentages relative to scheduled operating hours.
5. Analyze labor utilization and staffing levels Identify how many staff are assigned to each shift or processing line. Consider automation or cross-training opportunities to improve efficiency.
6. Determine financial performance Compare the revenue from material sales against operational expenses (labor, energy, maintenance). Investigate market price trends for key commodities.
7. Develop an improvement action plan Use insights from throughput and efficiency data to recommend equipment upgrades, staff training, process adjustments, or new sorting technology.


## 📤 Output format
- A table summarizing inbound tonnages, recovered tonnages, throughput rates, and contamination rates
- Graphs depicting trends in throughput, recovery rate, and downtime over time
- Financial statements or charts showing revenue from recovered materials compared to operational costs
- Action plan listing recommended process and equipment improvements


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A high throughput rate coupled with a high recovery rate suggests efficient sorting and minimal downtime.
- Elevated contamination rates can indicate issues with inbound material quality or ineffective sorting steps.
- Persistent downtime or bottlenecks may point to equipment failures or inadequate maintenance practices.
- Comparing financial returns (revenue minus costs) highlights the profitability and viability of MRF operations.

## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Upgrade or maintain sorting equipment (e.g., optical sorters, screens) to reduce contamination and increase processing speed.
2. Provide ongoing training for staff to ensure effective material recognition and reduce mis-sorts.
3. Introduce preventative maintenance schedules to minimize unplanned downtime and prolong equipment life.
4. Collaborate with municipalities or suppliers to improve inbound material quality and reduce contamination.
5. Monitor commodity market trends and adjust the material mix or processing focus to maximize revenue opportunities.
