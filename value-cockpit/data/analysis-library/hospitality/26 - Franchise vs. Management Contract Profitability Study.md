# 26 · Franchise vs. Management Contract Profitability Study

> **Part:** [[F - Ownership & Financial Structures]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Compare the financial returns and operational implications of running the hotel under a franchise agreement versus a management contract, guiding ownership decisions.


## 📊 Data required
- Detailed financials for hotels operating under franchise agreements (franchise fees, marketing fees, reservation fees, etc.).
- Detailed financials for hotels operating under management contracts (base management fees, incentive fees, owner costs).
- Revenue, occupancy, ADR, and RevPAR data for each model.
- Operational costs, including payroll, training, and any brand‐mandated expenses.
- Length and terms of both franchise and management agreements.
- Market performance data and competitive benchmarking.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather financial data by agreement type. Separate and label all hotels or properties by whether they are under a franchise agreement or a management contract.
2. Categorize fees and costs. For franchise properties, list franchise fees, brand marketing contributions, and reservation system fees. For management‐contract properties, list base fees, incentive fees, and any additional overhead or support service costs.
3. Compare key performance metrics. Evaluate occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, and GOP (Gross Operating Profit) or NOI (Net Operating Income) for each property, grouped by agreement type.
4. Assess brand and operational support. Document the level of brand resources, training, marketing, and reservation support provided under each agreement.
5. Identify revenue vs. cost trade‐offs. Analyze whether the stronger brand affiliation or operational expertise under a management contract or franchise leads to higher top‐line revenue—and at what cost.
6. Examine owner autonomy and control. Note the flexibility owners have regarding property decisions (pricing, hiring, renovation plans) under each model.

7. Review long‐term impact. Consider contract lengths, exit clauses, performance guarantees, and potential for brand alignment or brand fatigue over time.
8. **Calculate profitability ratios. For each property, determine metrics such as:** ○ EBITDA margin or GOP margin ○ Return on Investment (ROI) ○ Cost of franchisor or management fees as a percentage of revenue
9. Summarize findings. Identify which model generally yields better returns, taking into account variations in market, property size, and branding strength.


## 📤 Output format
- A comparison table or spreadsheet listing properties by agreement type and showing financial indicators (occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, fee structure, profitability metrics).
- Charts or graphs illustrating average profitability or fee percentages under each model.
- A written report outlining advantages, disadvantages, and situational considerations for each arrangement.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Higher net operating income under a particular model suggests that arrangement may be more profitable in that market/segment.
- A low fee percentage does not always equate to higher profitability if revenue generation under that agreement is weaker.
- Greater brand presence or operational support can lead to higher rates and occupancy, but fees must be weighed against these benefits.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Negotiate favorable contract terms. Whether franchise or management contract, reduce fees, secure performance clauses, or limit brand‐mandated costs where possible.
2. Optimize brand support utilization. Leverage marketing, loyalty programs, and training resources included in the agreement to boost revenue.
3. Monitor property‐level performance closely. Compare fee structures and brand support benefits quarterly or annually to ensure ongoing value.
4. Maintain flexibility. If possible, include exit or renegotiation clauses to switch models or rebrand should market conditions or brand performance change.

5. Consider hybrid approaches. In some cases, partial management assistance or a soft brand affiliation may offer a balance between full franchise and management contract models.
