# 24 · Product Portfolio Profitability by Chemical Type

> **Part:** [[D - Sales & Marketing]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Chemical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to evaluate the profitability of different chemical types within the company’s product portfolio. By understanding profitability by chemical type, the company can make informed decisions on resource allocation, product focus, and pricing strategies, ultimately optimizing portfolio performance and aligning with market demand.


## 📊 Data required
1. Revenue generated by each chemical type
2. Cost of goods sold (COGS) for each chemical type, including raw materials, production, and distribution costs
3. Operating expenses allocated to each chemical type (e.g., marketing, R&D, sales)
4. Sales volume data by chemical type
5. Gross margin and net margin calculations per chemical type
6. Industry benchmarks for profitability by chemical segment (if available)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Gross Margin for Each Chemical Type:**
`Gross Margin (%) = [(Revenue - COGS) / Revenue] x 100`
For example, if revenue for a chemical type is $1 million and COGS is $600,000:
`Gross Margin = [(1,000,000 - 600,000) / 1,000,000] x 100 = 40%`
This metric provides insight into each chemical type’s profitability before accounting for operating expenses.
2. **Calculate Net Margin for Each Chemical Type:**
`Net Margin (%) = [(Revenue - Total Costs) / Revenue] x 100`
`Total Costs = COGS + Operating Expenses (e.g., marketing, R&D, distribution)`
For instance, if total costs for a chemical type are $800,000 on $1 million in revenue:
`Net Margin = [(1,000,000 - 800,000) / 1,000,000] x 100 = 20%`

3. **Determine Contribution Margin by Chemical Type:**
`Contribution Margin (%) = [(Revenue - Variable Costs) / Revenue] x 100`
Variable costs are costs directly tied to production volume (e.g., raw materials, energy). This metric shows how much each chemical type contributes to covering fixed costs and profits.
4. **Analyze Profitability by Volume and Revenue:** Assess the sales volume and revenue generated by each chemical type. High-volume, low-margin products may have different strategic importance compared to low-volume, high-margin products. This helps understand if certain chemical types are profitable primarily due to volume or margin.
5. **Benchmark Against Industry Standards:** Compare gross and net margins for each chemical type with industry benchmarks to determine competitiveness. Higher or lower-than-average margins may reveal competitive strengths or potential cost inefficiencies.
6. **Identify High- and Low-Profit Chemical Types:** Rank chemical types based on profitability metrics to identify high-profit and low-profit segments. This provides insights for potential strategic shifts, such as focusing on high-profit types or improving efficiency in low-profit types.


## 📤 Output format
1. Table showing revenue, COGS, operating expenses, gross margin, and net margin by chemical type
2. Contribution margin summary by chemical type
3. Benchmark comparison for profitability metrics against industry standards
4. List of high-profit and low-profit chemical types with strategic recommendations


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High gross and net margins:** Indicate that a chemical type is highly profitable and could be a focus area for expansion or increased marketing efforts.
- **Low margins or low profitability:** Suggests that these chemical types may need cost reduction strategies, pricing adjustments, or potential repositioning within the portfolio.
- **High volume with low margin:** Shows a reliance on volume-driven profitability, which may benefit from cost efficiencies or scale economies.

## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize Pricing Strategy:** Re-evaluate pricing for high-demand, low-margin products to improve profitability without sacrificing volume.
2. **Reduce Production Costs:** Explore opportunities for cost reductions in raw materials, production, and distribution, especially for lower-margin chemical types.
3. **Focus on High-Profit Chemical Types:** Allocate resources, such as marketing and R&D, to high-margin chemical types to maximize their market presence and profitability.
4. **Consider Portfolio Restructuring:** Evaluate whether to discontinue or repurpose low-profit chemical types that do not align with strategic goals.
5. **Enhance Product Differentiation:** Differentiate high-margin products with value-added features to increase demand and justify premium pricing.
