# 21 · Policy Conversion Profitability Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Profitability and Product Performance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the financial performance of policy conversions, such as term-to-permanent conversions, and assess their impact on profitability. The analysis identifies trends, cost drivers, and opportunities to optimize conversion strategies for improved margins and customer retention.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policy-level data:** ○ Policies converted (e.g., term-to-whole life, term-to-universal life). ○ Premiums before and after conversion. ○ Policyholder demographics (age, gender, location).
2. **Financial data:** ○ Acquisition costs associated with conversion. ○ Claims data post-conversion (mortality and morbidity). ○ Reserves and cash values for converted policies.
3. **Actuarial assumptions:** ○ Mortality rates, lapse rates, and discount rates. ○ Expected claims and expenses over the converted policy duration.
4. **Persistency data:** ○ Retention rates for converted policies compared to non-converted policies.
5. **Benchmark data:** ○ Conversion trends, profitability, and persistency rates from industry peers.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Segment Conversion Data ○ Group converted policies by product type (e.g., term-to-whole life, term-to-universal life). ○ Further segment data by demographics, such as age, gender, and policy duration before conversion.
2. Calculate Profitability Metrics ○ Measure profitability for converted policies using the formula:
`Profitability = Post-Conversion Premium Revenue - (Claims Paid + Acquisition Costs + Maintenance Expenses)`
○ Compare profit margins between converted policies and original term policies:
`Profit Margin (%) = (Profitability / Premium Revenue) x 100`
3. **Analyze Persistency Trends:** 60 ○ Evaluate persistency rates for converted policies:
`Persistency Rate (%) = (Number of Active Converted Policies / Total Converted Policies) x 100`
○ Compare with non-converted policies to identify retention differences.
4. Measure Claims Experience ○ Analyze mortality claims post-conversion relative to actuarial assumptions. ○ Calculate the Loss Ratio to assess claims performance:
`Loss Ratio (%) = (Claims Paid / Premium Revenue) x 100`
5. Assess Cost of Conversion ○ Quantify acquisition and administrative costs incurred during the conversion process. ○ Compare costs to profitability margins to assess financial efficiency.
6. Benchmark Performance ○ Compare conversion profitability, persistency, and loss ratios to industry benchmarks to identify areas of underperformance or opportunity.
7. Perform Sensitivity Analysis ○ Test the impact of adverse assumptions, such as increased mortality rates or higher lapse rates, on profitability.
8. Visualize Results ○ Summarize conversion profitability, persistency, and claims in tables. ○ Generate charts comparing profit margins and loss ratios for converted policies vs. original term policies.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing profitability, claims experience, and persistency rates for converted policies
- Charts showing trends in conversion profitability and loss ratios by product segment
- Sensitivity testing results illustrating the impact of key assumptions on profitability
- Key findings identifying high-performing segments and areas requiring optimization


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. High profitability for converted policies indicates successful retention and strong contribution to margins.
2. Low profit margins or high loss ratios post-conversion suggest issues with pricing, underwriting, or mortality assumptions.
3. Lower persistency rates for converted policies compared to benchmarks may highlight dissatisfaction or affordability concerns.

4. High acquisition and administrative costs relative to profitability indicate inefficiencies in the conversion process.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Offer incentives to policyholders for early conversions to optimize retention and improve margins.
2. Refine underwriting practices for conversions to align mortality assumptions with actual experience.
3. Improve communication strategies to educate policyholders on the long-term benefits of conversions, reducing lapse risk.
4. Streamline administrative processes to lower conversion-related costs and improve efficiency.
5. Monitor claims experience regularly to adjust pricing and reserve assumptions for converted policies.
6. Benchmark conversion strategies against industry best practices to identify pricing, product, or process improvements.
7. Develop targeted campaigns to identify high-value policyholders likely to benefit from conversions, ensuring alignment with profitability goals.
