# 21 · Contract & Pricing Structures Review

> **Part:** [[D - Commercial & Contractual Structures]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Third Party Logistics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate existing contractual agreements and pricing models to ensure alignment with service-level expectations, market conditions, and cost-efficiency goals.


## 📊 Data required
- Current contracts with customers, carriers, and suppliers
- Pricing models or rate sheets (fixed, variable, cost-plus, etc.)
- Historical billing and invoice data
- Service-level agreements and performance metrics
- Market rate benchmarks and competitor pricing information
- Any volume incentives, accessorial charges, or surcharge details


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Compile All Existing Contracts:** Gather customer-facing and supplier-facing agreements. Note key terms (pricing structures, minimum volume commitments, contract durations).
2. **Identify Pricing Models in Use:** Determine whether contracts employ fixed rates, variable rates tied to market indices, cost-plus arrangements, or hybrid models. Document how surcharges (fuel, peak season) are handled.
3. **Analyze Historical Billing & Invoices:** Compare actual charges with contractual rates to spot discrepancies or patterns of underbilling/overbilling. Calculate the proportion of accessorial charges and their root causes.
4. **Benchmark Rates & Terms Against the Market:** Research current market rates or indices (e.g., spot market freight rates, fuel indices). Identify any significant deviations from standard industry practices.
5. **Review Service-Level Requirements:** Correlate pricing structures with performance obligations (on-time delivery, fill rates). Determine if penalties or incentives are appropriately driving compliance.
6. **Assess Contract Flexibility & Risks:** Evaluate clauses for volume fluctuations, force majeure, or price escalation. Identify contracts that may be too rigid or expose the company to excessive risk.
7. **Document Potential Cost-Saving or Optimization Opportunities:** Outline recommended changes to pricing structures, negotiation strategies, or contract terms that could improve profitability or mitigate risk.

## 📤 Output format
- A contract inventory table listing each agreement, pricing model, and key clauses
- A summary report comparing current rates to market benchmarks (spot or contract rates)
- A gap analysis highlighting contracts that are significantly above/below market or contain suboptimal terms
- Recommendations document outlining potential renegotiation strategies and expected outcomes


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Significant Deviations from Market Rates:** Could indicate overpayment or undercharging, leading to potential margin erosion or missed revenue.
- **High Proportion of Accessorial Charges:** Suggests underlying operational or planning inefficiencies that trigger extra fees.
- **Rigid Contract Terms:** May hinder agility in responding to market swings, capacity constraints, or demand changes.
- **Misaligned Service Levels & Pricing:** Signals that incentives or penalties do not adequately support performance objectives.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Renegotiate Contracts Strategically:** Use market data and performance insights to secure more favorable rates, flexible terms, or incentive-based models.
2. **Implement Tiered Pricing Models:** Offer volume discounts or performance incentives that align customer demand with capacity utilization goals.
3. **Streamline Accessorial Charges:** Identify root causes of recurring surcharges and address them operationally (e.g., improved scheduling to reduce detention).
4. **Regularly Benchmark & Update Rates:** Monitor market conditions and conduct periodic rate reviews to keep contracts competitive and profitable.
5. **Leverage Data Analytics:** Employ robust tracking of costs and performance metrics to inform contract negotiations and pricing strategies on an ongoing basis.
