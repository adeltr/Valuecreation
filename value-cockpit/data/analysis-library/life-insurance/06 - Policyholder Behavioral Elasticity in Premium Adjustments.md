# 6 · Policyholder Behavioral Elasticity in Premium Adjustments Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Policyholder Behavior and Retention]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate how changes in premium amounts influence policyholder behavior, such as retention, lapse rates, and renewal decisions. This analysis helps determine the sensitivity of policyholders to premium adjustments and supports strategic pricing decisions.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Historical policy data, including:** ○ Policyholder demographics (age, income, gender, location). ○ Policy types and coverage details (e.g., term, whole life, universal life).
2. **Premium adjustment history, including:** ○ Records of premium increases or decreases. ○ Timing and magnitude of premium adjustments.
3. **Policyholder behavior data post-adjustment, such as:** ○ Retention rates. ○ Lapse rates. ○ Renewal rates.
4. Macroeconomic data (e.g., inflation, interest rates) for contextual insights.
5. Industry benchmarks for price elasticity of demand in insurance.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Define Behavioral Elasticity Formula
`○ Elasticity = (% Change in Policyholder Behavior / % Change in Premium Amount).`
○ Behavior could include lapse rate, retention rate, or renewal rate.
2. Segment Data ○ Group policyholders based on demographics, policy type, and payment frequency. ○ Create segments for those affected by premium increases vs. decreases.
3. Calculate Behavioral Changes ○ Measure percentage changes in key behaviors (e.g., lapse rates, retention rates) before and after premium adjustments.
4. Assess Elasticity for Each Segment ○ Apply the elasticity formula to each segment to determine how sensitive policyholders are to premium changes. ○ Identify segments with the highest and lowest sensitivity.

5. Analyze External Factors ○ Examine the role of macroeconomic conditions, such as inflation or unemployment, on policyholder behavior during premium adjustments.
6. Benchmark Against Industry Standards ○ Compare elasticity results with industry benchmarks to understand the company’s competitive positioning.
7. Visualize Results ○ Use scatter plots to show the relationship between premium adjustments and policyholder behavior. ○ Create bar charts to compare elasticity across segments.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing elasticity values by segment (e.g., demographics, policy type).
- Scatter plots illustrating the relationship between premium changes and lapse/retention rates.
- Summary reports identifying high-sensitivity and low-sensitivity segments.
- Key findings highlighting trends and actionable insights.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. High elasticity in certain segments indicates policyholders are highly sensitive to premium adjustments, which may lead to increased lapses or reduced renewals.
2. Low elasticity in other segments suggests these policyholders are less price-sensitive, offering opportunities for premium optimization.
3. Segments with negative elasticity values (e.g., improved retention following premium decreases) may indicate areas for competitive pricing or promotional strategies.
4. Comparisons with industry benchmarks reveal whether the company’s pricing strategy aligns with or deviates from market norms.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Design flexible premium structures, such as tiered pricing or loyalty discounts, to retain high-sensitivity policyholders.
2. Target high-elasticity segments with communication campaigns explaining the value of policies to mitigate the impact of premium increases.
3. Use predictive analytics to forecast policyholder reactions to future premium adjustments.
4. Conduct A/B testing with small premium adjustments to measure behavioral responses before implementing broader changes.

5. Introduce product options with adjustable coverage levels, allowing policyholders to maintain affordability during premium increases.
6. Regularly monitor market conditions and competitor pricing to remain competitive in high-elasticity segments.
