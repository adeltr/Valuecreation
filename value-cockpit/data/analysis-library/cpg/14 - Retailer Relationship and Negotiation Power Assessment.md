# 14 · Retailer Relationship and Negotiation Power Assessment

> **Part:** [[B - Sales]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Retailer Relationship and Negotiation Power Assessment in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to evaluate the strength of a company's relationships with its retail partners and assess its negotiation power in areas such as pricing, shelf placement, promotional opportunities, and distribution. This analysis helps CPG companies understand how they can leverage their position to secure more favorable terms, enhance product visibility, and optimize profitability in collaboration with retailers. Strong retailer relationships and effective negotiation strategies are crucial for maintaining a competitive edge in the marketplace.


## 📊 Data required
1. Sales data by retailer
2. Profit margins by retailer (e.g., after distribution, fees, and marketing contributions)
3. Shelf space and placement data (e.g., eye-level vs. lower shelf)
4. Retailer contract terms (e.g., pricing, promotional allowances, payment terms)
5. Slotting fee and promotional support details (e.g., costs for special displays, endcaps)
6. Distribution and supply chain costs per retailer
7. Sales volume and share of category for each retailer
8. Competitor performance within the same retail accounts
9. Retailer-specific marketing and promotional spend
10. Feedback from account managers and field sales teams regarding retailer interactions 78


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the objectives of the assessment:** Identify the key goals of assessing retailer relationships and negotiation power. These might include improving shelf placement, reducing slotting fees, securing better promotional terms, or strengthening overall profitability with retail partners.
2. **Analyze sales and profitability by retailer:** Collect sales and profitability data for each retail partner. Compare how each retailer contributes to the overall revenue and profit margin of the company. Calculate gross margin per retailer using the formula:
`Gross Margin (%) = ((Revenue from Retailer - Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue from Retailer) x 100`
For example, if sales through Retailer A generate $500,000 in revenue with a cost of goods sold (COGS) of $350,000:
`Gross Margin = (($500,000 - $350,000) / $500,000) x 100 = 30%`
Retailers with higher sales volumes but lower margins may need a focus on negotiation to improve terms and profitability.
3. **Evaluate shelf space and product placement:** Assess how your products are positioned in stores for each retail partner. Eye-level or prominent shelf placement can significantly impact sales. Work with sales teams to understand if the current placement is optimal or if there are opportunities to negotiate better visibility (e.g., on endcaps or through special displays).
4. **Review retailer contract terms and slotting fees:** Analyze the contract terms with each retailer, including slotting fees, promotional allowances, and payment terms. High slotting fees and other financial commitments may erode profitability, especially if sales are not growing in that retailer's stores. Consider how your brand’s power in the marketplace can influence contract renegotiation.
5. **Assess promotional opportunities and support:** Track how each retailer supports your brand's promotional activities, such as in-store promotions, digital promotions, or special displays. Review the level of retailer cooperation in 79 offering promotional spaces or endcap displays. If certain retailers are less supportive or demand higher fees, it may indicate an opportunity to strengthen negotiations. Analyze the return on marketing investment (ROMI) for promotions with each retailer:
`ROMI = (Revenue Attributable to Promotion - Promotion Costs) / Promotion`
Costs x 100 For example, if a promotion with Retailer B generates $300,000 in revenue with a $50,000 promotional investment:
`ROMI = (($300,000 - $50,000) / $50,000) x 100 = 500%`
6. **Measure retailer share of category and sales growth:** Review how much market share each retailer holds in your product category and how much your sales are growing within their stores. High market share or sales growth can strengthen your negotiation power with that retailer, as it shows your products are key drivers for their category performance. Conversely, lower category share may require demonstrating the potential for growth in future negotiations.
7. **Benchmark competitor performance:** Compare how your competitors are performing with the same retailers. If competitors are getting better shelf placement, lower fees, or stronger promotional support, this may indicate areas where your brand is under-leveraged. Analyze competitor pricing, visibility, and the extent of their partnerships with retailers to identify gaps or opportunities for improvement.
8. **Evaluate distribution and supply chain efficiency:** Analyze the costs and efficiency of distributing products to each retailer. Some retailers may impose higher distribution or stocking requirements, which can affect the overall cost-to-serve. If distribution costs are disproportionately high for certain retailers, explore ways to negotiate more efficient terms or streamline supply chain operations.
9. **Gather feedback from sales and account teams:** Consult with your sales and account teams who manage day-to-day interactions with retailers. These teams 80 can provide valuable insights into the strength of relationships, current negotiation dynamics, and any challenges or opportunities that may exist in improving retailer partnerships.
10. **Assess overall negotiation power:** Determine your company's negotiation leverage with each retailer by assessing the following factors:
- **Brand strength and consumer demand:** Strong brands with high consumer demand have more leverage in negotiations.
- **Sales volume and retailer dependence:** If your brand contributes significantly to a retailer’s sales in a specific category, you have more negotiation power.
- **Competitor alternatives:** If competitors offer similar products or brands, the retailer has more alternatives, reducing your negotiation leverage.
- **Category exclusivity:** If your product offers category exclusivity or differentiation (e.g., a leading organic or sustainable option), this increases your leverage.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Retailer Relationship and Negotiation Power Assessment typically includes:
- Sales and profitability analysis by retailer, including gross margins and sales volumes.
- Shelf placement and visibility insights for each retailer, with recommendations for improvement.
- A review of contract terms, including slotting fees, payment terms, and promotional allowances.
- An assessment of retailer-specific promotions and their effectiveness, including ROMI.
- Competitor benchmarking data showing how your brand compares in terms of pricing, shelf space, and promotional support.

- A summary of distribution and supply chain costs for each retailer.

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High sales volume with low gross margin:** Retailers that drive significant sales but erode profitability through high fees or low margins may require renegotiation. Consider leveraging your sales performance to secure better contract terms, promotional opportunities, or reduced slotting fees.
- **Strong shelf placement and high visibility:** Retailers that provide prominent shelf placement and strong promotional support are valuable partners. Continue to cultivate these relationships while looking for ways to increase collaboration and maximize sales growth.
- **Low share of category but high potential:** If your brand holds a low share in a particular retailer's category but has strong growth potential, focus on building a case for increased promotional support or better shelf placement to unlock further growth.
- **Higher fees compared to competitors:** If competitors are securing better terms or lower fees, it may indicate that your negotiation power is being underutilized. Use this data to strengthen your position in future negotiations.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Strengthen brand presence:** Invest in brand-building activities that increase consumer demand, which will, in turn, give your brand more leverage in 82 negotiations with retailers. Strong consumer demand can help secure better shelf placement and lower fees.
2. **Demonstrate category leadership:** Use data to demonstrate how your brand contributes to the retailer’s overall category growth. Showing how your product drives foot traffic, sales, or consumer loyalty can improve your bargaining position.
3. **Optimize slotting fees and promotional costs:** Work to reduce slotting fees or promotional expenses by negotiating for more favorable terms. This can include seeking shared promotional investments with retailers or requesting more cost-effective display options.
4. **Increase collaboration with key retailers:** Strengthen retailer relationships by collaborating on joint business planning, co-branded marketing campaigns, or exclusive product launches. Building trust and value for both parties can lead to better negotiation outcomes.
5. **Leverage data in negotiations:** Present data that highlights your brand’s value to the retailer, including market share growth, consumer demand, and sales performance. Use this data to negotiate better shelf placement, lower fees, or improved payment terms.
6. **Expand into new retail channels:** If certain retailers are resistant to negotiation, consider expanding your distribution into new channels where your brand may have more leverage or growth potential. This could include e-commerce, direct-to-consumer, or emerging retail partnerships.
