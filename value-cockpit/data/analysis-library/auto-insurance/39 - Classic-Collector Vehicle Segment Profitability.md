# 39 · Classic-Collector Vehicle Segment Profitability

> **Part:** [[I - Specialty & Niche Segments]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate how profitably the company is underwriting classic and collector vehicles, identify specific cost drivers or unique risks, and determine adjustments to underwriting or pricing to enhance margin in this niche.


## 📊 Data required
- List of in-force classic/collector vehicle policies (written premium, earned premium, policy count)
- Claims data specific to classic/collector vehicles (loss frequency, severity, total incurred losses)
- Vehicle valuation data (agreed value vs. stated value vs. market value)
- Underwriting and eligibility guidelines (storage requirements, usage limitations)
- Benchmark or competitor data on collector car premiums and loss ratios


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify all policies covering classic/collector vehicles, organizing each policy’s written and earned premium, policy inception dates, stated or agreed vehicle values, and applicable coverage limits.
2. Calculate claims frequency and severity by using Claim Frequency = (Number of Claims / Number of Policies) x 100 and Severity = (Total Incurred Losses / Number of Claims). Compare these metrics to standard auto segments for perspective.
3. Compute the loss ratio for the segment using Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + LAE) / Earned Premium. Analyze trends over several policy terms (e.g., 3–5 years) to see if profitability is improving or declining.
4. Review valuation methods used in policies (agreed value vs. stated value vs. market value). Identify how these methods impact claim settlement amounts and whether there are consistent discrepancies from actual repair or replacement costs.
5. Assess underwriting criteria specific to classic/collector vehicles, such as storage conditions, usage mileage caps, or driver eligibility. Look for patterns in claims resulting from lax enforcement or unclear guidelines.

6. Benchmark against competitor offerings if possible, noting any differences in rates, deductible structures, or coverage limitations. Investigate whether competitor positioning suggests an opportunity to refine product features.
7. Analyze unique cost drivers such as hard-to-find parts, specialized repair shops, and potential for higher towing or transit costs. Check if claim settlements often exceed initial projections due to these factors.
8. Document conclusions and propose adjustments to pricing or underwriting guidelines. Suggest refinements like stricter mileage or storage requirements, updated premium calculations for rare vehicles, or tiered coverage options based on exclusivity.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts showing claim frequency, severity, and loss ratios for classic/collector vehicles over time
- Written summary outlining key risk factors, unique cost drivers, and how they differ from standard auto segments
- Recommendations on underwriting guidelines, valuation methods, and pricing to improve overall segment profitability


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Persistently high loss ratios suggest the need for premium adjustments or tighter underwriting (e.g., stricter storage requirements).
- Low claim frequency but occasional high-cost claims reflect the niche’s concentrated risk in rare/valuable vehicles.
- Significant differences between declared/agreed values and settlement amounts could signal misaligned valuation practices.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Refine underwriting rules around storage conditions, driver qualifications, and permissible vehicle usage to reduce loss exposure.
2. Introduce or adjust premium surcharges or discounts based on verified secure storage, alarm systems, and limited-use agreements.
3. Collaborate with specialty appraisers or vintage car associations to establish accurate valuations and reduce claims disputes.
4. Conduct regular reviews of competitor rates and industry trends, ensuring the product remains competitive yet profitable.
