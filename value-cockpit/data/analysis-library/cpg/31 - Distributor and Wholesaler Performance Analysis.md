# 31 · Distributor and Wholesaler Performance Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Operations]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Distributor and Wholesaler Performance Analysis in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to evaluate the effectiveness, reliability, and efficiency of distribution and wholesale partners. This analysis focuses on assessing their ability to meet service-level agreements (SLAs), deliver products on time, maintain adequate stock levels, manage promotions, and contribute to overall sales growth. Optimizing distributor and wholesaler performance ensures a smooth supply chain, increased market penetration, and higher profitability.


## 📊 Data required
1. Sales data by distributor and wholesaler (e.g., revenue generated, volume of products sold)
2. Order accuracy and fulfillment rates by distributor and wholesaler
3. On-time delivery performance for each distributor and wholesaler
4. Inventory turnover rates at distributor and wholesaler locations
5. Stockout frequency and order lead times at the distributor or wholesaler level
6. Promotional execution and compliance (e.g., adherence to promotions, discount depth, marketing spend)
7. Return rates and product defect data (e.g., number of returns, reasons for returns)
8. Contract terms and service-level agreements (SLAs) with distributors and wholesalers
9. Customer satisfaction or feedback regarding distributor performance
10. Competitor benchmarking data for distributor and wholesaler performance 184


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the objectives of the analysis:** Establish clear goals for evaluating distributor and wholesaler performance. Objectives may include improving sales growth through better distribution, reducing stockouts, improving order fulfillment accuracy, and ensuring timely delivery. For example, a goal could be to improve on-time delivery rates by 10% while reducing stockouts at distributor warehouses.
2. **Collect sales data by distributor and wholesaler:** Gather sales data for each distributor and wholesaler, including revenue generated, volume of products sold, and market share contribution. Analyze how each distributor or wholesaler contributes to overall sales growth and whether they meet sales targets. The formula for sales growth is:
`Sales Growth (%) = ((Current Sales - Previous Sales) / Previous Sales) x 100 For example, if a distributor’s sales grew from $1 million to $1.2 million: Sales Growth = (($1.2 million - $1 million) / $1 million) x 100 = 20%`
3. **Evaluate order accuracy and fulfillment rates:** Measure how accurately distributors and wholesalers fulfill orders by comparing orders placed to orders delivered. Inaccuracies or incomplete orders can lead to lost sales and customer dissatisfaction. The formula for order fulfillment rate is:
`Order Fulfillment Rate (%) = (Number of Orders Fulfilled Correctly / Total Number of Orders) x 100 For example, if 950 out of 1,000 orders were fulfilled correctly: Order Fulfillment Rate = (950 / 1,000) x 100 = 95%`
4. **Track on-time delivery performance:** Review on-time delivery rates for each distributor and wholesaler. Timely delivery ensures that products are available for sale at retail locations when needed, minimizing stockouts and lost sales. The formula for on-time delivery rate is:
`On-time Delivery Rate (%) = (Number of On-time Deliveries / Total Number of Deliveries) x 100`
185 For example, if a wholesaler made 480 out of 500 deliveries on time:
`On-time Delivery Rate = (480 / 500) x 100 = 96% High on-time delivery rates indicate that the distributor or wholesaler is reliable in getting products to market as scheduled.`
5. **Analyze inventory turnover and stock levels:** Assess how efficiently distributors and wholesalers manage their inventory levels by calculating inventory turnover rates. A low turnover rate may indicate overstocking or inefficiencies in demand forecasting, while a high turnover rate suggests that products are moving efficiently through the supply chain. The formula for inventory turnover is:
`Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) / Average Inventory For example, if a distributor had $500,000 in COGS and an average inventory value of $250,000: Inventory Turnover = $500,000 / $250,000 = 2`
6. **Monitor stockout frequency and lead times:** Track how often distributors and wholesalers experience stockouts and measure the lead times for restocking products. Frequent stockouts or long lead times can disrupt the supply chain and result in lost sales. By reducing stockouts and improving lead times, distributors can ensure product availability.
7. **Assess promotional execution and compliance:** Evaluate how well distributors and wholesalers execute promotions and adhere to promotional agreements. This includes ensuring that promotional materials are in place, discounts are applied correctly, and marketing efforts are consistent with the company’s strategies. Track sales uplift during promotions to determine the effectiveness of distributor support.
8. **Review return rates and product defects:** Analyze return rates and product defects for products delivered by distributors and wholesalers. High return rates may indicate issues with product handling, transportation, or storage, leading to damaged goods. The formula for return rate is:
`Return Rate (%) = (Number of Products Returned / Total Products Sold) x 100`
186 For example, if 50 units were returned out of 10,000 sold:
`Return Rate = (50 / 10,000) x 100 = 0.5%`
9. **Ensure compliance with service-level agreements (SLAs):** Review distributor and wholesaler adherence to SLAs, including on-time delivery, order accuracy, promotional support, and inventory management. Regularly assessing performance against SLAs helps ensure that distributors and wholesalers are meeting the company’s expectations.
10. **Collect customer feedback on distributor performance:** Gather feedback from retailers, customers, or other stakeholders on the performance of distributors and wholesalers. This feedback can provide insights into issues such as delivery delays, missing products, or inconsistent promotional execution.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Distributor and Wholesaler Performance Analysis typically includes:
- Sales data by distributor and wholesaler, including revenue generated and sales growth percentages
- Order accuracy and fulfillment rate analysis
- On-time delivery performance metrics by distributor and wholesaler
- Inventory turnover rates and stockout frequency for each distributor and wholesaler
- Promotional execution and compliance data, including sales uplift during promotions
- Return rate and defect data for products handled by distributors and wholesalers
- Compliance review with service-level agreements (SLAs) for each distributor and wholesaler
- Customer feedback and satisfaction analysis regarding distributor performance 187
- Recommendations for improving distributor and wholesaler relationships, reducing stockouts, and enhancing performance


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Low sales growth with frequent stockouts:** If sales growth is low and stockouts are frequent, this suggests inefficiencies in inventory management or insufficient demand forecasting. Improving stock levels and demand planning may help distributors better meet sales targets and reduce lost sales.
- **Low order accuracy with high return rates:** If order accuracy is low and return rates are high, it may indicate issues with fulfillment processes or product handling. Working with distributors to improve logistics and handling processes can reduce returns and ensure that orders are fulfilled correctly.
- **Poor on-time delivery performance:** If on-time delivery rates are below target, this suggests that distributors are struggling to meet delivery schedules. Improving delivery planning and holding distributors accountable for delays can enhance reliability.
- **Inconsistent promotional execution:** If promotional sales uplift is low or promotional materials are not consistently applied, this may indicate poor promotional support from distributors. Working with distributors to ensure that promotions are properly executed can increase sales during promotional periods.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Strengthen distributor relationships:** Collaborate with distributors and wholesalers to ensure they understand the company’s expectations and goals. Regular communication helps align their performance with your strategic objectives and improves overall results.
188
2. **Improve order accuracy and fulfillment processes:** Work with distributors to enhance their order fulfillment processes, focusing on reducing errors, increasing order accuracy, and minimizing return rates. This can be achieved through better training, technology adoption, and improved logistics systems.
3. **Optimize inventory levels and reduce stockouts:** Help distributors optimize their inventory levels based on demand forecasts and seasonal trends. Reducing stockouts ensures product availability and prevents lost sales at retail locations.
4. **Increase promotional support and compliance:** Ensure that distributors and wholesalers have the necessary resources and training to execute promotions correctly. Regularly audit promotional compliance to ensure that promotions are contributing to sales growth.
5. **Hold distributors accountable for SLAs:** Monitor distributor performance against SLAs regularly and hold them accountable for any failures. This ensures that distributors are delivering products on time, managing inventory efficiently, and meeting order accuracy targets.
6. **Use data to track and improve performance:** Leverage data-driven insights to continuously monitor distributor and wholesaler performance. Implement performance dashboards to track KPIs such as sales growth, inventory turnover, and delivery times, allowing for proactive adjustments and improvements.
