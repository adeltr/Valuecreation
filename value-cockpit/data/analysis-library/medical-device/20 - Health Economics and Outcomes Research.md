# 20 · Health Economics and Outcomes Research

> **Part:** [[C - Reimbursement & Market Access]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Medical Device Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Investigate and quantify the economic and clinical value of a medical device. This includes measuring cost-effectiveness, comparative clinical outcomes, and quality-of-life impacts to inform payers, providers, and regulatory bodies.


## 📊 Data required
- Clinical trial data and real-world evidence showing device efficacy and safety
- Healthcare cost data (for example, hospital costs, outpatient care, medication use)
- Quality-of-life metrics (for example, patient-reported outcomes, utility scores)
- Relevant comparator data (standard of care or alternative treatments)
- Epidemiological data on the target patient population
- Payer policies and reimbursement rates
- Existing health economic models or published literature in the therapeutic area


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Define key economic and outcome endpoints. Clarify which measures (for example, cost per QALY, incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER), hospitalization rates) are most relevant to stakeholders.
2. Gather clinical and cost data. Collect comprehensive information on direct and indirect healthcare costs, as well as clinical outcomes from published studies or internal trials.
3. Select appropriate model framework. Determine whether to use cost-effectiveness, budget impact, or other modeling approaches (for example, Markov models, decision tree analyses) based on the device’s characteristics and available data.
4. Build or adapt a health economic model. Input the clinical and cost data, define time horizons, and set up model parameters (for example, discount rates, transition probabilities).
5. Validate model assumptions. Conduct sensitivity analyses, scenario testing, and peer reviews to ensure the model’s accuracy and robustness.
6. Calculate key results. Estimate outcomes such as cost per QALY gained, total cost savings over a defined period, or improvement in patient quality of life.
7. Benchmark against comparators. Compare model outputs to existing treatments, focusing on cost-effectiveness thresholds or value-based pricing benchmarks relevant to target markets.

8. Summarize findings for stakeholders. Present clear economic and clinical outcomes to payers, providers, and regulatory agencies, highlighting potential budget impacts and patient benefits.


## 📤 Output format
- A written report detailing model methods, results, and conclusions
- A summary table or dashboard presenting cost-effectiveness ratios, budget impact figures, and clinical outcome measures
- Sensitivity analysis charts illustrating how variations in key parameters affect cost-effectiveness
- An executive summary highlighting the device’s economic value and implications for reimbursement or pricing


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- An incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) below commonly accepted thresholds (for example, USD 50,000 per QALY) suggests favorable cost-effectiveness.
- Large budget impact savings can indicate strong value for payers and healthcare systems.
- Sensitivity analyses help identify which variables (for example, device price, complication rates) most significantly affect outcomes, guiding risk management and pricing strategies.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
- Gather robust, high-quality clinical and economic data, both in trials and real-world settings, to strengthen model credibility.
- Collaborate with payers and health economics experts early to shape relevant endpoints and study designs.
- Update the model as new data emerges or as market conditions shift (for example, price changes, new competitor entries).
- Provide training and explanatory materials to stakeholders (for example, hospital administrators, insurers) to help them understand and apply model insights.
- Employ continuous feedback loops, refining health economic models in partnership with external research organizations and key opinion leaders.
