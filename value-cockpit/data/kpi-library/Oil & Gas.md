# KPI — Oil & Gas

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Energy & Natural Resources]]

46 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| 3D Seismic Coverage | For exploration companies, this KPI indicates the extent of 3D seismic survey coverage. A higher percentage typically indicates a more extensive understanding of the subsurface, aiding exploration decisions. |
| Asset Turnover Ratio | This ratio measures how effectively a company uses its assets to generate revenue. It's calculated by dividing the total revenue by the company's total assets. Higher values indicate better utilization of assets. |
| Average Reservoir Pressure | A decline in reservoir pressure could indicate that the reservoir is nearing depletion, impacting the production rate and costs. |
| Barrels of Oil Equivalent per Day (BOEPD) per Employee | This KPI gives an insight into how efficiently a company is utilizing its human resources. |
| Break-Even Oil Price | This KPI represents the oil price at which a company or a project must sell its oil to cover its expenses. It provides an understanding of the vulnerability of the company or project to fluctuations in oil prices. |
| Carbon Intensity | With the rising importance of ESG factors, this KPI measures the CO2 emissions per unit of output. Lower carbon intensity indicates a lesser environmental impact. |
| Cash Operating Costs per Barrel | This is the cash cost to produce one barrel of oil equivalent, excluding non-cash costs like depreciation. It helps compare operational efficiency across companies. |
| Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) | This shows the average number of days that receivables remain outstanding before they are collected. Though used in many industries, it's important in oil & gas where contract payment terms can be lengthy. |
| Drilling Success Rate | This is a measure of the number of successful wells drilled as a proportion of total wells drilled. A higher success rate might indicate superior operational efficiency or geological acumen. |
| Energy Returned on Energy Invested (EROEI) | This measures the amount of usable energy obtained from a particular energy resource divided by the amount of energy expended to obtain that energy resource. It provides an indication of the efficiency and sustainability of different energy sources. |
| Environmental Compliance Costs | Given the increasing importance of sustainability, tracking the costs associated with environmental compliance can be key. These could include costs for waste management, emission controls, spill cleanup, or environmental impact assessments. |
| Exploration Success Rate | This ratio measures the number of discoveries (successful explorations) to the number of exploration wells drilled. A high ratio indicates strong exploration performance. |
| Fuel Efficiency | For downstream companies, this KPI assesses the quantity of fuel produced per unit of input. This could be an indicator of the efficiency of a company's refining process. |
| Fuel Loss Rate | This represents the percentage of total fuel that is lost during transportation. A lower rate indicates higher efficiency in transportation and distribution. |
| Gas Flaring Volume | The amount of gas that is burned off rather than captured during oil extraction. Reducing this volume is environmentally beneficial and also indicates that the company is maximizing the value of its resource extraction. |
| Gas/Oil Ratio (GOR) | This KPI indicates the volume of gas produced per unit volume of oil. It provides an insight into the composition of the reserves and can impact revenues depending on oil and gas price ratios. |
| Health, Safety, and Environment (HSE) Metrics | While these may not be financial KPIs, they are increasingly important in the industry. They can include metrics like Lost Time Injury Frequency (LTIF), Total Recordable Incident Rate (TRIR), and environmental impact measures (like CO2 emissions). |
| Hydrocarbon Pore Volume (HPV) | HPV is an estimate of the total volume of pores in a reservoir that can be filled with hydrocarbons. It's a measure of a reservoir's potential productivity. |
| Lifting Costs | Also known as production costs, they are the costs associated with the operation of oil and gas wells to bring hydrocarbons to the surface after wells (facilities necessary for the extraction) have been drilled. |
| Lost and Unaccounted for Gas (LAUF) | For midstream companies, this KPI measures the difference between the amount of gas or NGL that enters a pipeline or processing system and the amount that eventually gets delivered or processed. |
| Maturity of Reserves | This is a measure of the development stage of a company's reserves. Reserves can be classified as undeveloped, developed non-producing, and developed producing. |
| Natural Gas Liquids (NGL) Yield | This represents the volume of NGLs produced per unit of natural gas processed. Higher yields can lead to increased revenues for companies with gas processing operations. |
| Netback Price | The revenue per unit of oil or gas after all the costs to bring it to market (including royalties, operating costs, and transportation) have been subtracted. This can be used to compare profitability across companies or fields. |
| Non-Productive Time (NPT) | NPT is any time spent on activities that do not contribute directly to drilling a well, such as equipment failures or safety incidents. Lower NPT can indicate better operational efficiency and safety performance. |
| Oil and Gas Reserve Valuation | This KPI involves estimating the present value of a company's reserves. Analysts often apply a range of price and cost assumptions to estimate the net present value of future cash flows from the reserves. |
| Operating Netback | Operating netback is a measure of oil and gas sales revenue minus royalties and production costs. It's expressed on a per-unit basis (such as per barrel or per thousand cubic feet) and helps to compare profitability between companies or assets. |
| Pipeline Utilization Rate | Also for midstream companies, this indicates the amount of product transported versus the total transport capacity of their pipelines. |
| Plugging and Abandonment Costs (P&A) | These are the costs associated with sealing off a well that is no longer in use. Lower P&A costs can lead to higher end-of-life profitability. |
| Processing Capacity | For midstream companies, this refers to the total volume of oil or gas that can be processed in their facilities in a given period. |
| Rate of Penetration (ROP) | For drilling operations, ROP measures the speed at which a drill bit breaks the rock under it to deepen the well—it's usually given in feet per hour or meters per hour. Higher ROP implies more efficient drilling operations. |
| Rate of Return (RoR) on New Wells | This is a measure of the profitability of newly drilled wells. It is calculated by dividing the annual net income from the well by the total investment in drilling and completion. |
| Ratio of Proved Undeveloped Reserves (PUD) to Total Reserves | This KPI provides insight into the level of future capital investments required by the company. A higher ratio may imply more capital expenditure needed to develop these reserves. |
| Refinery Utilization Rate | For companies that have downstream operations, the refinery utilization rate measures the amount of input refined crude oil that is being used out of the total refining capacity available. Higher rates can signify optimized operations. |
| Reserve Life Index (RLI) | This is calculated by dividing the company's total proved reserves by its annual production. The resulting figure shows the number of years the company can maintain its current production level with its existing reserves. A longer reserve life may indicate a more sustainable business, but this also depends on the economic viability of extracting those reserves. |
| Reserve Replacement by Region | The replacement of reserves can also be evaluated on a regional basis to assess a company's performance in different geographies. |
| Reserve Replacement Cost | It measures the cost of replacing produced reserves. It's calculated as the total costs of exploration and development divided by the amount of new proven reserves found. |
| Reserves Replacement Ratio (RRR) | This is the amount of proved reserves added to a company's reserve base during the year relative to the amount of oil and gas produced. This ratio reflects a company's ability to maintain or expand its production levels. A ratio greater than 100% suggests that more reserves have been added than produced, which is generally a positive sign. |
| Reserves-to-Production Ratio (R/P) | Similar to RLI, the R/P ratio measures how long the reserves would last at the current rate of production. It's usually calculated annually and expressed in years. |
| Reservoir Saturation | This indicates the proportion of a reservoir's pore space filled with hydrocarbons. It's important in assessing the potential productivity of a reservoir. |
| Spud to Sales Time | This measures the time it takes from when a well is first drilled ("spudded") to when the oil or gas is ready for sale. Shorter times could indicate operational efficiency. |
| Turnaround Time (TAT) for Maintenance | TAT measures the time taken to complete maintenance tasks, including preventive, predictive, and corrective maintenance. Lower TAT can lead to higher operational availability. |
| Upstream Capital Cost Index (UCCI) | The UCCI measures the cost inflation for physical assets in the upstream sector. It’s used to monitor the changes in the cost of equipment, facilities, and other capital inputs. |
| Water Cut | This KPI refers to the percentage of water produced compared to the volume of total liquids (i.e., oil, gas, water). A rising water cut may indicate that a field is maturing and might soon deplete. |
| Well Productivity Index | This measures a well's production rate relative to the pressure difference between the reservoir and the well. It can provide insight into the reservoir's properties and the well's performance. |
| Wellhead Price | The wellhead price is the price of oil or gas at the wellhead (the point at which the oil leaves the ground). This price does not include the cost of refining, transportation, or taxes. It's used to evaluate the raw profitability of extraction operations. |
| Wells Drilled per Rig | This ratio gives a measure of the efficiency and speed of drilling operations. An increase in this metric could imply improved operational efficiency. |
