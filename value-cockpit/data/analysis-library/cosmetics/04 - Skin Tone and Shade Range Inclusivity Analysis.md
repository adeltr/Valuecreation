# 4 · Skin Tone and Shade Range Inclusivity Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Product and Research & Development (R&D)]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Cosmetics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Skin Tone and Shade Range Inclusivity Analysis is to assess the diversity and inclusivity of a cosmetics company's product offerings, particularly in foundation, concealers, and other skin-tone-related products. This analysis ensures that the brand's products cater to a broad spectrum of skin tones, enabling the company to meet the needs of a diverse customer base. The analysis also helps identify gaps in the product range and provides insights on how to improve inclusivity.


## 📊 Data required
- Product shade range for each category (e.g., foundation, concealer, powder).
- Sales data by shade (units sold, revenue).
- Consumer demographics (age, ethnicity, geographical data).
- Customer feedback and reviews on shade offerings.
- Competitor shade range data (if available).
- Market data on skin tone demographics (e.g., population data by skin tone).
- Marketing and advertising material highlighting product inclusivity.
- Return rates by shade (to identify potential dissatisfaction with shade matches).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Catalog the existing shade range.
Create a list of all available shades across relevant product categories (e.g., foundation, concealer, powder) and group them by undertone categories (e.g., cool, neutral, warm).
2. Analyze shade distribution.
Determine whether the current range covers light, medium, and dark skin tones adequately. Categorize shades into groups such as fair, light, medium, tan, deep, and very deep. Calculate the proportion of shades in each group.
3. Assess sales by shade.
Analyze sales data to understand which shades are performing well and which are underperforming. Use the following formula to calculate each shade’s share of sales:
`Shade Sales Share = (Sales of Specific Shade / Total Sales of All Shades) x 100`
4. Evaluate consumer feedback.
Review customer reviews and feedback specifically related to shade inclusivity. Identify common complaints about the lack of shades for certain skin tones or undertones.

5. Review market data.
Use demographic data to assess whether the company's shade offerings align with the skin tone diversity of its target market. For example, if the company operates in a region with a high proportion of customers with deeper skin tones, the product range should reflect that.
6. Compare to competitors.
Research and compare the company's shade range to key competitors. If possible, analyze competitor success in offering a broader shade range and their marketing strategies around inclusivity.
7. Analyze return rates by shade.
Review the return rates for each shade to identify patterns where customers may be dissatisfied with shade matching or formulation. Higher return rates may indicate that certain shades are not meeting customer expectations.
8. Audit marketing materials.
Analyze the company’s marketing campaigns and product promotions to ensure that they represent diverse skin tones in their messaging and visuals. Inclusivity should not only be present in product offerings but also branding.


## 📤 Output format
- A chart or pie graph showing the distribution of shades by skin tone category (light, medium, deep, etc.).
- A table summarizing sales performance, consumer feedback, and return rates by shade.
- A report comparing the company’s shade range to competitors and market demographics.
- A summary of customer feedback, identifying gaps in shade range coverage or common complaints.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Balanced shade distribution with strong sales across categories:** Indicates that the brand is successfully catering to a diverse customer base. There are sufficient options for all skin tones, and the product range is performing well across the board.
- **Limited shade range in certain categories:** If certain skin tones (e.g., deep or very fair) are underrepresented, this suggests an inclusivity gap. Products may need to be expanded to better cover the full spectrum of skin tones.
- **Underperforming shades with high return rates:** Shades with low sales and high return rates may indicate poor color matching or a lack of demand. These may require reformulation, better marketing, or discontinuation.

- **Lack of representation in marketing:** If marketing materials do not reflect the diversity of the brand’s product offerings, this could hinder the perception of inclusivity and alienate potential customers.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Expand the shade range:** Introduce more shades, especially in underrepresented categories such as deeper or very fair skin tones. Focus on shades with different undertones (cool, neutral, warm) to ensure a better match.
2. **Incorporate customer feedback into product development:** Use customer feedback to refine existing shades and develop new ones. Conduct focus groups with diverse participants to better understand their needs.
3. **Collaborate with diverse influencers and makeup artists:** Partner with influencers and professionals from different backgrounds to test and promote new shades. This helps the company reach diverse customer groups and ensures shades are tested across various skin tones.
4. **Improve shade matching tools:** Implement or improve tools such as online shade finders, virtual try-ons, or in-store sampling to help customers find the perfect shade.
5. **Focus marketing efforts on inclusivity:** Ensure that advertising campaigns reflect a diverse range of models with varying skin tones. Emphasize inclusivity in all marketing and product messaging.
6. **Monitor return rates and sales by shade:** Continuously track sales and returns for each shade. Use this data to refine future launches and remove shades that are not performing well.
7. **Benchmark against competitors regularly:** Regularly review and compare your shade range against key competitors to ensure that your brand remains competitive in terms of inclusivity.
