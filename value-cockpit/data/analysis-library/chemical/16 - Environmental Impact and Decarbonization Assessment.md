# 16 · Environmental Impact and Decarbonization Assessment

> **Part:** [[C - Regulatory & Compliance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Chemical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to assess the environmental impact of production activities, focusing on greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, waste generation, water usage, and overall energy consumption. By identifying decarbonization opportunities, the company can work towards reducing its carbon footprint, improving sustainability, and aligning with regulatory and stakeholder expectations on environmental responsibility.


## 📊 Data required
1. Direct and indirect greenhouse gas emissions (Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3 emissions, if possible)
2. Energy consumption by source (e.g., fossil fuels, electricity, renewables)
3. Water usage and wastewater generation data
4. Waste generation data, categorized by type (hazardous, non-hazardous)
5. Environmental regulatory limits and compliance data
6. Industry benchmarks for environmental impact and carbon intensity
7. Decarbonization costs and ROI for potential initiatives (e.g., renewable energy adoption, energy efficiency improvements)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Total Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Emissions:** Total GHG Emissions (tons CO2e) = Scope 1 (direct emissions) + Scope 2 (indirect emissions from purchased energy) + Scope 3 (other indirect emissions)
Scope 1 includes emissions from on-site combustion; Scope 2 includes emissions from purchased electricity; Scope 3 includes emissions from the supply chain, waste disposal, and product distribution.
2. **Determine Carbon Intensity per Unit of Production:**
`Carbon Intensity = Total GHG Emissions / Production Output (tons or units)`
This metric provides insight into the emissions generated per unit of production, helping identify processes or products with the highest carbon footprint.
For example, if total GHG emissions are 10,000 tons CO2e and output is 5,000 tons:
`Carbon Intensity = 10,000 / 5,000 = 2 tons CO2e per ton of product`

3. **Analyze Energy Consumption by Source:** Calculate the percentage of total energy sourced from fossil fuels, electricity, and renewables. This breakdown highlights the facility’s reliance on high-emission energy sources and identifies opportunities for transitioning to low-carbon energy sources.
Renewable Energy Use (%) = (Renewable Energy Consumption / Total Energy Consumption) x 100
4. **Evaluate Water Usage and Waste Generation:** Track total water use, wastewater generation, and waste production volumes. Measure water intensity (e.g., liters per ton of product) and waste generation per unit of production. Identify high-impact areas and compliance with environmental regulations.
5. **Benchmark Against Industry Standards:** Compare GHG emissions, carbon intensity, energy mix, water usage, and waste generation with industry averages. Facilities with higher-than-average environmental impacts or lower renewable energy use may benefit from targeted decarbonization strategies.
6. **Identify Decarbonization and Sustainability Opportunities:** Explore specific initiatives for reducing emissions, such as:
- **Energy Efficiency Improvements:** Optimize equipment and processes to reduce energy use.
- **Renewable Energy Adoption:** Transition to solar, wind, or other renewable energy sources.
- **Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS):** Capture and store carbon emissions from high-emission processes.
- **Process Redesign:** Modify production methods to reduce carbon intensity and waste.
7. **Calculate Decarbonization Costs and Project ROI:** Estimate the costs associated with potential decarbonization projects and calculate expected ROI, considering factors such as energy savings, tax incentives, and carbon credits.


## 📤 Output format
1.   Total GHG Emissions and Carbon Intensity metrics
2.   Energy Consumption by source (fossil fuels, renewables, etc.)
3.   Water usage and waste generation metrics
4.   Benchmark comparison for environmental impact indicators
5.   List of recommended decarbonization initiatives with estimated costs and ROI


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
How to Analyze a Chemicals Company
- **High carbon intensity and fossil fuel reliance:** Indicates that the facility has significant potential for decarbonization and may benefit from transitioning to renewable energy and energy-efficient processes.
- **High water usage and waste generation:** Points to opportunities to reduce resource consumption and improve sustainability by recycling, waste reduction, or water efficiency initiatives.
- **Low environmental impact compared to industry:** Suggests that the facility is already performing well, but further improvements can help maintain leadership in environmental sustainability.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Increase Renewable Energy Use:** Invest in renewable energy sources, such as on-site solar or wind, or purchase renewable energy credits (RECs) to offset emissions.
2. **Improve Process Efficiency:** Optimize production processes to reduce energy and water use, thereby lowering emissions and operational costs.
3. **Implement Carbon Capture and Storage:** For high-emission facilities, CCS can help reduce carbon output, particularly for processes where emissions are difficult to avoid.
4. **Adopt Circular Economy Practices:** Recycle waste materials or explore ways to repurpose by-products, reducing overall waste and lowering environmental impact.
5. **Set Emission Reduction Targets:** Establish measurable, time-bound GHG emission reduction targets to align with regulatory trends and market expectations.
