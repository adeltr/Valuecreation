# 18 · Engagement-to-Subscription Rate

> **Part:** [[B - Sales & Marketing]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Engagement-to-Subscription Rate analysis is to measure how effectively a SaaS company is converting engaged users (e.g., free trial users or freemium customers) into paying subscribers. This metric helps assess the quality of the user experience, the product’s perceived value, and the effectiveness of the company’s sales or marketing efforts aimed at converting active users into paying customers.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Number of engaged users:** The total number of users who actively engage with the product during a given period (e.g., free trial users, freemium users).
2. **Number of users who convert to a paid subscription:** The total number of users who subscribe to a paid plan during the same period.
3. **User engagement metrics (optional):** Detailed engagement data such as frequency of use, feature adoption, or time spent on the platform to better understand patterns that drive conversions.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Deﬁne what constitutes an "engaged user":
o    Establish clear criteria for engagement. For instance, an engaged user could be someone who has logged in multiple times during a free trial period, used core features of the platform, or completed key onboarding steps. These criteria may vary depending on the business model and the product’s features.
2. Determine the number of engaged users during a speciﬁc period:
o    Count the total number of users who meet the engagement criteria during a given period (e.g., a month or a quarter). These users could be free trial users, freemium users, or users interacting with key features of the product.
o    For example, if 2,000 users sign up for a free trial and engage with the product over a month, the number of engaged users would be 2,000.
3. Calculate the number of users who convert to paid subscriptions:
o    Identify the number of engaged users who upgrade to a paid subscription within the same time frame. This data can typically be pulled from subscription or billing records.
56 o    For example, if 500 of the 2,000 engaged users convert to paying customers, the number of converted users is 500.
4. Calculate the Engagement-to-Subscription Rate:
o    The Engagement-to-Subscription Rate measures the percentage of engaged users who convert to a paid subscription.
o    Use the formula:
`Engagement-to-Subscription Rate (%) = (Number of Users Who Convert to Paid Subscription / Number of Engaged Users) x 100`
o    For example, if 500 users out of 2,000 engaged users convert to paying subscribers:
`Engagement-to-Subscription Rate = (500 / 2,000) x 100 = 25%`
5. Analyze engagement patterns (optional):
o    Further segment users based on their engagement levels, such as frequent users vs. occasional users, to better understand which behaviors or usage patterns correlate with higher conversion rates. This can help reﬁne your product or marketing strategies.


## 📤 Output format
The output is typically presented as the Engagement-to-Subscription Rate along with supporting metrics such as the number of engaged users and the number of users who converted to paying subscriptions. Example format:
•   Number of Engaged Users: 2,000 •   Number of Conversions to Paid Subscription: 500 •   Engagement-to-Subscription Rate: 25% Optionally, you can include segmented analysis, such as conversion rates for different engagement levels or customer segments.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   High Engagement-to-Subscription Rate: A high conversion rate indicates that engaged users are ﬁnding signiﬁcant value in the product and are willing to pay for a subscription. This suggests that the product’s value proposition is strong, and the onboarding process or trial experience effectively demonstrates its beneﬁts.
•   Low Engagement-to-Subscription Rate: A low conversion rate suggests that while users may be engaging with the product, they are not sufficiently convinced to pay for it. This could be due to several factors, including unclear product value, pricing misalignment, or issues with the free-to-paid user experience.
57 Benchmarking this rate against industry standards and historical data is crucial to understanding whether the company is performing well relative to its peers or if improvements are needed.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Enhance the free trial or freemium experience:** Make sure that engaged users experience the product’s core value as quickly as possible. Streamline onboarding and guide users to explore key features that are most likely to drive conversions.
2. **Optimize pricing and plans:** Ensure that the pricing structure aligns with the perceived value of the product. Offering ﬂexible pricing plans, introductory discounts, or incentives to upgrade can encourage conversions from free to paid tiers.
3. **Improve user engagement during the free period:** Use email marketing, in-app messaging, and personalized tips to keep users engaged during their trial or freemium usage. Highlight features and success stories to build a connection between user needs and product capabilities.
4. **Use data to identify high-intent users:** Segment engaged users based on behaviors that indicate higher conversion potential, such as frequent use or interaction with premium features. Focus personalized marketing efforts on these users to improve conversion rates.
5. **OKer targeted incentives to convert:** Provide special offers, such as extended trial periods, discounted ﬁrst-month pricing, or premium feature unlocks, to nudge highly engaged users into subscribing.
6. **Address user objections:** Collect feedback from users who engage but do not convert, identifying their pain points or objections to subscribing. Use this feedback to adjust the product, pricing, or messaging to better address user concerns.
