# 38 · Export and Import Credit Agency Utilization Review

> **Part:** [[H - Specialized and Industry-Specific Solutions]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Commercial Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine how effectively the bank leverages export and import credit agency programs to provide trade finance solutions, mitigate credit risks, and support client transactions.


## 📊 Data required
- Portfolio of loans or guarantees supported by export credit agencies (ECAs) or import credit agencies
- Transaction details (e.g., industry, country, product type) showing how ECA coverage was applied
- ECA program terms, eligibility criteria, and fee structures
- Operational and administrative costs associated with ECA application and compliance processes
- Historical performance data (e.g., defaults, claims paid, recovery rates) on ECA- backed transactions
- Peer or industry benchmarks on ECA usage rates and success stories


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Identify all transactions within the bank’s portfolio that utilize ECA-backed financing or guarantees. Gather detailed information, including the amounts financed, borrowing terms, and countries or sectors involved.
Step 2: Review the bank’s internal procedures for ECA application, underwriting, and documentation, noting where the bank might face bottlenecks or inefficiencies in leveraging these programs.
Step 3: Calculate the incremental benefits of ECA support by comparing risk-adjusted returns (e.g., interest spreads, fee income) and default rates for ECA-backed deals versus non-ECA transactions.
Step 4: Assess the compliance and administrative costs linked to ECA usage, such as application fees, ongoing reporting requirements, or additional due diligence steps. Compare these costs to the risk mitigation and revenue benefits gained.
Step 5: Benchmark the bank’s utilization rates and success in obtaining ECA coverage against peer banks or industry norms, identifying opportunities to expand into untapped ECA programs or regions.
81 Step 6: Consolidate findings into a comprehensive report, highlighting best practices, gaps in current usage, and recommendations to maximize the advantages of ECA- backed financing and mitigate associated risks.


## 📤 Output format
- Spreadsheet or table enumerating ECA-supported deals, including volumes, fees, maturities, and profitability metrics
- Charts comparing default or delinquency rates for ECA-backed vs. non-ECA- backed transactions
- Narrative summary discussing the cost-benefit balance, any procedural hurdles, and recommended strategies


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High profitability and low default rates on ECA-backed deals generally indicate effective risk mitigation and a strong utilization strategy.
- Excessive administrative costs or lengthy processes may outweigh the benefits of ECA coverage, suggesting the need to refine internal workflows.
- Underutilization in certain regions or industry segments might highlight growth potential if the bank can streamline processes or deepen relationships with ECAs.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Streamline internal processes for ECA-backed deals by centralizing documentation and standardizing due diligence procedures.
2. Train relationship managers and credit officers on the scope and eligibility requirements of various ECA programs.
3. Negotiate or collaborate with ECAs to speed up approval cycles or reduce administrative overhead.
4. Develop targeted marketing or outreach to clients in sectors or countries that could benefit most from ECA coverage.
5. Continuously monitor regulatory changes or new ECA initiatives to expand product offerings and stay competitive.
