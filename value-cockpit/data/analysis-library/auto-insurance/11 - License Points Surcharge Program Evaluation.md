# 11 · License Points Surcharge Program Evaluation

> **Part:** [[A - Pricing & Product Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Assess the effectiveness of the license points surcharge program in reflecting driver risk and maintaining profitability, while evaluating the impact on policyholder behavior and retention.


## 📊 Data required
- Driver license point records for current policyholders (including surcharges applied)
- Historical loss data (frequency, severity, total incurred losses) segmented by license point levels
- Premium and exposure data (written premium, earned premium, number of policies) for surcharged and non-surcharged drivers
- Retention and renewal data segmented by point levels and surcharge status
- Regulatory guidelines on point-based surcharges (if applicable)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify all policies subject to the license points surcharge and collect detailed information on surcharges applied, driver point totals, earned premium, and incurred losses. Organize data for at least 2–3 policy terms to see trend patterns.
2. Calculate claim frequency and severity for both surcharged and non-surcharged drivers. Use Claim Frequency = (Number of Claims / Number of Policies) x 100 and compare results across different point brackets (e.g., 1–2 points, 3–4 points).
3. Determine the surcharge program loss ratio by applying Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + Loss Adjustment Expenses) / Earned Premium for surcharged drivers. Compare to non-surcharged drivers to identify whether the surcharge appropriately offsets higher risk.
4. Assess the impact on retention by reviewing renewal rates for drivers in various point brackets. Look for any correlation between higher surcharges and increased policy churn.
5. Examine driver behavior changes by tracking whether license point levels decrease over time, possibly indicating safer driving habits. Note if surcharged drivers reduce violations or switch insurers.

6. Benchmark against regulatory or industry standards if point surcharges are regulated or widely used in your market. Look for significant deviations that might suggest misalignment with norms or risk trends.
7. Review current surcharge amounts and intervals (e.g., every additional point adds a set percentage to premium). Determine if adjustments are needed to better correlate with actual risk or to mitigate excessive churn.
8. Summarize findings and recommend program adjustments such as increasing or decreasing surcharge amounts, adjusting thresholds, or introducing a point forgiveness feature. Present the evaluation to key stakeholders for potential refinement.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts illustrating loss ratios, claim frequencies, and retention rates by point brackets
- Written summary of how well current surcharges align with actual loss trends
- Recommendations on surcharge adjustments and possible impacts on risk selection and retention


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Surcharged drivers with disproportionately high loss ratios suggest surcharges may still be insufficient to offset higher risk levels.
- If surcharged drivers are exiting at a high rate, it may indicate surcharges are too steep, potentially driving away moderate-risk policyholders.
- Lack of change in license point patterns suggests surcharges may not be incentivizing safer driving or could be poorly understood by policyholders.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Introduce driver education programs or discounts that encourage improved driving behaviors, potentially reducing points.
2. Adjust surcharge amounts or thresholds to more accurately reflect the relationship between points and claims risk.
3. Consider flexible programs, such as point forgiveness after a period of safe driving, to retain customers while still managing risk.
4. Communicate surcharge rationale and potential benefits of safe driving to policyholders, enhancing transparency and retention.
