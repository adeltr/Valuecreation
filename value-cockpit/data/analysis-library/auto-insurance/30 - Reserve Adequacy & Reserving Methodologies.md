# 30 · Reserve Adequacy & Reserving Methodologies

> **Part:** [[D - Financial Performance & Reserving]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate whether existing reserves are sufficient to cover future claim obligations and assess the effectiveness of current actuarial methodologies (e.g., chain-ladder, Bornhuetter-Ferguson) used to determine those reserves.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Historical claims data (paid and incurred losses, loss adjustment expenses)
- ​ Exposure data (earned premium, policy counts)
- ​ Run-off triangles for various lines of business (accident year, development period)
- ​ External factors (inflation rates, legal environment changes)
- ​ Current reserve levels by line of business


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Gather all relevant claims data over a consistent time frame to construct run-off triangles or other actuarial exhibits. Ensure the data includes both paid and incurred losses for each accident or policy year. 2.​ Select appropriate actuarial reserving techniques such as chain-ladder or Bornhuetter-Ferguson based on data maturity and line of business. Apply the chosen methodologies to estimate ultimate losses for each segment. 3.​ Compare estimated ultimate losses with current booked reserves to calculate Reserve Adequacy = (Booked Reserves - Actuarial Estimate of Ultimate Losses). Identify lines of business where reserves appear deficient or redundant. 4.​ Review historical reserve accuracy by examining prior actuarial estimates against actual outcomes. Pinpoint recurring variances and possible biases in assumptions or methodology. 5.​ Analyze external cost drivers and trends (e.g., inflation, litigation environment) to refine assumptions. Adjust projections if significant, quantifiable shifts are observed. 6.​ Benchmark your reserve position against industry or competitor data if available. Look for major deviations that could suggest over-aggressive or overly conservative reserving. 7.​ Develop a range of best-estimate reserves using multiple methods or scenarios. Validate the reasonableness of each scenario and reconcile differences.
65 8.​ Document findings and propose changes to methodologies or assumptions that materially impact reserve levels. Present these results to finance, risk management, and executive teams for approval or further action.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ Tables or charts showing paid and incurred loss development triangles, ultimate loss projections, and reserve adequacy calculations
- ​ Written summary detailing assumptions used in each reserving methodology and comparing booked vs. indicated reserves
- ​ Sensitivity analysis that illustrates how changes in key variables (e.g., inflation) impact reserve adequacy


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ A negative reserve adequacy figure suggests a shortfall and potential need for strengthening reserves to align with expected losses.
- ​ Large divergences between different methods signal high uncertainty in the underlying data or assumptions, warranting deeper investigation.
- ​ Consistent prior-year reserve deficiencies or redundancies indicate potential biases in the reserving process.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Implement regular reserve reviews using multiple actuarial methods to gain a comprehensive view of potential outcomes. 2.​ Enhance data quality and granularity, including segmentation by coverage type, to produce more refined projections. 3.​ Incorporate robust scenario testing (e.g., economic downturns, inflation spikes) to assess reserve adequacy under stress conditions. 4.​ Continuously update models to reflect legal, technological, and economic changes that may alter claim patterns.
