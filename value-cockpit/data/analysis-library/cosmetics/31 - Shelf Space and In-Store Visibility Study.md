# 31 · Shelf Space and In-Store Visibility Study

> **Part:** [[C - Sales and Distribution]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Cosmetics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Shelf Space and In-Store Visibility Study is to assess how effectively the brand’s products are displayed and positioned within retail stores to maximize visibility, consumer engagement, and sales. This analysis evaluates the brand’s shelf placement, share of shelf space, product presentation, and promotional visibility compared to competitors. The insights help the company optimize its in-store presence, ensuring that its products attract consumer attention and are placed in locations that encourage purchase decisions.


## 📊 Data required
- Store layout and planogram data, showing the brand’s product placement and shelf positioning.
- Shelf space allocation data, comparing the brand’s share of shelf space relative to competitors.
- Consumer traffic patterns and hot spot data within stores (e.g., high-traffic areas, checkout zones).
- Sales data segmented by store and product category (linked to shelf space and visibility).
- Retailer agreements and negotiations for shelf space and promotional displays.
- In-store promotional data (e.g., endcaps, signage, special displays).
- Store-level product placement audits (e.g., photos or reports of shelf conditions and compliance with planograms).
- Competitor shelf space and visibility data.
- Consumer behavior data regarding in-store product discovery and impulse buying.
- Retailer feedback on the brand’s in-store performance and opportunities for better visibility.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Analyze shelf placement and store layout:** a. Collect planogram data and in-store photos to evaluate where the brand’s products are positioned in the store. b. Determine if the products are placed in high-traffic areas (e.g., eye level, near the front of the store) or low-visibility locations (e.g., bottom shelves, back aisles).
2. **Measure shelf space allocation:** a. Compare the amount of shelf space allocated to the brand’s products relative to competitors in the same category. b. Assess whether the brand is underrepresented or overrepresented in terms of shelf space compared to its market share.
3. **Evaluate consumer traffic and hot spots:** a. Use store traffic data to identify high-traffic zones where products are more likely to be seen and purchased.
113 b. Analyze whether the brand’s products are placed in or near these hot spots, such as near checkout lanes, promotional displays, or popular product sections.
4. **Link shelf space to sales performance:** a. Analyze sales data for each store, comparing performance across locations with different levels of shelf space and visibility. b. Identify whether increased shelf space or prime product placement correlates with higher sales volumes.
5. **Review retailer agreements for shelf space:** a. Examine contracts and agreements with retailers regarding shelf space allocation, promotional displays, and product visibility. b. Assess whether the brand is receiving fair shelf space or if competitors have negotiated better terms.
6. **Assess in-store promotional visibility:** a. Track the use of in-store promotional tools such as endcaps, signage, floor displays, and seasonal promotions. b. Evaluate how effectively these promotional displays drive consumer engagement and whether they increase product visibility.
7. **Conduct store-level product placement audits:** a. Audit stores to verify that products are placed according to planograms and meet agreed-upon shelf positioning requirements. b. Identify any deviations from planned shelf space allocations or visibility agreements and work with retailers to correct them.
8. **Benchmark competitor shelf space and visibility:** a. Compare the brand’s shelf space and in-store visibility to that of key competitors, focusing on high-demand product categories. b. Analyze whether competitors have better shelf positioning, larger space allocations, or more frequent promotional displays.
9. **Analyze consumer behavior regarding product discovery:** a. Gather data on how consumers discover products in-store, including whether they actively seek out the brand or make impulse purchases based on visibility. b. Identify any barriers that prevent consumers from noticing or purchasing the brand’s products.
10. **Collect retailer feedback on in-store performance:** a. Conduct interviews or surveys with retailers to gather feedback on how the brand performs in terms of sales, visibility, and product placement compliance. b. Use retailer insights to identify opportunities for improving shelf space negotiation, in-store marketing, and product presentation.


## 📤 Output format
- A shelf space allocation report comparing the brand’s share of shelf space relative to competitors across different stores.
- A shelf placement analysis showing where the brand’s products are positioned in stores, including whether they are in high-visibility or low-visibility areas.

- A sales performance report linked to shelf space and in-store visibility, showing how different levels of shelf presence correlate with sales.
- A comparison of in-store promotional visibility, highlighting the brand’s use of endcaps, signage, and special displays versus competitors.
- A store-level product placement audit report, including compliance with planograms and retailer agreements.
- A competitor benchmarking report analyzing shelf space, visibility, and promotional presence in stores.
- A consumer traffic and hot spot analysis, showing how the brand’s product placement aligns with high-traffic areas in stores.
- Recommendations for optimizing shelf space negotiations, improving in-store visibility, and leveraging promotions to drive consumer engagement.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High shelf space and strong sales correlation:** If stores with more shelf space allocated to the brand’s products show higher sales, this indicates that visibility plays a key role in driving purchases. The brand should negotiate for more space or better positioning in underperforming stores.
- **Low shelf space relative to competitors:** If competitors consistently receive more shelf space or better visibility, the brand may be losing sales opportunities. It should renegotiate terms with retailers or offer promotional incentives to secure better placements.
- **Inconsistent placement across stores:** If there is inconsistency in how the brand’s products are displayed across stores (e.g., placed at eye level in some stores but on the bottom shelf in others), the company should work with retailers to enforce more consistent planogram compliance.
- **Limited use of promotional displays:** If the brand’s use of in-store promotional displays is limited or less effective than competitors, the brand should invest more in high-visibility promotions such as endcaps, seasonal displays, or interactive in-store experiences.
- **Retailer feedback on visibility and performance:** If retailers suggest that better placement or promotional support could improve sales, the brand should prioritize these strategies to strengthen in-store performance.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Negotiate for prime shelf space:** Work closely with retailers to negotiate better shelf positioning, particularly at eye level or in high-traffic areas. Offer incentives such as co-marketing or promotional support to secure these placements.
2. **Expand shelf space allocation:** If competitors are dominating shelf space, the brand should work with retailers to increase its share of space, particularly in stores where demand is high but visibility is low.

3. **Invest in in-store promotions:** Utilize endcaps, signage, and other in-store promotional displays to boost product visibility. Coordinate with retailers to feature products in high-traffic zones such as near checkout lanes or store entrances.
4. **Ensure planogram compliance:** Conduct regular store audits to ensure that products are placed according to agreed-upon planograms and shelf space allocations. Address any discrepancies by working directly with retailers to resolve issues.
5. **Monitor competitor strategies:** Keep track of how competitors use in-store visibility and promotions to drive sales. Learn from their successes and adopt similar tactics or develop differentiated strategies to stand out.
6. **Leverage consumer behavior insights:** Use insights on consumer traffic patterns and product discovery to adjust shelf placement and promotional strategies. Place products in areas where consumers are more likely to make impulse purchases or notice new offerings.
7. **Strengthen retailer partnerships:** Foster strong relationships with retailers by offering tailored support, including co-marketing initiatives, sales incentives, and training programs for in-store staff to improve product knowledge and recommendations.
