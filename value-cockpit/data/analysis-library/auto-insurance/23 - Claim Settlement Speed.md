# 23 · Claim Settlement Speed

> **Part:** [[C - Claims & Loss Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Measure how quickly claims progress from first notice of loss (FNOL) to final settlement, understand factors influencing settlement duration, and develop strategies to accelerate claims closure.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical claims data (date of FNOL, date of settlement, type of claim)
- Cycle time metrics for each stage of the claims process (e.g., investigation, documentation, approval)
- Complexity indicators (type of damage, number of parties involved)
- Claims adjuster caseload data
- Internal service-level agreements (SLAs) or benchmarks for claim closure


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather historical claims records noting the time stamps for each significant claim milestone. Calculate total days from FNOL to settlement for each claim.
2. Segment the data by claim type (e.g., property damage only, bodily injury, total loss) and level of complexity (single-vehicle incident vs. multi-vehicle, straightforward vs. litigious). Compare average settlement times across these categories.
3. Identify the bottlenecks in the claim lifecycle by examining how many days are spent in each phase (e.g., investigation, documentation gathering, repair estimate, negotiation). Look for patterns that consistently extend settlement times.
4. Calculate the average and median settlement times (days or weeks) for each segment. Compare these metrics to any internal or external benchmarks (e.g., industry-wide averages) to identify potential underperformance.
5. Correlate settlement speed with other variables such as adjuster workload, technology usage (digital channels vs. manual), or policyholder demographics. Look for trends that could inform targeted process improvements.
6. Evaluate customer feedback (if available) regarding timely settlements. Check whether longer cycle times correlate with lower satisfaction or higher complaint rates.
7. Benchmark results against competitor data or industry standards to see where your claim settlement speed stands relative to the market.

8. Document findings and develop targeted improvement plans (e.g., process automation, staff training, revised guidelines for high-complexity cases). Present these strategies to claims leadership and operations teams.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts showing average and median settlement times by claim type, complexity, or region
- A written summary of key bottlenecks and recommended process optimizations
- Projections of how proposed improvements could reduce settlement durations and improve policyholder satisfaction


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Higher settlement times in certain segments may indicate training gaps, inefficient workflows, or heavier caseloads for certain adjusters.
- Large variances between actual and benchmark settlement speeds highlight focus areas for process reengineering.
- Correlations between slow settlements and negative customer feedback underscore the impact of timeliness on retention and reputation.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Automate repetitive tasks (e.g., digital document submission, AI-powered claim triage) to accelerate early claim stages.
2. Optimize adjuster workloads, ensuring balanced case assignments and specialized training for complex claims.
3. Introduce or refine service-level agreements with third parties (repair facilities, medical providers) to reduce external delays.
4. Provide policyholders with transparent updates and self-service tools, alleviating call center loads and building trust during the claim process.
