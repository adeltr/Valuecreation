# 40 · Climate Resilience and Stormwater Management for Landfills

> **Part:** [[G - Regulatory Compliance & Environmental Impact]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Waste Management Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate a landfill’s vulnerability to increasing weather extremes (e.g., heavy rainfall, storms) and develop strategies for managing stormwater and other climate-related risks, ensuring long-term site stability and regulatory compliance.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Historical precipitation and storm event data (rainfall intensity, duration, frequency)
- ​ Landfill design and drainage system specifications (liners, berms, leachate collection, stormwater channels)
- ​ Topography and soil characteristics of the landfill and surrounding area
- ​ Climate projections or future weather scenarios (e.g., 100-year storm events)
- ​ Current stormwater management practices (detention ponds, perimeter drainage, pumps)
- ​ Regulatory requirements for stormwater discharge or flood protection
- ​ Maintenance and inspection records for drainage infrastructure


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Assess current site conditions and stormwater infrastructure​ Review engineering drawings, topographic maps, and any existing reports on stormwater flow. Identify critical points (e.g., channels, berms, culverts) prone to overflow or erosion.
2. Analyze historical and projected precipitation data​ Compile records of peak rainfall events and compare them with design assumptions (e.g., 25-year or 100-year storm). Use climate forecasts to understand how extreme rainfall may change.
3. Conduct hydraulic and hydrologic modeling​ Model how stormwater flows through and around the landfill under different rainfall intensities. Evaluate current system capacity (ditches, ponds, pipes) against peak flow projections.
4. Evaluate leachate management during extreme events​ Determine if high-intensity storms could overwhelm leachate collection systems, raising the risk of contaminated runoff or groundwater infiltration.

5. Identify vulnerabilities and potential failure points​ Document areas where existing infrastructure may fail or become insufficient under more frequent or severe storms (e.g., undersized culverts, insufficient freeboard in stormwater ponds).
6. Develop resilience measures​ Propose structural upgrades (e.g., larger drainage channels, reinforced berms, expanded detention ponds) and operational improvements (e.g., enhanced inspections, contingency pumping).
7. Create a climate resilience plan​ Outline specific actions, timelines, and costs for implementing stormwater upgrades, improving leachate controls, and addressing future climate scenarios. Include training for staff and ongoing monitoring.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ A stormwater and climate resilience report detailing the landfill’s current drainage capacity, vulnerabilities, and projected future risks
- ​ Hydraulic/hydrologic modeling outputs (e.g., flow maps, peak water levels) under various storm scenarios
- ​ Tables or charts comparing current design standards vs. climate-adjusted requirements
- ​ Action plan listing recommended upgrades, maintenance schedules, and estimated costs


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ Significant gaps between existing infrastructure capacity and projected storm intensity indicate an urgent need for upgrades.
- ​ Frequent overflow or near-capacity events suggest that even moderate improvements (e.g., debris removal, slope grading) may help mitigate risk.
- ​ Incorporating climate projections into landfill planning ensures that stormwater management remains robust over the landfill’s operational life and post-closure phase.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Update landfill design criteria to include more frequent storm events or higher rainfall intensities based on emerging climate models. 2.​ Implement real-time monitoring of stormwater pond levels, drainage flows, and leachate levels to detect potential issues quickly.
