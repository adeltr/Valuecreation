# 13 · Revenue Management Integration with Local-Seasonal Event Calendars

> **Part:** [[A - Revenue & Pricing Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Align pricing and inventory strategies with demand peaks around local events and seasons to capitalize on high-demand periods and minimize low-occupancy times.


## 📊 Data required
- A detailed calendar of local events, holidays, and conferences.
- Historical occupancy and revenue data correlated with past events.
- Competitor pricing and availability (if accessible).
- Forecasted event attendance, if available, or historical attendance trends.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Compile a comprehensive local event calendar. Include festivals, trade shows, sporting events, and holiday periods relevant to your market.
2. Map historical performance data to event dates. Identify spikes or dips in occupancy and ADR that correspond to these events in prior years.
3. Estimate expected demand for upcoming events. Use factors like projected attendance, event size, or past performance for each.
4. Adjust pricing strategies around high‐demand events. Implement yield management tactics to capture premium rates when demand peaks.
5. Develop targeted promotions for low‐demand periods. Align special offers with quieter periods or smaller events to stimulate bookings.
6. Coordinate marketing efforts. Update promotional materials and messaging to highlight proximity to or tie‐ins with local events.
7. Monitor competitive set pricing during event periods. Track rate changes among comparable hotels to stay competitive while maximizing revenue.
8. Review event performance post‐period. Evaluate actual vs. forecast occupancy and ADR to refine future strategies.


## 📤 Output format
- A calendar or timeline mapping each local/seasonal event to projected and actual occupancy, ADR, and RevPAR.
- Tables comparing pre‐event forecasts and post‐event outcomes (occupancy, ADR, revenue).
30
- Written assessment summarizing successes, missed opportunities, and recommended changes for future events.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Significant occupancy or ADR upticks tied to event dates indicate successful event‐driven pricing strategies.
- Underperformance vs. forecast may signal overly aggressive pricing or insufficient marketing.
- Tracking differences between event types (e.g., citywide conventions vs. smaller festivals) helps tailor future tactics.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Collaborate with local tourism boards or event organizers for insight on projected attendee counts and demographic data.
2. Create event‐specific room packages (e.g., discounted parking for concertgoers, special welcome amenities).
3. Monitor booking pace closely during lead‐up to major events and adjust rates in real time.
4. Conduct a post‐event audit to refine forecasting accuracy and enhance strategies for subsequent events.
