# KPI — Financial Services

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Banking & Financial Services]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Commercial Bank - Analyse]], [[Retail Bank - Analyse]]

50 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Active Users (Financial Technology Firms) | This KPI measures the number of active users for a financial technology platform. It can indicate the reach and popularity of a platform. |
| Alpha (Investment Industry) | Alpha measures the performance of an investment relative to a benchmark index. A positive alpha means the investment has outperformed the benchmark. |
| Annuity Surrender Rate (Insurance Industry) | This is the ratio of the number of annuities surrendered by customers to the total number of annuities in force. It's an important measure for insurance companies offering life insurance and annuity products. |
| Assets Under Management (AUM) (Asset Management) | This KPI measures the total market value of all the financial assets which an investment company manages on behalf of its clients and themselves. |
| Average Cost Per Trade (Brokerage Firms) | This metric measures the average cost that a brokerage firm incurs to execute a trade. It's a key profitability metric for brokerage firms. |
| Average Trading Value (Brokerage Firms) | This measures the average value of trades executed on the brokerage platform. It can provide insights into customer activity and preferences. |
| Basel III Ratios (Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1), Tier 1 Capital Ratio, and Total Capital Ratio) | These are international regulatory banking standards. They set out the minimum capital requirements for banks, which helps investors evaluate a bank's resilience to financial stress. |
| Beta (Investment Industry) | Beta measures the volatility or systematic risk of an investment or a portfolio compared to the market as a whole. A beta greater than 1 indicates the investment is more volatile than the market. |
| Claims Ratio (Insurance Industry) | This is the ratio of claims paid to policyholders to the premiums earned by the insurance company. A high ratio could mean that an insurer is not pricing its products appropriately or has high losses. |
| Claims Settlement Ratio (Insurance) | This is the ratio of the number of insurance claims settled by an insurer to the total number of claims received. A high claims settlement ratio means the insurer is reliable when it comes to settling claims. |
| Combined Ratio (Insurance Industry) | This ratio is used by insurance companies to measure the profitability of their underwriting operations. It's calculated by taking the sum of incurred losses and expenses and then dividing them by the earned premium. A ratio below 100% indicates profitable underwriting. |
| Combined Ratio (Insurance) | The combined ratio measures the profitability of an insurance company by adding the loss ratio (losses to earned premiums) and the expense ratio (expenses to earned premiums). A combined ratio under 100% indicates underwriting profitability. |
| Cost-to-Income Ratio (CIR) | This KPI shows a company’s costs in relation to its income, used by the banks to measure operational efficiency. The lower the percentage, the more efficient the bank’s operations. |
| Credit Card Charge-Off Rate (Consumer Financial Services) | This measures the amount of debt a credit card company has given up on collecting, as a percentage of its total debt. A high charge-off rate can be a sign of risk in a credit card portfolio. |
| Default Rate (Lending) | This is the rate at which borrowers fail to remain current on their loans. It's a crucial risk metric for lending businesses. |
| Dividend Yield (General) | This is the annual dividend payment shareholders receive for each share they own, divided by the share price. While used across industries, it's a crucial metric for income-focused investors in the financial services sector. |
| Duration Gap (Banking) | This is a measure of a bank's interest rate risk. It considers the sensitivity of the market values of the bank's assets and liabilities to changes in interest rates. |
| Earnings per Share (EPS) | While it's common across all industries, it’s crucial for financial services firms given the regulatory oversight and capital-intensive nature of the industry. EPS measures the portion of a company's profit allocated to each outstanding share of common stock. |
| Expense Ratio (Mutual Funds/Asset Management) | This ratio measures the costs of managing a fund relative to the total fund assets. It is a key KPI for mutual funds and asset management companies. A lower expense ratio usually indicates a cost-effective fund management. |
| Fee Income to Total Income (Banking) | This measures the extent to which a bank relies on fees (as opposed to interest) for its income. In a low-interest-rate environment, a higher ratio can be a good sign as it signals diversified income streams. |
| Funds from Operations (FFO - REITs) | This is a measure of cash generation for Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) and is used to compare the cash performance of REITs. |
| Incurred Loss Ratio (Insurance) | This ratio compares losses due to claims plus loss adjustment expense to the premiums earned. It measures the profitability of an insurer's underwriting operations. |
| Insurance Expense Ratio | This measures a company's operational costs in relation to its total net premiums. It's a measure of an insurance company's efficiency. |
| Insurance Loss Ratio | For insurance companies, this ratio measures the percentage of premiums earned that were paid out as claims. A high ratio may indicate that the insurance company is regularly paying out large claims or that it's priced its insurance too cheaply. |
| Interest Rate Spread | This metric is particularly important for banks and lenders. It represents the difference between the average rate at which they lend and the average rate at which they borrow. It can serve as an indicator of a bank’s profitability. |
| Leverage Ratio (Banking & Investment Industry) | This ratio compares a company's total debts to its total shareholders' equity. A higher leverage ratio indicates a company that's more aggressively financed by debt. |
| Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) | This KPI is specific to banking. It measures a bank's ability to meet its short-term liquidity needs in a 30-day stress scenario. The ratio is calculated by dividing high-quality liquid assets by total net cash outflows over a 30-day period. |
| Loan Loss Reserves to Non-performing Loans | This is a ratio that measures the total amount of reserves a bank has in place to cover potential loan losses compared to the total amount of non-performing loans. It indicates the level of risk associated with a bank's loan portfolio. |
| Loan-to-Deposit Ratio (LDR) | This ratio compares a bank's total loans to its total deposits, which can give investors an indication of a bank's liquidity and ability to cover any sudden runs. |
| Loss Ratio (Insurance) | This ratio measures the losses an insurer incurs due to paid claims as a percentage of premiums earned. A lower loss ratio implies more profitability. |
| Net Interest Margin (Banking) | Net interest margin is the difference between the interest income generated by banks and the amount of interest paid out to their lenders, relative to the amount of their interest-earning assets. |
| Net Interest Margin (NIM) | This is a measure of the difference between the interest income generated by banks or other financial institutions and the amount of interest paid out to their lenders (for example, deposits), relative to the amount of their (interest-earning) assets. It's a key measure of a bank's profitability. |
| Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) | This banking KPI measures a bank's ability to maintain stable funding over a longer-term time horizon of one year. It’s calculated by dividing available stable funding by required stable funding. |
| Non-interest income to total income (Banking) | This ratio measures the proportion of a bank's income not derived from interest. It could include income from fees, transaction charges, or trading operations. A higher ratio suggests the bank is less dependent on traditional lending operations for its income. |
| Non-Performing Loan Ratio (NPL Ratio) | This ratio measures the percentage of a bank's loan portfolio that consists of non-performing loans (loans that are in default or close to being in default). This KPI is important because high levels can indicate a bank's potential losses and financial health. |
| Overhead Ratio (General) | This ratio measures operating expenses as a percentage of revenue. In financial services, it can indicate efficiency and the scale of non-interest, non-claim related expenses. |
| Persistency Ratio (Insurance Industry) | It measures the number of policies remaining in force with the insurer without being lapsed or being paid out in claims. High persistency ratio indicates customers' trust and satisfaction with their insurance provider. |
| Premium to Surplus Ratio (Insurance Industry) | This is an indication of an insurer's financial strength and its capacity to underwrite risk. It measures the size of an insurer's premium writings relative to its surplus (assets minus liabilities). |
| Provision for Loan Losses to Loans | This KPI measures the provision that a bank sets aside to cover potential losses from defaulted loans as a proportion of the total loans issued. A higher ratio indicates an expectation of higher loan defaults. |
| Reserve Ratio (Insurance Industry) | This ratio compares an insurance company's reserves set aside for future claims to the company's collected premium revenues. A higher ratio indicates a more conservative and potentially more stable company. |
| Return on Investment (ROI) (Asset Management) | ROI measures the gain or loss made on an investment relative to the amount invested. It's a key performance indicator for asset managers and hedge funds. |
| Risk-Adjusted Return on Capital (RAROC) | RAROC is a risk-based profitability measurement framework for analysing risk-adjusted financial performance and providing a consistent view of profitability across different types of businesses. |
| Risk-Weighted Assets (Banking) | This represents a bank's assets, adjusted for their associated risks. This KPI helps assess the minimum amount of capital that must be held by the bank, and is crucial for risk management and regulatory compliance. |
| Risk-Weighted Assets (Banking) | Risk-weighted assets take into account credit risk, market risk, and operational risk, providing a measure of a bank's assets, weighted according to risk. |
| Sharpe Ratio (Investment Industry) | This ratio measures risk-adjusted returns. It’s used to understand the return of an investment compared to its risk. The higher the Sharpe Ratio, the better the investment's returns relative to the risk taken. |
| Solvency Ratio (Insurance Industry) | The solvency ratio is a measure of the risk an insurer faces of claims that it cannot absorb. It is a measure of financial stability, as it shows how well an insurer can meet all its financial obligations. |
| Solvency Ratio (Insurance) | This ratio measures a company's ability to meet its long-term obligations. In the insurance industry, the solvency ratio is particularly important as it provides insight into an insurance company's ability to meet its claims. |
| Total Brokerage Accounts (Brokerage Firms) | A measure of the total number of customer accounts. Growth in this number can indicate a successful acquisition strategy. |
| Trading Volume | For brokerages, the volume of trades made is a direct indication of business activity. Greater trading volumes typically translate to increased commission income. |
| Turnover Ratio (Mutual Funds/Asset Management) | This measures how often the portfolio of a fund changes in a year. A high turnover ratio means more transaction costs and could have tax implications for investors. |
