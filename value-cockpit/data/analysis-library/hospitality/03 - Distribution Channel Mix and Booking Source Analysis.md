# 3 · Distribution Channel Mix and Booking Source Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Revenue & Pricing Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Examine which booking channels generate the most reservations and revenue, helping you optimize channel usage, reduce acquisition costs, and improve profitability.


## 📊 Data required
- Total bookings by channel for the selected period.
- Revenue and cost of acquisition (commissions, fees) by channel.
- Customer demographics, if available, for each channel.
- Historical channel performance data for comparison.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Determine the time frame for the analysis. Decide if you want daily, weekly, or monthly data, or perhaps a longer period for trend analysis.
2. Compile booking data by channel. Collect information from all distribution channels (e.g., direct reservations, OTAs, global distribution systems, corporate bookings).
3. Calculate total room nights and revenue by channel. This includes identifying the gross revenue from each channel before commissions or fees.
4. Identify costs associated with each channel. Include commission rates, transaction fees, and marketing expenses if applicable.
5. Compare net revenue per channel. Subtract channel costs from gross revenue to see which channels are most profitable.
6. Analyze booking volume trends. Look for increases or decreases in bookings over time for each channel.
7. Determine the booking window for each channel. Identify how far in advance guests typically book through different sources.
8. Assess the customer profiles. If possible, analyze the demographics or travel purpose of guests coming through each channel.


## 📤 Output format
- A table listing each channel, total bookings, total revenue, cost of acquisition, and net revenue.
- A bar chart or pie chart showing the percentage of total bookings by channel.
- A line graph illustrating channel performance trends over time.

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A high-volume channel with high net revenue is typically desirable.
- A channel producing high revenue but also high commission or acquisition costs may be less profitable.
- A consistent growth trend in one channel could indicate a need to allocate more marketing resources there.
- Disparities in booking window and lead time can inform targeted promotions or yield management.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Negotiate lower commission rates or fees with distribution partners.
2. Invest in marketing efforts for profitable channels to increase direct bookings.
3. Optimize channel mix by shifting focus toward lower-cost distribution sources without sacrificing volume.
4. Segment marketing campaigns based on the booking window and demographics of each channel’s audience.
