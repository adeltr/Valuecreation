# KPI — Retail

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Consumer & Retail]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Retail Company - Analyse]]

55 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Average Discount Depth | The average discount given on sold items during a specific period. This metric can help understand the impact of discounting strategies on sales and profitability. |
| Average Order Value (AOV) for E-commerce | The average total of every online order placed over a certain period. Higher AOV means customers are spending more each time they place an order. |
| Average Time to Purchase | The average time it takes a customer from entering the store (or website) to making a purchase. It helps understand customer behavior and can indicate the effectiveness of the sales process. |
| Average Transaction Value (ATV) | This KPI represents the average amount spent by customers per transaction. An increasing ATV suggests customers are purchasing more items or higher-priced items, which can signify effective marketing or sales strategies. |
| Average Units per Transaction (UPT) | This metric measures the average number of items a customer buys in a single transaction. High UPT could signify successful marketing strategies or in-store promotions. |
| Bounce Rate (For E-commerce) | The percentage of visitors who navigate away from an e-commerce site after viewing only one page. A high bounce rate could suggest problems with a retailer's online user experience. |
| Brand Awareness | Although it's harder to quantify, this measures the extent to which potential customers recognize a brand. Higher brand awareness can lead to more sales and a stronger market position. |
| Checkout Abandonment Rate (In-Store and Online) | This metric tracks the number of customers who leave without making a purchase after reaching the checkout. High rates may suggest issues with the checkout process, such as long lines in-store or technical problems online. |
| Churn Rate | This is the rate at which customers stop doing business with a retail entity within a given period. This metric is particularly important for retailers with subscription-based services. |
| Click-And-Collect Usage Rate | For retailers offering a 'click-and-collect' service, this KPI measures the percentage of customers who choose this option. This can be a key indicator of the success of a retailer's omnichannel strategy. |
| Cost per Square Foot | This metric assesses the cost of operating a retail space per square foot. It's relevant for brick-and-mortar retailers and can highlight efficiency in utilizing store space. |
| Cross-Selling and Upselling Rates | The percentage of sales where customers purchase additional related products (cross-selling) or upgraded, more expensive versions of chosen items (upselling). These rates provide insight into the effectiveness of the retailer's sales strategies and staff training. |
| Customer Engagement Rate | This KPI measures the level of customer engagement through various channels like social media, in-store events, or email campaigns. |
| Customer Lifetime Value (CLTV) | This KPI calculates the total revenue a business can reasonably expect from a single customer account. It's particularly relevant for retailers with a membership or loyalty program. |
| Customer Profitability Score | This measures the profit margins obtained from each customer. It's a significant metric for retailers offering differentiated products or services to different customer segments. |
| Customer Retention Rate | This measures the proportion of customers that return and make repeat purchases. High customer retention signifies strong customer loyalty and satisfaction, key aspects of a successful retail business. |
| Customer Satisfaction Score (CSAT) | This metric asks customers to rate their satisfaction with a retailer's product or service. The score can provide insight into overall customer experience and product quality. |
| Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) | This KPI measures the average number of days that inventory is held before being sold. Lower DIO can indicate efficient inventory management and quick sales turnaround. |
| Delivery On-Time Rate | This measures the percentage of deliveries made on time. It's an important KPI for online retailers, as late deliveries can negatively impact customer satisfaction and repeat business. |
| Dwell Time | This is the average length of time a customer spends in the store. Higher dwell time can indicate customer engagement and increase the chances of higher-value purchases. |
| E-commerce Cart Abandonment Rate | The percentage of online shoppers who add items to their cart but don't complete the purchase. A high rate could suggest issues with the online shopping experience, like high shipping costs or a complex checkout process. |
| Email Open and Click-Through Rates | For retailers using email marketing, these metrics measure how many recipients open the emails (open rate) and click on content within the email (click-through rate). |
| Footfall Conversion Rate | This is the ratio of the number of purchases to the total footfall (number of customers entering the store). This KPI helps evaluate the effectiveness of in-store sales tactics and can be critical for brick-and-mortar retailers. |
| Gift Card Redemption Rate | For retailers offering gift cards, this measures the percentage of sold gift cards that are actually redeemed, which can affect future revenue projections. |
| Gross Margin Return on Inventory Investment (GMROII) | This metric evaluates the profitability of inventory investment. It is calculated as the gross margin divided by the average inventory cost. A high GMROII shows effective inventory management and good profit margins. |
| Inventory Turnover Ratio | This is the ratio of the cost of goods sold (COGS) to the average inventory during a certain period. It measures how effectively a retailer is managing its stock. High turnover indicates good sales performance, while low turnover may suggest overstocking or issues with product desirability. |
| Loyalty Program Participation Rate | The percentage of customers who participate in a retailer's loyalty program. It can indicate the effectiveness of the program in promoting customer loyalty. |
| Market Share | This KPI measures a retailer's sales as a percentage of total market sales. It shows the retailer's position compared to competitors, which is particularly relevant in competitive segments. |
| Merchandise Return to Sales Ratio | This KPI assesses the ratio of returned merchandise to total sales, which can provide insights into product quality, customer satisfaction, and the effectiveness of return policies. |
| Mobile Sales as a Percentage of Total Online Sales | This KPI tracks the portion of online sales made via mobile devices, reflecting the effectiveness of a retailer's mobile shopping experience. |
| Net Promoter Score (NPS) | A measure of customer loyalty and brand advocacy, calculated by asking customers how likely they are to recommend the company to others. High NPS suggests satisfied customers who can bring in business through word-of-mouth. |
| Online Sales as a Percentage of Total Sales | This KPI gives an idea of how well a retailer is performing in the e-commerce space. A growing percentage often indicates a successful digital strategy, which is vital given the increasing importance of online shopping. |
| Online Visits before Purchase | This KPI measures the average number of online visits a customer makes before purchasing, providing insights into customer decision-making behavior. |
| Online Visits to Purchase | This is the number of visits an online customer makes before making a purchase. This KPI provides insights into online customer behavior and the effectiveness of online sales strategies. |
| Percentage of Ethical or Sustainable Sales | For retailers focusing on sustainability or ethical sourcing, this KPI tracks the percentage of sales from ethical or sustainable products. |
| Percentage of Local vs. Non-local Sales | For retailers with a strong local focus, this KPI measures the balance of sales made to local vs. non-local customers, providing insights into their local market penetration. |
| Percentage of Sales from Promotions | The proportion of sales resulting from promotional activities. A high percentage may suggest a dependency on discounts to drive sales, which could affect profit margins. |
| Product Affinity | This KPI measures the likelihood that products are purchased together. It's used to inform product placement strategies both in-store and online, and can be highly valuable for personalizing marketing efforts. |
| Product Category Performance | This KPI compares the sales performance of different product categories, which can help retailers decide how to allocate resources and determine strategic direction. |
| Product Return Rate | The percentage of sold products that customers return. A high return rate could signify issues with product quality, customer satisfaction, or return policies. |
| Rate of Out-of-Stocks | This measures the frequency at which products are out of stock. A high rate could lead to lost sales and customer dissatisfaction. |
| Rate of Sale (ROS) | The speed at which a product sells over a given period. It's particularly useful for forecasting demand, managing inventory, and planning purchases. |
| Repeat Customer Rate | The percentage of customers who have shopped more than once. It's a key measure of customer loyalty and can reflect the success of customer retention strategies. |
| Retail Conversion Rate (Online and Offline) | The percentage of visitors who make a purchase. It can be broken down by online and offline channels and is a key metric in determining the effectiveness of sales and marketing strategies. |
| Retail Fulfillment Speed | For online retailers, this KPI measures the average time taken to fulfill an order from the moment of purchase to the point of delivery. |
| Revenue per Customer | This KPI measures the average revenue generated per customer over a specific period. It can reveal insights into purchasing behavior and the overall value each customer brings to the business. |
| Sales per Employee | This metric determines the average sales revenue each employee generates. It's useful in assessing staff productivity and the efficiency of labor costs, especially in labor-intensive retail formats. |
| Sales Per Square Foot | This KPI calculates the average revenue a retailer generates for every square foot of sales space. This helps analysts assess the effectiveness of store layout and product placement strategies. |
| Same-Store Sales Growth (SSSG) | This metric compares sales of stores that have been open for a year or more. It allows investors to determine how much of a company's growth is due to opening new stores versus growth from existing ones. |
| Seasonal Sales Performance | This KPI measures sales performance during key shopping seasons or events (like Black Friday, Christmas). It can help to identify trends, planning, and strategy effectiveness during these periods. |
| Sell-Through Rate | This measures the percentage of units sold versus the number of units that were available for sale. A high sell-through rate indicates strong demand and effective inventory management, while a low rate may suggest overstocking or lack of demand. |
| Shrinkage Rate | This KPI calculates the percentage of inventory lost due to errors, theft, or damage. High shrinkage rates can significantly impact a retailer's bottom line and suggest issues with loss prevention or inventory management. |
| Stock-Out Rate | The percentage of times customers can't buy a product because it's out of stock. High stock-out rates can lead to lost sales and customer dissatisfaction. |
| Store Traffic Trend | This KPI tracks the number of people entering the store over time, helping to identify patterns and predict future sales opportunities. |
| Window Conversion Rate | For brick-and-mortar stores, this measures the percentage of window shoppers who end up entering the store, providing insights into the effectiveness of window displays and promotions. |
