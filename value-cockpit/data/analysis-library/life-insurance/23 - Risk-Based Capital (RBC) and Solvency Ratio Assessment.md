# 23 · Risk-Based Capital (RBC) and Solvency Ratio Assessment

> **Part:** [[D - Capital and Solvency Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the adequacy of a life insurance company’s capital relative to its risk exposure and assess its solvency position. This ensures compliance with regulatory requirements, identifies risks, and supports strategies for maintaining financial stability.


## 📊 Data required
- **Financial data:** total assets, liabilities, reserves, and available capital
- **Risk exposure data:** credit risk, market risk, insurance risk, and operational risk
- **Capital structure details:** available capital levels and required capital under RBC frameworks
- Historical solvency and RBC ratios for trend analysis
- Regulatory benchmarks for solvency margins and RBC thresholds


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate Risk-Based Capital (RBC) ○ Measure required capital for key risk categories: credit risk (e.g., defaults on bonds), market risk (e.g., interest rate fluctuations), insurance risk (e.g., mortality deviations), and operational risk. ○ Combine risk components to calculate the total RBC requirement:
`RBC Requirement = Credit Risk Capital + Market Risk Capital + Insurance Risk Capital + Operational Risk Capital.`
2. Determine Available Capital ○ Identify surplus, adjusted reserves, and retained earnings to determine total available capital.
3. Calculate RBC Ratio ○ Use the formula:
`RBC Ratio (%) = (Available Capital / RBC Requirement) x 100.`
○ A ratio above 200% indicates strong capital adequacy, while below 100% suggests heightened solvency risk.
4. Calculate Solvency Ratio ○ Solvency Ratio assesses capital adequacy relative to liabilities using the formula:
`Solvency Ratio (%) = (Available Capital / Total Liabilities) x 100.`
5. **Segment Risk Contribution:** 65 ○ Break down RBC requirements by risk category (e.g., credit, market, insurance). ○ Identify key risk drivers contributing the most to capital strain.
6. Perform Trend Analysis ○ Analyze historical RBC and solvency ratios to identify patterns of improvement or deterioration over time.
7. Benchmark Against Industry Standards ○ Compare RBC and solvency ratios to regulatory thresholds and peer performance to assess competitive positioning.
8. Conduct Stress Testing ○ Model scenarios such as asset value declines, higher-than-expected claims, or increased lapse rates. ○ Measure the impact of these scenarios on RBC and solvency ratios.
9. Visualize Results ○ Present tables showing RBC ratio, solvency ratio, and risk contributions. ○ Use charts to display trends and sensitivity analysis results for adverse scenarios.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing RBC ratio, solvency ratio, and capital by risk component
- Charts illustrating historical trends in RBC and solvency ratios
- Stress test results showing impacts of adverse scenarios on capital adequacy
- Key findings highlighting capital strengths, vulnerabilities, and areas of concern


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. An RBC ratio above 200% indicates the company has sufficient capital to cover its risk exposures.
2. A solvency ratio meeting or exceeding regulatory thresholds signals strong financial stability.
3. A declining RBC or solvency ratio over time may indicate capital erosion due to excessive risks or under-reserving.
4. Risk segments contributing disproportionately to RBC requirements suggest areas requiring better risk management.
5. Stress test results highlight how sensitive the company’s capital is to adverse market or claims scenarios.

1. Increase capital reserves through retained earnings or external capital raising.
