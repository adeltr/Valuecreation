# 39 · Product Yield and Scrap Rate Analysis

> **Part:** [[E - Product and Process Development]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Manufacturing Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Product Yield and Scrap Rate Analysis is to assess the efficiency of the production process by measuring how much of the raw materials and components are successfully converted into finished products (yield) and how much is wasted (scrap). This analysis helps identify inefficiencies, reduce waste, lower costs, and improve overall production quality. By optimizing yield and minimizing scrap, companies can enhance profitability and sustainability.


## 📊 Data required
- **Production Output Data:** Information on the number of finished products produced during a given time period.
- **Raw Material Input Data:** The amount of raw materials or components used in the production process.
- **Scrap Data:** Data on materials or products that are discarded, including reasons for scrapping (e.g., defects, damage, or excess material).
- **Rework Data:** Information on products that were initially defective but later corrected through rework.
- **Defect Rate Data:** Detailed records of defects and quality issues at various stages of the production process.
- **Material Costs:** The cost of raw materials and components used in production, including the financial impact of scrapped materials.
- **Process and Equipment Performance:** Data on machine performance, equipment settings, and production conditions that may contribute to variations in yield or scrap.
- **Quality Control Metrics:** Results from quality control checks, including pass/fail rates, rework times, and scrap-related quality issues.
- **Labor Costs:** Costs associated with labor used in rework or scrap handling processes.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Collect Production and Scrap Data:** ○ Gather data on the number of finished products produced and the amount of raw material used during the production process. Also, collect detailed scrap data, including reasons for scrap (e.g., defects, damage, incorrect specifications, or overproduction).

2. **Calculate Product Yield:** ○ Calculate the yield as a percentage of the total raw materials or components that are successfully converted into finished products. A high yield indicates that most of the raw material is being efficiently used to produce salable products.
`○ Product Yield (%) = (Number of Good Units Produced / Total Units Started) x 100`
3. **Calculate Scrap Rate:** ○ Determine the scrap rate as a percentage of the total materials or products discarded during production. This includes materials that cannot be reused or reworked and directly impacts production costs.
`○ Scrap Rate (%) = (Amount of Scrap / Total Raw Materials Used) x 100`
4. **Analyze the Causes of Scrap:** ○ Review the scrap data to identify the main causes of waste. These may include defects, process errors, material handling issues, or machine malfunctions. Categorize the causes and prioritize areas for improvement.
5. **Assess the Financial Impact of Scrap:** ○ Calculate the financial impact of the scrap on the production process by determining the cost of discarded materials. Include labor costs for handling and disposing of scrap, as well as any costs related to rework or quality control.
6. **Examine Process and Equipment Performance:** ○ Analyze the performance of production equipment and processes that may contribute to yield variations or scrap generation. Equipment settings, machine calibration, and process variability can impact product quality and waste levels.
7. **Evaluate Rework Rates:** ○ Assess the frequency and success of rework activities. High rework rates may indicate deeper process issues that are contributing to scrap and inefficiency. Rework, while useful for recovering defective products, also increases labor and material costs.
8. **Monitor Defect Rates and Quality Control:** ○ Track defect rates throughout the production process to identify specific stages where quality issues arise. Quality control metrics such as pass/fail rates, first-pass yield, and defect analysis help in pinpointing production steps that need improvement.
9. **Benchmark Yield and Scrap Rates:** ○ Compare the company’s product yield and scrap rates to industry benchmarks or internal targets. If the company’s yield is below average or 169 scrap rates are significantly higher than competitors, focus on improving production efficiency and reducing waste.
10. **Identify Opportunities for Improvement:** ○ Based on the analysis, identify specific actions to improve yield and reduce scrap. These may include optimizing machine settings, improving material handling, enhancing worker training, or using higher-quality materials. Implement continuous improvement processes such as Lean or Six Sigma to minimize waste.


## 📤 Output format
- **Yield and Scrap Rate Report:** A summary of the product yield and scrap rates, including key performance indicators (KPIs) such as yield percentage, scrap rate percentage, and reasons for scrap.
- **Financial Impact of Scrap Report:** A detailed analysis of the financial costs associated with scrap, including material, labor, and handling costs, along with the potential savings from reducing scrap.
- **Process Performance Analysis:** An evaluation of production process and equipment performance, identifying inefficiencies or issues that contribute to scrap generation.
- **Defect Rate and Quality Control Report:** A report on defect rates, rework efforts, and quality control metrics, with recommendations for improving product quality and reducing defects.
- **Improvement Action Plan:** A prioritized action plan for improving product yield and reducing scrap, including specific process improvements, machine optimizations, and employee training initiatives.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Yield, Low Scrap Rate:** A high product yield and low scrap rate indicate that the production process is efficient, with minimal waste. This suggests that materials are being used effectively and the quality control process is well-managed.
- **Low Yield, High Scrap Rate:** A low yield and high scrap rate indicate inefficiencies in the production process, resulting in excessive waste and higher costs. These issues may be related to poor equipment performance, process variability, or material defects.
- **High Rework and Defect Rates:** Frequent rework and high defect rates can signal deeper issues in production that need to be addressed. Rework, while 170 useful for recovering defective products, increases costs and may indicate that the initial production process needs improvement.
- **Financial Impact of Scrap:** High scrap costs can significantly impact profitability. If the financial impact of scrap is large, reducing scrap through process improvements, better materials, or improved quality control should be a priority.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Implement Process Improvements:** ○ Use Lean manufacturing principles or Six Sigma techniques to identify and eliminate waste in the production process. Focus on reducing variability in equipment performance, process steps, and material handling.
2. **Optimize Equipment and Machine Settings:** ○ Regularly calibrate equipment and adjust machine settings to improve yield and minimize scrap. Automation and digital monitoring tools can help ensure consistency and reduce process variability.
3. **Improve Material Handling and Storage:** ○ Ensure that materials are handled and stored properly to reduce the risk of damage or defects before production begins. Better inventory management can help reduce waste from expired or degraded materials.
4. **Enhance Quality Control Procedures:** ○ Strengthen quality control measures at key stages in the production process to detect defects early and prevent the need for rework or scrapping later on. Implement in-line quality checks to monitor product quality in real time.
5. **Train Employees on Best Practices:** ○ Provide regular training for workers on best practices for reducing scrap and improving product yield. Employee involvement in continuous improvement initiatives can help identify areas where scrap is being generated and find solutions.
6. **Use Higher-Quality Materials:** ○ If scrap is caused by material defects, consider using higher-quality materials or working with suppliers to ensure better consistency in raw materials. Reducing material variability can improve product quality and yield.
7. **Track and Monitor Yield and Scrap Metrics:** ○ Continuously track yield and scrap rates using automated systems or real-time data collection tools. Regular monitoring helps identify trends, 171 spot inefficiencies, and make timely adjustments to the production process.
