# 1 · Loss Ratio Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Underwriting]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Loss Ratio Analysis is to evaluate the profitability of underwriting activities by examining the relationship between claims paid and premiums earned. A lower loss ratio typically indicates better profitability, while a higher ratio may suggest underpricing or poor risk selection.


## 📊 Data required
1. Total claims paid during the period
2. Total earned premiums during the period


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the Period:** Select the period for analysis (e.g., monthly, quarterly, or yearly).
2. **Calculate the Loss Ratio:** Use the formula:
`Loss Ratio = (Total Claims Paid / Total Earned Premiums) x 100`
3. **Segment the Data:** To gain deeper insights, segment the analysis by various dimensions such as product line, region, or customer segment if the data is available.
4. **Adjust for Reinsurance:** If applicable, adjust for reinsurance by subtracting reinsured claims and premiums. The adjusted loss ratio would then be:
`Adjusted Loss Ratio = (Net Claims Paid / Net Earned Premiums) x 100`
5. **Compile Results:** Organize the results in a table or chart format for easy comparison across different time periods or segments.


## 📤 Output format
The output should ideally be a table showing:
- Period (e.g., Q1 2023)
- Total Claims Paid
- Total Earned Premiums
- Loss Ratio (%)
Optionally, use a line or bar chart to show the trend of loss ratio over time.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A loss ratio below 100% indicates that premiums earned are greater than claims paid, showing profitability in underwriting.
- A loss ratio above 100% implies that claims paid exceed premiums earned, indicating potential underwriting losses.
- **Benchmarking:** Compare against industry averages or historical company performance to determine if the ratio is within an acceptable range.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Enhance Underwriting Standards Refine underwriting guidelines to select risks that align with the company’s risk appetite.
2. Adjust Pricing Strategies Review pricing models to ensure premiums adequately reflect risk, especially in high-loss segments.
3. Focus on Claims Management Implement more efficient claims handling procedures to reduce claims leakage and improve expense control.
4. Consider Reinsurance Options Utilize reinsurance more effectively to spread risk and reduce exposure to large losses.
5. Conduct Periodic Loss Ratio Reviews Regularly assess the loss ratio by product line or region to identify and address areas with consistently high loss ratios.
