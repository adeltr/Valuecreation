# 15 · Retailer-Specific Marketing and Joint Business Planning (JBP)

> **Part:** [[B - Sales]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Retailer-Specific Marketing and Joint Business Planning (JBP) analysis in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to align the marketing and business objectives of both the manufacturer and the retailer through a collaborative process. This analysis focuses on creating tailored marketing strategies and long-term plans that meet both parties’ goals, such as increasing sales, market share, and profitability. By engaging in Joint Business Planning, CPG companies can secure stronger partnerships, gain better shelf visibility, and leverage co-branded marketing initiatives to drive mutual growth.


## 📊 Data required
1. Historical sales data by retailer
2. Retailer-specific market share data
3. Consumer behavior data (e.g., purchase frequency, preferences)
4. Previous marketing and promotional spend per retailer
5. Retailer and manufacturer marketing goals and KPIs
6. Competitor performance and market positioning within the retailer
7. Joint promotional plans, including past promotional effectiveness (e.g., ROMI, sales lift)
8. Category insights and growth trends for the retailer
9. Contract terms related to marketing and promotional support
10. Feedback from account managers and retailer partners


## 🪜 Step-by-step
84
1. **Define the goals of Joint Business Planning:** Start by defining mutual goals for the JBP process between the manufacturer and retailer. These goals could include increasing sales within certain categories, boosting market share, growing foot traffic, or improving product visibility. Clearly identify both parties' objectives to ensure alignment.
2. **Assess historical sales performance by retailer:** Analyze sales data for each retailer to understand how the manufacturer’s products have historically performed. Focus on sales trends, market share growth, and category performance. For example, if the retailer has shown growth in premium product segments, this could indicate opportunities to push higher-margin products in future business planning.
3. **Review past marketing and promotional effectiveness:** Evaluate the success of past joint marketing campaigns and promotions with the retailer. Calculate the return on marketing investment (ROMI) for each campaign to understand its effectiveness:
`ROMI = (Revenue Attributable to Marketing - Marketing Spend) / Marketing`
Spend x 100 For example, if a joint campaign generated $200,000 in revenue and had a marketing spend of $50,000:
`ROMI = (($200,000 - $50,000) / $50,000) x 100 = 300%`
Use this data to identify which types of campaigns (e.g., in-store promotions, digital ads) work best with the retailer and where there is room for improvement.
4. **Identify consumer insights and category trends:** Leverage consumer data to identify key behaviors and trends within the retailer’s stores. For example, analyze which product categories are growing, how consumer preferences are shifting, and which demographic segments are driving sales. Use these insights to align future marketing efforts with the retailer’s category growth strategies.
5. **Set joint KPIs and growth targets:** Work with the retailer to establish key performance indicators (KPIs) and growth targets for the upcoming business planning period. KPIs can include sales volume growth, market share 85 improvement, promotional uplift, or category leadership. These targets should be measurable, realistic, and aligned with both the retailer’s objectives and the manufacturer’s brand strategy.
6. **Develop retailer-specific marketing strategies:** Create tailored marketing strategies that address the retailer’s specific needs and customer base. This could include: ○ In-store promotions and merchandising: Work on improving product placement, securing prime shelf space, and developing retailer-specific endcap or seasonal displays. ○ Digital and social media marketing: Collaborate on digital campaigns that leverage the retailer’s online platform and customer data to target specific audiences. ○ Co-branded marketing initiatives: Develop co-branded campaigns that align with the retailer’s branding and leverage both parties’ marketing assets (e.g., email lists, social media reach).
7. **Analyze competitor performance within the retailer:** Benchmark your brand’s performance against competitors in the same category at the retailer. Identify where competitors may be outperforming in terms of product visibility, promotions, or pricing. This analysis can help you position your brand more effectively and adjust strategies to gain a competitive edge.
8. **Plan joint promotions and events:** Develop a calendar of joint promotions, special events, and marketing campaigns that align with the retailer’s sales cycle and key selling periods (e.g., holidays, back-to-school, seasonal promotions). Use data from previous promotions to determine which timeframes and promotional strategies are likely to be most successful.
9. **Align distribution and supply chain planning:** Ensure that distribution and supply chain operations are aligned with the marketing and business planning efforts. Plan for potential spikes in demand during promotional periods and collaborate with the retailer to ensure efficient inventory management, on-time deliveries, and stock availability during campaigns.
86
10. **Establish governance and communication processes:** Create a clear governance structure and communication plan to ensure ongoing collaboration between the manufacturer and retailer. Set regular review meetings to assess progress against KPIs, address challenges, and adjust strategies as needed. Strong communication ensures that both parties remain aligned throughout the business planning period.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Retailer-Specific Marketing and Joint Business Planning (JBP) analysis typically includes:
- A summary of historical sales performance and market share growth for each retailer.
- Insights into consumer behavior, category trends, and growth opportunities within the retailer.
- Results from past joint marketing efforts, including promotional effectiveness and ROMI.
- Clearly defined KPIs and growth targets for the upcoming business period.
- A retailer-specific marketing strategy, including in-store, digital, and co-branded campaigns.
- A promotional calendar detailing key events and marketing activities.
- Competitor benchmarking within the retailer’s stores.
- Distribution and supply chain plans aligned with promotional activities.
- A communication plan and governance structure for ongoing collaboration.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
87
- **High ROMI with strong sales growth:** Indicates that joint marketing campaigns with the retailer have been successful in driving sales and increasing market share. Continue building on these successes by scaling similar promotional efforts and expanding co-branded initiatives.
- **Weak sales growth despite high promotional spend:** If significant marketing resources have been allocated to a retailer but have not translated into strong sales growth, it may indicate that the strategy is misaligned with the retailer’s customer base. Revisit the promotional tactics or product selection to better meet the needs of the retailer’s consumers.
- **Competitor outperforming in the same category:** If competitors are performing better within the retailer, analyze their pricing, promotions, and product placement. Use this information to refine your strategy and negotiate for better terms, visibility, or promotional support.
- **Category growth alignment:** If your brand is well-positioned within a growing category at the retailer, focus on increasing visibility and support in that area. Conversely, if category growth is slow, consider introducing new products or expanding into faster-growing segments to maintain sales momentum.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Enhance collaboration with the retailer:** Strengthen your relationship by increasing the level of collaboration, particularly in category planning and co-marketing initiatives. Joint Business Planning should be a continuous, integrated process, not just a one-time event.
2. **Tailor marketing to the retailer’s consumer base:** Ensure that all marketing efforts are designed to align with the retailer’s specific customer segments and shopping behaviors. Tailor promotions, messaging, and product offerings to resonate with the retailer’s core audience.
88
3. **Leverage consumer insights:** Use data on consumer behavior and preferences to refine the retailer-specific marketing strategy. Insights into purchasing patterns, seasonal trends, and customer demographics can help craft campaigns that drive higher engagement and sales.
4. **Negotiate for better visibility and support:** Use JBP as an opportunity to negotiate for better shelf placement, in-store visibility, and promotional support. Highlight the potential for sales growth and category leadership to secure more favorable terms.
5. **Increase co-branded marketing efforts:** Collaborate with retailers on co-branded marketing initiatives that leverage both parties’ brand equity and reach. Co-branded efforts can help align your product with the retailer’s reputation and increase consumer trust.
6. **Monitor progress regularly:** Set up regular reviews with the retailer to monitor progress toward the agreed KPIs and adjust plans as needed. Flexibility and ongoing communication are key to adapting strategies based on market changes or emerging trends.
