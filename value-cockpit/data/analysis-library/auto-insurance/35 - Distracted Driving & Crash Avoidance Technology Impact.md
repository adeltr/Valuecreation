# 35 · Distracted Driving & Crash Avoidance Technology Impact Analysis

> **Part:** [[E - Technology, Mobility & Operational Efficiency]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Identify how driver distractions (e.g., mobile phone use) influence claim frequency and severity, and determine if crash avoidance systems (e.g., automatic emergency braking, lane departure alerts) effectively mitigate those risks.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical claims data noting cause of accident (e.g., confirmed or suspected distracted driving)
- Vehicle feature data specifying presence of crash avoidance technology
- Telematics or usage data capturing phone usage, abrupt braking, or lane departures
- Premium, exposure, and loss ratio details segmented by vehicles with vs. without crash avoidance features
- External reports or studies on distracted driving trends and technology effectiveness


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Compile claims data indicating potential distracted driving as a contributing factor by reviewing accident descriptions, police reports, or telematics logs. Segment these claims based on vehicle type and driver demographics. Gather vehicle feature data to determine which cars had crash avoidance systems.
2. Calculate distracted driving claim frequency using Claim Frequency = (Number of Distracted Driving Claims / Total Claims) x 100. Compare to overall claim frequency and note variations by geography or vehicle type. Identify which vehicles are equipped with crash avoidance features to see if these systems reduce claim occurrences.
3. Analyze crash severity by determining average claim cost for distracted vs. non-distracted incidents. Compare severity for vehicles with crash avoidance technology, looking for lower average payouts or reduced injury claims. Assess whether these safety features mitigate or offset the impact of driver distraction.
4. Review telematics or usage data for correlations between phone-related behavior (e.g., screen unlocks, texting events) and accident likelihood. Evaluate 77 real-time interventions by crash avoidance tech, such as automatic braking in near-collision scenarios. Determine if certain driving habits (time of day, traffic conditions) exacerbate or lessen distraction risks.
5. Benchmark results against industry data on distracted driving trends, crash avoidance adoption rates, and average claim outcomes. Identify significant variances that suggest underpricing or overpricing based on driver behavior or vehicle features. Investigate any anomalous findings in specific driver groups or vehicle models.
6. Estimate financial impact of crash avoidance tech by calculating potential savings in loss costs. Subtract incremental premiums (or discounts) for vehicles with these systems to gauge net effect on profitability. Model different adoption scenarios to see how widespread use of crash avoidance might alter future claim patterns.
7. Document findings and propose underwriting/pricing updates based on observed risks associated with distraction and benefits of safety features. Present the results to product, claims, and executive teams for consideration in policy design and marketing. Suggest targeted driver education or telematics programs incentivizing less phone use behind the wheel.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts comparing distracted vs. non-distracted claim frequency and severity
- Segmented analysis of crash avoidance-equipped vehicles vs. non-equipped vehicles
- Written summary with recommendations on underwriting guidelines, premium discounts or surcharges, and driver education programs


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A consistently higher claim frequency or severity for distracted driving confirms the need for targeted underwriting or policy measures (e.g., telematics-based monitoring).
- Vehicles with advanced crash avoidance systems showing lower severity may justify premium discounts or specialized coverage.
- Discrepancies between telematics data and claimed driver behavior suggest further investigation or stricter enforcement of distracted driving prohibitions.

## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Offer premium incentives or discounts for vehicles equipped with effective crash avoidance systems and verify operational status.
2. Implement or expand telematics programs to identify high-risk distracted driving behavior, providing feedback or surcharges when warranted.
3. Work with regulatory bodies and industry groups to strengthen distracted driving legislation or awareness campaigns.
4. Refine underwriting and marketing to attract safer drivers who value and properly use crash avoidance technology.
