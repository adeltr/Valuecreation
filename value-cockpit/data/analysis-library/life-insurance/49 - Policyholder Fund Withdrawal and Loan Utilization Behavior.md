# 49 · Policyholder Fund Withdrawal and Loan Utilization Behavior Study

> **Part:** [[K - Market Strategy and Competitive Positioning]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate policyholder behavior related to fund withdrawals and policy loans, including patterns, financial impact, and underlying drivers. The analysis helps optimize product design, reduce unexpected liquidity strain, and manage profitability while ensuring policyholder satisfaction.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policy-level data:** ○ Number of policies with withdrawals or loans, loan amounts, and withdrawal frequency ○ Policy types (e.g., whole life, universal life, cash value policies) ○ Policyholder demographics (age, income, gender, location)
2. **Financial data:** ○ Total loan balance, withdrawal amounts, and interest income from loans ○ Surrender charges, loan repayment patterns, and default rates ○ Impact of withdrawals on reserves, persistency, and profitability
3. **Actuarial data:** ○ Mortality and lapse rates for policies with loans or withdrawals vs. those without ○ Cash value accumulation and loan utilization trends
4. **Benchmark data:** ○ Industry benchmarks for loan utilization, withdrawal behavior, and repayment trends

1. Measure Fund Withdrawal and Loan Utilization Rates a. Calculate the withdrawal rate as a percentage of total policies:
`Withdrawal Rate (%) = (Number of Policies with Withdrawals / Total Policies) x 100`

## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing withdrawal rates, loan utilization, and repayment patterns segmented by demographics and policy types

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. High withdrawal and loan utilization rates may indicate policyholder liquidity needs but can lead to reduced cash value growth and higher lapses.
2. Significant loan defaults signal repayment challenges and financial risks for the company.
3. High utilization among specific demographics or policy types highlights areas requiring product or communication adjustments.
4. Variances between actual and expected behavior suggest the need to recalibrate actuarial assumptions and risk models.
5. Benchmark comparisons reveal whether the company’s experience aligns with industry norms or requires targeted interventions.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Educate policyholders on the long-term impact of fund withdrawals and loans on cash value growth and policy sustainability.
2. Introduce structured loan repayment plans to reduce defaults and enhance repayment rates.
3. Offer incentives for partial loan prepayments or early repayments to minimize financial risks.
4. Refine product designs to limit excessive withdrawals while maintaining flexibility to meet policyholder needs.
5. Implement predictive analytics to identify at-risk segments likely to withdraw or default and proactively engage with them.
6. Strengthen communication strategies to promote alternative solutions, such as rider benefits or payment extensions, during financial hardship.
7. Regularly review and recalibrate actuarial assumptions related to withdrawal and loan behavior to improve reserve accuracy and risk management.
