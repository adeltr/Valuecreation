# 6 · Line-Haul Network Planning & Optimization

> **Part:** [[A - Transportation & Network Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Third Party Logistics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Identify opportunities for cost savings, improved transit times, and enhanced capacity utilization across the line-haul network, ensuring efficient movement of freight between major hubs or distribution centers.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Historical freight volumes by lane or corridor
- ​ Lane-level transit times and distances
- ​ Fleet capacity, utilization rates, and operating costs (fuel, maintenance, labor)
- ​ Service-level agreements (delivery speed, reliability)
- ​ Forecasted demand or seasonal volume fluctuations
- ​ External market data (fuel prices, infrastructure constraints)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Map the Existing Network: Document all current line-haul routes, major hubs, and transfer points. Include lane distances and estimated transit times. 2.​ Gather Historical Volume & Cost Data: Compile shipment volumes, total costs (fuel, driver wages, maintenance), and any accessorial fees associated with each lane. 3.​ Calculate Current Efficiency Metrics: Determine average cost per mile, average load factor (actual load weight or volume vs. capacity), and transit time variance.​ Cost per Mile = (Total Line-Haul Cost / Total Miles)​ Load Factor = (Actual Load / Vehicle Capacity) x 100 4.​ Identify High-Cost or Low-Utilization Lanes: Compare each lane’s metrics to the network average. Flag lanes with disproportionately high costs, low load factors, or inconsistent transit times for deeper investigation. 5.​ Model Alternative Scenarios: Use data-driven simulation or route optimization tools to explore changes such as consolidating loads, adjusting departure times, or rerouting through different hubs. 6.​ Incorporate Demand Forecasts: Integrate predicted peak periods or growth regions into the model. Plan lane expansions or shifts in capacity to accommodate future demand without bottlenecks. 7.​ Evaluate Impact on Service Levels: Verify how proposed optimizations affect delivery speed and reliability. Balance cost-savings opportunities with any potential risks to service quality.
17 8.​ Summarize Findings & Recommendations: Consolidate the modeling results, highlight the most beneficial route changes, and provide an action plan to implement these improvements.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ A lane-by-lane performance table showing cost per mile, load factor, and transit time
- ​ A network map highlighting high-cost/low-utilization routes and proposed changes
- ​ Scenario simulation reports illustrating cost and service implications of alternative routes
- ​ A prioritized list of recommended optimizations with an estimated ROI


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **​ High Cost per Mile:** May indicate underutilized lanes or inefficient routes.
- **​ Low Load Factor:** Suggests excess capacity, unbalanced freight flows, or scheduling misalignments.
- **​ Excessive Transit Time Variance:** Points to congestion, route planning issues, or possible driver scheduling conflicts.
- **​ Successful Optimization Scenarios:** Show measurable reductions in transportation costs, improved transit times, or more balanced load factors without compromising service levels.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Consolidate Loads: Combine shipments to increase load factors and reduce partial truckload movements. 2.​ Implement Route Optimization Tools: Leverage software that factors in traffic, road conditions, and real-time capacity data. 3.​ Strategically Position Hubs: Relocate or add network hubs closer to major demand centers to shorten line-haul distances. 4.​ Refine Scheduling & Dispatch: Coordinate departure times and driver shifts to reduce idle time and accommodate peak volume periods. 5.​ Continuous Network Review: Regularly reassess the line-haul network to adapt to shifting customer demand, market conditions, and cost drivers.
