# 18 · Critical Illness and Riders Profitability Assessment

> **Part:** [[C - Profitability and Product Performance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the profitability of critical illness benefits and policy riders (e.g., accidental death, waiver of premium, and disability income). This analysis determines the financial contribution of these add-on features, identifies loss-making riders, and highlights opportunities to optimize pricing and risk management.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policy-level data:** ○ Types of riders attached to policies (e.g., critical illness, accidental death). ○ Premium revenue collected for riders. ○ Rider claims paid and frequency of claims.
2. **Actuarial assumptions:** ○ Morbidity rates for critical illness benefits. ○ Incidence rates for specific riders (e.g., accident and disability). ○ Lapse rates and persistency trends for riders.
3. **Expense data:** ○ Acquisition costs (commissions and marketing). ○ Maintenance expenses for managing rider claims and policies.
4. **Benchmark data:** ○ Competitive pricing for similar riders. ○ Industry claim incidence rates for critical illness and other common riders.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Segment Rider Data ○ Separate policy data by rider type (e.g., critical illness, accidental death, waiver of premium). ○ Group riders by demographics, such as age, gender, and policy duration.
2. Calculate Profitability per Rider ○ Use the formula:
`Profitability = Premium Revenue - (Claims Paid + Acquisition Costs + Maintenance Expenses) ○ Calculate the profit margin for each rider as a percentage of premium revenue: Profit Margin (%) = (Profitability / Premium Revenue) x 100`
3. Analyze Claims Experience ○ Compare observed morbidity and incidence rates for riders (e.g., critical illness claims) against actuarial assumptions.
51 ○ Calculate the Claim Ratio:
`Claim Ratio (%) = (Total Claims Paid / Premium Revenue) x 100`
4. Identify Underperforming Riders ○ Rank riders by profitability and claim ratios to identify underperforming add-ons that are eroding margins.
5. Persistency Analysis ○ Evaluate lapse and persistency rates for policies with riders. High lapse rates may indicate that riders are perceived as low value or unaffordable.
6. Conduct Sensitivity Testing ○ Test the impact of adverse conditions, such as higher morbidity rates or increased claims frequency, on profitability. ○ Example: Increase morbidity rates by 10% and recalculate profit margins.
7. Benchmark Against Competitors ○ Compare premium pricing, claims experience, and features of critical illness and riders with competitor products. ○ Identify market gaps or features that could enhance competitiveness.
8. Visualize Results ○ Summarize rider profitability in tables and charts. ○ Create heatmaps or bar charts highlighting high- and low-performing riders based on profit margins and claim ratios.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables showing premium revenue, claims paid, expenses, and profitability by rider type
- Claim ratio and profit margin analysis for each rider
- Charts illustrating trends in claims frequency, persistency rates, and profitability
- Sensitivity analysis results showing impacts of changes in morbidity or incidence rates


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Riders with high claim ratios and low or negative profit margins indicate loss-making add-ons requiring corrective action.
2. Riders with high profitability and persistency rates contribute positively to overall margins and may be targeted for growth.
3. Lower-than-expected claims experience suggests that pricing may be conservative, presenting opportunities for competitive adjustments.
4. High lapse rates for specific riders may indicate affordability issues or low perceived value among policyholders.
52
5. Competitive gaps in pricing or features highlight opportunities for product innovation or repositioning.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Reprice underperforming riders to better align premiums with claims experience and expenses.
2. Conduct experience studies regularly to update morbidity and incidence rate assumptions for critical illness and other riders.
3. Improve underwriting processes for critical illness riders to manage risk and reduce adverse selection.
4. Enhance customer education to improve the perceived value of riders, thereby improving persistency.
5. Introduce innovative features or flexible rider options to attract new policyholders and retain existing ones.
6. Monitor competitor pricing and claims experience to ensure market competitiveness while maintaining profitability.
7. Optimize distribution strategies to emphasize high-performing riders with strong margins.
