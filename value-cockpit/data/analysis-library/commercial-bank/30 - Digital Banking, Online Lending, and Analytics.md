# 30 · Digital Banking, Online Lending, and Analytics

> **Part:** [[F - Digital and Technology Transformation]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Commercial Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
Assess the performance, adoption, and profitability of the bank’s digital channels and online lending services, leveraging analytics to enhance customer experience and operational efficiency.


## 📊 Data required
- Digital platform usage statistics (e.g., mobile app logins, online transactions)
- Online loan application data (application volumes, approval rates, funding times)
- Customer journey analytics (drop-off points, conversion rates, user feedback)
- Operational metrics (cost to serve, turnaround times)
- Comparative data on branch-based vs. digital-based transaction volumes
- Industry benchmarks and competitor insights


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Consolidate all digital channel usage data—mobile, web, and any automated lending platforms—into a single dataset, ensuring each channel’s metrics (e.g., logins, application starts) are clearly identified.
Step 2: Segment users by relevant attributes (e.g., business size, industry, online loan usage frequency) to understand which customer groups are most active digitally, which are underserved, and where growth opportunities lie.
Step 3: Analyze online lending performance by reviewing application volumes, approval rates, average decisioning times, and funding turnaround. Compare these metrics with in-branch or traditional lending processes to identify gaps or advantages.
Step 4: Examine customer journey analytics to pinpoint high drop-off points or bottlenecks in the digital interface, using heatmaps, funnel analysis, or feedback surveys to determine why users may abandon transactions.
Step 5: Conduct cost-to-serve analysis by comparing the operational expenses of digital channels (e.g., platform maintenance, support) against those of physical channels, noting where digital channels could drive efficiency gains or require further investment.
65 Step 6: Compare the bank’s digital adoption rates, online lending penetration, and customer satisfaction scores against industry benchmarks or competitor offerings to assess competitive positioning.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables breaking down user activity, application outcomes, and customer segments for digital vs. traditional channels
- Charts illustrating trends in digital adoption, loan conversion rates, and satisfaction metrics
- Narrative summary highlighting key insights, bottlenecks, and cost-benefit findings


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Higher adoption rates and seamless user journeys typically indicate that digital offerings are meeting customer expectations and providing efficiency gains.
- Persistent drop-off points or low conversion rates signal the need for improved user experience, simpler interfaces, or more streamlined application processes.
- Comparing cost-to-serve metrics helps determine whether the bank’s digital investments are yielding sufficient returns and which aspects need further refinement.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Enhance user experience by refining digital interfaces, improving navigation, and offering personalized features or recommendations.
2. Integrate advanced analytics and machine learning to streamline credit decisioning processes, reducing turnaround times for online lending.
3. Implement continuous testing and optimization (A/B testing, customer journey mapping) to identify small changes that can significantly boost digital conversion rates.
4. Offer targeted digital onboarding and training for segments less familiar with online banking, ensuring broader adoption.
5. Expand partnerships with fintechs or technology providers to access cutting-edge digital capabilities and maintain a competitive edge in the marketplace.
