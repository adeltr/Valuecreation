# 29 · Meeting and Event Space Utilization

> **Part:** [[G - Events & Alternative Offerings]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Measure how well your meeting and event facilities are used, highlighting ways to adjust pricing, marketing, or space configuration to increase bookings and revenue.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Historical booking data for meeting rooms and event spaces (number of events, type of events, attendee count, revenue).
- ​ Occupancy or utilization metrics for each space (how many days/hours used vs. available).
- ​ Rate structures and any associated costs (room rental fees, packages, F&B minimums).
- ​ Ancillary revenue generated from events (F&B sales, AV equipment, parking).
- ​ Booking pace and lead times (how far in advance events are booked).
- ​ Client feedback or satisfaction ratings related to event execution and facilities.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Gather historical usage data. Compile records of all events held in each meeting room or event space over a chosen time period (monthly, quarterly, annually). 2.​ Calculate utilization rates. Determine what percentage of available time each space is occupied by events. For instance:​ Utilization Rate (%) = (Total Hours Booked / Total Hours Available) x 100 3.​ Segment events by type and size. Categorize bookings (e.g., corporate meetings, weddings, social gatherings, conferences) and note the number of attendees and duration. 4.​ Analyze revenue per event space. Break down total revenue from room rental fees, F&B spend, and ancillary services (AV, décor, etc.) by event space. 5.​ Assess booking patterns. Note peak seasons, days of the week, or times of day when demand is highest. Compare short‐lead vs. long‐lead bookings. 6.​ Compare actual usage to capacity. Identify underutilized spaces that have significant downtime or lower revenue compared to others. 7.​ Correlate with operational costs. Factor in labor, setup/teardown, cleaning, and any specialized equipment or staffing for certain types of events.
64 8.​ Gather event feedback and satisfaction scores. Determine whether meeting planners or event guests are satisfied with room setup, technology, and service.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ A utilization table or dashboard listing each event space, total hours booked, total hours available, utilization rate, and total revenue generated.
- ​ Charts or graphs illustrating occupancy trends by time of year, day of the week, or event segment.
- ​ A summary narrative highlighting high‐demand vs. underutilized spaces, peak booking periods, and revenue contributions.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ High utilization with strong revenue suggests the space is in demand and appropriately priced.
- ​ Low utilization or low revenue may indicate over‐pricing, lack of marketing, or mismatch between the space’s features and market needs.
- ​ Tracking satisfaction scores alongside usage can reveal whether issues with layout, service quality, or amenities deter repeat bookings.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Refine pricing strategies based on demand. Implement variable pricing for prime dates/times or offer discounts and packages for off‐peak periods. 2.​ Enhance marketing efforts targeting event planners and organizers for spaces with low utilization, highlighting unique features or bundling deals (e.g., room blocks, F&B credits). 3.​ Optimize room configurations to allow flexible setups and cater to multiple event types, making the space more adaptable and marketable. 4.​ Leverage technology (e.g., online booking portals, virtual tours) to reach a broader audience of event planners and simplify the booking process. 5.​ Improve service standards through staff training and updated equipment to deliver a seamless event experience, boosting word‐of‐mouth and repeat business. 6.​ Solicit and act on feedback from clients to continually refine offerings, address pain points, and stay competitive in the local events market.
