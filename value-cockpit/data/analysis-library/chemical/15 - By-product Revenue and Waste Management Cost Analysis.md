# 15 · By-product Revenue and Waste Management Cost Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Finance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Chemical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to evaluate the revenue potential of by-products generated during production and to assess costs associated with waste management. By understanding the value of by-products and managing waste efficiently, a company can improve profitability, reduce waste disposal costs, and enhance sustainability.


## 📊 Data required
1. Types and quantities of by-products generated in each production process
2. Market value or selling price of each by-product
3. Total revenue from by-product sales
4. Waste generated, categorized by type and volume
5. Waste disposal and treatment costs (e.g., transportation, disposal fees, treatment expenses)
6. Environmental regulations affecting waste disposal and by-product sales
7. Industry benchmarks for by-product revenue and waste management costs


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate By-product Revenue:**
`By-product Revenue = Quantity of By-product x Selling Price per Unit`
For example, if 100 tons of a by-product are sold at $50 per ton, then:
`By-product Revenue = 100 x 50 = $5,000`
This step quantifies the revenue generated from by-product sales.
2. **Determine Waste Management Costs:** Total Waste Management Cost = (Disposal Cost per Unit x Waste Volume) + Treatment Costs + Transportation Costs For example, if disposal costs are $20 per ton, treatment costs are $5,000, and transportation costs are $1,000 for 50 tons of waste:
`Total Waste Management Cost = (20 x 50) + 5,000 + 1,000 = $7,000`
3. **Analyze Net Contribution from By-products and Waste Management:**
`Net Contribution = Total By-product Revenue - Total Waste Management Cost`
This metric shows the overall financial impact of by-product sales and waste management, helping identify if by-product revenue offsets or exceeds waste disposal costs.

4. **Identify Opportunities for By-product Revenue Optimization:**
- **Market Exploration:** Research potential buyers or markets for by-products. Some industries may value by-products as raw materials, offering higher prices.
- **By-product Quality Enhancement:** Evaluate ways to improve the quality or purity of by-products, as higher-grade by-products may command premium prices.
5. **Evaluate Waste Reduction and Cost-saving Opportunities:**
- **Process Optimization:** Review production processes to minimize waste generation and reduce disposal needs.
- **Recycling or Reuse Options:** Investigate if any waste can be recycled back into the production process or repurposed as a secondary raw material.
- **Alternative Disposal Methods:** Explore less expensive or more sustainable disposal methods, such as partnering with waste-to-energy facilities.
6. Benchmark Against Industry Standards Compare the company’s by-product revenue and waste management costs with industry averages. A lower-than-average net contribution may indicate opportunities for improvement in by-product sales or waste cost management.


## 📤 Output format
1.   By-product Revenue and Waste Management Cost table by product/process
2.   Net Contribution from by-products and waste management
3.   List of potential by-product markets and alternative waste disposal options
4.   Benchmark comparison of by-product revenue and waste management costs vs. industry standards


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Positive net contribution:** Indicates that by-product revenue sufficiently offsets or exceeds waste management costs, contributing positively to profitability.
- **High waste management costs:** May indicate inefficiencies in waste handling or opportunities to reduce waste volume through process improvements or recycling.
- **Low by-product revenue compared to industry:** Suggests that by-product sales opportunities may not be fully optimized, pointing to the need for market exploration or quality enhancement.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Increase By-product Revenue:** Explore new markets or buyers for by-products and consider product enhancements to increase market value.
2. **Optimize Waste Management Costs:** Identify cost-saving opportunities in waste handling, transportation, or treatment, such as consolidating waste streams or using bulk transport.

3. **Minimize Waste Generation:** Optimize production processes to reduce waste generation at the source, which directly reduces disposal and treatment costs.
4. **Implement Recycling and Reuse Programs:** Establish internal recycling programs or partnerships with other industries that can use waste as raw material.
5. **Invest in Sustainable Disposal Options:** Consider sustainable waste management alternatives like waste-to-energy programs that may reduce environmental impact and offer cost benefits.
