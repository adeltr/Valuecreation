# 34 · Product Stickiness and Retention Rate Analysis

> **Part:** [[D - Product & Engagement]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Product Stickiness and Retention Rate analysis is to measure how frequently customers use a SaaS product and how likely they are to stay as paying customers over time. Product stickiness evaluates the depth of engagement, while Retention Rate measures customer loyalty and longevity. Both metrics are essential for understanding user satisfaction and long-term customer value.


## 📊 Data required
1. Daily Active Users (DAU)
2. Monthly Active Users (MAU)
3. Number of customers at the start and end of a period
4. Number of customers lost (churned) during the period
5. Usage frequency data (e.g., login activity, feature engagement)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Product Stickiness Calculation:
1. **Determine the period for analysis:** Choose a time period (e.g., monthly) for which you want to analyze product stickiness and retention.
2. **Collect Daily Active Users (DAU):** Gather the total number of unique users who interact with the product daily over the chosen period. DAU is often tracked using analytics platforms.
3. **Collect Monthly Active Users (MAU):** Gather the total number of unique users who interact with the product over the month. MAU is also tracked through analytics tools.
4. **Calculate Product Stickiness:** Use the following formula to calculate product stickiness:
`Product Stickiness (%) = (DAU / MAU) x 100`
For example, if you have 1,000 DAU and 5,000 MAU in a given month, your stickiness rate would be:
`Product Stickiness (%) = (1,000 / 5,000) x 100 = 20%`

1. **Collect the number of customers at the start of the period:** Determine how many paying customers were active at the beginning of the analysis period.
2. **Determine the number of customers retained at the end of the period:** Record the number of paying customers who remained at the end of the period (excluding any newly acquired customers).
3. **Calculate the Retention Rate:** Use the following formula to calculate retention rate:
`Retention Rate (%) = (Number of Customers at End of Period / Number of Customers at Start of`
Period) x 100 For example, if you started with 500 customers and retained 450 by the end of the month, the retention rate would be:
`Retention Rate (%) = (450 / 500) x 100 = 90%`


## 📤 Output format
The output for product stickiness and retention rate is typically expressed as percentages. For example:
Product Stickiness: 20% Retention Rate: 90%


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   High Product Stickiness: A high stickiness rate (e.g., above 20%) indicates that users are frequently engaging with the product, which suggests strong user satisfaction and product value. This means that users ﬁnd the product essential for their daily operations.
•   Low Product Stickiness: A low stickiness rate (e.g., below 10%) suggests that users engage with the product infrequently, which may indicate that they do not ﬁnd enough value in the product or that the product is used only occasionally.
•   High Retention Rate: A high retention rate (e.g., above 90%) is a positive indicator of customer satisfaction and loyalty. It suggests that the product delivers sustained value and that customers are willing to continue paying for the service.
•   Low Retention Rate: A low retention rate (e.g., below 80%) may indicate issues with customer satisfaction, product ﬁt, or competitive pressure. This could be a warning sign that customers are not seeing long-term value and are at risk of churning.

## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Improve onboarding and user education:** Focus on optimizing the onboarding process to help new users quickly understand and extract value from the product. Offering interactive tutorials and guides can help users become more engaged, increasing stickiness.
2. **Promote feature adoption:** Encourage users to explore and use key features more regularly. This can be done through targeted in-app messaging, email campaigns, and customer success outreach. More frequent use of valuable features boosts engagement and stickiness.
3. **Segment users for personalized engagement:** Segment users by engagement levels (e.g., high-frequency vs. low-frequency users) and tailor your communication to each group. Highly engaged users may be offered advanced features, while low-frequency users can be given tips to increase their engagement.
4. **OKer incentives for retention:** Consider offering incentives such as discounts for long-term contracts or special offers for early renewals to boost retention. This can also include introducing a loyalty or rewards program for long-term users.
5. **Improve customer support and success eKorts:** Proactively reach out to at-risk customers and offer personalized support. Customer success teams can work to identify early warning signs of churn, such as declining usage patterns, and take corrective action to retain those customers.
6. **Gather feedback and address pain points:** Continuously gather feedback from customers, particularly those who churn, to identify common reasons for dissatisfaction or unmet needs. Use this information to improve the product and address pain points that may be impacting stickiness and retention.
7. **Launch new features or updates based on user needs:** Regularly introduce new features or updates that align with the needs and preferences of your users. Keeping the product fresh and responsive to user feedback can help drive engagement and long-term retention.
