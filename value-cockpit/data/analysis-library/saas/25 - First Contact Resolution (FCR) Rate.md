# 25 · First Contact Resolution (FCR) Rate

> **Part:** [[C - Customer Retention & Success]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the First Contact Resolution (FCR) Rate analysis is to measure the percentage of customer support issues resolved during the initial interaction with the support team. This metric helps SaaS companies evaluate the efficiency and effectiveness of their customer support services, improve customer satisfaction, and reduce repeat inquiries, which can lower support costs and increase customer retention.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Total number of customer support cases:** The total number of cases or tickets handled by the support team during a speciﬁed time period (e.g., monthly or quarterly).
2. **Number of cases resolved on ﬁrst contact:** The number of support cases resolved during the initial interaction without requiring follow-up communication.
3. **Customer feedback (optional):** Data on customer satisfaction or feedback regarding whether the issue was fully resolved during the ﬁrst contact, if available.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Deﬁne what constitutes a resolved case:
o    A resolved case is one where the customer’s issue is fully addressed and resolved during the ﬁrst interaction, without needing follow-ups. Ensure that the criteria for "resolution" are clearly deﬁned (e.g., problem ﬁxed, question answered, no further action needed).
2. Collect data on total customer support cases:
o    Track the total number of customer support cases (e.g., tickets, chats, or calls) handled by the support team during the analysis period. This will be used as the denominator in the FCR calculation.
3. Track the number of cases resolved on ﬁrst contact:
o    Record the number of cases that were resolved during the initial interaction without requiring additional follow-up. This can be done through case status in the ticketing system or by tracking agent activity.
4. Calculate the First Contact Resolution (FCR) Rate:
`o    Use the following formula to calculate the FCR rate: FCR Rate (%) = (Number of Cases Resolved on First Contact / Total Number of Customer Support Cases) x 100`
79 o    For example, if 700 out of 1,000 support cases were resolved on the ﬁrst contact:
`FCR Rate = (700 / 1,000) x 100 = 70%`
5. Collect customer feedback (optional):
o    If available, gather customer feedback through post-support surveys to determine whether they feel the issue was resolved during the ﬁrst contact. This can provide additional validation for the FCR metric.


## 📤 Output format
The output is typically presented as the First Contact Resolution (FCR) Rate along with supporting metrics, such as:
•   Total Number of Customer Support Cases: 1,000 •   Number of Cases Resolved on First Contact: 700 •   FCR Rate: 70% This provides a clear snapshot of how effectively the support team is resolving customer issues during the ﬁrst contact.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   High FCR Rate: A high FCR rate indicates that the support team is resolving most customer issues during the ﬁrst interaction. This is a positive sign that the team is effective, knowledgeable, and equipped to handle customer queries efficiently. High FCR rates generally correlate with high customer satisfaction and lower operational costs.
•   Low FCR Rate: A low FCR rate suggests that many issues require multiple contacts to resolve, which can frustrate customers and increase support costs. This may indicate a lack of sufficient agent training, inadequate tools, or complex product-related issues that are difficult to resolve quickly.
•   Benchmarking: The industry standard for FCR rate varies by sector, but SaaS companies often aim for an FCR rate of 70-80% or higher to ensure efficient support.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Enhance agent training:** Provide more comprehensive training to support agents, focusing on common customer issues, troubleshooting techniques, and product knowledge. Well-trained agents are more likely to resolve issues during the ﬁrst interaction, improving FCR.

2. **Improve internal knowledge resources:** Create and maintain a robust internal knowledge base that agents can quickly refer to when handling customer inquiries. Having access to accurate and up-to-date information allows agents to resolve issues faster and more effectively.
3. **Empower support agents with the right tools:** Equip agents with better support tools, such as customer interaction histories, automated workﬂows, and diagnostics tools, to help them resolve issues on the ﬁrst contact. The more context agents have, the quicker they can solve problems.
4. **Encourage collaboration between teams:** Foster better communication between the customer support team and other departments, such as product development or engineering, to quickly resolve more complex technical issues. Ensuring that agents have access to expertise from other teams can improve resolution times.
5. **Implement feedback loops:** Collect feedback from customers after each interaction to determine if they felt their issue was resolved satisfactorily. This can help identify areas where the team needs improvement, particularly for recurring issues that may require changes to the support process.
6. **Monitor and reﬁne common issue workﬂows:** Analyze the types of issues that frequently require follow-ups and implement more streamlined workﬂows or automated responses for those problems. Reducing friction in the resolution process can signiﬁcantly boost FCR.
7. **OKer proactive support:** Use tools such as in-app messaging, automated FAQs, or chatbots to resolve simple customer issues before they escalate into full support cases. Proactive support can reduce the number of contacts needed for resolution.
