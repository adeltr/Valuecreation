# KPI — Pulp & Paper

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Manufacturing & Industrial]]

58 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Basis Weight Variation | This refers to the consistency in the weight of paper, typically measured in terms of weight per unit area. More consistent basis weight can result in higher quality paper. |
| Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD) of Effluent | This measures the amount of oxygen needed to break down the organic material in the mill's wastewater. Lower BOD values indicate a lower environmental impact. |
| Black Liquor Solids Production | Black liquor is a byproduct of the Kraft process and contains valuable chemicals. The amount of solids in the black liquor indicates the potential for chemical recovery. |
| Bleaching Efficiency | For producers that bleach their pulp to make white paper, the efficiency of this process can have a significant impact on cost and environmental footprint. |
| Brightness Stability | Over time, the brightness of paper can deteriorate. The rate of this deterioration can impact the paper's suitability for certain applications. |
| Bursting Strength | This is a measure of the pressure required to burst a sheet of paper, relevant to packaging paper companies. It indicates the durability and quality of the paper. |
| Chemical Usage Efficiency | This indicator measures the amount of pulp produced per unit of chemicals used in the pulping process. It reflects the efficiency of the company's chemical usage. |
| CO2 Emissions per Ton of Production | This measures the amount of CO2 emitted per ton of product, a key indicator of the company's carbon footprint. |
| Creep (or Stretch) under Load | This measures the deformation of paper or board under prolonged tension. It's particularly important for packaging grades, where deformation can lead to package failure. |
| Curl | This measures the paper's tendency to curl under changing humidity and temperature conditions, a significant factor for printing grades of paper. |
| De-inking Efficiency | For companies that recycle paper, the efficiency of the de-inking process (removing ink from used paper) is an important indicator of both the quality of the output and the cost of the process. |
| Effluent Treatment Efficiency | This measures the efficiency of the wastewater treatment process in removing contaminants from the mill's wastewater. |
| Energy Self-Sufficiency | Many pulp and paper mills use co-generation systems that burn waste material to produce electricity. A company's degree of energy self-sufficiency can significantly impact its operational costs and environmental footprint. |
| Fiber Efficiency | This indicator measures the weight of the final paper product as a percentage of the weight of wood fiber input. A higher value indicates better efficiency in the production process. |
| Fiber Sourcing Percentage | This measures the percentage of raw materials (wood fiber) a company sources from certified sustainable sources. It reflects a company's commitment to sustainable practices and may influence its public image, customer relationships, and risk profile. |
| Filler Content | Fillers are added to paper to improve properties like smoothness, brightness, and opacity. The filler content can impact the paper's characteristics as well as the production cost. |
| Fluff Pulp Production | For companies that produce fluff pulp (used in absorbent products like diapers), the quantity and quality of fluff pulp produced is a key performance indicator. Quality measures may include fiberization efficiency (how easily the pulp "fluffs up") and absorbency. |
| Fold Endurance | This refers to the number of double folds a paper can withstand under a specified load before breaking. It's especially important for paper used in printing, packaging, and notebooks. |
| Formation | This refers to the uniformity of the paper sheet's fiber distribution. Good formation results in a uniform, high-quality paper with consistent printing properties. |
| Internal Bond Strength | This measures the force required to delaminate a sheet of paper or paperboard, providing an indication of the internal strength of the product. |
| Lignin Removal Efficiency | In the pulping process, lignin is removed from wood to produce pulp. The efficiency of this process can impact the quality of the pulp and the overall process efficiency. |
| Lime Mud Reburning Efficiency | In the Kraft pulping process, reburning lime mud helps recover chemicals and reduce operating costs. Efficiency in this process is key to controlling costs. |
| Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR) | This KPI measures the frequency of workplace injuries that result in lost time, per million hours worked. Safety is a critical aspect in the heavy industry such as pulp and paper, and LTIFR is a key measure of a company's safety performance. |
| Moisture Content | The amount of water in the final paper product can affect the paper's properties and the efficiency of the drying process, which is a significant energy user in paper production. |
| Paper Break Frequency on Paper Machines | This measures the frequency with which the paper web breaks during the production process. Frequent breaks can disrupt production and decrease efficiency. |
| Paper Dusting | This is a measure of the amount of small particles or "dust" that comes off the paper during printing or handling. Excessive dusting can lead to problems in printing equipment and decrease the quality of the final printed product. |
| Paper Grade Change Frequency | This KPI measures the frequency of changes in the grade of paper being produced, which can impact production efficiency and downtime. |
| Paper Machine Efficiency | This is the ratio of the actual production rate to the maximum possible rate. Higher efficiency indicates better use of capital assets and lower costs per unit of output. |
| Paper Smoothness | For many types of paper, particularly printing and writing paper, the smoothness of the paper is a key quality parameter. |
| Particulate Emissions | This is a measure of the amount of particulate matter emitted by a mill's operations. It is a key environmental and regulatory compliance metric. |
| Percent of Non-Wood Fiber | Some companies use non-wood fibers (like straw, bamboo, or cane bagasse) as raw materials. The proportion of these fibers can impact the properties of the pulp and paper, and also reflect a company's sustainability initiatives. |
| Ply Bond Strength | This is the force needed to separate the plies of multi-ply paper or board. This strength is crucial in packaging grades to ensure durability and robustness of the final product. |
| Pulp Bleaching Sequences | The sequences and chemicals used in bleaching pulp can have implications for the quality of the resulting paper and the environmental impact of the process. |
| Pulp Consistency in Paper Machine Headbox | This is a crucial operational parameter in paper production. It can impact the formation of the paper sheet and hence the quality of the final product. |
| Pulp Detrimental Contaminants Level | This measures the amount of undesirable contaminants (like metals, plastics, etc.) in the pulp. A lower level of contaminants improves the quality of the paper and reduces wear on the processing equipment. |
| Pulp Dryness After Washing | The dryness of the pulp after washing can impact the energy usage in the drying process and the quality of the final product. |
| Pulp Freeness | This is a measure of the rate at which water drains from a pulp slurry. It's an important parameter that affects how pulp behaves in the paper-making process. |
| Pulp Yield Percentage | This is specific to pulp producers and measures the percentage of pulp obtained from a given amount of raw material. High pulp yield indicates better efficiency and cost-effectiveness. |
| Pulping Liquor Concentration | In the Kraft pulping process, the concentration of the pulping liquor can impact the quality and properties of the pulp. |
| Ratio of Coated to Uncoated Paper Production | Coated papers are used for higher-quality printing applications, while uncoated papers are used for a wide variety of applications. This ratio can provide insight into the company's product mix and market positioning. |
| Ratio of Mechanical to Chemical Pulp | Mechanical pulp retains more of the wood fiber than chemical pulping, but the paper it produces is not as strong. This ratio can impact the characteristics and quality of the final product. |
| Ratio of Softwood to Hardwood Pulp | Depending on the end-product, mills might use different proportions of softwood and hardwood pulp. This ratio could impact the strength, texture, and other characteristics of the final product. |
| Recycled Fiber Usage | This is the percentage of recycled fiber used in the paper production process. Higher usage of recycled fiber can lower costs and improve a company’s sustainability profile. |
| Retained Ash Content | This measures the percentage of ash remaining in the paper after incineration. The ash content can impact the paper's appearance and printing properties. |
| Stiffness | This KPI measures the paper or board's resistance to bending. Stiffness is a significant factor in box and carton paper grades. |
| Sulfur Emissions | In Kraft pulping, sulfur compounds are produced which can lead to unpleasant odors and environmental concerns. This KPI measures the amount of sulfur emissions per ton of pulp produced. |
| Tear Resistance | This measures the paper's ability to withstand tearing forces, an important quality characteristic for certain types of paper, like packaging and specialty papers. |
| Tensile Strength | This measures the maximum amount of tensile stress that paper or board can withstand before breaking. It's essential for packaging grades of paper and board. |
| Thermal Conductivity of Paper | This measures the rate at which heat passes through a paper product, which is particularly important for certain specialty papers. |
| Total Reduced Sulfur (TRS) Emissions | TRS compounds contribute to the characteristic smell of pulp mills and can be a significant source of air pollution. Measuring these emissions is essential for regulatory compliance and operational efficiency. |
| Volatile Organic Compounds (VOC) Emissions | These compounds can contribute to air pollution and health issues. This KPI measures the amount of VOCs emitted per unit of production. |
| Waste Paper Recovery Rate | In the recycled paper segment, this measures the amount of usable paper recovered from waste paper. A higher rate can lower raw material costs and enhance a company’s sustainability profile. |
| Water Use per Ton of Product | This measures the amount of water consumed in producing a ton of pulp or paper. Lower figures indicate more efficient use of water and a smaller environmental footprint. |
| Whiteness | Different from brightness, whiteness measures the paper's color perception under standard light. Whiteness is a critical quality factor in the printing and writing paper segment. |
| Wood Chip Hardness | The hardness of the wood chips used can affect the quality of the pulp produced and the wear on the equipment. |
| Wood Chip Size Consistency | The size and consistency of wood chips used in pulping can impact the efficiency of the process and the quality of the pulp. |
| Wood Chip Yield | This measures how much pulp a company can produce from a given volume of wood chips. Higher yield indicates more efficient use of raw materials. |
| Wood Usage Efficiency | This measures the amount of pulp produced per unit of wood used. It can vary based on the type of wood used and the efficiency of the pulping process. |
