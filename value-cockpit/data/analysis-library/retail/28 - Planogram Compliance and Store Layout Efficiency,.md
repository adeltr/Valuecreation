# 28 · Planogram Compliance and Store Layout Efficiency, including Endcap Performance

> **Part:** [[D - Merchandising]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Retail Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Planogram Compliance and Store Layout E9iciency Analysis is to ensure that the store’s layout and product placements follow the predetermined planogram (the visual representation of product arrangements) to maximize sales, enhance customer experience, and boost endcap performance. This analysis helps retail companies ensure that products are displayed in the most e9ective manner to drive sales and encourage customer engagement.


## 📊 Data required
•   Planogram for each store or department.
•   Actual store layout and product placement information.
•   Sales data by product, shelf location, and endcap location.
•   Customer foot tra9ic data (optional but useful for evaluating layout e9iciency).
•   Compliance audit data (e.g., frequency of audits, discrepancies in planogram compliance).
•   Labor or operational costs associated with implementing and maintaining the planogram.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Conduct a planogram compliance audit:** Perform an in-store audit by comparing the actual product placement to the planogram. Record any discrepancies, such as missing items, incorrect product placements, or wrong facing counts (the number of product facings on the shelf). Calculate the planogram compliance rate using the following formula:
`Planogram Compliance (%) = (Number of Correct Placements / Total Planned Placements) x 100 This measures how closely the store adheres to the planned layout.`
2. **Analyze sales data by shelf location and endcap performance:** Collect sales data for products based on their shelf location and endcap placement. Use the following equation to assess the sales performance of endcap displays:
`Endcap Sales Performance (%) = (Sales from Endcap Products / Total Store Sales) x 100 This shows the contribution of endcap displays to overall store sales.`
3. **Calculate sales per square foot:** To evaluate overall layout e9iciency, calculate the sales per square foot for each section of the store:
`Sales per Square Foot = Total Sales for Section / Total Square Footage of Section Compare this across di9erent sections of the store to identify areas that generate high or low sales relative to their size.`
4. **Assess customer ﬂow and foot traMic (optional):** If foot tra9ic data is available, analyze how customers move through the store and which sections receive the most or least tra9ic. This helps evaluate the e9ectiveness of the layout in guiding customers to key areas, including endcaps.
5. **Identify high- and low-performing product placements:** Analyze sales data to identify which product placements (e.g., eye-level, endcaps) generate the most sales and which are underperforming. Adjust the planogram as needed to optimize these placements.
6. **Review labor costs for maintaining compliance:** Calculate the labor or operational costs associated with setting up and maintaining planogram compliance. This helps evaluate whether the compliance process is e9icient and cost-e9ective.
Potential complications that can arise with this analysis •   Inconsistent audits: If compliance audits are not conducted regularly or accurately, it may lead to incomplete or misleading data on planogram adherence.
•   Seasonal variations: Customer behavior and sales patterns can vary seasonally, which may skew the analysis of layout e9ectiveness.
•   Changing customer preferences: Customer preferences can shift over time, which means that even a compliant planogram may not deliver optimal sales if it doesn’t align with current trends.
•   Layout constraints: Physical limitations of the store (e.g., aisle width, shelf space) may prevent full compliance with the planogram, making it di9icult to optimize the layout.


## 📤 Output format
The output typically includes key metrics such as planogram compliance rates, sales per square foot, and endcap performance percentages. These metrics can be presented in tables, graphs, or heat maps to show the relative performance of di9erent sections of the store.
Example output:
•   Planogram compliance rate for Q2 2024: 90% •   Sales per square foot:
o   Grocery section: $500 o   Electronics section: $750 o   Clothing section: $300 •   Endcap sales performance:
o   Sales contribution from endcaps: 15% of total store sales o   Best-performing endcap: $10,000 in sales o   Worst-performing endcap: $4,000 in sales


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   High planogram compliance rate (above 90%): Indicates that the store is following the planned product layout e9ectively, which is essential for maximizing sales.
•   Low planogram compliance rate (below 80%): Suggests that there are signiﬁcant deviations from the planogram, which may lead to missed sales opportunities. Corrective action is needed to improve compliance.
•   High sales per square foot: Indicates that the store layout is driving strong sales relative to its available space. This is a sign of good layout e9iciency.
•   Low sales per square foot: Suggests that the store layout may not be optimized, and certain sections of the store are underperforming.
•   Strong endcap performance: Endcap displays that contribute a high percentage of sales are e9ectively capturing customer attention and driving impulse purchases.
•   Weak endcap performance: Endcaps with low sales may need to be reconﬁgured or rotated with new products to improve their e9ectiveness.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Improve audit frequency and accuracy:** Conduct regular and thorough planogram compliance audits to ensure that stores adhere to the intended layout and product placements.
2. **Optimize endcap displays:** Regularly rotate endcap displays with new or high-margin products to capture customer attention and increase impulse purchases.
3. **Reﬁne store layout based on customer ﬂow:** Use foot tra9ic data to adjust store layouts, ensuring that high-tra9ic areas feature key products and promotions.
4. **Enhance employee training:** Train store employees on the importance of maintaining planogram compliance and how to set up displays according to the planogram.
5. **Test new layouts:** Experiment with di9erent store layouts or product placements in underperforming sections to ﬁnd more e9ective conﬁgurations.
6. **Align layout with seasonal trends:** Adjust planograms seasonally to align with changing customer preferences, such as placing seasonal items in high-tra9ic areas or endcaps.
7. **Use data-driven decisions:** Continuously monitor sales and layout performance data to make informed decisions about where and how to display products for maximum sales impact.
