# 17 · Non-Standard Auto Market & Subprime Segment Evaluation

> **Part:** [[B - Underwriting & Risk Segmentation]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Assess the profitability, growth potential, and underwriting effectiveness within the non-standard auto market and subprime driver segment to ensure accurate pricing and sustainable portfolio expansion.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Non-standard auto policies (written premium, earned premium, policy count)
- ​ Driver profiles indicating credit scores, prior coverage lapses, and violation history
- ​ Claims data (frequency, severity, incurred losses) specific to subprime segments
- ​ Renewal and retention metrics broken out by credit tiers or subprime indicators
- ​ Benchmark or industry data on non-standard auto rates, losses, and growth trends


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Identify non-standard auto policies and subprime drivers by extracting data on credit scores, coverage gaps, or prior violation history. Compile premium, loss, and retention information for these segments. 2.​ Calculate loss ratios and claim frequency/severity for subprime drivers using Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + LAE) / Earned Premium. Compare these figures to standard market segments to measure relative risk and profitability. 3.​ Segment non-standard policies by additional risk factors such as driver age, vehicle age, or geographic location. Examine each segment’s performance to detect which sub-groups drive higher losses or churn rates. 4.​ Review underwriting guidelines for subprime drivers, noting credit score thresholds, surcharges, or coverage limitations. Identify potential gaps where premiums may not fully compensate for the elevated risk. 5.​ Assess retention rates among subprime segments to see if higher premiums or stricter underwriting lead to lower renewal. Examine whether certain sub-groups respond better to incentive programs or flexible payment plans. 6.​ Benchmark your subprime segment performance against industry norms or competitor data if available. Detect any major discrepancies in claim costs or premium adequacy that may indicate mispricing. 7.​ Identify opportunities to refine non-standard offerings such as telematics-based programs, specialized driver training, or creative payment 39 structures. Evaluate whether these initiatives can reduce risk while attracting subprime drivers. 8.​ Summarize your findings and propose enhancements to underwriting rules, pricing tiers, and policy features. Present recommendations to product, pricing, and risk management teams for approval and rollout.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ Tables or charts showing subprime vs. standard claim frequency, severity, and loss ratios
- ​ Segment-level summaries of retention rates and credit tiers
- ​ Written report with key conclusions, potential pricing or product adjustments, and projected financial outcomes


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ Higher-than-expected loss ratios or claim frequency in subprime segments may require stricter underwriting or targeted surcharges.
- ​ Significant variance in retention compared to standard segments could signal price sensitivity or gaps in non-standard product design.
- ​ Clustering of high losses in certain sub-groups indicates the need for more granular underwriting or additional driver improvement strategies.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Implement tiered pricing or telematics solutions specifically for subprime drivers, rewarding safer driving behaviors. 2.​ Offer flexible payment plans or incentive programs to improve retention among subprime segments without sacrificing underwriting rigor. 3.​ Periodically review and adjust credit score thresholds to reflect actual loss outcomes rather than broad assumptions. 4.​ Monitor competitor strategies to maintain a competitive yet profitable presence in the non-standard auto market.
