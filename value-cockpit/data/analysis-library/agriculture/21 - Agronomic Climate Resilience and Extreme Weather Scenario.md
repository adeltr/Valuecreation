# 21 · Agronomic Climate Resilience and Extreme Weather Scenario Evaluation

> **Part:** [[C - Environmental Sustainability & Resource]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Agriculture Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Assess the farm’s susceptibility to climate variability and extreme weather events, identify vulnerabilities in cropping systems, and develop strategies to strengthen resilience and minimize losses.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical climate data (temperature, rainfall, drought/flood frequency)
- Crop performance records under various weather conditions
- Soil characteristics (drainage capacity, organic matter, erosion risk)
- Irrigation infrastructure and water availability
- Local or regional climate projections (short- and long-term)
- Existing risk mitigation measures (e.g., shelterbelts, water storage)
- Economic data related to weather-induced losses (yield drops, replanting costs)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Examine historical climate patterns. Review temperature ranges, precipitation totals, droughts, floods, and major storms over the past decade or more. Note how these events affected crop yield.
Step 2: Identify crop vulnerabilities. Analyze which crops (or varieties) are most sensitive to extremes (e.g., heat stress, waterlogging). Correlate yield performance with weather anomalies.
Step 3: Assess soil and water management. Check soil health indicators (organic matter, infiltration rates) and irrigation capabilities. Determine whether current practices can handle drought or excessive rainfall.
Step 4: Evaluate infrastructure and protective measures. Document existing windbreaks, drainage systems, or protective structures (e.g., greenhouses, shade nets) and their effectiveness against extreme events.
Step 5: Review climate projections. Obtain short-term and long-term climate forecasts for the region. Focus on temperature trends, precipitation shifts, and increased likelihood of extreme events.
47 Step 6: Develop scenario plans. Model different weather extremes (drought, flood, heatwave) and estimate potential impacts on yields, costs, and farm operations. Identify specific vulnerabilities in each scenario.
Step 7: Recommend resilience strategies. Suggest measures such as drought-tolerant crop varieties, improved water storage, soil organic matter enhancement, or early warning systems. Outline their costs and expected benefits.


## 📤 Output format
- A table or matrix mapping each crop to climate risks (e.g., drought, heatwave, flood) and potential yield impacts
- A short narrative report explaining historical weather impacts, projected vulnerabilities, and recommended resilience tactics
- Scenario-based financial projections showing estimated losses and mitigation costs under different extreme weather events


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Higher risk scores indicate greater vulnerability to climate stressors, prioritizing certain crops or fields for adaptation measures.
- Identifying consistent yield drops in specific weather conditions points to areas where improved practices or varietal changes are needed.
- Clear ROI from recommended strategies (e.g., adopting drought-resistant seeds) suggests financially viable paths to enhance resilience.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Diversify crop choices or varieties to reduce the risk of total failure from a single extreme event.
2. Increase soil organic matter through cover crops, compost, or reduced tillage to enhance water retention and soil structure.
3. Invest in water conservation and irrigation infrastructure (e.g., drip systems, rainwater harvesting) for drought resilience.
4. Implement early warning systems (weather stations, forecast alerts) and develop rapid response protocols.
5. Explore insurance products or government programs that provide financial buffers against extreme weather.
6. Pursue agroforestry or shelterbelt planting to protect fields from wind damage and help stabilize microclimates.
