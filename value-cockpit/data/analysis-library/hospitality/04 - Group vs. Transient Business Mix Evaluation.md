# 4 · Group vs. Transient Business Mix Evaluation

> **Part:** [[A - Revenue & Pricing Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Compare the revenue contributions of group and transient segments, helping you balance occupancy, rate strategies, and business risk across these different guest types.


## 📊 Data required
- Number of room nights sold to group vs. transient segments for a given period.
- Revenue associated with each segment, including ADR or net revenue per room.
- Channel costs or sales expenses (if available) that differ between group and transient segments.
- Historical data for comparison and trend analysis.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify the time period for your analysis. Determine if you’ll look at monthly, quarterly, or yearly data.
2. Collect segment-level data. Gather room nights, revenue, ADR, and other relevant metrics for both group and transient bookings.
3. Calculate the proportions. Determine what percentage of total room nights and total revenue each segment represents.
4. Compare performance metrics for each segment. Look at Occupancy Rate, ADR, RevPAR, and profitability by segment.
5. Analyze trends over time. Identify whether group or transient business is growing or declining in terms of volume and revenue.
6. Correlate with seasonality or events. Note if specific periods (e.g., convention season) drive group volume, or if leisure travel influences transient bookings.


## 📤 Output format
- A table showing Group vs. Transient bookings, room nights, revenue, ADR, and share (%) of total.
- Visual charts (bar or pie) illustrating each segment’s share of occupancy and revenue.
- Trend lines over multiple periods to reveal shifts in the business mix.

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A higher share of group business can provide consistent base occupancy but may yield lower ADR if discounted.
- Transient business often commands higher rates, but can be more volatile and subject to market fluctuations.
- Evaluating changes in the mix helps identify potential over-reliance on one segment.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Adjust sales strategies to target high-margin group business during shoulder or low-demand seasons.
2. Focus on attracting higher-paying transient guests during peak periods to optimize rate.
3. Diversify promotional activities to balance the mix, ensuring stability and maximizing revenue potential.
4. Collaborate with local convention and tourism bureaus to secure group bookings or citywide events.
