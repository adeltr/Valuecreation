# 4 · Pricing Adequacy Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Underwriting]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Pricing Adequacy Analysis is to determine whether current premium rates adequately cover the expected costs of claims, expenses, and a target profit margin. This analysis helps ensure that the company’s pricing strategies align with profitability goals and risk levels.


## 📊 Data required
1. Historical claims data, including frequency and severity by policy type
2. Underwriting expenses, including fixed and variable costs
3. Target profit margin (as a percentage of premiums)
4. Premiums earned from each policy or product line


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Segment the Portfolio:** Segment policies by line of business, policy type, or customer segment to ensure pricing analysis is targeted.
2. **Calculate Expected Claims Cost per Policy:** Determine the expected claims cost using historical data, including:
- **Loss Frequency:** Average number of claims per policy.
- **Loss Severity:** Average cost per claim. Expected Claims Cost per Policy = Loss Frequency x Loss Severity
3. **Calculate Total Expected Cost per Policy:** Total Expected Cost per Policy = Expected Claims Cost per Policy + Underwriting Expenses per Policy (Underwriting expenses may vary by policy type or distribution channel, so factor these accordingly.)
4. **Calculate the Adequate Premium Rate:** Incorporate the target profit margin to calculate the adequate premium rate:
`Adequate Premium Rate = (Total Expected Cost per Policy / (1 - Target Profit Margin))`
5. **Compare Current Premiums to Adequate Premium Rate:** Compare actual premiums earned per policy to the calculated Adequate Premium Rate:
- **Adequate Pricing:** Current premiums meet or exceed the calculated rate.
- **Inadequate Pricing:** Current premiums fall below the calculated rate, suggesting potential losses or underpricing.
6. **Compile Results:** Summarize findings in a table, showing each policy segment and comparing the actual premium to the adequate rate.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns such as:
- Policy Segment (e.g., auto, homeowners)
- Expected Claims Cost per Policy
- Underwriting Expenses per Policy
- Target Profit Margin
- Adequate Premium Rate
- Current Premium Rate
- Adequacy Status (Adequate or Inadequate)
A visual bar or line chart comparing Adequate Premium Rate and Current Premium Rate for each segment can provide additional insights.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Adequate Pricing (Current Premium Rate >= Adequate Premium Rate):** Pricing is sufficient to cover expected costs and desired profit margin, indicating sound pricing strategy.
- **Inadequate Pricing (Current Premium Rate < Adequate Premium Rate):** Pricing may not cover expected costs and profit targets, suggesting that adjustments may be needed.
Benchmarking against competitors or industry averages can also offer insights into market positioning and pricing competitiveness.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Refine Pricing Models Use updated actuarial models and more granular data to improve pricing accuracy, adjusting for risk factors more precisely.
2. Implement Regular Pricing Reviews Periodically assess pricing adequacy, especially in segments with fluctuating loss frequency or severity trends.
3. Segment Pricing by Risk Level Consider adjusting premiums for high-risk and low-risk customers within each segment to better match costs with expected claims.
4. Adjust Target Profit Margins by Segment Set realistic profit targets that align with each segment’s risk profile, allowing for competitive yet adequate pricing.
5. Enhance Data Quality Use high-quality claims data and advanced analytics (e.g., machine learning) to improve predictions of expected claims costs.
6. Incorporate Reinsurance Costs Factor in reinsurance costs, especially for segments with high volatility, to better capture the total expected cost per policy.
