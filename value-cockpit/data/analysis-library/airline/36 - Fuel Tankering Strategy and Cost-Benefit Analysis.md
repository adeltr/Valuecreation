# 36 · Fuel Tankering Strategy and Cost-Benefit Analysis

> **Part:** [[G - Cost Management and Financial Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Airline Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Fuel Tankering Strategy and Cost-Benefit Analysis is to evaluate the financial benefits and operational implications of carrying extra fuel on specific flights to avoid purchasing fuel at higher-cost airports. This analysis aims to reduce fuel expenses by taking advantage of lower fuel prices at certain locations, balancing the cost savings against the increased weight and fuel burn associated with tankering.


## 📊 Data required
- Fuel prices at departure and destination airports for each route
- Fuel consumption rates by aircraft type, including extra fuel burn associated with tankering
- Distance and average duration for each route
- Aircraft fuel capacity and maximum takeoff weight
- Additional costs associated with tankering, such as increased fuel burn due to added weight
- Weather and seasonal data that may impact fuel burn (e.g., headwinds)
- Industry benchmarks on fuel tankering practices and cost savings


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Fuel Price Differential by Route:** Identify routes with significant fuel price differences between the departure and destination airports. Calculate the cost savings from purchasing fuel at the lower-priced airport, using:
`Fuel Price Differential = Price at Destination - Price at Departure`
Prioritize routes with the highest price differential for tankering assessment.
2. **Estimate Additional Fuel Burn Due to Tankering:** Calculate the additional fuel burn that results from carrying extra fuel, as added weight increases fuel consumption. Use the airline’s data or industry averages for fuel burn due to tankering, which is typically expressed as a percentage increase in fuel consumption per unit of extra fuel weight.
3. **Calculate Potential Cost Savings per Flight:** For each eligible route, estimate the cost savings from tankering by comparing the fuel costs saved against the additional fuel burned due to extra weight. Use the formula:
`Cost Savings per Flight = (Fuel Price Differential x Tankered Fuel Quantity) - (Additional Fuel Burn x Fuel Price at Departure)`
4. **Analyze Maximum Tankering Capacity and Operational Constraints:** Check aircraft fuel capacity and maximum takeoff weight limits to ensure that the extra fuel carried does not exceed operational limits. Take into account payload restrictions, as tankering may limit the weight available for cargo or passengers on specific flights.
5. **Assess Seasonal and Weather Variations on Fuel Burn:** Analyze how seasonal factors (e.g., strong headwinds) may impact fuel burn on specific routes, as this can affect the cost-effectiveness of tankering. For instance, high fuel burn in winter months may reduce or eliminate savings on some routes.
6. **Benchmark Against Industry Tankering Practices:** Compare the airline’s tankering strategy with industry standards to determine if the current approach aligns with best practices. Benchmarking can help optimize tankering practices and identify routes where tankering has the most financial impact.
7. **Run Scenario Testing for Tankering Feasibility by Route:** Test various tankering scenarios, including full tankering (maximum fuel), partial tankering, or no tankering, on high differential routes. Measure the projected cost savings, operational impacts, and any trade-offs in payload capacity to determine the optimal tankering approach.


## 📤 Output format
- **Fuel Price Differential Report by Route:** A table showing fuel price differences between departure and destination airports, identifying routes with high savings potential.
- **Additional Fuel Burn and Cost Impact Analysis:** A breakdown of the additional fuel burn and cost for each tankered route, highlighting the net cost or savings.
- **Maximum Tankering Capacity and Operational Constraints Summary:** A report on tankering capacity and weight limitations, identifying any payload or operational constraints by route.
- **Seasonal Variation Impact on Fuel Burn:** An analysis of seasonal and weather impacts on fuel burn for tankered routes.
- **Industry Benchmarking on Tankering Practices:** A comparison of the airline’s tankering approach with industry standards, highlighting routes with optimal tankering potential.
- **Scenario Analysis Results for Tankering Strategies:** Charts or tables showing projected savings, fuel burn, and payload impacts for different tankering strategies by route.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Fuel Price Differential Report by Route highlights routes with the greatest fuel price differences, guiding tankering opportunities for cost savings.
- Additional Fuel Burn and Cost Impact Analysis shows the net cost or savings from tankering, helping identify which routes are financially viable for tankering.
- Maximum Tankering Capacity and Operational Constraints Summary ensures tankering strategies do not compromise aircraft weight or payload capacity, maintaining operational safety.
- Seasonal Variation Impact on Fuel Burn indicates how weather patterns affect the viability of tankering, supporting seasonal adjustments to tankering practices.
- Industry Benchmarking on Tankering Practices offers insight into competitive positioning and opportunities for optimization relative to industry standards.
- Scenario Analysis Results for Tankering Strategies provide forecasts of potential savings and operational impacts from different tankering strategies, supporting data-driven decisions for each route.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Implement Selective Tankering on High-Savings Routes:** Focus on routes with high fuel price differentials and manageable additional fuel burn to maximize cost savings from tankering.
2. **Monitor Fuel Price Fluctuations and Adjust Tankering Accordingly:** Regularly track fuel price changes at major airports and adjust tankering strategies dynamically to capture new savings opportunities.
3. **Optimize Tankering by Season:** Adjust tankering practices seasonally to account for weather variations that may impact fuel burn and cost-effectiveness, especially on long-haul routes.
4. **Use Partial Tankering for Balanced Savings:** Where full tankering may limit payload, consider partial tankering to achieve savings while retaining flexibility for cargo and passengers.
5. **Benchmark and Refine Tankering Strategy:** Continuously benchmark tankering practices against industry standards, refining strategy based on new insights to maintain optimal cost efficiency.
