# 13 · Telematics & Usage-Based Insurance Performance

> **Part:** [[B - Underwriting & Risk Segmentation]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate how effectively telematics programs capture and price risk based on real-world driving behaviors, and determine whether usage-based insurance (UBI) leads to improved loss ratios and driver safety.


## 📊 Data required
- Telematics data from in-vehicle devices or mobile apps (mileage, speed patterns, braking, cornering)
- Claims data (frequency, severity, total incurred losses) for telematics vs. non-telematics policies
- Premium and exposure data (written premium, earned premium, number of policies)
- Retention, renewal, and customer satisfaction metrics for telematics users
- Market/competitor benchmarks on telematics adoption and performance (if available)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify all policies under telematics or usage-based insurance and collect relevant data on miles driven, driving behavior scores, and associated claims. Ensure you have at least 12 months of data to capture a full cycle of driving behavior patterns.
2. Segment drivers based on telematics scores or key driving events such as hard braking, speeding, or late-night travel. Calculate each segment’s loss ratio, retention rate, and average premium to identify performance differences.
3. Compare claim frequency and severity between telematics policyholders and similar risk groups without telematics. Determine if data-driven adjustments in pricing or coverage have significantly improved overall profitability.
4. Assess how premium adjustments correlate to telematics data, for example, whether safer drivers receive meaningful discounts or if risky drivers face surcharges. Monitor any changes in driver behavior post-enrollment, such as reduced harsh driving events.
5. Review the impact of telematics on customer retention and loyalty. Check if usage-based pricing creates a perception of fairness that leads to higher renewal rates.

6. Examine correlations between specific driving behaviors and claim likelihood, such as frequent high-speed events or late-night driving. Identify thresholds or patterns that are highly predictive of future losses.
7. Benchmark internal telematics results against competitor or market data if available. Look for significant variances in adoption rates, average discounts, or loss ratios that might indicate missed opportunities or aggressive pricing.
8. Document findings and propose enhancements to the telematics program. Present recommendations to adjust pricing algorithms, introduce real-time feedback to drivers, or refine data collection methods.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts comparing loss ratios, claim frequency, severity, and retention across telematics vs. traditional policies
- Segmented results by key driver behaviors and telematics score brackets
- Written summary detailing significant findings, recommended program adjustments, and projected financial impact


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Lower loss ratios and safer driving scores among telematics users typically indicate program success and effective data-based segmentation.
- Minimal differences between telematics and non-telematics groups suggest potential misalignment of scoring models or inadequate incentives for safer driving.
- Patterns of driver improvement (e.g., fewer harsh braking events) following telematics enrollment may justify deeper discounts or loyalty benefits.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Refine telematics scoring algorithms to better differentiate high-risk from low-risk driving behaviors.
2. Provide real-time feedback or rewards to encourage safer driving habits and reinforce the value of UBI participation.
3. Regularly review and update pricing structures to ensure fair discounts or surcharges based on observed driving data.
4. Collaborate with technology vendors or in-house IT teams to enhance data accuracy, reduce costs, and streamline user experience.
