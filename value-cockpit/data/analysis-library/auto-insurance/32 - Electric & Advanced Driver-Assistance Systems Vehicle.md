# 32 · Electric & Advanced Driver-Assistance Systems Vehicle Impact Assessment

> **Part:** [[E - Technology, Mobility & Operational Efficiency]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate how electric vehicles (EVs) and vehicles equipped with advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS) influence claims frequency, severity, and profitability, and determine if current underwriting and pricing strategies accurately capture these emerging risks.


## 📊 Data required
- Policy and vehicle data specifying electric vs. internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles, plus ADAS features (e.g., lane assist, automatic emergency braking)
- Claims records (frequency, severity, total incurred losses) segregated by EV/ADAS vs. traditional vehicles
- Repair cost data (parts availability, specialized labor rates) for EVs and ADAS components
- Telematics or usage data indicating how ADAS features are utilized
- Industry/competitor benchmarks on EV/ADAS adoption rates and loss trends


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify all policies insuring EVs and vehicles with ADAS by extracting make/model details, ADAS capabilities, and coverage specifics. Gather matching claims data to compare performance against conventional vehicles. Collect repair cost information and any telematics insight on ADAS usage.
2. Calculate claim frequency for EV/ADAS vehicles compared to traditional counterparts, using Claim Frequency = (Number of Claims / Number of Policies) x 100. Then examine claim severity by computing Severity = (Total Incurred Losses / Number of Claims). Compare how EV/ADAS vehicles differ from baseline norms.
3. Assess repair complexities and parts availability by analyzing average repair times and costs, noting if specialized labor or expensive components inflate severity. Investigate whether ADAS-related components increase the likelihood of total loss scenarios if they are heavily damaged.
4. Review telematics or usage data to see if ADAS engagement correlates with reduced collision frequency. Segment drivers who frequently use ADAS features to detect whether their loss experience is demonstrably better.

5. Benchmark internal results against external or competitor data, noting differences in claim costs, adoption rates, and underwriting approaches for EV/ADAS vehicles. Identify any outliers that point to underpricing or overpricing within your portfolio.
6. Analyze battery replacement or ADAS recalibration claims to understand cost drivers unique to EV/ADAS repairs. Pinpoint coverage gaps (e.g., specialized roadside assistance) or policy limits that might not suffice.
7. Examine underwriting and rating criteria to determine if they properly reflect EV/ADAS risk factors (e.g., costlier replacement parts, advanced safety features). Check if discounts or surcharges align with actual outcomes.
8. Document findings and recommend updates to underwriting, pricing, and product design based on observed EV/ADAS risk variations. Present the analysis to stakeholders for product, pricing, and operational adjustments.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables comparing claim frequency, severity, and total loss rates for EV/ADAS vs. traditional vehicles
- Charts illustrating repair cost and repair duration for specialized components
- Written summary detailing coverage gaps, unique risk factors, and recommended underwriting/pricing changes


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Lower claim frequency but higher severity for EV/ADAS suggests safer driving outcomes offset by pricier repairs.
- Persistently high total loss rates or disproportionate repair times may indicate a need for revised coverage limits or surcharges.
- Large deviations from competitor benchmarks could point to uncompetitive pricing or missed underwriting opportunities in the EV/ADAS space.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Refine rating models to account for the higher repair costs of ADAS sensors and EV battery components, potentially coupling discounts for safety features with appropriate surcharges for replacement complexities.
2. Partner with certified repair shops or OEM networks that specialize in EV/ADAS to manage costs and reduce claim duration.
3. Use telematics data to offer behavior-based incentives, rewarding policyholders who consistently leverage ADAS safely.
