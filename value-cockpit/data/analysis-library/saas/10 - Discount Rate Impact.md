# 10 · Discount Rate Impact

> **Part:** [[A - Finance & Revenue]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Discount Rate Impact analysis is to evaluate how discounting affects a SaaS company's overall revenue, proﬁtability, and customer acquisition efforts. Discounts can be used to attract new customers, increase contract sizes, or drive renewals, but they can also erode margins and lower the perceived value of the product if not managed properly. This analysis helps SaaS companies understand the ﬁnancial impact of offering discounts and identify ways to optimize discount strategies.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Total revenue:** The total revenue generated over a speciﬁc period (e.g., monthly, quarterly, or annually).
2. **Revenue from discounted deals:** The revenue generated from customers who received a discount during the same period.
3. **Average discount rate:** The average percentage discount applied to deals during the period.
4. **Customer acquisition and retention data:** The number of customers acquired or retained through discounted offers.
5. **Gross margin:** The gross margin percentage to evaluate the proﬁtability impact of discounts.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate the total revenue from discounted deals:
o    Identify the total revenue generated from deals where a discount was applied. This is the actual revenue recognized after discounts have been factored in.
2. Determine the average discount rate:
o    Calculate the average percentage discount applied to discounted deals. This can be done by taking the average of all percentage discounts offered during the analysis period.
`o    Use the formula: Average Discount Rate (%) = (Total Discount Amount / Total Original Contract Value) x 100`
o    For example, if the total discount amount across all deals is $50,000 and the original
`contract value before discounts is $500,000: Average Discount Rate = ($50,000 / $500,000) x 100 = 10%`

3. Calculate the revenue loss due to discounts:
o    Determine the revenue that was “lost” due to discounts by comparing the total contract value before and after discounts.
`o    Use the formula: Revenue Loss = Total Original Contract Value - Revenue from Discounted Deals`
o    For example, if the original contract value is $500,000 and the revenue after
`discounts is $450,000: Revenue Loss = $500,000 - $450,000 = $50,000`
4. Assess the proﬁtability impact:
o    Calculate the impact of the discount on proﬁtability by adjusting the gross margin to account for the reduced revenue. This helps determine if discounts are still proﬁtable after accounting for the lower revenue.
`o    Use the formula: Adjusted Gross Proﬁt = Revenue from Discounted Deals x Gross Margin`
o    For example, if the gross margin is 70% and the revenue after discounts is $450,000:
`Adjusted Gross Proﬁt = $450,000 x 0.70 = $315,000`
5. Analyze customer acquisition and retention impact:
o    Assess the effectiveness of discounts in driving customer acquisition and retention. Track the number of customers acquired or retained through discounted deals and compare this to the total number of customers.
`o    Use the formula: Customer Acquisition/Retention via Discounts (%) = (Number of Customers Acquired/Retained via Discounts / Total Number of Customers Acquired/Retained) x 100`
o    For example, if 50 out of 200 new customers were acquired through discounts:
`Acquisition via Discounts = (50 / 200) x 100 = 25%`
6. Calculate the lifetime value (LTV) of discounted customers (optional):
o    To understand the long-term impact of discounts, calculate the customer lifetime value (LTV) of customers who received discounts. Compare this to non-discounted customers to assess the quality of discounted customers.


## 📤 Output format
The output typically presents the Discount Rate Impact on revenue, proﬁtability, and customer acquisition, along with supporting metrics. Example format:
•   Total Revenue from Discounted Deals: $450,000 •   Average Discount Rate: 10% 33 •   Revenue Loss Due to Discounts: $50,000 •   Adjusted Gross Proﬁt: $315,000 •   Customer Acquisition via Discounts: 25% •   Lifetime Value of Discounted Customers: $2,000 (vs. $2,500 for non-discounted customers)


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   Moderate discount rate with positive acquisition: If discounts drive signiﬁcant customer acquisition or retention and the company maintains healthy proﬁtability, discounts can be seen as an effective growth strategy. The beneﬁts of acquiring new customers may outweigh the revenue loss from discounts.
•   High discount rate with low proﬁtability: A high average discount rate coupled with a large revenue loss may indicate that the company is relying too heavily on discounts, which could erode proﬁtability. If gross margins drop signiﬁcantly after applying discounts, it may signal that discounts are too deep or too frequent.
•   Discounted customer LTV vs. non-discounted: If the LTV of discounted customers is signiﬁcantly lower than non-discounted customers, it suggests that discounts are attracting lower-quality customers who may churn early or spend less over time.
•   Customer acquisition or retention impact: If discounts are responsible for a large percentage of customer acquisition or retention, the company may be overly dependent on discounting to grow its customer base. This could create price sensitivity among customers, making it harder to sell at full price in the future.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Limit discount depth and frequency:** Set guidelines on the maximum allowable discount rates and limit how often discounts can be offered. This helps protect margins while still allowing ﬂexibility in competitive situations.
2. **Focus on value-based pricing:** Instead of discounting, focus on emphasizing the value of the product to justify the price. Show how the product solves customer pain points, improves productivity, or generates ROI, reducing the need for deep discounts.
3. **OKer discounts with conditions:** Offer discounts tied to speciﬁc conditions, such as longer contract terms, annual billing, or commitment to additional services. This ensures that the company secures long-term value from discounted deals.

4. **Target discounts strategically:** Use discounts strategically for customer segments where price sensitivity is high or where discounts are likely to have the highest impact on acquisition or retention. Avoid blanket discounts that apply to all customers.
5. **Monitor discount eKectiveness:** Regularly track the impact of discounts on both short-term revenue and long-term customer retention. Evaluate whether discounts are leading to high-quality customers or simply attracting price-sensitive buyers who are more likely to churn.
6. **Test diKerent discount structures:** Experiment with different types of discounts, such as percentage discounts, dollar-oW deals, or bundling discounts, to see which structure has the best balance of driving growth while protecting proﬁtability.
