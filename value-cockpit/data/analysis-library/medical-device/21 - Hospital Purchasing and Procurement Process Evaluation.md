# 21 · Hospital Purchasing and Procurement Process Evaluation

> **Part:** [[C - Reimbursement & Market Access]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Medical Device Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Assess how hospitals identify, evaluate, and purchase medical devices. Understand the decision-making process, key stakeholders, and cost-containment strategies that influence procurement. Determine how to optimize product positioning, pricing, and communication to align with hospital procurement requirements.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Hospital procurement policies and standard operating procedures (for example, tender processes, value analysis committee guidelines)
- ​ Vendor qualification and supplier performance records
- ​ Hospital purchasing history and spend data (for example, volume, frequency, average purchase price)
- ​ Budget cycles and capital expenditure schedules
- ​ Feedback from hospital procurement teams, clinicians, and supply chain managers
- ​ Competitor pricing and contracting strategies


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Map the hospital procurement workflow. Identify all stakeholders (for example, procurement officers, physicians, finance) and outline each step of device evaluation and purchasing. 2.​ Review vendor qualification criteria. Determine how hospitals assess new suppliers, including compliance, quality standards, and financial stability. 3.​ Analyze decision-making committees. Investigate the role of value analysis committees or other cross-functional groups that vet new devices and set product formularies. 4.​ Examine budgeting and payment structures. Explore the hospital’s budget cycles, including capital vs. operational expenditure, and note any relevant payment models (for example, DRG, bundled payments). 5.​ Evaluate contract types and pricing models. Check whether the hospital prefers volume-based contracts, long-term agreements, or risk-sharing arrangements. 6.​ Review historical purchasing data. Identify trends in purchase frequency, order size, and spending patterns to see how the hospital manages device inventory.
50 7.​ Gather stakeholder feedback. Conduct interviews or surveys with purchasing managers, clinicians, and finance teams to understand product selection drivers and pain points. 8.​ Summarize findings and recommend improvements. Pinpoint how the device company can better meet hospital procurement standards, reduce costs, and streamline product adoption.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ A written report detailing the hospital procurement flow, key decision-makers, and purchase criteria
- ​ A gap analysis comparing current device offerings and sales approaches against hospital procurement expectations
- ​ A recommendations roadmap highlighting tactics for improving pricing, contracting, or vendor relationships
- ​ An executive summary that outlines strategic adjustments to enhance competitiveness in hospital purchasing


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ A well-structured procurement process with clear evaluation criteria suggests a hospital’s decision-making is systematic and data-driven, guiding how best to position the device.
- ​ Frequent price negotiations or preference for bulk buying may indicate cost-sensitive purchasing practices.
- ​ Delays or repeated committee reviews can signal the need for stronger clinical or economic evidence to expedite approval.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
- ​ Develop targeted value propositions and cost-justification materials for the hospital’s procurement and clinical stakeholders.
- ​ Offer flexible contracting models (for example, volume discounts, lease-to-own) to accommodate varying budget constraints.
- ​ Strengthen relationships with value analysis committees by providing clinical data, real-world evidence, or training that demonstrates the device’s advantages.
- ​ Train sales teams on navigating hospital procurement protocols and understanding the roles of key influencers.
- ​ Regularly review market feedback and purchasing data to refine pricing strategies and align with hospital priorities.
