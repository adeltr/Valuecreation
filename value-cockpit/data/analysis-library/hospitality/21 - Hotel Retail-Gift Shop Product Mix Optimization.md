# 21 · Hotel Retail-Gift Shop Product Mix Optimization

> **Part:** [[D - Operations & Hospitality Services]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Discover which products are most popular and profitable in the hotel’s retail outlets, enabling you to refine product selection and inventory levels.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical sales data by product category (quantity sold, revenue, margins).
- Cost of goods sold (COGS) for each product.
- Inventory records (on‐hand inventory, turnover rates).
- Guest demographic information or feedback about retail preferences (if available).
- Seasonal or event‐based sales patterns (e.g., peak seasons, holiday items).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather historical sales and cost data. Collect revenue, units sold, and COGS for each product or product category.
2. Calculate profit margins. Use the formula:
`Profit Margin = [(Selling Price - COGS) / Selling Price] x 100`
3. Assess sales velocity. Determine how quickly each product sells by looking at average daily or monthly sales rates.
4. Identify top‐selling and high‐margin products. Flag items that generate the highest revenue and profit.
5. Analyze inventory turnover. Use the formula:
`Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory Value`
Identify slow‐moving products that tie up capital.
6. Segment items by category or price point. Group products into categories (snacks, local souvenirs, essentials, etc.) to see where sales are strongest.
7. Review guest feedback. Determine if certain products are frequently requested or if there are gaps in the product assortment.
8. Make data‐driven decisions on product mix. Decide which items to discontinue, increase, or introduce based on profitability and demand.

## 📤 Output format
- A table listing each product, units sold, total revenue, COGS, profit margin, and inventory turnover.
- Charts or graphs illustrating product sales by category or profit margin distribution.
- Narrative section summarizing top performers, underperformers, and overall recommendations for product assortment.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High margin and high sales velocity products are strong contributors to gift shop profitability.
- Low turnover or negative margin items may need to be replaced or discounted to free up shelf space and inventory costs.
- Seasonal spikes or event periods might justify adding specialized products that meet temporary demand.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Negotiate better supplier terms or bulk discounts for popular items with high turnover.
2. Introduce new products based on guest demographics (e.g., family‐friendly items, local artisanal goods).
3. Optimize pricing and adjust displays or marketing efforts to promote high‐margin items.
4. Monitor real‐time sales data to quickly identify shifts in demand and reallocate inventory accordingly.
5. Continuously gather feedback from guests and staff to stay attuned to changing preferences.
