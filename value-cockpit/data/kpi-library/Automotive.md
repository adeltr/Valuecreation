# KPI — Automotive

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Automotive & Transportation]]

49 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Autonomous Driving Level | This KPI measures the advancement of a company's autonomous driving technology, often rated on a scale of 0-5 as defined by the Society of Automotive Engineers. Higher levels indicate more sophisticated self-driving capabilities. |
| Average Incentive per Vehicle Sold | This KPI tracks the average value of financial incentives (like cash rebates or discounted financing) offered to customers per vehicle sold. High values can indicate aggressive sales tactics but may also affect profitability. |
| Average Time Vehicles Spend on Dealer Lots | Also known as "days to turn," this measures how long, on average, vehicles stay on a dealer's lot before being sold. Shorter times can indicate strong demand. |
| Average Vehicle Age in the Market | This KPI indicates how long vehicles from a particular manufacturer tend to be in use. A higher average vehicle age could reflect durability and quality. |
| Average Warranty Cost per Vehicle | The average cost that a company incurs for warranty claims per vehicle sold. High warranty costs can signal quality control issues and reduce profitability. |
| Battery Range | For electric vehicle manufacturers, the distance that a vehicle can travel on a single charge is a key competitive differentiator. Greater range can signal superior technology and increase the appeal of vehicles to consumers. |
| Battery Range for Electric Vehicles | For EV manufacturers, the average battery range of their vehicles can be a key selling point, influencing consumer purchase decisions. |
| Brand Ranking in Third-party Auto Surveys | This could include rankings in surveys conducted by organizations like J.D. Power and Consumer Reports. These rankings often consider factors like vehicle reliability, customer satisfaction, and resale value. |
| Car Segment Market Share | This KPI tracks a company's market share within specific vehicle categories (e.g., SUVs, sedans, pickup trucks, electric vehicles). It can highlight areas of strength or weakness within a company's product portfolio. |
| CO2 Emissions | The average CO2 emissions of a manufacturer's fleet. This is becoming increasingly important due to tightening environmental regulations and the shift towards greener transportation. |
| Compliance with Emission Standards | The degree to which a manufacturer meets or exceeds regulatory emissions standards can impact its reputation and can potentially lead to fines or penalties. |
| Connected Car Services Revenue | As vehicles become more integrated with technology, the revenue generated from connected services (like navigation, streaming services, etc.) can become a significant income stream. |
| Cost per Vehicle Produced | The average cost incurred by the manufacturer for each vehicle produced. This KPI provides insight into the efficiency of the production process and overall cost management. |
| Dealer Satisfaction Score | Dealers are critical stakeholders in the automotive industry. This KPI measures the satisfaction of dealers with the manufacturer, encompassing aspects such as support from the manufacturer, profitability, and vehicle lineup. |
| Electrification Rate | Given the ongoing shift towards electric vehicles (EVs), the proportion of a company's sales made up by EVs or hybrids is becoming increasingly important. Higher rates could indicate a successful transition towards the future of the automotive industry. |
| Employee Safety Incident Rate | The frequency of safety incidents involving employees, particularly in manufacturing facilities. This can impact employee morale, productivity, and potential liabilities. |
| Energy Consumption per Vehicle Produced | This KPI measures the amount of energy used to manufacture each vehicle. It can be an indicator of a company's environmental impact and efficiency. |
| Fast Charging Time | This measures the amount of time it takes for an electric vehicle to charge to a certain percentage (often 80%) using a fast charger. Faster charging times can improve the practicality of an EV and make it more appealing to consumers. |
| Fleet Sales as a Percentage of Total Sales | For some manufacturers, a significant portion of sales might come from corporate or fleet buyers. High fleet sales can sometimes lead to lower resale values. |
| Fleet Sales Percentage | This is the percentage of total sales that are made to fleet customers such as car rental companies, taxi services, government entities, etc. While fleet sales can boost total sales figures, they typically carry lower profit margins than retail sales. |
| Fuel Efficiency | Measured in miles per gallon (MPG) or kilometers per liter (km/l), this KPI is particularly relevant for manufacturers of internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles. Higher fuel efficiency can be a key selling point. |
| Hybrid Vehicle Sales | For manufacturers with a diverse portfolio, this metric represents the number or percentage of hybrid vehicles sold. It can indicate consumer response to a company's hybrid offerings. |
| Maintenance and Repair Revenue | For companies with a strong focus on after-sales services, this KPI tracks the revenue generated from maintenance and repair services. |
| Model Ageing | The average age of the models in a car manufacturer's lineup can provide an indication of how fresh or outdated the company's product offering is. Older model lineups may be at a competitive disadvantage and could require higher spending on marketing and incentives to maintain sales. |
| Number of Dealerships | The total number of dealerships a manufacturer has can reflect its market presence and reach. It can also affect sales volume and brand visibility. |
| Number of Unique Models | The number of distinct models offered by a manufacturer can indicate its ability to cater to different market segments and customer preferences. |
| Number of Vehicles per Dealership | This ratio indicates the average number of vehicles sold per dealership, reflecting dealership productivity and demand. |
| Off-Lease Vehicles Return Rate | This metric measures the percentage of leased vehicles that return to the automaker at the end of the lease term. These returned vehicles can then be sold as certified pre-owned cars. |
| Parts Per Million (PPM) Quality Level | This is a measure of the number of defects in vehicles or parts per million units produced. Lower PPM indicates better quality control and fewer defects. |
| Penetration of Services in Vehicle Sales | The proportion of vehicles sold that include add-on services such as maintenance packages, insurance, financing options, etc. These services often provide higher margins than the vehicle sales themselves. |
| Percentage of Autonomous Vehicles in Fleet | For companies investing in self-driving technology, this KPI can indicate progress in rolling out autonomous capabilities. |
| Percentage of EV Charging Infrastructure | For EV manufacturers, the extent of their charging network can be a significant factor in sales. It can be measured as the number of charging points or the geographic coverage. |
| Percentage of Luxury Cars in Total Sales | For manufacturers that produce both mass-market and luxury vehicles, this KPI can provide insight into the sales mix and profitability, as luxury cars often carry higher profit margins. |
| Percentage of Revenue from Ride-Sharing, Car-Sharing, or Other Mobility Services | As the automotive industry evolves, some companies are diversifying into new areas like ride-sharing and car-sharing. This KPI can track the contribution of these new business areas to the company's overall performance. |
| Percentage of Sales from New Models | A high percentage here can indicate successful product launches and a strong capacity for innovation. It can also point to consumer acceptance of a company's latest offerings. |
| Percentage of Sales from New Models | The proportion of sales derived from models introduced in the last few years. This can provide insight into how well consumers are responding to a company's latest offerings. |
| Percentage of Self-Driving Cars in Fleet | For companies developing autonomous vehicles, this KPI measures the proportion of their fleet that has self-driving capabilities. This can indicate progress in this strategic area. |
| Percentage of Vehicles Sold with Advanced Safety Features | This KPI tracks the proportion of a manufacturer's vehicles sold that include advanced safety features like automatic braking, lane-keeping assist, etc. |
| Platform Commonality | The number or percentage of parts shared across different models or vehicle lines. Higher platform commonality can lead to cost savings and operational efficiencies. |
| Production Volume | This is the total number of vehicles produced over a specific period. It's an indicator of a company's manufacturing capacity and efficiency. If production volumes aren't in line with sales volumes, it may indicate issues with demand forecasting, production efficiency, or inventory management. |
| Revenue from Licensing Technology to Other Companies | Especially relevant for companies at the forefront of new technologies (like electric or autonomous vehicles), licensing can provide a significant additional revenue stream. |
| Vehicle Connectivity Features | As cars become more like "smartphones on wheels", the range and quality of connectivity features a manufacturer offers (from infotainment to navigation to software updates) can differentiate their vehicles in the market. |
| Vehicle Customization Rate | The percentage of vehicles sold with optional extras or custom features. This can be an important source of additional revenue and profit. |
| Vehicle Inventory Levels | The number of cars a company has in its inventory. High levels may suggest issues with product desirability or overproduction, while very low levels may indicate high demand, but could also signal supply chain or production issues. |
| Vehicle Residual Value | The expected depreciation of a vehicle over time. Vehicles with higher residual values can command higher lease rates and can indicate higher quality or more desirable products. |
| Vehicle Safety Ratings | Ratings assigned by organizations like the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) and Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) can impact a vehicle's appeal to consumers and hence its sales. |
| Vehicle Telematics Data Utilization | The degree to which a company collects and uses data from connected vehicles to improve their products, services, and operations. |
| Warranty Claim Rate | This measures the frequency of customers filing warranty claims. High rates may indicate quality control issues in manufacturing, which can lead to increased costs and damage to brand reputation. |
| Zero Emission Vehicle (ZEV) Credits | In certain regions (like California and some European countries), automakers are required to earn a certain number of ZEV credits, which are gained from manufacturing electric or plug-in hybrid vehicles. The number of credits held, sold or bought can impact a company's regulatory compliance and financial performance. |
