# 15 · Grade Control and Dilution Management Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Operational Efficiency and Cost Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Metals & Mining Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to evaluate and optimize grade control practices to minimize ore dilution and maximize ore recovery. Effective grade control ensures consistent feed quality to processing plants, improves operational efficiency, and enhances profitability.


## 📊 Data required
- Geological models and block models of the ore deposit.
- Ore and waste boundaries (planned vs. actual).
- Sampling and assay data from drill holes and blast holes.
- Production data (ore tonnage, waste tonnage, grade delivered to the plant).
- Processing plant feed grade and recovery data.
- Cost data for mining, processing, and waste handling.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define Ore and Waste Boundaries:** ○ Use geological and block models to delineate ore and waste zones. ○ Compare planned boundaries with actual boundaries from mining operations.
2. **Analyze Sampling Data:** ○ Collect sampling data from drill holes, blast holes, and face mapping to assess in-situ grade distribution. ○ Validate sampling accuracy to ensure reliable grade predictions.
3. **Calculate Dilution Metrics:** ○ Use the formula to calculate dilution:
`Dilution (%) = (Tonnage of Diluted Material / Total Ore Tonnage) x 100`
○ Compare planned dilution (model-based) with actual dilution.
4. **Evaluate Grade Control Practices:** ○ Review blast hole sampling and modeling practices to ensure accurate grade control. ○ Assess the alignment between mining and processing targets for grade consistency.
5. **Analyze Ore Recovery Efficiency:** ○ Calculate recovery efficiency using the formula:
`Ore Recovery (%) = (Ore Tonnage Recovered / Planned Ore Tonnage) x 100`
○ Identify discrepancies between planned and actual ore recovery.
46
6. **Assess Economic Impact of Dilution:** ○ Estimate the cost impact of dilution using the formula:
`Cost Impact ($) = (Diluted Material x Mining Cost per Ton) + (Lost Grade x Processing Cost per Unit).`
7. **Identify Root Causes of Dilution:** ○ Analyze factors such as poor blasting practices, inaccurate ore boundaries, or improper equipment usage.
8. **Benchmark Against Industry Standards:** ○ Compare dilution rates and grade control practices with peer companies or industry benchmarks.
9. **Develop Improvement Strategies:** ○ Propose adjustments to blasting techniques, equipment usage, or grade control protocols based on findings.


## 📤 Output format
- Dilution and Recovery Summary Table with planned vs. actual metrics for tonnage, grade, and recovery rates.
- Grade Distribution Chart showing planned and actual feed grades.
- Economic Impact Report quantifying the cost of dilution and recovery inefficiencies.
- Narrative Report summarizing findings, risks, and actionable recommendations.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Low Dilution and High Recovery Rates:** Indicate effective grade control and operational alignment.
- **High Dilution Rates:** Suggest inefficiencies in blasting, grade control, or equipment usage.
- **Significant Variances in Recovery Rates:** Highlight operational or sampling issues that need correction.
- **Consistent Grade Feed to Processing Plants:** Reflects effective alignment between mining and processing targets.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Enhance grade control accuracy by increasing the frequency and quality of blast hole sampling.
2. Improve blasting precision through optimized blast designs and modern explosives.
3. Train operators in grade control techniques to reduce ore misclassification.

4. Leverage real-time monitoring tools (e.g., GPS-enabled equipment) to improve ore and waste boundary adherence.
5. Recalibrate geological and block models regularly based on new sampling data.
6. Implement selective mining techniques to focus on high-grade zones while minimizing dilution.
7. Collaborate across geology, mining, and processing teams to align operational goals and ensure grade consistency.
