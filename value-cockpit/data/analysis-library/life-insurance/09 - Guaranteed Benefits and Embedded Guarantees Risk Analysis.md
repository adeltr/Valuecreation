# 9 · Guaranteed Benefits and Embedded Guarantees Risk Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Actuarial and Reserve Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To assess the financial and actuarial risks associated with guaranteed benefits and embedded guarantees within life insurance policies. The objective is to evaluate the sufficiency of reserves, understand the potential liabilities under adverse scenarios, and develop strategies to mitigate risk.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policy-level data:** ○ Types of guaranteed benefits (e.g., minimum death benefit, guaranteed cash value, maturity guarantees). ○ Policyholder demographics (e.g., age, gender, location). ○ Policy terms, coverage amounts, and premium structures.
2. **Financial data:** ○ Current reserves allocated for guarantees. ○ Historical claims related to guarantees.
3. **Actuarial assumptions:** ○ Discount rates, mortality rates, lapse rates, and investment returns.
4. **Stress testing parameters:** ○ Adverse scenarios such as low-interest-rate environments, higher-than-expected mortality rates, or significant policy lapses.
5. Industry benchmarks for guarantees and risk management practices.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Catalog Guaranteed Benefits a. Identify all guarantees offered in the product portfolio (e.g., guaranteed minimum income, maturity benefits, surrender values). b. Classify guarantees by product type (e.g., whole life, universal life, annuities).
2. Quantify Current Liabilities a. Calculate the current value of guaranteed liabilities using actuarial models. b. Use the formula:
`Guaranteed Liability = (Present Value of Expected Guaranteed Payouts - Present Value of Expected Premiums)`
3. Review Reserve Allocations a. Assess the adequacy of reserves allocated for guaranteed liabilities. b. Compare reserve levels with calculated liabilities.
4. **Evaluate Risk Factors:** 26 a. Identify the sensitivity of guarantees to key risk factors, such as: ■ Market risks (e.g., interest rate fluctuations, investment performance). ■ Policyholder behavior (e.g., lapses, early surrenders). ■ Demographic risks (e.g., mortality and longevity).
5. Conduct Stress Testing a. Test adverse scenarios, such as prolonged low-interest-rate environments, unexpected mortality spikes, or lapses concentrated in high-guarantee cohorts. b. Quantify the financial impact of each scenario on reserves and profitability.
6. Benchmark Against Industry Practices a. Compare the company’s exposure to guarantees with industry averages. b. Review risk mitigation practices, such as hedging and reinsurance strategies, used by competitors.
7. Visualize Results a. Create charts showing reserve adequacy under baseline and stress scenarios. b. Use heatmaps to highlight segments with the highest guarantee risk exposure.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing guarantee liabilities by product type and policy cohort.
- Stress test results showing financial impact under different scenarios.
- Charts comparing reserves vs. liabilities under baseline and stress conditions.
- Key findings report highlighting high-risk products and mitigation priorities.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Reserve shortfalls indicate insufficient coverage of guaranteed liabilities, which could pose solvency risks.
2. Products with higher sensitivity to market or policyholder behavior risks require immediate attention to improve pricing, reserve levels, or hedging strategies.
3. High exposure to guarantees compared to industry benchmarks may signal a need for more conservative risk management practices.
4. Stress test results reveal the company’s resilience to extreme market conditions and policyholder behavior shifts.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Adjust pricing for new products to better account for the cost of guarantees, ensuring sustainable profitability.
27
2. Strengthen reserve allocations for products with significant guarantee liabilities to reduce financial vulnerability.
3. Implement hedging strategies to offset market risks, such as low interest rates or volatile asset returns.
4. Optimize reinsurance agreements to transfer a portion of guarantee-related risks to third-party insurers.
5. Enhance customer engagement to reduce lapses, particularly among policies with high embedded guarantees.
6. Regularly review and update actuarial assumptions, including mortality rates and investment returns, to ensure accurate liability valuation.
7. Develop predictive models to monitor emerging risks in real-time and adjust reserve strategies proactively.
