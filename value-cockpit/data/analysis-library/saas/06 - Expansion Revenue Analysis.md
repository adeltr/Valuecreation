# 6 · Expansion Revenue Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Finance & Revenue]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Expansion Revenue Analysis is to measure the additional revenue generated from existing customers through upsells, cross-sells, or upgrades over a speciﬁc period. This analysis helps SaaS companies understand how effectively they are growing revenue within their current customer base, which can be a key driver of long-term growth and customer lifetime value (CLV).


## 📊 Data required
1. Total Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) or Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) from existing customers at the start of the period
2. Revenue added through upsells, cross-sells, or plan upgrades during the period
3. Number of customers who upgraded or expanded their services
4. **Optional:** Breakdowns by customer segment, product, or service


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Determine the period for analysis:** Choose a period for which you want to analyze expansion revenue, such as monthly, quarterly, or annually.
2. **Collect the total MRR or ARR from existing customers at the start of the period:** Gather data on the total revenue from existing customers at the beginning of the period, excluding any revenue from newly acquired customers during the period.
3. **Calculate the additional revenue from expansions:** Identify the revenue generated through customer upsells, cross-sells, or upgrades. This can include customers moving to higher-tier plans, purchasing additional features, or expanding their usage.
4. **Calculate the percentage of expansion revenue:** Use the following formula to calculate the percentage of expansion revenue relative to the total revenue from existing customers:
`Expansion Revenue (%) = (Revenue from Expansions / Revenue at Start of Period) x 100`
For example, if the starting MRR is $100,000 and expansions contribute an additional $15,000, the expansion revenue percentage would be:
`Expansion Revenue (%) = ($15,000 / $100,000) x 100 = 15%`

5. **Analyze expansion trends by customer segment (optional):** Break down expansion revenue by customer segment (e.g., small business, mid-market, enterprise) to see which segments are contributing the most to revenue growth. This helps target future upsell efforts more effectively.


## 📤 Output format
The output is typically presented as both the total amount of expansion revenue and its percentage relative to the existing customer base. For example:
Expansion Revenue: $15,000 Expansion Revenue Percentage: 15%


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   High Expansion Revenue: A high percentage of expansion revenue (e.g., above 10-20%) indicates that the company is successfully growing its revenue from existing customers. This is a strong sign of product value, customer satisfaction, and effective upsell/cross-sell strategies.
•   Low Expansion Revenue: A low percentage of expansion revenue suggests that the company may not be fully capitalizing on its existing customer base. This could indicate a lack of upsell opportunities, insufficient customer engagement, or limited product expansion paths.
Expansion revenue is a critical component of customer lifetime value (CLV) and can signiﬁcantly contribute to a SaaS company’s overall revenue growth without needing to rely on new customer acquisition.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Create targeted upsell campaigns:** Use customer segmentation and data to identify customers who are ready for an upgrade or could beneﬁt from additional features. Create personalized upsell campaigns that highlight the value of upgrading or expanding their service.
2. **OKer tiered pricing and premium features:** Design tiered pricing plans with clear value differences between levels. Offering premium features or advanced services at higher tiers encourages customers to upgrade to plans that better ﬁt their needs as they grow.
3. **Introduce usage-based pricing:** Implement a usage-based pricing model where customers pay more as they use more of the product or service. This encourages expansion as customers’ needs grow naturally.

4. **Engage with customer success teams:** Customer success teams should proactively engage with customers, helping them discover additional features and services that align with their needs. The goal is to demonstrate ongoing value and present opportunities for expansion.
5. **Track product usage and engagement:** Monitor how customers use the product and identify those who are using it heavily or nearing the limits of their current plan. These customers are prime candidates for upsells or plan expansions.
6. **Provide incentives for upgrades:** Offer time-sensitive discounts or special promotions to encourage customers to upgrade their plans. For example, providing a 10% discount on a higher-tier plan for customers who upgrade within the next 30 days.
7. **Improve product onboarding:** Ensure that new customers are fully aware of the product’s value and features during onboarding. Educating customers early on about premium features can help drive future expansions.
8. **Develop add-on services:** Continuously expand the product offering with new features, services, or integrations that customers can add to their existing plans. This gives customers more reasons to expand their subscriptions and drives more revenue from the existing customer base.
