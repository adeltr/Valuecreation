# 39 · Commodity Finance Structure and Risk Assessment

> **Part:** [[H - Specialized and Industry-Specific Solutions]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Commercial Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
Analyze the bank’s commodity finance operations to ensure well-structured deals, adequate risk controls, and alignment with market conditions.


## 📊 Data required
- Breakdown of commodity finance exposures by commodity type (e.g., energy, metals, agriculture)
- Transaction structures and financing instruments (e.g., pre-export finance, warehouse receipt financing, repo agreements)
- Pricing and hedging strategies (e.g., futures, swaps)
- Credit risk metrics (e.g., borrower rating, collateral valuation, default rates)
- Operational processes for collateral management and logistics tracking
- Market data (commodity price indices, volatility trends)
- Regulatory or internal guidelines for commodity finance


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Identify all outstanding commodity finance transactions, categorizing them by commodity type and financing structure (e.g., structured trade finance, producer finance). Document key terms such as tenor, pricing, and collateral arrangements.
Step 2: Collect data on hedging tools or risk mitigation measures in place, including the use of commodity derivatives (futures, options) or insurance products. Assess whether these align with the size and volatility of each exposure.
Step 3: Evaluate the collateral management process by reviewing collateral valuation reports, warehouse receipt processes, and inspection frequencies. Verify whether collateral values accurately reflect market changes and whether ownership is enforceable.
Step 4: Examine credit risk metrics—such as borrower ratings, default histories, and concentration limits—to gauge the bank’s exposure to individual commodities, counterparties, or regions. Identify any breaches of risk appetite or policy limits.
Step 5: Compare the bank’s pricing, fees, and deal structures to market benchmarks or peer data, noting whether the bank is adequately compensating for the associated commodity, credit, and operational risks.
83 Step 6: Summarize key findings in a report that highlights areas of strong risk management and potential vulnerabilities, along with recommended improvements (e.g., enhanced collateral monitoring, refined hedging strategies, more robust due diligence).


## 📤 Output format
- Tables listing transactions by commodity type, structure, and outstanding exposure
- Charts illustrating commodity price volatility, collateral values, and risk metrics over time
- Narrative summary outlining compliance with internal limits, identified risks, and proposed action plans


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Well-hedged positions and consistent collateral valuations typically indicate robust structures that shield the bank from commodity price swings.
- Excessive exposure in a single commodity or region may signal concentration risk and warrant diversification or stricter underwriting standards.
- Large discrepancies between deal pricing and market benchmarks could suggest opportunities to optimize margins or improve risk-based pricing.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Refine hedging strategies and adopt real-time price monitoring to better manage commodity price fluctuations.
2. Strengthen collateral management practices through more frequent inspections, third-party verification, or updated valuation models.
3. Diversify the commodity finance portfolio to reduce concentration risk, exploring a broader range of commodity classes and geographies.
4. Review credit policies to ensure borrower and transaction eligibility criteria match the bank’s current risk appetite and market dynamics.
5. Enhance analytics and reporting tools that provide ongoing visibility into commodity exposures, hedging effectiveness, and policy compliance.
