# 28 · Product Substitution and Competitive Threats Analysis

> **Part:** [[D - Sales & Marketing]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Chemical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to assess the risk of product substitution and identify competitive threats in the market. By understanding potential substitutes and emerging competitors, the company can develop strategies to protect market share, adapt to industry shifts, and strengthen its competitive position.


## 📊 Data required
1. List of current products and their primary uses/applications
2. Alternative products and substitutes available in the market
3. Competitor data, including new entrants, pricing, market share, and product innovations
4. Customer feedback and preferences regarding potential substitutes
5. Industry trends influencing substitution risks (e.g., technology, regulatory changes, sustainability)
6. Cost and performance comparison between the company’s products and substitutes
7. Industry benchmarks and competitor analysis reports


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify Potential Product Substitutes in the Market Research existing and emerging products that could serve as substitutes for the company’s offerings. Evaluate substitutes based on functionality, performance, price, and availability. Focus on alternatives that customers might consider in place of the company’s products.
2. Assess the Likelihood and Impact of Substitution Evaluate the risk of substitution for each product based on customer preferences, pricing, and industry trends. Use customer feedback, surveys, or sales data to determine how likely customers are to switch to substitutes and the impact substitution would have on revenue.
3. Analyze Key Competitors and Their Offerings Identify competitors offering similar products, focusing on their market share, pricing, and unique selling points. Include new entrants or emerging companies that may disrupt the market with innovative products or lower prices. This competitive mapping will help identify where the company’s products are most vulnerable.
4. Evaluate Cost and Performance of Substitutes vs. Company Products Compare the company’s products to substitutes in terms of cost, quality, and performance. Assess whether substitutes offer a cost advantage, additional features, or performance benefits. Products with higher costs or lower performance relative to substitutes may face higher substitution risk.
5. Review Industry Trends Impacting Substitution Examine industry trends that may influence substitution risks, such as technological advances, shifts toward sustainable products, or regulatory changes. For example, How to Analyze a Chemicals Company increased demand for eco-friendly materials may favor substitutes with lower environmental impact.
6. Benchmark Against Competitors’ Strategic Responses Review how competitors are addressing substitution risks, such as by introducing differentiated products, adjusting pricing, or adopting new technologies. Benchmarking helps identify potential strategies the company could adopt to maintain a competitive edge.


## 📤 Output format
1. List of potential substitutes and risk level for each product
2. Competitive landscape analysis, including key competitors and their competitive advantages
3. Cost and performance comparison between company products and substitutes
4. Summary of industry trends impacting substitution risks
5. Benchmark comparison of strategic responses to substitution threats


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High substitution risk with viable alternatives:** Indicates a need to enhance product differentiation, improve value propositions, or adjust pricing to retain customers.
- **Strong competitive advantages in performance or cost:** Suggests that the company’s products are well-positioned against substitutes, reducing immediate threats.
- **Industry trends favoring substitutes:** Signals a need to adapt product offerings to align with market shifts, particularly if substitutes address emerging customer preferences.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Enhance Product Differentiation:** Invest in product features, quality, or performance enhancements that make it harder for substitutes to compete directly.
2. **Develop a Customer Retention Strategy:** Strengthen customer relationships through loyalty programs, service enhancements, and tailored solutions to reduce the appeal of substitutes.
3. **Adjust Pricing or Cost Structures:** If substitutes offer a price advantage, explore ways to reduce costs, optimize pricing, or offer volume discounts to stay competitive.
4. **Monitor and Adapt to Industry Trends:** Regularly review industry trends, such as sustainability or regulatory shifts, and adjust product development strategies to preemptively address these changes.
5. **Expand into Complementary Markets:** Consider developing complementary products that integrate with current offerings, providing added value and reducing the likelihood of substitution.
