# 26 · Lifecycle Management for Legacy Devices

> **Part:** [[D - Product Development & Lifecycle]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Medical Device Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Ensure that older or established medical devices remain compliant, competitive, and profitable. This includes updating technical documentation, addressing regulatory changes, evaluating market demand, and planning upgrades, extensions, or phase-outs as needed.


## 📊 Data required
- Current device history file (DHF), including design documentation and manufacturing processes
- Post-market surveillance (PMS) data (complaints, adverse events, recalls)
- Sales and market share trends for the legacy device
- Regulatory requirements (for example, new standards like EU MDR/IVDR, updates to ISO 13485)
- Cost structure and profit margins for the device
- Competitor products and emerging technologies
- Customer feedback and clinical performance data


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Compile comprehensive device documentation. Gather all relevant technical files, risk management records, labeling, and quality system documents.
2. Evaluate regulatory compliance status. Check if the device meets current regulations (for example, FDA updates, EU MDR). Identify gaps in documentation, labeling, or post-market requirements.
3. Analyze market performance. Review sales data, competitive positioning, and customer feedback. Determine whether the device is maintaining, losing, or gaining market share.
4. Assess technological relevance. Compare the device’s features and technology to newer options on the market. Evaluate if upgrades or design changes are needed to stay competitive.
5. Review cost structure and profitability. Determine if manufacturing, materials, or maintenance costs have become unsustainable relative to device pricing or reimbursement levels.
6. Identify opportunities for enhancements or line extensions. Explore minor updates (for example, software upgrades) or potential expansions to address new clinical indications.

7. Consider discontinuation or replacement strategies. If the device is no longer viable, plan an orderly phase-out, including notifications, service support, and potential migration paths for existing customers.
8. Summarize findings and develop a lifecycle plan. Outline actions for regulatory updates, product improvements, or market exit, prioritizing based on risk, cost, and strategic alignment.


## 📤 Output format
- A written lifecycle management plan that outlines regulatory obligations, product refresh opportunities, or discontinuation tactics
- A gap analysis table showing compliance shortfalls, market needs, and potential design improvements
- An updated risk management file reflecting any changes in intended use, technology, or market demands
- An executive summary with final recommendations and a high-level timeline


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A legacy device with strong market demand and compliance can benefit from incremental improvements, prolonging its lifecycle and maintaining revenue streams.
- Devices facing regulatory or market obstacles may require significant redesign or strategic repositioning to remain viable.
- Poor profitability, high maintenance costs, or limited growth prospects may indicate that discontinuation or replacement is the most prudent course.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
- Regularly re-assess legacy devices against new regulations and standards to avoid compliance lapses or costly redesigns.
- Implement a proactive product monitoring strategy to capture real-world usage data and identify opportunities for incremental improvements.
- Cultivate strong customer relationships, gathering feedback on desired upgrades or unresolved clinical needs.
- Explore strategic partnerships or acquisitions to bring new technologies into older product lines.
- Maintain a clear roadmap that includes critical milestones for updating or phasing out products, ensuring clear communication with regulators and customers.
