# 26 · Asset and Liability Management

> **Part:** [[D - Capital, Liquidity, and Funding]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Commercial Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
Manage the bank’s balance sheet structure to optimize liquidity, interest rate risk, and profitability while meeting regulatory and strategic objectives.


## 📊 Data required
- Balance sheet composition (loans, deposits, investments, wholesale funding)
- Maturity profiles and repricing schedules for assets and liabilities
- Interest rates on both sides of the balance sheet
- Forecasted cash flows, including loan repayments and deposit withdrawals
- Market data (interest rate curves, economic indicators)
- Regulatory requirements or internal limits related to interest rate risk, liquidity, and capital


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Identify and catalog all major balance sheet items, classifying them by type (e.g., loans, deposits, securities, wholesale funding) and noting their interest rates and maturity or repricing schedules.
Step 2: Create a maturity gap or repricing gap analysis by aligning assets and liabilities according to the time buckets in which they either mature or reprice, helping to visualize potential mismatches.
Step 3: Calculate key metrics such as the Net Interest Income (NII) at Risk and Economic Value of Equity (EVE) under different interest rate scenarios, using appropriate modeling techniques (e.g., duration analysis, simulation models).
Step 4: Assess liquidity needs by examining cash flow projections, focusing on potential inflows (e.g., loan repayments) and outflows (e.g., deposit withdrawals), and compare to the bank’s stock of liquid assets.
Step 5: Compare the identified gaps and model results to the bank’s internal policy limits and regulatory requirements, noting any breaches or areas that require closer monitoring.
57 Step 6: Document and communicate findings, including recommended strategies such as adjusting product pricing, hedging interest rate risk, or diversifying funding sources, to senior management and the board for decision-making.


## 📤 Output format
- Gap analysis tables or charts outlining repricing mismatches across various time buckets
- Scenario-based outcomes for NII, EVE, or other relevant ALM metrics
- Written summary highlighting potential vulnerabilities, policy breaches, and recommended actions


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Large positive or negative gaps in specific time buckets indicate exposure to interest rate changes, requiring hedging or asset-liability adjustments.
- Liquidity shortfalls discovered during cash flow projections can signal the need for additional funding lines or more robust deposit-gathering strategies.
- A stable balance sheet with limited mismatches and strong liquidity typically points to efficient ALM practices that sustain profitability and regulatory compliance.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Use hedging instruments (e.g., interest rate swaps) to reduce exposure to adverse rate movements.
2. Diversify funding sources to include a balanced mix of retail deposits, wholesale funding, and long-term debt.
3. Implement dynamic pricing strategies for loans and deposits based on current and projected interest rates.
4. Regularly update and stress test ALM models to capture emerging risks in market conditions or regulatory environments.
5. Integrate ALM considerations into strategic planning, ensuring that product growth initiatives align with the desired balance sheet structure.
