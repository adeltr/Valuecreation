# 8 · Revenue Concentration

> **Part:** [[A - Finance & Revenue]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Revenue Concentration analysis is to determine how dependent a SaaS company is on its top customers for revenue generation. This analysis helps assess the risk of revenue loss if key customers churn and identiﬁes opportunities to diversify the customer base or increase revenue from smaller customers. High revenue concentration suggests that the company’s ﬁnancial health is heavily reliant on a few customers, while low concentration indicates a more balanced and stable revenue stream.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Total revenue:** The total revenue generated by the company during a speciﬁc period (e.g., monthly or annually).
2. **Revenue per customer:** The revenue generated from each individual customer or account during the same period.
3. **Top customers:** The number or percentage of customers contributing the most revenue (e.g., top 5, top 10, or top 20 customers).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate the total revenue for the period:
o    Gather the total revenue generated by the company for the time period under analysis (e.g., a month, quarter, or year).
2. Identify the revenue generated by top customers:
o    Sort the list of customers by the amount of revenue they contribute, from highest to lowest. Identify the top customers, either by percentage (e.g., top 5% of customers) or by a speciﬁc number (e.g., top 10 customers).
3. Calculate the percentage of total revenue generated by top customers:
o    For each selected top customer group (e.g., top 5, top 10, top 20 customers), calculate their contribution to total revenue using the formula:
`Revenue Concentration (%) = (Revenue from Top Customers / Total Revenue) x 100`
o    For example, if the top 10 customers generated $1 million in revenue and total company revenue is $5 million:
`Revenue Concentration = ($1M / $5M) x 100 = 20%`
4. Calculate the cumulative revenue concentration (optional):
25 o    Create a cumulative revenue concentration chart that shows the percentage of total revenue contributed by different groups of customers (e.g., top 5%, top 10%, top 20%, etc.). This provides a visual representation of revenue dependency.


## 📤 Output format
The output is typically presented as the Revenue Concentration Percentage for key customer groups, such as the top 5, 10, or 20 customers, along with supporting metrics like total revenue and revenue per customer.
Example output:
•   Total Revenue: $5 million •   Revenue Concentration:
o    Top 5 Customers: 15% of total revenue o    Top 10 Customers: 25% of total revenue o    Top 20 Customers: 40% of total revenue This format allows easy comparison of revenue concentration and highlights the company’s reliance on its top customers.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   High revenue concentration: If a large portion of total revenue comes from a small number of customers, this indicates high revenue concentration. This can be risky, as losing one or more key customers would signiﬁcantly impact the company’s ﬁnancial stability. High concentration may be acceptable in enterprise-focused SaaS companies but requires careful monitoring.
•   Low revenue concentration: Low concentration suggests a more balanced revenue distribution across customers, reducing the risk of signiﬁcant revenue loss from customer churn. This scenario indicates a healthy and diversiﬁed customer base and implies greater ﬁnancial stability.
•   Cumulative concentration patterns: A steep cumulative revenue concentration curve, where the top 5% or 10% of customers account for a signiﬁcant portion of revenue, highlights a potential over-reliance on a few key accounts.

## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Diversify the customer base:** Actively work on acquiring more customers in smaller segments or industries to reduce dependency on a few key clients. A more diversiﬁed customer base spreads risk and increases revenue stability.
2. **Increase revenue from smaller customers:** Implement strategies to upsell or cross-sell to smaller or mid-tier customers, gradually increasing their revenue contribution. This could involve introducing new features, expanding pricing tiers, or offering premium support packages.
3. **Monitor key customer health closely:** For companies with high revenue concentration, it is essential to regularly monitor customer satisfaction and engagement levels of top accounts to reduce the risk of churn. Establish strong customer success teams and maintain close relationships with key clients.
4. **Develop long-term contracts or subscription commitments:** Lock in high-value customers with long-term contracts or annual billing to secure recurring revenue from these accounts. Offering discounts for longer commitments can also provide more revenue predictability.
5. **Expand into new market segments:** Explore new customer segments or geographies to further diversify revenue streams and reduce reliance on speciﬁc markets or industries. This will spread out revenue risk across a wider base of customers.
6. **OKer tiered pricing models:** Introduce more pricing plans or features aimed at different customer sizes or needs. This helps increase adoption across smaller customer bases and reduces reliance on larger customers for revenue.
