# 5 · Closed Landfill Redevelopment Feasibility

> **Part:** [[A - Landfill Management & Operations]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Waste Management Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To determine the viability of repurposing a closed landfill site for alternative uses (e.g., solar farm, recreational space, commercial development) by assessing technical, financial, environmental, and regulatory factors.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Site-specific data (location, size, topography, soil stability)
- ​ Historical landfill records (closure date, cap design, monitoring data)
- ​ Environmental monitoring results (gas emissions, groundwater quality)
- ​ Local zoning and land-use regulations
- ​ Cost estimates for site remediation or infrastructure improvements
- ​ Market demand or community interest in proposed redevelopment uses
- ​ Potential revenue sources (e.g., lease income from solar projects, park concessions)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Assess site conditions and regulatory constraints​ Review closure documentation and post-closure monitoring reports to confirm regulatory compliance, evaluate cap integrity, and identify any ongoing environmental liabilities (e.g., gas venting, groundwater monitoring).
2. Identify potential redevelopment options​ Evaluate various reuse scenarios based on community needs, market demand, and site feasibility (e.g., solar farm, park, commercial structure). Ensure each option meets applicable zoning requirements and does not compromise the landfill cap.
3. Estimate redevelopment costs​ Compile anticipated expenditures for site preparation, infrastructure improvements, permitting, and ongoing maintenance.
4. Develop revenue or benefit projections​ If applicable, forecast potential revenue from the chosen redevelopment option (e.g., power purchase agreements for solar, user fees for recreational facilities).
5. Perform financial feasibility analysis​ Calculate the Net Present Value (NPV) or Return on Investment (ROI) to determine if the project will generate sufficient returns.

6. Evaluate environmental and community impacts​ Consider any additional pollution control measures or environmental monitoring needed, and assess stakeholder acceptance or support for the proposed redevelopment.
7. Finalize feasibility report​ Summarize findings, highlight key risks, and outline recommendations. Include contingency plans for addressing potential cap maintenance or regulatory changes.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ A feasibility study report detailing site conditions, proposed reuse options, and associated costs
- ​ Financial models (e.g., spreadsheets) showing NPV, ROI, or payback period for each redevelopment scenario
- ​ Risk assessment matrix identifying environmental, financial, and regulatory challenges
- ​ Conceptual site plans or renderings illustrating potential redevelopment designs


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ A positive NPV or favorable ROI typically indicates financial viability.
- ​ Significant risks or high remediation costs may warrant a less intensive redevelopment option or require external funding.
- ​ Strong community support can enhance project success, whereas local opposition may delay approvals or escalate costs.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Conduct thorough environmental due diligence to minimize unforeseen remediation expenses. 2.​ Engage with community stakeholders early to build support and gather feedback on preferred site uses. 3.​ Explore public-private partnerships or grants to offset redevelopment costs. 4.​ Invest in ongoing site monitoring and maintenance to preserve cap integrity and ensure long-term project sustainability. 5.​ Diversify redevelopment options (e.g., mixed-use concepts) to spread risk and maximize potential revenue.
