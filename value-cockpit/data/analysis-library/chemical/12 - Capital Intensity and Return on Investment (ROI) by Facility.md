# 12 · Capital Intensity and Return on Investment (ROI) by Facility

> **Part:** [[B - Finance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Chemical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to evaluate the capital intensity of each production facility and measure its return on investment (ROI). This provides insights into how effectively capital is being utilized at each location, identifying high-performing facilities as well as those that may benefit from improvements or investment adjustments.


## 📊 Data required
1.   Total capital investment for each facility (initial and ongoing investments)
2.   Annual operating costs per facility
3.   Annual revenue generated by each facility
4.   Depreciation and maintenance costs
5.   Production output (in tons or units) by facility
6.   Industry benchmarks for capital intensity and ROI


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Capital Intensity for Each Facility:**
`Capital Intensity = Total Capital Investment / Annual Production Output`
For example, if a facility has a capital investment of $10 million and produces 50,000 tons per year:
`Capital Intensity = 10,000,000 / 50,000 = $200 per ton`
This metric reflects the amount of capital required per unit of output, indicating how capital-intensive the facility is.
2. **Calculate Return on Investment (ROI) for Each Facility:** ROI (%) = [(Annual Revenue - Operating Costs - Depreciation) / Total Capital Investment] x 100 For instance, if a facility generates $5 million in revenue, incurs $3 million in operating costs, and $500,000 in depreciation with a capital investment of $10 million:
`ROI = [(5,000,000 - 3,000,000 - 500,000) / 10,000,000] x 100 = 15%`
3. **Analyze Operating Efficiency and Maintenance Costs:** Calculate operating costs per unit of production to assess the facility's operating efficiency.
`Operating Cost per Unit = Annual Operating Costs / Annual Production Output`

4. **Compare Against Industry Benchmarks:** Compare each facility’s capital intensity and ROI to industry benchmarks. Facilities with higher-than-average capital intensity and lower-than-average ROI may be candidates for process optimization, cost reduction, or capital reallocation.
5. **Assess Facility Performance Over Time:** Track the ROI and capital intensity metrics for each facility over multiple periods. This helps identify trends, showing whether capital utilization is improving or declining, and aids in understanding the impact of capital investments over time.


## 📤 Output format
1. Table of Capital Intensity and ROI by Facility
2. Operating Cost per Unit metric by facility
3. Benchmark comparison for capital intensity and ROI across similar facilities or industry standards
4. Trend analysis chart for ROI and capital intensity over time


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Low capital intensity with high ROI:** Indicates a facility that is highly efficient in capital usage, with strong returns on invested capital.
- **High capital intensity with low ROI:** Suggests a facility that may be overcapitalized or underperforming, pointing to potential inefficiencies or excess capital that is not yielding high returns.
- **Comparison to benchmarks:** Facilities with capital intensity and ROI metrics significantly different from industry standards may need investment re-evaluation or operational improvements.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize Capital Allocation:** Reallocate capital to higher-performing facilities with strong ROI, or reduce investment in underperforming facilities.
2. **Implement Cost-Saving Measures:** Identify and reduce high operating or maintenance costs to improve ROI without additional capital expenditure.
3. **Upgrade Equipment and Processes:** Invest in technology upgrades or process improvements that lower capital intensity by increasing production output or reducing operating costs.
4. **Divest or Repurpose Underperforming Assets:** For facilities with persistently low ROI, consider divestiture or repurposing of assets to maximize capital efficiency.
