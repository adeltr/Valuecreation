# 22 · Claims Frequency & Severity Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Claims & Loss Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Measure how often claims occur (frequency) and how costly they are on average (severity), in order to identify trends, evaluate risk, and guide pricing and underwriting decisions.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Historical claims data (dates, causes, incurred amounts)
- ​ Exposure and premium data (written premium, earned premium, policy counts)
- ​ Claim payment details (reserves, paid amounts, allocated loss adjustment expenses)
- ​ External data on inflation, repair costs, or legal environment affecting claims
- ​ Demographic and vehicle-specific details for segmentation (age, vehicle model, location)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Collect historical claims data spanning at least 3–5 years to ensure a robust sample. Make sure to include both paid and reserved losses. 2.​ Calculate claim frequency by using Frequency = (Number of Claims / Number of Exposures) x 100. Break down by relevant segments such as driver age, coverage type, or region. 3.​ Compute claim severity by using Severity = (Total Incurred Losses / Number of Claims). Compare severity across segments to spot unusually high-cost categories. 4.​ Examine trends over time by plotting frequency and severity for each segment year-over-year. Identify external factors (e.g., inflation, parts costs) that might drive fluctuations. 5.​ Perform segmentation analysis to isolate factors contributing to higher frequency or severity. Use driver attributes, vehicle profiles, and location data to refine the analysis. 6.​ Assess impact of coverage limits and deductibles by examining how claim outcomes vary across different policy structures. Note whether lower deductibles lead to higher frequency of small claims.
49 7.​ Combine results with external data like economic indicators, weather events, or legal changes. Determine how these external factors correlate with shifts in claim patterns. 8.​ Document findings and propose data-driven changes to underwriting, pricing, or policy design. Present the summarized results to cross-functional teams for decision-making.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ Tables and charts illustrating claim frequency and severity across time and segments
- ​ Written summary highlighting key trends and correlations
- ​ Segmented reports detailing high-risk vs. low-risk categories with recommendations for pricing or underwriting adjustments


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ Higher frequency typically signals more frequent but possibly smaller claims, which may require changes in underwriting guidelines or coverage terms.
- ​ Increasing severity points to costlier claims, often driven by external factors like repair costs or litigation trends, necessitating premium adjustments or stricter coverage criteria.
- ​ Notable spikes in certain segments may indicate unprofitable niches or emerging risks that require targeted interventions.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Implement predictive analytics to identify early indicators of rising claim frequency or severity. 2.​ Adjust underwriting criteria or pricing models to reflect identified risk factors more accurately. 3.​ Work with preferred repair networks or negotiate favorable rates to manage repair costs and reduce severity. 4.​ Continuously monitor external factors (economic, legal, technological) and update strategies accordingly.
