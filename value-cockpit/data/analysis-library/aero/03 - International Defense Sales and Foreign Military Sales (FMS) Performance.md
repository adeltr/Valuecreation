# 3 · International Defense Sales and Foreign Military Sales (FMS) Performance

> **Part:** [[A - Strategy & Market Position]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Aerospace & Defense Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the International Defense Sales and Foreign Military Sales (FMS) Performance analysis is to evaluate a company’s effectiveness in securing and executing international defense sales contracts, particularly through government-led FMS programs. This analysis helps identify strengths, weaknesses, and opportunities in the company’s international sales efforts, focusing on compliance with FMS regulations, contract performance, and alignment with global defense needs.


## 📊 Data required
- List of international defense contracts and FMS agreements, including values, clients, and timelines
- Breakdown of FMS contracts by country/region
- Contract performance data (on-time delivery, cost overruns, penalties)
- FMS-specific compliance data (export licenses, ITAR compliance, FMS procedures)
- Historical trends in international defense sales (number of contracts won, contract values, regions)
- Competitor performance in international defense markets (if available)
- Data on foreign defense budgets and priorities
- Client satisfaction metrics or feedback (if applicable)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Gather international sales data:** Compile all relevant data on international defense sales, including both commercial and FMS contracts. Ensure that the data includes contract values, timelines, and key performance indicators (KPIs) like delivery time and cost management.
2. **Segment by FMS and non-FMS contracts:** Separate contracts obtained through the FMS program from direct commercial sales to foreign governments. This distinction is critical as FMS contracts have unique regulatory requirements and often involve more complex government oversight.
3. **Evaluate FMS compliance performance:** Assess the company’s compliance with FMS-specific requirements, such as obtaining export licenses (e.g., ITAR), adherence to U.S. government regulations, and coordination with U.S. defense agencies (e.g., DSCA). Look for delays or penalties that may have occurred due to compliance failures.
4. **Analyze contract performance metrics:** For each contract, evaluate performance indicators such as delivery timelines, cost control, and penalties incurred. Pay special attention to the on-time delivery rate, as delayed deliveries in FMS contracts can strain relationships with both the U.S. government and foreign buyers.
5. **Review international defense sales growth trends:** Analyze historical sales trends to identify patterns in international defense markets. Break down the data by region to assess which regions have shown growth and which have seen declines in contract wins or values.
6. **Benchmark against competitors:** If available, compare the company’s performance in securing international defense contracts to its main competitors. Focus on regions where competitors may be outperforming, and identify reasons for potential market share loss.
7. **Examine foreign defense budget alignment:** Review foreign defense spending trends and compare them to the company’s contract portfolio. This helps to assess whether the company is pursuing contracts in regions where defense spending is growing or stagnating.
8. **Evaluate client satisfaction (if available):** If client satisfaction data is available, include this in the analysis. High satisfaction can indicate strong performance and opportunities for follow-on contracts, while low satisfaction may point to issues that need to be addressed.


## 📤 Output format
- The output should include the following sections:
- Summary of total international sales, broken down by FMS and non-FMS contracts
- Performance report card for key contracts, showing metrics such as on-time delivery and cost performance
- Graphical representation of regional sales distribution and growth trends (bar charts, pie charts)
- FMS compliance report, showing adherence to regulatory requirements and any penalties incurred
- Competitor comparison table (if data available)
- Foreign defense spending trends by region, overlaid with company performance


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Strong international performance:** If the company shows consistent growth in international sales, especially in regions with increasing defense budgets, it suggests that the company is well-positioned to capitalize on global defense opportunities.
- **FMS compliance issues:** If the company has recurring compliance issues with FMS regulations, this could indicate operational inefficiencies or insufficient alignment with U.S. government requirements. This is a critical area to address, as non-compliance can lead to penalties or loss of future contracts.
- **Underperformance in key markets:** Low sales growth in regions where defense spending is increasing may indicate a strategic misalignment. The company may need to reconsider its approach to market entry or sales execution in these areas.
- **Delayed deliveries and cost overruns:** Poor contract performance, especially in terms of delivery delays or cost overruns, can harm relationships with both the U.S. government and foreign buyers, reducing future contract opportunities.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
- **Strengthen FMS compliance:** Implement more robust internal controls and training programs to ensure full compliance with FMS and ITAR regulations. This can reduce delays and penalties associated with non-compliance.
- **Improve contract performance:** Focus on enhancing project management capabilities to reduce cost overruns and ensure on-time delivery of contracts. This is especially important for international clients, where delays can strain diplomatic relationships.
- **Expand market presence:** Identify regions with increasing defense budgets and assess potential for expanding sales efforts. This may include hiring regional experts, enhancing local partnerships, or bidding for more diversified contracts.
- **Enhance government relationships:** Strengthen relationships with U.S. and foreign defense agencies to increase the company’s standing in FMS procurements. Demonstrating a strong track record in previous contracts can help secure more opportunities.
- **Competitor benchmarking:** Regularly review competitor performance in international defense markets and adjust strategy to stay competitive, whether by improving bid quality, pricing strategies, or technological offerings.
