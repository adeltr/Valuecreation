# 1 · Network Footprint & Capacity Utilization Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Transportation & Network Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Third Party Logistics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate the geographic spread of the 3PL’s logistics network and determine how effectively the available capacity (warehouses, trucks, trailers, and other assets) is being utilized to serve customer demand.


## 📊 Data required
- List of all facilities (warehouses, cross-docks, distribution centers) and their locations
- Transportation asset details (number of trucks, trailers, and their capacities)
- Historical shipment data (origin-destination pairs, shipment volumes, load factors)
- Facility throughput data (inbound/outbound volumes)
- Forecasted demand by region or major route


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Map the Network:** Identify and map all existing facilities and major transport lanes on a geographic chart. Note each facility’s operational capacity (total storage space, handling capability).
2. **Capture Shipment and Volume Data:** Aggregate historical shipment volumes, including average daily volume, peak volumes, and seasonal fluctuations, for each facility and transport lane.
3. **Calculate Capacity Utilization:** For each facility or transport asset, compute utilization rates. Example formula for facility utilization: Facility Utilization = (Total Volume Processed / Facility Throughput Capacity) x 100 Similarly, for transport assets, evaluate load factors by dividing actual cargo volume by maximum available capacity.
4. **Identify Gaps and Constraints:** Compare current throughput and volume data with existing capacity. Highlight facilities or routes operating at or near capacity limits and those underutilized.
5. **Conduct Geographical Coverage Gap Analysis:** Determine if the network footprint adequately covers major demand centers. Look for potential coverage blind spots or excessive overlap between facilities.
6
6. **Incorporate Forecast Demand:** Layer forecasted shipment data over the current state to evaluate future capacity needs and identify potential areas of network expansion or consolidation.
7. **Summarize Findings and Prioritize Opportunities:** Rank key pressure points where expansion or reconfiguration is most urgent. Provide recommendations for realignment or optimization.


## 📤 Output format
- A geographical map showing each facility and its utilization rate
- A tabular report listing each facility’s capacity, throughput, and utilization percentage
- A summary page highlighting high-utilization facilities and underutilized facilities
- A capacity vs. demand forecast chart for major routes


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High utilization (above 80-90 percent):** Indicates potential constraints or risks of bottlenecks, requiring capacity expansion or alternate routing.
- **Moderate utilization (50-80 percent):** Likely operating with adequate capacity, but should be monitored for seasonal or unexpected demand spikes.
- **Low utilization (below 50 percent):** May signal overcapacity, excess fixed costs, or an opportunity to redeploy or repurpose assets.
- **Network coverage:** Gaps in geographic coverage may indicate missed opportunities to serve new or existing customers or higher transport costs due to longer hauls.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Balance the Network:** Shift volume to underutilized facilities or routes to ease pressure on high-utilization sites.
2. **Optimize Capacity:** Adjust warehouse layouts, invest in flexible storage systems, or leverage shared-use capacity to handle peak periods.
3. **Employ Network Modeling:** Use optimization tools to evaluate scenarios for facility location changes or transport lane restructuring.
4. **Expand or Consolidate Strategically:** Add new facilities in high-demand areas or consolidate overlapping sites to reduce costs and improve service.
5. **Establish Collaborative Partnerships:** Collaborate with partners or shippers to combine volumes, improving utilization across shared routes and facilities.
