# 13 · Renewal Rate Analysis by Product Line

> **Part:** [[A - Underwriting]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Renewal Rate Analysis by Product Line is to assess customer retention across different insurance product lines, allowing the company to understand which products maintain strong customer loyalty and which may face retention challenges. High renewal rates indicate customer satisfaction and product fit, while low rates may signal issues in pricing, service, or competition.


## 📊 Data required
1. Total number of policies eligible for renewal in each product line
2. Number of policies renewed in each product line
3. Segment data for additional insights (e.g., customer demographics, policy age)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the Period for Analysis:** Select the time period for analysis (e.g., quarterly, annually) to ensure consistency.
2. **Calculate the Renewal Rate by Product Line:** Use the formula:
`Renewal Rate = (Number of Policies Renewed / Number of Policies Eligible for Renewal) x 100`
Calculate this rate for each product line separately to understand product-specific renewal performance.
3. Segment Data for Deeper Insights (Optional)
If available, further segment renewal rates by demographics, policy age, or region within each product line to identify trends among specific customer groups.
4. **Track Renewal Rates Over Multiple Periods:** Calculate renewal rates over multiple periods to observe trends and detect any changes in renewal behavior.
5. **Compile Results:** Summarize the results in a table or chart format to display renewal rates by product line, allowing for easy comparison across products and over time.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Product Line (e.g., auto insurance, homeowners insurance)
- Number of Policies Eligible for Renewal
- Number of Policies Renewed
- Renewal Rate (%)
Additionally, a line chart can visualize renewal trends over time, and a bar chart can display renewal rates by product line for quick comparison.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Renewal Rate:** Indicates strong customer loyalty and satisfaction within that product line, likely due to competitive pricing, favorable product features, or positive service experiences.
- **Low Renewal Rate:** May suggest customer dissatisfaction, competitive pricing pressures, or lack of product fit. Low rates in a product line require further investigation to identify root causes.
Comparing renewal rates to industry benchmarks or historical company averages can provide context and reveal if rates are within an expected range.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Enhance Customer Engagement Programs Develop targeted renewal campaigns and proactive communication strategies to encourage renewals, especially before the policy expiry date.
2. Review Pricing Competitiveness Analyze pricing strategies to ensure that premium rates remain competitive in the market, particularly for products with low renewal rates.
3. Offer Loyalty Incentives Introduce loyalty programs, discounts, or other incentives for long-term customers to boost renewal rates.
4. Address Common Reasons for Non-Renewal Conduct customer surveys or analyze customer feedback to understand why policies are not renewed. Adjust services, products, or pricing based on insights.
5. Segment-Specific Renewal Initiatives Focus on tailored renewal strategies within customer segments facing the greatest retention challenges, offering personalized communications or discounts.
6. Regularly Monitor Renewal Trends Continuously track renewal rates by product line to detect any shifts early and adapt strategies promptly to maintain high retention.
