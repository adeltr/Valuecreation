# 16 · Stress Testing and Scenario Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Risk Management and Compliance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Commercial Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine how the bank’s balance sheet and capital position would withstand adverse economic or financial conditions to ensure adequate risk mitigation strategies.


## 📊 Data required
- Current balance sheet data (loans, deposits, investments)
- Historical performance metrics (e.g., default rates, loss rates)
- Macroeconomic variables and forecasts (GDP growth, interest rates, unemployment rates)
- Internal risk models and assumptions (probability of default, loss given default)
- Regulatory guidelines or stress testing frameworks (e.g., Basel requirements)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Define the scope of stress tests by selecting the portfolios or business units to be evaluated, considering their materiality and risk significance.
Step 2: Establish baseline and adverse scenarios based on macroeconomic indicators, drawing from historical data or forward-looking economic forecasts; ensure each scenario captures relevant risk factors (e.g., interest rate shocks, market downturns).
Step 3: Map each scenario to the bank’s risk parameters and models, such as expected default frequencies or loss given default, to estimate the impact on asset quality, liquidity, and capital.
Step 4: Apply the scenarios to the bank’s balance sheet and loan portfolios, calculating potential changes in key metrics like non-performing loans, net interest income, and capital adequacy ratios.
Step 5: Summarize the results of each scenario, highlighting any significant deterioration in capital, liquidity, or profitability measures, and compare with regulatory or internal thresholds to determine if additional capital buffers or risk mitigations are needed.
Step 6: Communicate findings to senior management and the board, proposing contingency plans, such as de-risking strategies or capital strengthening measures, to address vulnerabilities identified in the stress tests.

## 📤 Output format
- Scenario-by-scenario tables summarizing impact on key risk metrics (capital ratios, liquidity coverage, default rates)
- Graphs illustrating projected losses or capital depletion under various stress scenarios
- Narrative report detailing methodology, assumptions, and recommended actions


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A scenario leading to capital or liquidity ratios falling below regulatory minimums indicates the bank may need to adjust its risk profile or raise additional capital.
- Consistent performance across multiple stress scenarios suggests robust risk management and resilient capital positioning.
- Large variability in outcomes under different scenarios may signal concentration risks or overexposure to certain macroeconomic factors.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Refine scenario designs to capture emerging risks, including geopolitical events or climate-related factors.
2. Enhance model calibration by incorporating more granular loan-level data and updated macroeconomic assumptions.
3. Integrate stress testing into strategic planning, aligning potential outcomes with business decisions (e.g., credit growth targets).
4. Conduct regular back-testing and model validation to confirm the reliability of stress test projections.
5. Strengthen governance around stress testing by establishing clear accountability and documentation for methodology, assumptions, and results.
