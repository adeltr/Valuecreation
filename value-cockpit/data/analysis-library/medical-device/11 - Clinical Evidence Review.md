# 11 · Clinical Evidence Review

> **Part:** [[B - Clinical & Post-Market]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Medical Device Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate the clinical data supporting the safety and efficacy of a medical device. Determine whether existing evidence meets regulatory requirements, identify gaps that require additional studies or data, and provide recommendations to strengthen clinical documentation.


## 📊 Data required
- Clinical study protocols and reports (for example, feasibility, pivotal, post-market studies)
- Ethical approvals, informed consent forms, and institutional review board (IRB) documentation
- Study design details (endpoints, inclusion/exclusion criteria, statistical methods)
- Patient population demographics and baseline characteristics
- Adverse event and complication rates
- Published literature or real-world evidence supporting device use
- Device risk-benefit profile and competitor benchmarking data


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify the scope and regulatory context of the clinical evidence. Determine the type of submission or claim (for example, 510(k), PMA, CE Mark) and define the level of clinical proof needed.
2. Collect and organize all relevant clinical documentation. Gather protocols, raw data, summary reports, and any post-market studies or real-world evidence.
3. Evaluate study design and methodology. Check if the clinical study design aligns with recognized standards (for example, randomized controlled trials, observational studies) and if endpoints are appropriately defined.
4. Assess patient population and sample size. Determine whether the sample size, patient characteristics, and follow-up duration are adequate to support the claimed indications.
5. Review clinical outcomes and statistical analyses. Verify that the results demonstrate safety and efficacy, and confirm that the statistical methods are valid and transparent.
27
6. Analyze adverse events and risk management. Confirm that all adverse events were appropriately recorded, classified, and mitigated, aligning with the overall device risk profile.
7. Benchmark device performance against standards of care. Compare clinical data to competitor devices or established alternatives to highlight unique benefits or confirm equivalence.
8. Summarize findings and identify data gaps. Document strengths, limitations, and recommended actions (such as additional trials or subgroup analyses) to address any gaps in the clinical evidence.


## 📤 Output format
- A written clinical evidence assessment report summarizing methodology, results, and conclusions
- A gap analysis table mapping actual evidence to regulatory requirements and best practices
- An executive summary highlighting key findings, unresolved questions, and recommended next steps
- Supporting appendices containing detailed data, references, or literature comparisons


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Positive, statistically significant outcomes with few serious adverse events suggest strong clinical evidence.
- Insufficient sample sizes, high dropout rates, or poorly defined endpoints may weaken the evidence, indicating a need for further studies.
- Discrepancies between clinical outcomes and regulatory requirements could indicate a risk of rejection or additional data requests from authorities.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
- Design robust clinical studies early, incorporating input from regulatory agencies and key opinion leaders.
- Strengthen post-market surveillance programs to collect real-world evidence and address open questions.
- Update risk management files continuously as new clinical data becomes available.
- Partner with contract research organizations (CROs) or academic centers for rigorous study design and data analysis.

- Leverage systematic reviews of published literature to complement internal clinical data and support regulatory submissions.
