# 30 · Supply Chain and Post-Harvest Logistics Review

> **Part:** [[E - Market Positioning & Value Chain]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Agriculture Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate the efficiency, reliability, and cost-effectiveness of the farm’s post-harvest handling processes and supply chain arrangements, ensuring product quality, minimizing waste, and maximizing market opportunities.


## 📊 Data required
- Harvest schedules and volumes for each crop
- Storage facilities and conditions (temperature, humidity, capacity)
- Transportation options and associated costs
- Post-harvest processing steps (washing, grading, packaging)
- Time-in-transit data for delivering produce to buyers
- Quality and spoilage rates at different stages of the supply chain
- Agreements or contracts with distributors, wholesalers, and retailers


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Map the post-harvest process flow. Document each stage after harvest—field to storage, storage to processing, packing to transport. Identify any bottlenecks or delays.
Step 2: Assess equipment and facilities. Evaluate the capacity, condition, and technology level of storage, grading, packaging, and cooling systems. Note any maintenance issues that affect efficiency.
Step 3: Analyze logistics and transportation. Review the routes, costs, and timing of deliveries to buyers or distribution centers. Identify if current transport methods align with shelf-life requirements or buyer expectations.
Step 4: Evaluate product quality and losses. Measure spoilage, shrinkage, or damage rates at each stage of the supply chain. Determine the financial impact of quality degradation or rejected shipments.
Step 5: Review supply chain partnerships. Examine contracts or informal arrangements with logistics providers, wholesalers, or retailers. Check whether service levels, costs, or terms need updating.
69 Step 6: Compare with best practices and industry benchmarks. Research typical logistics costs, target transit times, and acceptable spoilage rates for the relevant commodities. Identify gaps between current performance and standards.
Step 7: Summarize findings and recommend improvements. Compile data on costs, losses, timing, and system capacity. Propose targeted upgrades—improved cooling systems, route optimization, better packaging—to enhance overall supply chain efficiency.


## 📤 Output format
- A flowchart outlining post-harvest handling steps and key logistical milestones
- A table summarizing costs, transit times, and spoilage rates at each stage
- A concise report highlighting critical weaknesses and recommending prioritized solutions


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High spoilage or shrinkage suggests deficiencies in handling, storage, or transportation conditions.
- Excessive transit times or high freight costs may indicate inefficiencies or poor route planning.
- Closer alignment with industry benchmarks generally implies competitive supply chain performance and lower operational risks.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Upgrade storage facilities (e.g., temperature-controlled systems) to extend product shelf life.
2. Implement standardized quality checks at each stage of handling to reduce losses.
3. Optimize delivery routes and schedules to minimize transit time and costs.
4. Negotiate or renegotiate contracts with logistics partners for better rates or service-level agreements.
5. Use digital tracking systems (RFID, barcodes) for real-time visibility of inventory and shipments.
6. Provide staff training on proper post-harvest handling to reduce damage and ensure consistency.
