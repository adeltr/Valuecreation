# 1 · Content Portfolio & ROI Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Content Strategy & Portfolio]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Media & Entertainment Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate the performance and profitability of a company’s content library, determine which projects drive the highest ROI, and guide decisions on future investments and divestments within the portfolio.


## 📊 Data required
1. Historical production costs and marketing spend for each piece of content
2. Revenue breakdown (box office, streaming, syndication, licensing, merchandise, etc.)
3. Engagement metrics (viewership, time spent, social media traction)
4. Demographic information of the audience for each content piece
5. Competitive benchmarks and industry standards for ROI


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather and verify content performance data. Collect all production costs, marketing spend, and revenue data for each title in the content library. Ensure accuracy by cross-referencing internal financial statements with distribution and marketing partners’ data.
2. Normalize and categorize each content piece. Convert all financial data into a consistent format (for example, USD). Categorize content by genre, franchise, or release window to allow for apples-to-apples comparisons.
3. **Calculate ROI for each piece of content. Use a standard formula such as:** ROI (%) = (Total Revenue - Total Costs) / Total Costs x 100. This calculation should incorporate production, distribution, and marketing spend.
4. Rank content by profitability and strategic value. Identify top performers based on ROI as well as strategic considerations (award recognition, brand-building, franchise potential).
5. Identify portfolio gaps and over-concentration risks. Assess the mix of genres, audiences, and cost brackets to find areas where the content slate is either too concentrated (e.g., all high-budget blockbusters) or missing key demographics.
6. Analyze opportunity for content lifecycle extension. Review secondary revenue streams (syndication, licensing, spin-offs) to see how each content piece can continue generating returns beyond the initial launch.

7. Compare with industry benchmarks. Use external data or competitive intelligence to see if ROI levels align with market standards for similar content categories.


## 📤 Output format
- A spreadsheet or dashboard listing all titles, their associated costs, revenue, ROI percentages, and strategic scores
- Charts or graphs ranking content pieces by ROI and indicating portfolio mix (genre, target audience, budget level)


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High ROI content indicates successful investment and potential to expand or replicate
- Low ROI content may signal overinvestment, poor targeting, or ineffective marketing strategies
- Portfolio mix indicates whether the company is balanced across various genres and demographics
- Strategic scoring shows titles that may have longer-term brand-building potential despite lower short-term ROI


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Optimize content budgets by reallocating spend to projects or franchises with consistently high ROI
2. Develop a targeted marketing approach based on the audience segments most likely to engage with each content category
3. Invest in analytics and data-driven decision-making to forecast profitability and reduce risk
4. Diversify the content slate to reach different demographics and mitigate the risk of reliance on one genre or audience segment
5. Explore additional monetization opportunities such as merchandise, spin-offs, and licensing deals to extend the content lifecycle.
