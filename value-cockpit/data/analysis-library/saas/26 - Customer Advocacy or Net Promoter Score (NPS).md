# 26 · Customer Advocacy or Net Promoter Score (NPS)

> **Part:** [[C - Customer Retention & Success]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Customer Advocacy or Net Promoter Score (NPS) analysis is to measure customer satisfaction and loyalty by determining how likely customers are to recommend a SaaS company’s product or service to others. NPS provides insights into the overall health of customer relationships and helps predict churn, growth, and customer advocacy.


## 📊 Data required
1. **NPS survey responses:** Responses to the NPS question: “On a scale of 0 to 10, how likely are you to recommend our product to a friend or colleague?”
2. **Total number of respondents:** The number of customers who participated in the NPS survey.
3. **Customer segmentation (optional):** Segment NPS scores by customer type (e.g., small business, enterprise), product, or region to analyze advocacy across different groups.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Distribute the NPS survey:
o    Send the NPS survey to customers, asking the key question: “On a scale of 0 to 10, how likely are you to recommend our product to a friend or colleague?” Ensure the survey is distributed across a representative sample of your customer base.
2. Classify respondents into groups:
o    Based on their score, classify respondents into one of three categories:
   Promoters: Customers who respond with a score of 9 or 10. These are highly satisﬁed customers likely to recommend your product.
   Passives: Customers who respond with a score of 7 or 8. These customers are neutral and unlikely to actively promote or criticize your product.
   Detractors: Customers who respond with a score of 0 to 6. These customers are dissatisﬁed and may share negative feedback or even churn.
3. Calculate the percentage of promoters, passives, and detractors:
o    Determine the percentage of total respondents in each category.
o    Use the formula:
`   Promoter Percentage (%) = (Number of Promoters / Total Respondents) x 100`
82
`   Passive Percentage (%) = (Number of Passives / Total Respondents) x 100`
`   Detractor Percentage (%) = (Number of Detractors / Total Respondents) x 100`
o    For example, if 40 respondents are promoters, 30 are passives, and 30 are detractors out of 100 total respondents:
`   Promoter Percentage = (40 / 100) x 100 = 40%`
`   Passive Percentage = (30 / 100) x 100 = 30%`
`   Detractor Percentage = (30 / 100) x 100 = 30%`
4. Calculate the Net Promoter Score (NPS):
o    NPS is calculated by subtracting the percentage of detractors from the percentage of promoters.
`o    Use the formula: NPS = Promoter Percentage - Detractor Percentage`
o    For example, if the promoter percentage is 40% and the detractor percentage is
`30%: NPS = 40% - 30% = 10`
5. Segment NPS scores (optional):
o    Segment NPS results by customer type (e.g., free vs. paid users, small business vs. enterprise) or product line to assess how advocacy varies across different groups.
o    Use the same calculation steps for each segment to analyze NPS at a more granular level.


## 📤 Output format
The output typically includes the overall Net Promoter Score (NPS), along with supporting metrics such as the percentage of promoters, passives, and detractors, and segmented scores (if applicable). Example format:
•   Promoter Percentage: 40% •   Detractor Percentage: 30% •   NPS: 10 •   Segmented NPS:
o    Small Business Customers: NPS 15 o    Enterprise Customers: NPS 5 This format provides a clear overview of customer advocacy and satisfaction levels.

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   NPS of 50 or above: An NPS of 50+ is considered excellent and indicates that a large percentage of customers are highly satisﬁed and likely to recommend the product. This suggests strong customer loyalty and advocacy.
•   NPS of 20 to 49: A score in this range indicates good customer satisfaction, with a healthy proportion of promoters. However, there is still room to reduce detractors and turn passives into promoters.
•   NPS of 0 to 19: A low score indicates that customer advocacy is weak, and many customers are either neutral or dissatisﬁed. There is signiﬁcant potential for improvement, especially in converting detractors into passives or promoters.
•   Negative NPS (<0): A negative NPS indicates that detractors outnumber promoters. This is a concerning sign and suggests that many customers are dissatisﬁed and may be at risk of churning or sharing negative feedback.
•   Segmented NPS: If certain customer segments (e.g., enterprise clients) have much lower NPS than others, it suggests the need to improve the experience for those segments. Conversely, high-NPS segments can be leveraged for referrals and case studies.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Address detractor feedback:** Reach out to detractors to understand their pain points and take action to resolve their issues. Detractors are a key source of feedback that can highlight product weaknesses, service gaps, or other areas for improvement.
2. **Engage with promoters:** Leverage promoters by encouraging them to leave positive reviews, refer new customers, or participate in case studies and testimonials. Promoters can help drive new business and build the company’s brand reputation.
3. **Turn passives into promoters:** Analyze why passives are not promoting the product and work to elevate their experience. Offering additional value through new features, improved customer support, or better onboarding may help convert passives into promoters.
4. **Improve customer experience and support:** Continuously enhance the overall customer experience by providing proactive customer support, addressing product issues quickly, and offering personalized solutions to customer needs. A better customer experience can lead to higher satisfaction and NPS scores.
5. **Monitor and track NPS over time:** Regularly conduct NPS surveys and track changes over time. An increasing NPS score 84 indicates improved customer satisfaction and advocacy, while a declining score signals areas needing attention.
6. **Segment and personalize outreach:** Use NPS results to personalize customer success outreach. For example, focus on retention strategies for detractors and passives, while nurturing promoters to deepen their loyalty and drive referrals.
7. **Enhance product features:** If NPS feedback highlights gaps in product functionality, invest in improving or adding features that meet customer needs. A more complete product offering can drive higher customer satisfaction and advocacy.
