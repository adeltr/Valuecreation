# 10 · Roadside Assistance Utilization & Cost Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Pricing & Product Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine how often policyholders use roadside assistance benefits, evaluate the cost implications of these services, and identify opportunities to optimize pricing and service delivery.


## 📊 Data required
- Policy-level data showing who has elected roadside assistance coverage
- Historical roadside assistance usage records (frequency, type of service, provider details)
- Costs associated with each service call, including towing, on-site repairs, or other services
- Renewal and retention data for customers who frequently use roadside assistance
- Competitor or industry benchmarks on roadside service usage rates and average costs


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify the subset of policies with roadside assistance coverage and compile all usage events for a set time period (e.g., last 3 years). Record type of assistance provided, total cost per event, and customer demographics to enable segmentation.
2. **Calculate usage frequency by using the formula:** Roadside Assistance Frequency = (Number of Roadside Assistance Calls / Number of Policies with Coverage) x 100. Compare across different segments (e.g., vehicle age, driver age, location) to pinpoint high-usage groups.
3. Determine the average cost per roadside assistance call by summing total costs and dividing by the number of assistance events. Identify the most common types of events (e.g., towing, tire changes, jump starts) that contribute to cost variations.
4. Compute the roadside assistance coverage loss ratio using Loss Ratio = (Total Roadside Assistance Costs + Related Expenses) / Earned Premium for the coverage. Assess whether the program remains profitable or if costs exceed collected premiums.
5. Analyze trends in customer retention among high-usage segments. Investigate if frequent usage correlates with higher satisfaction and loyalty or if it increases the likelihood of premium shopping.

6. Benchmark internal data against external sources such as competitor usage rates and industry cost averages. Flag any major deviations that indicate potential overuse or underpricing of coverage.
7. Assess provider network performance by examining response times, customer satisfaction ratings, and negotiated service rates. Identify partners offering high-quality service at competitive costs vs. those driving up total expenses.
8. Document findings and propose recommendations like adjusting coverage limits, revising pricing, or enhancing provider networks. Prepare these insights for presentation to product management and actuarial teams.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts illustrating roadside assistance call frequency, average cost per event, and overall coverage loss ratio
- Segmented analysis (e.g., by vehicle age, geographic area, or demographic group)
- Written summary with key findings, proposed adjustments to coverage limits or pricing, and potential impact on profitability


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A growing frequency of roadside assistance calls could signal that certain policyholder segments are more prone to vehicle breakdowns and may warrant adjusted premiums or coverage terms.
- High average costs per call or a growing share of expensive services (e.g., long-distance towing) could negatively impact loss ratios.
- Favorable retention among high-usage customers might justify certain cost outlays if it leads to stronger long-term profitability.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Negotiate better rates with towing companies and service providers or consider creating a preferred vendor network to reduce average call costs.
2. Refine underwriting criteria for roadside assistance to limit coverage for high-frequency claimants or older vehicles in poor condition.
3. Offer proactive maintenance programs or discounts to policyholders who get regular vehicle check-ups, thereby reducing roadside incidents.
4. Periodically review and update coverage terms, such as maximum towing distance, to align with actual usage and cost trends.
