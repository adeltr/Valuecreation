# 3 · Underwriting Profitability and Combined Ratio Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Underwriting]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to assess the profitability of underwriting activities by combining the loss ratio and expense ratio. The Combined Ratio is a key measure of overall underwriting profitability, with a ratio below 100% indicating profitability and a ratio above 100% suggesting underwriting losses.


## 📊 Data required
1. Total claims paid during the period
2. Total underwriting expenses during the period
3. Total earned premiums during the period


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the Period:** Select the period for analysis (e.g., quarterly or yearly).
2. **Calculate the Loss Ratio:** First, calculate the Loss Ratio using the formula:
`Loss Ratio = (Total Claims Paid / Total Earned Premiums) x 100`
3. **Calculate the Expense Ratio:** Next, calculate the Expense Ratio with the formula:
`Expense Ratio = (Total Underwriting Expenses / Total Earned Premiums) x 100`
4. **Calculate the Combined Ratio:** Add the Loss Ratio and Expense Ratio to get the Combined Ratio:
`Combined Ratio = Loss Ratio + Expense Ratio`
5. Adjust for Reinsurance (if applicable)
To refine the ratio, use net claims, net underwriting expenses, and net earned premiums to calculate an adjusted Combined Ratio that reflects the impact of reinsurance.
6. **Compile Results:** Summarize the Loss Ratio, Expense Ratio, and Combined Ratio in a table or visual format for easy reference.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns showing:
- Period (e.g., Q1 2023)
- Loss Ratio (%)
- Expense Ratio (%)
- Combined Ratio (%)
Consider adding a line or bar chart to illustrate changes in the Combined Ratio over time, helping identify any patterns or trends in underwriting profitability.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A Combined Ratio below 100% indicates underwriting profitability, as total expenses and claims are less than earned premiums.
- A Combined Ratio of exactly 100% means the company is breaking even on underwriting activities.
- A Combined Ratio above 100% suggests underwriting losses, as expenses and claims exceed premiums earned.
- **Industry benchmarks:** Comparing the Combined Ratio to industry standards or historical performance helps gauge competitive positioning and operational effectiveness.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Enhance Pricing Accuracy Review pricing models to ensure they accurately reflect risk levels and market conditions, which can help increase earned premiums without overly impacting sales.
2. Refine Underwriting Standards Strengthen underwriting guidelines to improve risk selection, reducing the loss ratio over time.
3. Control Operational Expenses Implement expense management strategies to reduce underwriting expenses, which can directly improve the expense ratio component of the Combined Ratio.
4. Optimize Claims Management Streamline claims processing to control costs and reduce claims leakage, improving the Loss Ratio.
5. Leverage Reinsurance Use reinsurance effectively to manage large losses and smooth out the impact of high-loss events, which can stabilize the Combined Ratio.
6. Conduct Regular Combined Ratio Reviews by Segment Periodically assess Combined Ratios by product line, customer segment, or region to identify areas with high ratios and apply targeted improvements.
