# 6 · Landfill Mining and Resource Recovery Feasibility

> **Part:** [[A - Landfill Management & Operations]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Waste Management Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate whether reclaiming buried materials (e.g., metals, plastics, organics) and recovering landfill space through landfill mining is financially and technically viable, and to determine potential environmental benefits or risks associated with such operations.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical records of landfill composition (e.g., average metal content, recyclable plastics)
- Current and projected market prices for recoverable materials
- Estimated costs for excavation, separation, and processing (equipment, labor, disposal of unrecoverable residues)
- Permitting requirements and potential regulatory barriers
- Geotechnical data (stability of landfill slopes, soil cover, moisture content)
- Environmental impact data (possible odor, dust, or leachate issues during mining)
- Potential revenue from recovered airspace or from reselling reclaimed materials


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Characterize the landfill content Conduct waste characterization studies, core sampling, or geophysical surveys to estimate the volume and composition of potential recoverable materials.
2. Assess market conditions and commodity prices Gather current and forecasted market prices for scrap metals, plastics, and other reclaimable materials. Evaluate the volatility of these markets and potential demand constraints.
3. Determine operational requirements and costs Identify the equipment (e.g., excavators, trommels, sorting lines) and labor needed. Estimate the cost of excavation, transport, separation, and final disposal of non-recoverable fractions.
4. Evaluate environmental and regulatory implications Review applicable permits, assess potential environmental impacts (air quality, leachate, odor), and plan for mitigation measures that comply with regulations.

5. Calculate net economic value Compare the estimated value of recovered materials plus any revenue from extended landfill capacity against the total project cost.
Equation:
`Net Economic Value (USD) = (Revenue from Recovered Materials + Value of`
Reclaimed Airspace) - (Excavation and Processing Costs + Disposal of Residue)
6. Perform sensitivity and risk analysis Run scenarios adjusting key variables (commodity prices, recovery rates, labor costs) to determine the resilience of the project’s economic viability.
7. Finalize feasibility recommendation Compile findings into a comprehensive report, highlighting the economic, environmental, and regulatory considerations that inform whether to proceed with landfill mining.


## 📤 Output format
- A feasibility study document detailing composition analysis, market outlook, and proposed operational approach
- Financial modeling spreadsheets presenting projected revenues, costs, and net returns under different scenarios
- Environmental impact assessment summaries and any proposed mitigation strategies
- Risk matrix illustrating key uncertainties and their potential effects on project outcomes


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A positive net economic value combined with manageable environmental risks suggests that landfill mining may be a worthwhile investment.
- Unfavorable market conditions or high project costs could indicate that postponing or canceling the initiative is more prudent.
- Even if material recovery is marginal, reclaiming landfill airspace might justify the project for long-term capacity benefits.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Conduct more precise waste sampling to reduce uncertainty about recoverable materials and contamination levels.
20
2. Explore advanced sorting technologies or partnerships to maximize recovery rates for high-value commodities.
3. Negotiate long-term contracts or hedges for recovered materials to mitigate market price volatility.
4. Investigate potential funding or incentives from government agencies that support circular economy initiatives.
5. Develop robust environmental management plans to address emissions, odors, and potential public concerns during excavation.
