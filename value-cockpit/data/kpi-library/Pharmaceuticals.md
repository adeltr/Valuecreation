# KPI — Pharmaceuticals

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Healthcare & Life Sciences]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Pharmaceutical Company - Analyse]]

49 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Animal Testing Success Rate | This measures the success rate of animal testing for new drugs. High success rates can suggest potential for success in human trials. |
| API Sourcing Diversification | This KPI measures the diversity in a company's sourcing of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs). Greater diversification can mitigate supply chain risks. |
| Average Drug Shelf Life | This KPI measures the average shelf life of a company's drugs. Longer shelf lives can lead to lower waste and higher potential sales. |
| Biomarker Identification | In personalized medicine, this KPI refers to the discovery rate of biomarkers, which can improve the effectiveness of treatments. |
| Biosimilarity Index | In the biopharmaceutical industry, this KPI indicates the degree of similarity between a biosimilar and the original biologic drug. |
| CAR-T Therapy Success Rates | For companies in the cell and gene therapy segment, this KPI reflects the effectiveness of CAR-T therapies, a type of treatment that modifies patients' cells to fight diseases like cancer. |
| Clinical Trial Enrollment Rates | This measures how quickly a pharmaceutical company can recruit patients for clinical trials. Faster enrollment rates can accelerate the drug development timeline. |
| Cost per New Drug Development | This KPI measures the average cost the company incurs in the development of a new drug. It can give an indication of the company's cost efficiency in R&D. |
| Cost per Patient | This is the average cost incurred by the company for each patient taking its drug. This cost includes research, production, marketing, and any other costs associated with the drug. |
| Drug Counterfeit Rate | This KPI measures the prevalence of counterfeit versions of a company's drugs in the market, which can impact the company's reputation and revenues. |
| Drug Recall Frequency | This measures the frequency of drug recalls, which can highlight potential issues in manufacturing processes or drug safety. |
| Drug Sales per Therapeutic Area | This KPI shows the distribution of sales across various therapeutic areas (e.g., cardiovascular, oncology, neurology). It can highlight areas of strength and potential areas for diversification or focus. |
| Drug-to-Drug Interaction Rates | This KPI measures the incidence of adverse interactions between a company's drug and other drugs. High interaction rates can limit a drug's marketability and use. |
| Drugs Under Patent Protection | This indicator shows the number of drugs that are still under patent protection. These drugs are significant revenue generators as they can be sold without competition from generic manufacturers. |
| FDA Approval Rates | This KPI measures the success rate a company has in getting its drugs approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA). A higher success rate may indicate a more efficient R&D process and better prospects for future earnings. |
| First-to-Market Drugs | In the fast-paced pharmaceutical industry, being first-to-market with a new drug can offer significant competitive advantages. This KPI measures how often a company achieves this. |
| Generic Penetration Rate | In the segment of generic pharmaceuticals, this KPI measures the company's success in capturing market share once original drugs go off-patent. |
| Health Outcome Metrics | In value-based healthcare models, this KPI measures the effectiveness of a company's drugs in improving patient health outcomes. |
| Market Penetration in Specific Therapeutic Areas | This KPI measures the company's market share in specific therapeutic areas of interest. |
| Market Share of Key Drugs | This KPI shows how dominant the company's drugs are within their therapeutic areas. A higher market share could indicate strong brand loyalty, effective marketing, or lack of competition. |
| Medication Adherence Rate | For some pharmaceutical companies, especially those dealing with chronic diseases, this KPI measures the rate at which patients continue to use prescribed medication as directed over time. |
| Number of Clinical Trial Failures | This KPI tracks the number of drugs that fail during clinical trials. High failure rates may indicate issues with the company's research and development approach or capabilities. |
| Number of Drugs in Post-Market Clinical Trials | Even after a drug is approved and on the market, companies often continue to study it in what are called post-market clinical trials. This KPI can give an indication of the company's ongoing commitment to improving and understanding its drugs. |
| Number of Prescriptions | This KPI counts the number of prescriptions written for a particular drug. It's a direct indicator of the drug's market penetration and acceptance by the medical community. |
| Number of Strategic Alliances or Partnerships | In the pharmaceutical industry, companies often form strategic alliances or partnerships to share knowledge, resources, and risks. This KPI can give an indication of the company's collaborative efforts and network in the industry. |
| Orphan Drug Status | In the segment of rare diseases, this KPI indicates if a drug has obtained Orphan Drug Status, providing certain benefits like seven years of market exclusivity. |
| Patent Expiry Dates | This KPI is important in understanding the future earning potential of a company’s existing portfolio. Once a patent expires, other companies can produce generic versions of the drug, potentially leading to a significant reduction in the originating company's revenue. |
| Percentage of Revenue from Blockbuster Drugs | Blockbuster drugs are those that generate annual sales of over $1 billion. This KPI indicates a company's dependence on such high-earning drugs. |
| Percentage of Revenue from Contract Manufacturing | For companies that offer contract manufacturing services to other pharmaceutical firms, this KPI measures the importance of this line of business to their overall revenue. |
| Percentage of Revenue from Digital Health Solutions | For companies that offer digital health solutions (e.g., health apps, telemedicine), this KPI measures the importance of these offerings to overall revenue. |
| Percentage of Revenue from Personalized Medicine | This KPI indicates a company's involvement in personalized medicine, an area with significant growth potential due to its focus on individualized patient care. |
| Percentage of Sales from Emerging Markets | This KPI measures a company's exposure to and success in high-growth emerging markets, which can provide insights into future growth opportunities. |
| Percentage of Sales from Over-the-Counter (OTC) Drugs | For companies involved in both prescription and OTC drugs, this KPI provides an understanding of revenue sources and diversification. |
| Pipeline Strength | This refers to the number of potential drugs that a pharmaceutical company has in different stages of clinical trials. This KPI provides an idea of the potential future revenue of the company. The more drugs in later stages of trials, the more promising the pipeline. |
| Pricing Power | This indicates the ability of a pharmaceutical company to set and maintain prices for its drugs without losing market share. This can be influenced by the uniqueness of the drug, competition, and the severity of the condition it treats. |
| Product Backlog | This KPI indicates the number of orders received but not yet fulfilled, which can provide insight into demand for a company's drugs and the efficiency of its supply chain. |
| Quality Audit Results | This KPI tracks the results of quality audits, which can indicate how well a company meets industry standards and regulations related to drug manufacturing and safety. |
| R&D Expenditure as a Percentage of Sales | This KPI shows how much of the company's sales are reinvested into research and development. High R&D spending is common in the pharmaceutical industry due to the need for constant innovation and development of new drugs. However, this must be balanced against the potential for profitable returns. |
| Rate of Adverse Events | This measures the frequency of adverse events related to a drug, which can impact the drug's market acceptance and future sales. |
| Rate of Drug Approvals in First Submission | This KPI indicates the company's success in getting its drugs approved on the first submission to regulatory authorities. High success rates may suggest a well-run and efficient drug development process. |
| Rate of Drug Returns | This is the frequency at which drugs are returned. A high rate may indicate potential issues with product quality or customer satisfaction. |
| Revenue from Companion Diagnostics | For companies that produce both drugs and the corresponding diagnostic tools, this KPI tracks revenue from these diagnostics. This can also give an indication of how well integrated the company's therapeutic and diagnostic offerings are. |
| Revenue from Patent Licensing | For companies that license out their patented drug manufacturing processes or drug formulas, this KPI measures the revenue from such licensing. |
| Sales from Top Drugs | This KPI provides insight into how reliant a company is on its top-performing drugs for its revenue. Over-reliance on a few drugs can indicate vulnerability, especially if patents are due to expire. |
| Sales Growth of Biologics | For companies involved in biologics, this KPI tracks the sales growth of these complex, large-molecule drugs, often indicating a focus on cutting-edge and high-growth therapeutic areas. |
| Share of Market for Vaccines | For companies involved in vaccine development, this KPI measures their share of the vaccine market, which can be particularly relevant in times of global health crises. |
| Time from Initial Discovery to Clinical Trials | This KPI measures the time it takes for a drug to go from initial discovery to the start of clinical trials. A shorter time can indicate a more efficient drug discovery process. |
| Time from Patent Filing to Drug Approval | This KPI indicates the length of time from a company's initial patent filing to when a drug is approved. Shorter times can indicate a more efficient drug development and approval process. |
| Volume of Drug Production | This KPI reflects the total amount of drugs produced by the company, which can be useful for assessing manufacturing capacity and efficiency. |
