# 22 · Private Label Cannibalization and Erosion Impact

> **Part:** [[B - Sales]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Private Label Cannibalization and Erosion Impact analysis in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to evaluate how the growth of private label (store brand) products affects the sales, market share, and profitability of national (branded) products. As retailers increasingly promote their own private label offerings, branded CPG companies may experience a decline in sales (cannibalization) and market share erosion. This analysis helps CPG companies understand the extent of private label competition, identify affected product categories, and develop strategies to mitigate its impact.


## 📊 Data required
1. Sales data for national brands and private labels, segmented by category and SKU
2. Market share data for branded products and private labels
3. Pricing data for private labels and comparable branded products
4. Promotional spend and discounting strategies for private labels and branded products
5. Consumer behavior data (e.g., price sensitivity, brand loyalty, switching behavior)
6. Retailer-specific data on private label promotion and shelf placement
7. Profitability and margin data for both branded and private label products
8. Competitive analysis of private label performance (e.g., category growth, price positioning)
9. Shopper demographics and segmentation data (e.g., income level, shopping habits)
10. Feedback from retailers on private label growth strategies and promotional support 126


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the objectives of the analysis:** Establish clear goals for the analysis, such as quantifying the impact of private label growth on branded product sales, assessing market share erosion, and identifying at-risk product categories. For example, a goal could be to determine the percentage of sales lost to private labels in key product categories or to evaluate which SKUs are most vulnerable to cannibalization.
2. **Collect sales data for national brands and private labels:** Gather historical sales data for both branded and private label products within the same categories. Track trends over time to assess whether private labels are gaining market share at the expense of national brands. For example, if private label sales have increased while branded sales have declined, this indicates potential cannibalization.
3. **Calculate market share erosion for branded products:** Analyze the change in market share for branded products as private label offerings grow. The formula for market share erosion is:
`Market Share Erosion (%) = ((Previous Market Share - Current Market Share) / Previous Market Share) x 100 For example, if a branded product’s market share was 40% last year and has decreased to 30% this year: Market Share Erosion = ((40 - 30) / 40) x 100 = 25% This indicates that the brand has lost 25% of its market share to competitors, likely private labels.`
4. **Assess pricing and value positioning of private labels:** Compare the pricing strategies of private label products to those of branded products. Private labels are often positioned as lower-cost alternatives to national brands, and significant price gaps may encourage consumers to switch. Calculate the price gap between branded and private label products using the formula:
127 Price Gap (%) = ((Branded Price - Private Label Price) / Branded Price) x 100 For example, if a branded product costs $5 and a comparable private label product costs $3:
`Price Gap = (($5 - $3) / $5) x 100 = 40% A larger price gap suggests that private labels are positioned as value-driven alternatives.`
5. **Analyze consumer behavior and brand switching:** Review consumer behavior data to understand how price sensitivity and brand loyalty impact switching to private labels. Identify which consumer segments are most likely to switch to private labels, such as price-conscious shoppers or those with less brand loyalty. Track brand switching behavior and assess how promotions or discounts influence switching.
6. **Track promotional spend and support for private labels:** Evaluate how much promotional support retailers are providing to private label products compared to national brands. Private labels may receive more favorable shelf placement, higher promotional spend, or exclusive discounts, which can drive cannibalization of branded products. Compare the promotional impact on sales uplift for both private label and branded products. Promotional Uplift can be calculated as:
`Promotional Uplift (%) = ((Sales During Promotion - Baseline Sales) / Baseline Sales) x 100 For example, if private label sales increased from $50,000 to $75,000 during a promotion: Promotional Uplift = (($75,000 - $50,000) / $50,000) x 100 = 50% This shows the extent to which private label promotions contribute to market share gains.`
7. **Evaluate profitability and margin impact:** Assess the profitability and margin erosion caused by private label competition. If private label products consistently undercut branded prices, branded products may need to lower prices or increase promotions to remain competitive, which can erode margins. Track how much 128 margin has been lost in response to private label pricing pressure. Margin Erosion can be calculated as:
`Margin Erosion (%) = ((Previous Margin - Current Margin) / Previous Margin) x 100 For example, if a branded product’s margin was 40% but has decreased to 30% due to pricing pressure: Margin Erosion = ((40 - 30) / 40) x 100 = 25%`
8. **Segment impact by category and SKU:** Identify which product categories and SKUs are most affected by private label cannibalization. Private labels may be more successful in certain categories (e.g., commodity goods like milk or paper products) than in others (e.g., premium or niche products). Focus on categories where private labels are gaining the most traction and where branded products are losing the most market share.
9. **Monitor shopper demographics and preferences:** Segment shoppers by demographics (e.g., income level, age, family size) to understand which groups are most likely to switch to private labels. For example, lower-income shoppers may be more price-sensitive and more likely to opt for private labels, while higher-income consumers may be more loyal to premium branded products. Use this data to tailor pricing and promotional strategies to specific segments.
10. **Develop strategies to mitigate private label cannibalization:** Based on the findings, develop strategies to mitigate the impact of private label growth. This may include introducing value packs, enhancing product differentiation (e.g., through quality or innovation), leveraging brand equity, or increasing promotional support for branded products. Collaborating with retailers to protect shelf space and securing prime placements for national brands can also help limit cannibalization.


## 📤 Output format
129 The output of a Private Label Cannibalization and Erosion Impact analysis typically includes:
- A breakdown of sales trends and market share for branded products and private labels.
- Market share erosion data for branded products across different categories.
- Pricing gap analysis between branded products and private labels.
- Consumer behavior insights, including brand switching and price sensitivity.
- Promotional spend and effectiveness data for private label and branded product promotions.
- Profitability and margin impact of private label competition on branded products.
- Category and SKU-level analysis showing which areas are most vulnerable to private label competition.
- Shopper segmentation data, highlighting which demographics are most likely to switch to private labels.
- Recommendations for mitigating private label cannibalization and protecting branded product sales.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Significant market share erosion in key categories:** This indicates that private label products are successfully cannibalizing branded product sales. Immediate action should be taken to adjust pricing, promotions, or product positioning to mitigate further erosion.
- **High price gap with strong private label growth:** If the price gap between branded and private label products is wide and private label sales are increasing, this suggests that consumers are prioritizing price over brand loyalty. Consider introducing lower-priced alternatives or promotional offers to close the gap.
130
- **Low brand switching but significant promotional impact:** If private labels only gain market share during promotions, this suggests that branded products still have strong loyalty. Focus on enhancing brand value and maintaining promotional support to retain customers in non-promotional periods.
- **Margin erosion without significant sales loss:** If branded products have lowered prices to compete with private labels but have not experienced significant sales loss, it may indicate that pricing pressure is more related to retailer strategies than consumer demand. Explore options to improve margins while maintaining competitiveness.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Enhance product differentiation:** Focus on differentiating branded products from private labels by emphasizing superior quality, innovation, or premium features. This can justify a higher price point and reduce the appeal of private labels as substitutes.
2. **Adjust pricing strategies:** Close the price gap between branded products and private labels without eroding margins too significantly. Introduce value packs or bundle offers to provide greater perceived value while maintaining profitability.
3. **Strengthen brand equity:** Invest in brand-building activities to reinforce brand loyalty and reduce the likelihood of consumers switching to private labels. Highlight the unique attributes of your brand, such as quality, heritage, or sustainability.
4. **Increase promotional support:** Offer targeted promotions for branded products to compete with private label offerings, especially in categories where private labels are gaining market share. Use data to ensure that promotions are timed strategically to maximize impact.
5. **Collaborate with retailers:** Work closely with retailers to secure favorable shelf placement and promotional support for branded products. Position national 131 brands as category leaders and emphasize their importance to driving overall category growth.
