# 29 · Investment Portfolio Performance and Asset-Liability Matching (ALM) Review

> **Part:** [[E - Investment and Asset-Liability Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To assess the performance of the life insurance company’s investment portfolio and evaluate the effectiveness of asset-liability matching (ALM). The objective is to optimize investment returns while ensuring alignment between assets and liabilities to maintain financial stability and mitigate risks.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Investment portfolio data:** ○ Asset types (e.g., bonds, equities, real estate, alternative assets) ○ Yields, maturities, risk ratings, and market values ○ Investment returns over time (historical and current)
2. **Liability data:** ○ Policy-level reserves, future cash flow obligations, and durations ○ Liability types (e.g., annuities, whole life, term life)
3. **ALM metrics:** asset duration, liability duration, and gap analysis
4. **Benchmark data:** ○ Industry-standard returns for similar asset classes ○ Macroeconomic indicators, including interest rates, inflation, and yield curves


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Analyze Investment Portfolio Performance a. Calculate historical and current investment returns for each asset class:
`Investment Return (%) = (Investment Income + Capital Gains) / Portfolio Value x 100 b. Compare actual returns to benchmarks for each asset class (e.g., government bonds, equity indices).`
2. Evaluate Portfolio Risk and Asset Composition a. Assess the portfolio’s risk exposure by asset type, credit ratings, and geographic diversification. b. Quantify portfolio volatility and downside risks using metrics such as Value at Risk (VaR).
83
3. Measure Asset-Liability Matching (ALM) a. Calculate the duration and cash flow profile of both assets and liabilities:
`Duration Gap = Asset Duration - Liability Duration b. Identify mismatches where the duration of assets does not align with the timing of future liabilities.`
4. Perform ALM Gap Analysis a. Compare projected asset cash flows with liability cash flow obligations. b. Highlight gaps where asset maturities fall short of covering liability payouts, creating liquidity or reinvestment risk.
5. Stress Test ALM Under Adverse Scenarios a. Model the impact of macroeconomic shocks on asset returns and liability values, such as: ■ Interest rate increases or decreases (100–200 basis points). ■ Equity market declines. ■ Inflationary impacts on liabilities. b. Measure changes in portfolio returns and ALM gaps under each scenario.
6. Benchmark Portfolio and ALM Results a. Compare portfolio returns, asset allocation, and ALM metrics to industry peers and best practices. b. Assess alignment with regulatory requirements for asset-liability management.
7. Visualize Results a. Summarize portfolio performance, asset composition, and ALM gaps in tables. b. Use charts to illustrate trends in investment returns, asset durations vs. liability durations, and stress testing results.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing investment returns, asset composition, and ALM gaps
- Charts showing asset allocation, duration gaps, and portfolio performance trends
- Stress testing results highlighting the impact of adverse scenarios on returns and ALM alignment
- Key findings identifying risks, gaps, and opportunities for portfolio optimization


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Higher investment returns relative to benchmarks indicate strong portfolio performance.
2. Duration gaps highlight potential liquidity or reinvestment risks if liabilities are not matched with appropriate asset maturities.
84
3. Significant volatility or risk exposure in the portfolio may require adjustments to balance returns and capital preservation.
4. Stress testing results reveal the resilience of the investment portfolio and ALM strategy under adverse macroeconomic conditions.
5. Comparisons with industry benchmarks identify areas where asset allocation or ALM strategies lag behind competitors.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Adjust the asset mix to reduce duration gaps and ensure better alignment between assets and liabilities.
2. Increase allocation to long-duration assets, such as high-quality bonds, to match long-term liabilities.
3. Diversify the investment portfolio across asset classes and geographies to optimize returns while mitigating risks.
4. Improve risk management frameworks to monitor portfolio volatility and exposure to market risks.
5. Conduct regular stress testing and scenario analysis to validate the resilience of the ALM strategy.
6. Explore alternative investment strategies, such as infrastructure or real estate, for enhanced yield while maintaining ALM discipline.
7. Benchmark asset performance and ALM metrics against industry peers to identify best practices for optimization.
