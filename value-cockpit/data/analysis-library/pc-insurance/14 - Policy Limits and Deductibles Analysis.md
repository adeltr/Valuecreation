# 14 · Policy Limits and Deductibles Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Underwriting]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Policy Limits and Deductibles Analysis is to evaluate the distribution of policy limits and deductibles across the company’s portfolio. Understanding the trends and preferences in policy limits and deductibles allows the company to assess its exposure to large claims, ensure alignment with customer needs, and make data-driven decisions on pricing and risk management.


## 📊 Data required
1. Policy limits by product line or segment
2. Deductible amounts chosen by policyholders
3. Claims data, especially high-cost claims in relation to policy limits
4. Customer demographics or segments (optional, for deeper insights)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Collect Policy Limit Data by Product Line For each product line, gather data on the limits of liability chosen by policyholders, such as coverage limits for auto, home, or liability policies.
2. Analyze Deductibles by Product Line Record deductible amounts chosen by policyholders within each product line to understand customer preferences and risk tolerance.
3. Calculate Average Policy Limit and Deductible per Product Line Determine the average limit and deductible amount for each product line:
`○ Average Policy Limit = Total Policy Limits / Number of Policies`
`○ Average Deductible = Total Deductibles / Number of Policies`
4. Segment Analysis by Customer Demographics or Regions (Optional) If available, segment the data by customer demographics (e.g., income level, age group) or regions to identify patterns in policy limit and deductible preferences.
5. Compare Claims to Policy Limits Examine claims data, especially for high-severity claims, to see how often claims reach or exceed policy limits. This highlights any potential gaps in coverage or overly conservative limits.
6. Compile Results Organize findings in a table to show the distribution of policy limits and deductibles by product line and any relevant segments.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Product Line
- Average Policy Limit ($)
- Average Deductible ($)
- Distribution of Policy Limits (e.g., % with high, medium, low limits)
- Distribution of Deductibles (e.g., % with high, medium, low deductibles)
Consider a histogram to visualize the distribution of policy limits and deductibles for each product line, or a heatmap to show popular combinations of limits and deductibles.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Policy Limits with Low Deductibles:** Indicates higher exposure to claims, as customers are likely to make claims even for smaller losses.
- **Low Policy Limits with High Deductibles:** Suggests customers are assuming more personal risk, potentially leading to fewer but higher-cost claims when they do occur.
- **Policy Limits Near Claim Levels:** If claims frequently reach or approach policy limits, it may indicate a need for adjusting limit options to better cover customer needs and manage risk.
Comparing results to industry averages can reveal if the company’s limit and deductible structures are competitive or aligned with customer expectations.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Optimize Limit and Deductible Options Adjust available policy limits and deductible options based on customer preferences and claims patterns to better align coverage with risk exposure.
2. Encourage Higher Deductibles for Low-Risk Customers Offer incentives or discounts for low-risk customers who select higher deductibles, reducing small claims and encouraging more responsible risk-taking.
3. Introduce Tiered Deductible Options Create a wider range of deductible tiers, allowing customers to select a deductible that matches their financial tolerance and reduces unnecessary claims.
4. Educate Customers on Deductible Impacts Provide clear information to customers on how deductible choices impact premiums and claim costs, helping them make informed decisions.
5. Monitor Claims Reaching Policy Limits Regularly review claims data to monitor if certain limits are frequently reached, signaling the need to reassess policy limits for adequacy and risk exposure.
6. Conduct Periodic Analysis Perform this analysis regularly to adapt to shifting customer preferences and market conditions, ensuring that limits and deductibles stay relevant.
