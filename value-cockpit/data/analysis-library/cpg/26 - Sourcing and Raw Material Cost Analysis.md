# 26 · Sourcing and Raw Material Cost Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Operations]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Sourcing and Raw Material Cost Analysis in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to assess the efficiency, cost-effectiveness, and reliability of raw material sourcing strategies. This analysis focuses on identifying cost-saving opportunities, managing supplier relationships, reducing supply chain risks, and ensuring the quality and availability of raw materials needed for production. Optimizing sourcing strategies can help CPG companies reduce costs, enhance operational flexibility, and improve profitability while maintaining product quality and sustainability.


## 📊 Data required
1. Raw material cost data (e.g., historical and current costs by material)
2. Supplier data, including pricing, lead times, and reliability
3. Contract terms with suppliers, including pricing agreements, volume discounts, and payment terms
4. Market price trends and commodity pricing for key raw materials
5. Transportation and logistics costs associated with raw material sourcing
6. Supplier quality metrics (e.g., defect rates, product consistency, certifications)
7. Supplier performance data, including on-time delivery rates and responsiveness
8. Inventory levels of raw materials and safety stock requirements
9. Competitor benchmarking data for sourcing strategies and raw material costs
10. Sustainability and ethical sourcing requirements (if applicable)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
152
1. **Define the objectives of the analysis:** Set clear goals for the sourcing and raw material cost analysis, such as reducing raw material costs, improving supplier performance, diversifying sourcing options, and ensuring raw material availability. For example, a goal could be to achieve a 5% reduction in material costs while improving supplier reliability and maintaining product quality.
2. **Collect raw material cost data:** Gather historical and current cost data for all key raw materials used in production. This includes tracking the price of each material over time to identify fluctuations, seasonal trends, or changes in market demand. The formula for cost change is:
`Cost Change (%) = ((Current Cost - Previous Cost) / Previous Cost) x 100`
For example, if the cost of a raw material increased from $100 to $120 per unit:
`Cost Change = (($120 - $100) / $100) x 100 = 20%`
Tracking cost changes helps identify materials that are driving up overall production costs and provides a basis for negotiating with suppliers.
3. **Analyze supplier pricing and performance:** Evaluate the pricing offered by each supplier, considering factors such as volume discounts, long-term contract terms, and payment flexibility. Additionally, assess the reliability of each supplier by tracking on-time delivery rates and product quality metrics. The formula for on-time delivery rate is:
`On-time Delivery Rate (%) = (Number of On-time Deliveries / Total Number of Deliveries) x 100`
For example, if a supplier made 48 out of 50 deliveries on time:
`On-time Delivery Rate = (48 / 50) x 100 = 96%`
Reliable suppliers with competitive pricing are valuable partners, while those with poor performance may increase risk in the supply chain.
4. **Review market price trends and commodity pricing:** Monitor the market prices for raw materials, especially for commodity-based materials such as metals, plastics, paper, or agricultural products. Use industry data and forecasts to anticipate price fluctuations or supply constraints. This will help inform 153 sourcing decisions and contract negotiations with suppliers. Identify opportunities for locking in favorable pricing through long-term contracts or hedging strategies.
5. **Compare supplier lead times and flexibility:** Assess supplier lead times and their ability to respond to fluctuations in demand. Long lead times can increase inventory holding costs and increase the risk of stockouts or production delays. Compare the flexibility of different suppliers in accommodating changes in order volumes or delivery schedules. Lead Time can be calculated as:
`Lead Time = Time from Order Placement to Order Delivery`
Shorter lead times and more flexible suppliers can reduce the need for excess inventory and improve responsiveness to demand changes.
6. **Evaluate supplier quality and consistency:** Analyze supplier quality metrics, including defect rates, consistency of raw materials, and compliance with product specifications. High defect rates or inconsistent quality can lead to production delays, increased costs, and damage to the brand’s reputation. Calculate the defect rate using the formula:
`Defect Rate (%) = (Number of Defective Units / Total Units Received) x 100`
For example, if a supplier delivered 1,000 units and 20 were defective:
`Defect Rate = (20 / 1,000) x 100 = 2%`
7. **Consider transportation and logistics costs:** Account for transportation and logistics costs associated with sourcing raw materials, especially if suppliers are located far from production facilities or distribution centers. Evaluate the total landed cost, which includes the material cost plus transportation, handling, and other logistics expenses. Reducing transportation costs by sourcing closer to production facilities or optimizing shipping methods can lead to significant savings. Total Landed Cost = Material Cost + Transportation + Handling + Other Logistics Costs
8. **Assess inventory levels and safety stock requirements:** Review raw material inventory levels to ensure that safety stock levels are adequate without being 154 excessive. Excessive inventory ties up working capital, while insufficient inventory can lead to stockouts and production delays. Optimize inventory levels based on supplier lead times, order frequency, and production needs. Inventory Turnover Ratio = Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory
9. **Benchmark against competitors and industry standards:** Compare your sourcing strategies, supplier costs, and performance metrics against competitors and industry benchmarks. Understanding how your sourcing costs and supplier relationships compare to industry standards can help identify areas for improvement and negotiation.
10. **Incorporate sustainability and ethical sourcing considerations:** If sustainability or ethical sourcing is a priority, assess each supplier’s compliance with relevant certifications, environmental practices, and labor standards. Consumers and retailers are increasingly demanding transparency in sourcing practices, and aligning with these values can enhance brand reputation and create competitive advantages.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Sourcing and Raw Material Cost Analysis typically includes:
- A breakdown of raw material costs by supplier, including cost trends over time.
- Supplier performance data, such as on-time delivery rates, lead times, and product quality metrics.
- A comparison of current raw material costs to historical data, highlighting significant cost changes.
- Market price trends and commodity pricing forecasts for key raw materials.
- An analysis of transportation and logistics costs, including total landed costs for each material.

- Inventory levels and safety stock recommendations, optimized based on supplier lead times.
- Competitor benchmarking data for raw material sourcing strategies and cost comparisons.
- Recommendations for improving supplier relationships, renegotiating contracts, or diversifying sourcing options.
- Sustainability and ethical sourcing compliance, where applicable.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Rising raw material costs:** If raw material costs are increasing, assess whether it is due to market factors or supplier pricing changes. Consider negotiating better terms with suppliers, switching to alternative sources, or hedging against future price increases.
- **High defect rates and poor supplier reliability:** Suppliers with high defect rates or poor delivery performance may increase costs and production delays. Consider either working with the supplier to improve performance or switching to a more reliable partner.
- **Excessive inventory levels:** If raw material inventory levels are high and turnover is low, it may indicate over-ordering or long lead times. Adjust order quantities or improve demand forecasting to optimize inventory and free up working capital.
- **High transportation and logistics costs:** If transportation costs are significant, consider sourcing from suppliers located closer to your production facilities or optimizing shipping methods to reduce the total landed cost.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
156
1. **Diversify supplier base:** Reduce the risk of supply chain disruptions and price increases by diversifying your supplier base. Identify alternative suppliers that offer competitive pricing and better lead times to enhance sourcing flexibility.
2. **Negotiate better contract terms:** Renegotiate contracts with suppliers to secure favorable pricing, volume discounts, or more flexible lead times. Long-term contracts may help lock in lower prices, especially in volatile markets.
3. **Optimize transportation and logistics:** Analyze shipping methods and routes to minimize transportation costs. Consider sourcing raw materials from suppliers closer to production sites or using more efficient shipping methods (e.g., bulk shipments) to reduce costs.
4. **Improve inventory management:** Optimize raw material inventory levels by adjusting order quantities and reducing lead times. This ensures that materials are available when needed without excessive inventory, reducing holding costs.
5. **Invest in supplier relationships:** Build stronger relationships with key suppliers to ensure reliable delivery, better pricing, and improved communication. Regularly review supplier performance and collaborate on initiatives to improve efficiency and quality.
6. **Leverage sustainability initiatives:** If sustainability is a priority, work with suppliers to meet environmental and ethical standards. Sourcing from sustainable suppliers can improve brand reputation and align with consumer and retailer expectations.
