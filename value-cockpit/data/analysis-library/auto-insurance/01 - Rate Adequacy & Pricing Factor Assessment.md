# 1 · Rate Adequacy & Pricing Factor Assessment

> **Part:** [[A - Pricing & Product Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine if current premiums accurately reflect underlying risk and costs, and identify whether pricing factors (e.g., driver age, vehicle type, region) are appropriately weighted.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical claims data (loss frequency, loss severity, payouts)
- Premium and exposure data (written premium, earned premium, in-force policies)
- Key rating attributes (driver demographics, driving history, vehicle types, territories)
- Competitor rate filings or market benchmarking data
- External cost drivers (medical inflation, parts inflation, legal environment)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather historical premium and claims data for the period under review (for example, the last 3–5 years). Collect in-force policies, earned premium, incurred losses, and loss adjustment expenses in a consistent format.
2. **Calculate overall loss ratio using the formula:** Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + Loss Adjustment Expenses) / Earned Premium. Perform this calculation for each relevant rating segment (e.g., age group, territory).
3. Assess trends in claims frequency and severity within each segment. Pinpoint where frequency or severity significantly exceeds or falls below expectations.
4. Compare internal results with external benchmarks to evaluate market positioning. Reference competitor rate filings or industry data if available.
5. Examine each pricing factor’s current rating differential to determine if it aligns with observed loss experience. Adjust factors that appear misaligned (e.g., territory surcharges, driver age differentials).
6. Conduct elasticity analysis to understand how potential rate changes might affect retention or new business. Look for segments more price-sensitive and plan incremental adjustments if needed.
7. Develop recommended rate changes based on desired profitability targets and acceptable loss ratios. Validate through scenario testing to ensure compliance with regulatory guidelines.

8. Document final rate recommendations and clarify the rationale behind each adjustment. Present findings for internal sign-off or for regulatory filings as needed.


## 📤 Output format
- A table or matrix showing current vs. proposed rates by rating segment
- Graphs depicting historical and projected loss ratios
- Written summary highlighting key findings, recommended changes, and the expected financial impact


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A loss ratio consistently above the target range (e.g., 70–75 percent) indicates underpricing or adverse risk selection.
- A loss ratio well below target (e.g., under 60–65 percent) may suggest overpricing or overly conservative underwriting.
- Discrepancies between actual and expected results for specific factors indicate a need to reassess pricing assumptions.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Conduct regular (e.g., quarterly or annual) pricing reviews to stay ahead of claim cost trends.
2. Use advanced predictive analytics to refine rating models and capture emerging risk patterns.
3. Monitor competitor filings and benchmark data to maintain competitive positioning.
4. Collaborate closely with underwriting and claims departments to gather insights on evolving risks.
5. Maintain clear documentation and rationale for rate changes to satisfy regulatory requirements.
