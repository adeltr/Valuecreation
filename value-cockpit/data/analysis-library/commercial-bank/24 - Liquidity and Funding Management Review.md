# 24 · Liquidity and Funding Management Review

> **Part:** [[D - Capital, Liquidity, and Funding]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Commercial Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate both the strategic and day-to-day liquidity positions of the bank to ensure it can meet obligations under various market conditions, optimize funding costs, and comply with regulatory liquidity requirements.


## 📊 Data required
- Balance sheet composition (loans, deposits, investments, wholesale funding)
- Breakdown of funding sources by type, maturity, and cost (e.g., retail deposits, interbank borrowings, repurchase agreements)
- Maturity schedules and repricing dates for major assets and liabilities
- Key liquidity ratios (e.g., Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR), Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR))
- Daily or weekly cash flow forecasts and usage patterns
- Contingent funding sources (e.g., central bank lines, committed facilities)
- Regulatory guidelines and internal policy limits


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Classify the bank’s funding sources (such as demand deposits, term deposits, interbank borrowings, short-term wholesale instruments) by stability and maturity. This provides a clear picture of near-term versus long-term funding.
Step 2: Identify and aggregate liquid assets (e.g., cash, high-quality government securities), noting which qualify under regulatory definitions of “high-quality liquid assets” for calculating the LCR.
Step 3: Calculate the bank’s key liquidity ratios, including the LCR and NSFR, comparing actual ratios to regulatory minima and internal policy thresholds. Track these ratios over time to identify trends in funding stability.
Step 4: Analyze short-term liquidity needs by reviewing daily or weekly cash flow forecasts. Focus on projected inflows (e.g., loan repayments, fee income) and outflows (e.g., deposit withdrawals, debt maturities), flagging potential liquidity gaps or “pinch points.” Step 5: Conduct sensitivity analysis or stress testing on both short-term and structural liquidity positions. Model potential market disruptions (e.g., reduced wholesale market 52 access, deposit run-offs, sudden increases in funding costs) to gauge the bank’s resilience.
Step 6: Compare findings against peer or industry benchmarks, assessing whether the bank’s funding strategy is competitive, cost-effective, and adequately diversified.
Step 7: Document outcomes in a consolidated report that covers:
- Liquidity ratio results and any shortfalls
- Short-term funding concentrations and maturity mismatches
- Stress test results
- Recommended actions to enhance liquidity and reduce funding volatility


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing liquidity ratios, funding sources by type and maturity, and interest costs
- Graphs or charts displaying daily or weekly liquidity positions and gap analysis
- Written summary detailing stress test outcomes, short-term funding risks, and recommended remedial measures


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Sufficient liquidity ratios (e.g., LCR, NSFR) and minimal short-term mismatches generally suggest a healthy liquidity profile.
- Excessive reliance on volatile funding sources (such as large wholesale deposits or short-term borrowings) can heighten refinancing risk in adverse market conditions.
- Stress test outcomes showing significant short-term gaps or sharp declines in key ratios may indicate a need for stronger contingency funding plans or improved liquidity buffers.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Diversify funding sources by tapping multiple markets, maturities, and investor segments to reduce reliance on any single channel.
2. Strengthen contingency funding plans that outline clear actions and back-up facilities if wholesale markets tighten or outflows spike.
3. Align asset maturities with funding tenors through robust asset-liability management, reducing the risk of sudden shortfalls.
