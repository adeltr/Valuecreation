# 25 · Integrated Reinsurance Strategy and Cost Efficiency Assessment

> **Part:** [[C - Risk Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Integrated Reinsurance Strategy and Cost Efficiency Assessment is to evaluate the effectiveness of the company’s reinsurance program in managing risk exposure and controlling costs. This analysis ensures that the reinsurance strategy aligns with the company’s risk appetite while maximizing cost efficiency and minimizing financial vulnerabilities.


## 📊 Data required
1. Total reinsurance premiums paid
2. Reinsurance recoveries (amounts reclaimed from reinsurers after claims)
3. Retention limits and reinsurance coverage details (e.g., per occurrence, aggregate)
4. Total insured values and exposure levels by segment
5. Cost-benefit analysis of reinsurance programs (optional, for advanced insights)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Reinsurance Cost Efficiency Ratio:** To evaluate cost efficiency, calculate the reinsurance cost efficiency ratio:
`Reinsurance Cost Efficiency Ratio = (Reinsurance Recoveries / Reinsurance Premiums Paid) x 100`
A ratio above 100% indicates that reinsurance recoveries exceed premiums paid, suggesting high cost efficiency. A ratio below 100% suggests potential inefficiencies.
2. **Assess Retention Limits and Coverage Adequacy:** Review the retention limits (amounts retained by the company before reinsurance kicks in) and compare them to the company’s risk appetite and financial capacity. Ensure that retention levels are appropriate for minimizing exposure without overburdening the company financially.
3. **Evaluate Loss Scenarios Against Reinsurance Coverage:** Use historical claims data or catastrophe models to simulate loss scenarios (e.g., large single-event losses, aggregate loss accumulations) and determine how well the current reinsurance program covers these scenarios. This analysis helps confirm if coverage aligns with the company’s risk profile.
4. **Calculate Reinsurance Cost as a Percentage of Total Premiums:** Reinsurance Cost Percentage = (Total Reinsurance Premiums / Total Earned Premiums) x 100 This metric provides insight into the proportion of total premiums allocated to reinsurance, helping gauge cost efficiency relative to the company’s revenue.
5. **Analyze Reinsurance Recoveries by Event Type:** Segment reinsurance recoveries by event type (e.g., catastrophe, attritional losses) to understand the types of claims that most frequently trigger reinsurance. This can inform adjustments to reinsurance terms, such as adding catastrophe-specific coverage.
6. **Benchmark Against Industry Standards:** Compare the company’s reinsurance costs, retention levels, and recoveries against industry benchmarks to determine if the reinsurance strategy aligns with typical industry practices.
7. **Compile Results:** Summarize the analysis in a report, displaying reinsurance cost efficiency ratios, retention levels, reinsurance coverage adequacy for various loss scenarios, and benchmarks.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Metric (e.g., Reinsurance Premiums, Reinsurance Recoveries, Retention Level)
- Value (e.g., dollar amounts, percentages)
- Cost Efficiency Ratio (%)
- Reinsurance Cost Percentage (% of Total Premiums)
- Adequacy Assessment for Loss Scenarios (Covered or Not Covered)
A line or bar chart showing cost efficiency ratios and recoveries over time can provide a visual representation of reinsurance performance.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Cost Efficiency Ratio (> 100%):** Indicates effective reinsurance, as recoveries exceed the premiums paid, demonstrating cost-efficient risk transfer.
- **Low Cost Efficiency Ratio (< 100%):** Suggests potential inefficiency, with premiums paid outweighing recoveries, warranting a review of reinsurance terms or strategy.
- **Adequate Coverage for Loss Scenarios:** Confirms that the reinsurance program aligns with the company’s risk profile and can absorb potential large or catastrophic losses effectively.
- **High Reinsurance Cost Percentage:** Implies a significant portion of premiums is dedicated to reinsurance, which may affect profitability if not balanced by recoveries.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Optimize Retention Levels Adjust retention levels to ensure the company retains an appropriate amount of risk without overburdening finances, potentially reducing unnecessary reinsurance costs.
2. Diversify Reinsurance Coverage Explore multi-layered reinsurance options, such as catastrophe-specific coverage, aggregate reinsurance, or quota-share arrangements to tailor risk transfer to the company’s exposure profile.
3. Negotiate Reinsurance Terms Work closely with reinsurers to negotiate more favorable terms, especially in areas where the company’s risk profile has improved, potentially lowering reinsurance premiums.
4. Conduct Regular Reinsurance Cost-Benefit Reviews Perform periodic assessments to compare reinsurance costs with recoveries, allowing adjustments to ensure maximum cost efficiency.
5. Leverage Catastrophe Models to Refine Coverage Use catastrophe models to identify and secure coverage for high-risk areas, optimizing reinsurance allocation for regions or products with significant exposure.
6. Benchmark with Peer Companies Continuously benchmark reinsurance strategies against industry peers to remain competitive and adopt best practices in reinsurance structuring and cost management.
