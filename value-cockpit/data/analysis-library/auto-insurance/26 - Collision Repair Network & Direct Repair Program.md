# 26 · Collision Repair Network & Direct Repair Program Effectiveness

> **Part:** [[C - Claims & Loss Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Assess how effectively the company’s preferred collision repair network or direct repair program (DRP) manages repair quality, turnaround times, and cost control, thereby impacting overall customer satisfaction and claim severity.


## 📊 Data required
- List of collision repair partners in the network, including their contractual terms (labor rates, service-level agreements)
- Claims data indicating whether repairs were completed within the direct repair network vs. external shops
- Repair cost details (total repair bills, parts vs. labor breakdown) and average turnaround times
- Customer satisfaction or net promoter scores related to the repair process
- Industry or competitor benchmarks for DRP participation rates, cycle times, and cost metrics


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather claims data specifying whether a repair was completed through a DRP shop or an out-of-network facility. Collect information on total repair costs, parts usage, labor hours, and repair duration.
2. Calculate key performance indicators (KPIs) for each DRP partner, such as:
`○ Average repair cost = (Total Incurred Repair Costs / Number of Repairs) ○ Average cycle time (days from drop-off to completion) ○ Rework rate (percentage of repairs requiring additional work or corrections)`
3. Compare DRP performance against out-of-network repairs to see if in-network shops deliver lower costs, faster turnaround, or higher customer satisfaction. Note if there are specific shops with exceptional or subpar results.
4. Examine trends in parts usage (OEM vs. aftermarket) and labor charges among DRP shops. Determine if contract terms (e.g., negotiated labor rates) are effectively containing costs without compromising quality.
5. Assess customer satisfaction by reviewing feedback or survey results. Correlate satisfaction scores with factors such as repair quality, timeliness, and communication.

6. Benchmark your DRP metrics against industry or competitor data to identify gaps or best practices. Highlight any significant performance variances to focus improvement efforts.
7. Analyze potential cost savings from directing more claims into DRP facilities. Estimate how increased DRP utilization could affect claim severity, cycle times, and overall loss ratios.
8. Compile findings and recommend action steps such as updating DRP contracts, enhancing shop performance monitoring, or adjusting incentives to funnel more claims toward preferred partners. Present the final report to claims, operations, and executive teams for potential implementation.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts comparing DRP vs. non-DRP repair costs, cycle times, and customer satisfaction scores
- Written summary highlighting top-performing DRP partners and areas needing improvement
- Quantified financial impact of DRP utilization changes (e.g., potential loss ratio improvements)


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Consistently lower repair costs and shorter turnaround times in DRP shops indicate the program is effectively reducing claim severity and improving customer service.
- High rework rates or customer dissatisfaction might signal quality issues that require more robust oversight or revised partnership terms.
- Significant variance among DRP partners implies that some shops outperform others, suggesting an opportunity to refine your network or share best practices.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Strengthen DRP agreements with clear performance metrics and incentives for timely, cost-effective repairs.
2. Conduct ongoing quality inspections or audits at DRP facilities to maintain consistent repair standards.
3. Implement transparent tracking and reporting for repair status updates, enhancing policyholder experience.
4. Offer incentives or seamless referral processes to encourage customers to choose DRP shops for their repairs.
