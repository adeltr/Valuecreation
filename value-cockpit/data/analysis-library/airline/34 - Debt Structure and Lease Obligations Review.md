# 34 · Debt Structure and Lease Obligations Review

> **Part:** [[G - Cost Management and Financial Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Airline Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Debt Structure and Lease Obligations Review is to assess the airline’s debt obligations and lease structure to manage cash flow, minimize financial risks, and optimize capital efficiency. This analysis focuses on identifying high-cost debt or lease obligations, exploring refinancing or restructuring options, and improving the airline’s financial stability and flexibility.


## 📊 Data required
- Debt structure details, including principal amounts, interest rates, maturities, and payment schedules for all loans and bonds
- Lease obligations by aircraft type and location, including payment terms, residual value commitments, and expiration dates
- Debt covenants and restrictions attached to loans and leases
- Cash flow data segmented by operating cash flow and debt/lease servicing costs
- Industry benchmarks on debt structure, average lease terms, and financing costs
- Credit rating and risk assessment for the airline, if available
- Historical interest rate trends and forecasts


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Review Debt Structure and Terms:** List all debt obligations, detailing the principal, interest rates, remaining terms, and payment schedules. Calculate total annual debt servicing costs and identify any high-interest loans that may be candidates for refinancing. Use the formula:
`Annual Debt Servicing Cost = Principal x Interest Rate + Scheduled Repayments`
2. **Analyze Lease Obligations by Aircraft Type and Location:** Review all lease agreements, including terms for each aircraft type and location. Note any upcoming expirations, residual value obligations, and fixed vs. variable lease payments. Identify leases with high costs or restrictive terms that may impact financial flexibility.
3. **Evaluate Debt Covenants and Lease Restrictions:** Examine any covenants or restrictions attached to debt and lease agreements, such as minimum cash balances, asset maintenance requirements, or limitations on further borrowing. Assess the impact of these covenants on operational flexibility and financial health.
4. **Assess Cash Flow Impacts of Debt and Lease Servicing:** Calculate the percentage of operating cash flow allocated to debt and lease servicing. High ratios may indicate strain on cash flow, highlighting the need for restructuring or alternative financing. Use the formula:
`Debt/Lease Servicing Ratio = (Debt Servicing Cost + Lease Payments) / Operating Cash Flow x 100`
5. **Benchmark Debt and Lease Terms Against Industry Standards:** Compare the airline’s debt structure, interest rates, and lease terms to industry benchmarks, focusing on average financing costs, loan maturities, and lease terms. This provides insights into the airline’s competitive positioning in terms of financing costs.
6. **Identify Opportunities for Refinancing or Restructuring:** Analyze options for refinancing high-interest debt, renegotiating lease terms, or converting debt to equity if necessary. Evaluate the potential savings from refinancing or restructuring, especially for debt or leases with upcoming maturity dates.
7. **Run Scenario Testing for Debt and Lease Adjustments:** Test scenarios that adjust debt or lease structures, such as refinancing at lower interest rates, switching to shorter or longer lease terms, or paying down debt. Measure projected cost savings, cash flow improvements, and impacts on financial ratios for each scenario.


## 📤 Output format
- **Debt and Lease Summary:** A table listing all debt obligations and lease agreements, including principal amounts, interest rates, maturities, and lease terms.
- **Debt Servicing and Cash Flow Impact Report:** A report showing total debt and lease servicing costs as a percentage of operating cash flow.
- **Covenants and Restrictions Summary:** A breakdown of key covenants and restrictions, highlighting any limitations on flexibility or additional borrowing.
- **Industry Benchmarking on Debt and Lease Terms:** A comparison of the airline’s financing terms with industry standards, highlighting areas where costs may be reduced.
- **Scenario Analysis Results for Restructuring Options:** Charts or tables showing projected savings and cash flow improvements from debt and lease restructuring scenarios.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Debt and Lease Summary provides a detailed view of the airline’s financial obligations, highlighting high-cost or short-term debt that may need restructuring.
- Debt Servicing and Cash Flow Impact Report shows the financial burden of debt and lease payments on cash flow, guiding decisions on potential refinancing or restructuring needs.
- Covenants and Restrictions Summary identifies any constraints from existing agreements, informing adjustments to improve financial flexibility.
- Industry Benchmarking on Debt and Lease Terms offers insight into the airline’s competitive position in terms of financing costs, suggesting areas where terms may be renegotiated.
- Scenario Analysis Results for Restructuring Options provide forecasts of potential cost savings and cash flow benefits, supporting data-driven decisions for optimizing the debt and lease structure.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Refinance High-Interest Debt:** Seek refinancing options for high-interest loans to reduce annual interest expenses, improving cash flow and overall cost efficiency.
2. **Renegotiate Lease Terms:** Negotiate more flexible lease terms, including options for variable payments or residual value adjustments, to better align with cash flow needs.
3. **Monitor and Adjust Debt Covenants:** Reassess debt covenants to ensure they are not overly restrictive, potentially negotiating covenant terms to allow more operational flexibility.
4. **Consider Alternative Financing Options:** Explore financing alternatives, such as converting short-term debt to long-term obligations or issuing equity, to reduce immediate cash flow pressures.
5. **Optimize Lease-to-Buy Decisions:** Evaluate lease agreements approaching expiration and consider buying aircraft where financially beneficial, reducing long-term lease obligations and ownership costs.
