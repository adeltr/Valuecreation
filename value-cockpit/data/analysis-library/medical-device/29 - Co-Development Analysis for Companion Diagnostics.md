# 29 · Co-Development Analysis for Companion Diagnostics

> **Part:** [[D - Product Development & Lifecycle]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Medical Device Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine the feasibility, strategic fit, and regulatory pathway for developing a diagnostic test in parallel with a therapeutic product. Identify how the diagnostic will guide therapy decisions, evaluate market potential, and ensure alignment with both device and pharmaceutical regulatory requirements.


## 📊 Data required
- Information on the therapeutic product’s mechanism of action, target patient population, and clinical trial designs
- Biomarkers or relevant biological targets that correlate with therapeutic response
- Preliminary or existing clinical data supporting the use of the diagnostic for patient stratification or therapy monitoring
- Regulatory guidelines for companion diagnostics (for example, FDA, EMA, PMDA)
- Market research on the prevalence of targeted conditions, competitive landscape, and reimbursement perspectives for diagnostic tests
- Intellectual property (IP) status of biomarkers or diagnostic technologies


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Define the therapeutic and diagnostic link. Clearly articulate how the diagnostic test identifies the patient subset most likely to benefit from the therapy or monitors therapy response.
2. Assess technical feasibility and validation pathways. Evaluate the biomarker’s clinical relevance, required diagnostic performance metrics (sensitivity, specificity), and any analytical validation needs.
3. Review regulatory considerations. Determine whether a coordinated submission or parallel review process is applicable. Identify the lead agency groups (for example, FDA’s CDRH and CDER for the US).
4. Analyze clinical trial integration. Examine how the diagnostic will be incorporated into therapeutic clinical studies (for example, patient inclusion/exclusion, endpoint definitions, sample collection protocols).
5. Estimate development timelines and costs. Account for additional testing, regulatory fees, trial enrollment challenges, and manufacturing scale-up for the diagnostic component.

6. Evaluate market access and reimbursement factors. Consider whether payers will view the companion diagnostic as a clinically necessary tool, thus supporting coverage and coding pathways.
7. Identify potential strategic partners. Determine if the company should develop the diagnostic internally or collaborate with a diagnostic specialist, factoring in IP, technical expertise, and resource availability.
8. Develop a co-development roadmap. Outline major milestones, including assay design and validation, clinical trial integration, regulatory submissions, and commercial launch.


## 📤 Output format
- A co-development strategy document summarizing the rationale for linking the diagnostic and therapeutic
- A timeline or Gantt chart mapping parallel development activities for both the drug and the diagnostic
- A market analysis and financial model estimating potential revenue, costs, and return on investment
- An executive summary highlighting the clinical rationale, expected regulatory pathway, and recommended next steps


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A clear, evidence-based link between the diagnostic test and therapeutic efficacy indicates a strong case for a companion diagnostic approach, potentially improving market adoption and reimbursement.
- Regulatory complexity or significant data gaps can delay co-development, requiring additional validation or the involvement of specialized partners.
- The economic viability of a companion diagnostic often hinges on demonstrating improved patient outcomes, which justifies reimbursement for the test.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
- Engage early with regulatory authorities (for example, through pre-submission meetings) to clarify co-development requirements and coordinate review pathways.
- Collaborate with academic or biotech partners who have expertise in biomarker discovery and assay development.
- Secure robust clinical evidence by designing trials that clearly demonstrate how the diagnostic improves treatment selection or outcomes.

- Develop a comprehensive reimbursement strategy, detailing the clinical and economic value of the companion diagnostic to payers and providers.
- Continuously update the co-development plan as clinical, regulatory, or market dynamics evolve to maintain alignment and mitigate risks.
