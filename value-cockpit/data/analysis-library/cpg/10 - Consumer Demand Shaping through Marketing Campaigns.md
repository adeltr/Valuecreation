# 10 · Consumer Demand Shaping through Marketing Campaigns

> **Part:** [[A - Marketing]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Consumer Demand Shaping through Marketing Campaigns analysis in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to assess how marketing efforts influence consumer behavior and drive demand for specific products. This analysis focuses on identifying the effectiveness of marketing campaigns in altering purchasing patterns, increasing awareness, and ultimately shaping consumer demand. By understanding which tactics work best, CPG companies can optimize their marketing strategies to align with consumer needs, preferences, and market trends.


## 📊 Data required
1. Campaign performance data (e.g., reach, impressions, engagement rates)
2. Sales data before, during, and after marketing campaigns
3. Market share data for the campaign period
4. Customer demographic and psychographic data (e.g., age, income, preferences, lifestyle)
5. Channel-specific performance data (e.g., TV, digital, social media, in-store promotions)
6. Competitor marketing activity during the same period
7. Consumer purchase behavior data (e.g., frequency of purchase, product categories)
8. Product stock and distribution data during campaign periods
9. Survey or focus group data on consumer awareness and perception
10. Promotional investment data (e.g., ad spend, promotion costs, coupon redemptions)

## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the objectives of the campaign:** Set clear objectives for the marketing campaign. These could include increasing sales of a specific product, boosting brand awareness, entering a new market, or shifting consumer preferences toward a new product category.
2. **Collect pre-campaign baseline data:** Gather data on sales performance, consumer awareness, and market share before launching the campaign. This will serve as the baseline for measuring the impact of marketing efforts. Key metrics to track include: ○ Sales volume of the targeted product(s) ○ Market share in key categories ○ Brand awareness or recall rates
3. **Track marketing campaign performance:** Monitor the performance of the campaign across various channels (e.g., digital, TV, social media, print). Use key performance indicators (KPIs) like: ○ Impressions: The total number of times your ad was viewed. ○ Click-through rates (CTR): The percentage of people who clicked on your ad after seeing it. ○ Engagement: Likes, shares, comments, and overall interaction with the campaign. ○ Coupon redemption rates (if applicable): The percentage of distributed coupons that were redeemed.
4. **Measure the uplift in sales during and after the campaign:** Compare sales data from the campaign period with pre-campaign data to assess the increase in demand. Use the formula to calculate the percentage sales uplift:
`Sales Uplift (%) = ((Sales During Campaign - Sales Before Campaign) / Sales Before Campaign) x 100`
For example, if sales before the campaign were $100,000 and sales during the 55 campaign were $130,000:
`Sales Uplift (%) = (($130,000 - $100,000) / $100,000) x 100 = 30%`
5. **Assess market share changes:** Track how the campaign impacts the company’s market share in the targeted product categories. Calculate the change in market share using the formula:
`Market Share Change (%) = ((Market Share After Campaign - Market Share Before Campaign) / Market Share Before Campaign) x 100`
For example, if market share was 15% before the campaign and increased to 18% after:
`Market Share Change (%) = ((18 - 15) / 15) x 100 = 20%`
6. **Analyze consumer behavior shifts:** Review consumer behavior data to determine how the campaign has influenced purchasing patterns. Key questions include: ○ Did the campaign lead to increased purchase frequency? ○ Did it drive consumers to try new products or categories? ○ How did the campaign impact consumer loyalty or repeat purchases?
7. For example, if the goal of the campaign was to increase purchases of a new product, analyze if sales for that product have increased while maintaining or growing market share for other product lines.
8. **Evaluate channel effectiveness:** Break down the performance of each marketing channel to understand which were most effective in shaping demand. Compare engagement and conversion rates across digital, TV, in-store promotions, and social media. For example, if digital ads had a higher CTR but in-store promotions drove more sales, consider adjusting future spend toward higher-performing channels.
9. **Track the impact of promotions or discounts:** If the campaign includes promotional elements such as discounts, coupons, or loyalty rewards, evaluate their impact on consumer demand. Track redemption rates and compare them with historical data to determine if the promotion effectively influenced purchasing behavior.

10. **Collect post-campaign feedback:** Use surveys or focus groups to gather insights from consumers about their awareness of the campaign and how it influenced their decision to purchase. Key questions include: ○ Did consumers recall the campaign? ○ Was the messaging persuasive or memorable? ○ How did the campaign influence their perception of the brand or product?
11. **Benchmark against competitors:** Analyze the marketing efforts of competitors during the campaign period. Were competitors running similar campaigns? Did they gain or lose market share during this period? Benchmarking helps identify where your campaign succeeded and where adjustments may be needed to stand out in the market.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Consumer Demand Shaping through Marketing Campaigns analysis typically includes:
- A breakdown of campaign performance metrics (impressions, CTR, engagement, etc.).
- Sales uplift and market share changes during and after the campaign period.
- An analysis of changes in consumer behavior, including purchasing frequency and product loyalty.
- Channel performance metrics, showing which channels were most effective in driving demand.
- Insights into the effectiveness of promotions, discounts, or coupon strategies.
- A summary of post-campaign feedback from surveys or focus groups.
- Recommendations for optimizing future campaigns based on performance and consumer behavior shifts.

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High sales uplift and positive feedback:** This indicates the campaign successfully influenced consumer demand, leading to increased sales. If positive feedback from consumers aligns with high sales, the campaign messaging and channel strategy likely resonated well with the target audience.
- **Low sales uplift despite high engagement:** If the campaign generated high engagement (e.g., social media interactions, ad clicks) but did not translate into increased sales, there may be friction in the path to purchase. Review the product positioning, pricing, or checkout process for barriers to conversion.
- **Increased market share with minimal sales impact:** If the campaign drove an increase in market share but not a significant sales uplift, this could indicate that the company captured more of the existing market rather than expanding total category sales. Consider strategies to grow overall demand in future campaigns.
- **Promotions driving short-term demand but not loyalty:** If sales increased significantly during promotional periods but dropped afterward, the campaign may be too reliant on price-based promotions. Consider refining product value propositions or focusing more on long-term brand-building efforts.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize channel strategy:** Reallocate marketing budget toward the channels that drove the highest engagement and conversions. For example, if digital campaigns performed better than in-store promotions, increase investment in targeted online ads.
2. **Refine messaging based on consumer feedback:** Use feedback from surveys and focus groups to adjust campaign messaging to better resonate with the target audience. Highlight product features or benefits that align with consumer needs and preferences.
58
3. **Focus on long-term brand building:** While promotions can drive short-term demand, focus on campaigns that build long-term brand equity and loyalty. This can include emotional branding, storytelling, or sustainability initiatives that align with consumer values.
4. **Target high-potential consumer segments:** Use campaign data to identify segments that responded particularly well to marketing efforts. Tailor future campaigns to these high-potential groups, offering personalized content or exclusive offers to drive further engagement.
5. **Test and iterate:** Continuously test different campaign elements (e.g., messaging, offers, visuals) to identify what resonates most with consumers. Use A/B testing in digital channels to optimize performance before scaling the campaign across broader platforms.
6. **Strengthen post-purchase engagement:** Follow up with consumers after their initial purchase through email campaigns, loyalty programs, or social media engagement. This can increase repeat purchases and solidify long-term demand.
