# 14 · Embedded Value (EV) and Value of New Business (VNB) Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Profitability and Product Performance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To measure the current value of a life insurance company’s in-force business (Embedded Value) and assess the profitability of new business written during a period (Value of New Business). This analysis ensures alignment with shareholder expectations, financial reporting, and strategic growth objectives.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policy-level data:** policy type, premiums, expected claims, and expenses.
2. **Actuarial assumptions:** discount rates, mortality rates, lapse rates, expense inflation, and investment returns.
3. **Financial data:** ○ Reserves and net asset values for in-force policies. ○ Actual profits and cash flows for the reporting period.
4. **New business data:** policies written during the reporting period, including premiums and expected margins.
5. **Economic data:** risk-free interest rates, yield curves, and macroeconomic indicators.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Embedded Value (EV) EV consists of two components:** ○ Net Asset Value (NAV): Surplus or capital available for shareholders. ○ Present Value of Future Profits (PVFP): Discounted value of expected future profits from the in-force business. Formula:
`EV = NAV + PVFP`
○ Use cash flow models to project future premiums, claims, expenses, and investment income for the in-force business. ○ Discount future profits using an appropriate discount rate (e.g., risk-free rate + risk margin).
2. Calculate Value of New Business (VNB) VNB measures the profitability of policies sold during a specific period.
39 Formula:
`VNB = PVFP of New Business - Acquisition Costs ○ Estimate the future profits generated by new business written during the reporting period. ○ Subtract acquisition costs, such as commissions and administrative expenses, to determine VNB.`
3. **Analyze Key Drivers Break down EV and VNB by:** ○ Product type (e.g., term life, whole life, annuities). ○ Policyholder demographics (e.g., age, gender, region). ○ Distribution channel (e.g., agents, bancassurance, direct sales).
4. Sensitivity Testing Assess the impact of changes in key assumptions (e.g., discount rates, mortality, lapse rates) on EV and VNB. For example: ○ Increase discount rate by 1% and recalculate EV. ○ Increase lapse rates by 10% and observe changes in VNB.
5. **Benchmark Results Compare EV and VNB results against:** ○ Industry standards or peer companies. ○ Historical trends to identify growth or areas of concern.
6. Visualize Results ○ Summarize EV and VNB by product and segment in tables. ○ Use sensitivity charts to illustrate how EV and VNB vary under different assumptions.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables showing EV and VNB broken down by product, demographic, and distribution channel.
- Charts showing historical trends in EV and VNB.
- Sensitivity analysis results indicating the impact of assumption changes.
- Key findings summarizing profitability drivers and high-growth segments.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. A higher EV indicates strong value creation from the in-force business, reflecting profitable operations and prudent assumptions.
2. Positive VNB highlights profitable growth through new policies; a negative VNB suggests unprofitable acquisition due to high costs or mispriced products.

3. Sensitivity analysis reveals which assumptions (e.g., discount rate, mortality, lapse) have the greatest impact on EV and VNB, guiding risk management priorities.
4. Benchmark comparisons help assess competitive positioning and identify areas requiring improvement.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Optimize product pricing to ensure new business generates positive VNB.
2. Reduce acquisition costs by improving distribution efficiency and targeting profitable segments.
3. Enhance underwriting practices to minimize risks and improve future profitability.
4. Regularly review and update actuarial assumptions to reflect actual experience and economic conditions.
5. Improve investment strategies to increase returns on assets backing future liabilities.
6. Use predictive analytics to identify high-value policyholders and enhance cross-selling opportunities.
