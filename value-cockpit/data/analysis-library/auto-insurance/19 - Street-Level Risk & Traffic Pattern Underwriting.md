# 19 · Street-Level Risk & Traffic Pattern Underwriting

> **Part:** [[B - Underwriting & Risk Segmentation]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine how localized traffic conditions, road infrastructure, and neighborhood-specific factors affect auto insurance risk, enabling more precise underwriting and pricing decisions.


## 📊 Data required
- Local traffic accident records (crash frequency, severity, hot spots)
- GIS or mapping data showing road infrastructure, traffic volumes, intersection density
- Policyholder garaging addresses and daily commuting routes
- External data on neighborhood-level crime rates, road maintenance, and congestion patterns
- Current underwriting guidelines and rating factors


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Collect local-level traffic accident records from city, state, or GPS-based sources that detail crash frequency, severity, and specific intersections. Combine it with your existing underwriting data to correlate addresses with high-risk roads or intersections.
2. Overlay GIS or mapping data on top of your policyholder garaging and commuting routes to identify areas with high crash density or frequent congestion. Segment these areas based on risk indicators like average traffic speed, presence of traffic calming measures, or roadway conditions.
3. Determine claim frequency and severity within each localized risk cluster. Compare these local findings to broader regional or state-level data to see if additional surcharges or discounts are warranted.
4. Review current underwriting guidelines to check whether they factor in micro-geographic risk variations. Identify gaps where manual or generalized territory pricing may fail to reflect true street-level exposure.
5. Analyze potential correlates (e.g., road maintenance, lighting, crime) that might drive up claim costs or theft risk in certain neighborhoods. Combine these insights with policy data to adjust pricing, coverage limits, or underwriting restrictions.
6. Benchmark results against competitor or market data, if available, to ensure your localized risk assessment remains competitive. Flag major deviations that might indicate under- or over-estimations of street-level risk.

7. Develop refined underwriting criteria that incorporate street-level analytics, such as risk tiers for specific neighborhoods or traffic corridors. Determine if telematics data can further enhance accuracy in high-risk geographies.
8. Document your findings and recommendations for updated underwriting rules, localized surcharges or discounts, and potential product enhancements. Present these changes to cross-functional teams for alignment and execution.


## 📤 Output format
- Maps or tables showing collision hotspots, traffic volume, and corresponding claim frequency/severity
- Comparative summaries of street-level vs. traditional territory-based underwriting approaches
- A written report detailing key findings, refined risk segments, and suggested underwriting/pricing adjustments


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High claim frequency in certain micro-areas suggests a need for additional surcharges or stricter underwriting.
- Low risk clusters may warrant discounts or marketing campaigns aimed at safer neighborhoods.
- Inconsistencies between street-level and broader territorial data indicate the potential for more precise geographic segmentation.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Incorporate real-time data feeds or telematics to capture ongoing traffic patterns and adapt underwriting rules dynamically.
2. Work with local governments or civic organizations to stay informed of infrastructure changes that could affect risk (e.g., new traffic lights, road expansions).
3. Continuously refine mapping and data analytics to evolve your underwriting segmentation as neighborhoods shift and traffic flows change.
4. Educate agents and policyholders about higher-risk routes and potential discounts for alternative commuting options or low-mileage usage.
