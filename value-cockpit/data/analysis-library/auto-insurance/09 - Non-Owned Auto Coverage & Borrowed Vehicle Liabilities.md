# 9 · Non-Owned Auto Coverage & Borrowed Vehicle Liabilities Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Pricing & Product Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate the exposure, claim frequency, and severity of non-owned auto (borrowed or rented vehicle) coverage to ensure appropriate underwriting, pricing, and liability protection.


## 📊 Data required
- Policies indicating non-owned auto coverage endorsements
- Claims data involving borrowed vehicles, including frequency, severity, and total incurred losses
- Underwriting guidelines and procedures specific to non-owned auto exposures
- Legal and regulatory requirements regarding non-owned vehicle liability
- Market or competitor benchmarks related to non-owned auto coverage


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify policies with non-owned auto endorsements by extracting endorsement details, effective dates, and associated premiums. Determine how many policies carry this coverage and the total number of insureds using borrowed or rented vehicles.
2. Gather claims data specific to non-owned vehicles to evaluate both frequency and severity, and calculate Non-Owned Auto Claim Frequency = (Number of Non-Owned Auto Claims / Total Non-Owned Auto Policies) x 100. Assess any trends over time, focusing on common claim types such as liability losses or property damage.
3. Calculate the non-owned auto coverage loss ratio using Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + LAE for Non-Owned Auto) / Earned Premium for Non-Owned Auto. Compare these results to standard auto coverage to identify any abnormal patterns.
4. Review underwriting criteria and guidelines for non-owned auto coverage, including borrower driver profiles and vehicle types. Look for potential loopholes or gaps in eligibility that might expose the company to higher-than-anticipated losses.
5. Analyze the impact of legal and regulatory environments on non-owned auto liabilities, noting any state-by-state variances. Identify whether minimum coverage requirements or specific statutes could inflate claim costs or frequency.

6. Benchmark your performance against competitor or industry data on non-owned auto coverage, focusing on typical endorsements, pricing structures, and claim outcomes. Highlight any significant deviations that may indicate overpricing or underpricing.
7. Examine potential adverse selection by identifying patterns where high-risk drivers may be more likely to utilize borrowed vehicles under this coverage. Propose adjustments to underwriting or pricing if this segment shows elevated claim severity or frequency.
8. Document final recommendations such as revising coverage limits, adjusting premiums, or tightening underwriting guidelines. Share these findings with stakeholders for potential product updates and ensure compliance with regulatory obligations.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts showing non-owned auto coverage claim frequency, severity, and loss ratios
- Comparative review of underwriting guidelines vs. industry best practices
- Written summary highlighting key findings, recommendations, and projected financial implications


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High claim frequency or severity in non-owned coverage could indicate lax underwriting or a mismatch between premium and exposure.
- Persistent loss ratios above profitable thresholds suggest necessary rate adjustments or stricter coverage terms.
- A spike in claim activity in certain geographic regions or driver segments may warrant targeted underwriting adjustments.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Tighten eligibility criteria for borrowers to reduce adverse selection and align coverage more closely with risk profiles.
2. Refine pricing or implement usage limitations for non-owned auto coverage, ensuring premiums reflect potential liability exposures.
3. Regularly benchmark against competitors to maintain competitive yet profitable coverage offerings.
4. Stay current with regulatory changes and legal precedents that could affect non-owned auto liability.
