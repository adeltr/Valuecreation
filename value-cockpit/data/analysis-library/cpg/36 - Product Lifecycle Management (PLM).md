# 36 · Product Lifecycle Management (PLM)

> **Part:** [[D - Research & Development]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Product Lifecycle Management (PLM) analysis in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to evaluate and optimize the entire lifecycle of a product, from ideation and development to market introduction, growth, maturity, and eventual decline or discontinuation. This analysis helps CPG companies make informed decisions on product launches, portfolio optimization, marketing investments, and the timing of product phase-outs. Effective PLM ensures that resources are allocated efficiently, product lifecycles are maximized, and profitability is sustained throughout the product’s lifecycle stages.


## 📊 Data required
1. Sales data by product over time (e.g., monthly, quarterly, yearly)
2. Market share data for each product category
3. Product development costs (e.g., R&D, production, marketing)
4. Consumer feedback and sentiment data (e.g., product reviews, surveys, focus groups)
5. Product performance metrics (e.g., revenue growth, profitability, customer adoption rates)
6. Competitive benchmarking data for product lifecycle stages (e.g., competitor product lifecycles)
7. Marketing and promotional spend across different lifecycle stages
8. Inventory and supply chain data (e.g., stock levels, demand forecasting)
9. Product innovation and enhancement data (e.g., new features, versions, line extensions)
10. Profit margin and cost structure data for each product over time 215


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the objectives of the analysis:** Set clear objectives for the PLM analysis, such as optimizing product profitability across its lifecycle, identifying when to phase out or refresh products, and improving product development efficiency. For example, a goal might be to extend the maturity phase of a successful product by 12 months through targeted marketing investments and product enhancements.
2. **Track product lifecycle stages:** Identify the current stage of each product in the portfolio (e.g., introduction, growth, maturity, decline) based on sales trends, market share, and customer adoption rates. The stages of a product lifecycle are typically as follows: ○ Introduction: The product is new to the market, and sales grow slowly. ○ Growth: Sales accelerate as market adoption increases. ○ Maturity: Sales peak and begin to stabilize, with intense competition. ○ Decline: Sales decrease as the market becomes saturated or consumer preferences change.
3. **Example of lifecycle tracking:** ○ Product A: Growth stage, increasing market share and customer adoption. ○ Product B: Maturity stage, sales are leveling off, facing competitive pressure. ○ Product C: Decline stage, sales are declining due to market saturation.
4. **Analyze product performance at each lifecycle stage:** Assess product performance across each stage by reviewing sales growth, profitability, and market share. Use this data to determine whether products need additional investment (e.g., marketing spend, product upgrades) or if they are nearing the end of their lifecycle. The growth rate of a product can be calculated using:
`Growth Rate (%) = ((Current Sales - Previous Sales) / Previous Sales) x 100`
216 For example, if a product’s sales grew from $500,000 to $600,000 over a year:
`Growth Rate = (($600,000 - $500,000) / $500,000) x 100 = 20%`
5. **Evaluate marketing and promotional investments:** Review the marketing and promotional spend for each product at different lifecycle stages. Marketing investments are often higher during the introduction and growth stages, while less is typically spent during maturity and decline. Analyze the return on marketing investment (ROMI) to determine whether additional promotional efforts are warranted. ROMI (%) = ((Incremental Revenue - Marketing Spend) / Marketing Spend) x 100 For example, if an additional $100,000 in marketing spend generated $300,000 in incremental revenue:
`ROMI = (($300,000 - $100,000) / $100,000) x 100 = 200%`
6. **Identify product enhancement or innovation opportunities:** During the maturity stage, it may be necessary to innovate or refresh products to extend their lifecycle. Evaluate opportunities for line extensions, new features, or product upgrades that can revitalize consumer interest. Track consumer feedback and sentiment to identify areas where product improvements are needed.
7. **Monitor product profitability and cost structure:** Analyze profit margins and cost structures for each product at different lifecycle stages. Products in the growth and maturity stages typically contribute the most to profitability, while those in the decline phase may see shrinking margins. The formula for profit margin is:
`Profit Margin (%) = ((Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue) x 100 For example, if a product generates $1 million in revenue with $600,000 in COGS: Profit Margin = (($1 million - $600,000) / $1 million) x 100 = 40%`
8. **Assess inventory and supply chain management:** Review inventory levels and supply chain performance for products at various lifecycle stages. Products in the decline stage may require inventory reductions to avoid excess stock and 217 waste, while products in the growth stage may need more frequent restocking to meet increasing demand.
9. **Track competitor products and benchmarking data:** Benchmark the lifecycle stages of competitor products to understand how your portfolio compares in terms of market positioning and innovation timing. This data can help identify whether your products are behind or ahead of competitors and guide decisions on product refreshes or discontinuations.
10. **Plan for product phase-out or discontinuation:** For products in the decline stage, evaluate whether it makes sense to continue offering the product or phase it out. If sales are consistently declining, consider discontinuing the product to free up resources for newer, more profitable offerings. Plan the discontinuation process to minimize disruption for customers and retail partners.
11. **Optimize the portfolio for long-term growth:** Based on the findings, optimize the product portfolio by focusing on high-potential products in the growth and maturity stages, while considering innovation or phase-out strategies for products in decline. Ensure that the product development pipeline continues to feed the portfolio with new products that align with market trends and consumer demand.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Product Lifecycle Management (PLM) analysis typically includes:
- A detailed product lifecycle chart, identifying the stage of each product (introduction, growth, maturity, decline)
- Sales growth, profitability, and market share data for products at each lifecycle stage
- Marketing and promotional spend analysis, including return on marketing investment (ROMI)
- Opportunities for product enhancements, innovation, or line extensions 218
- Profit margin analysis and cost structure evaluation for each product
- Inventory and supply chain performance metrics, highlighting potential improvements
- Competitor benchmarking data for lifecycle stages and product innovation timing
- Recommendations for phasing out or discontinuing products in decline
- Actionable insights for optimizing product portfolio performance


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Stagnant sales in the maturity stage:** If a product is in the maturity stage and sales are leveling off, consider product enhancements, line extensions, or refreshed marketing campaigns to reignite growth and extend the product’s lifecycle.
- **Declining profitability in the decline stage:** If a product in the decline stage is becoming less profitable, it may be time to phase out the product. Analyze whether it is more cost-effective to discontinue the product or attempt to extend its life through minor adjustments.
- **High marketing spend with low ROMI:** If marketing spend is high but returns are low, particularly in the decline or late maturity stages, this suggests that the product may no longer be worth the investment. Adjust marketing budgets accordingly or focus on promoting more profitable products.
- **Strong growth with innovation potential:** Products in the growth stage that show strong sales growth and market share gains may benefit from additional innovation or investment to maximize their potential and extend the growth phase.

1. **Focus on product innovation during maturity:** To extend the lifecycle of a product in the maturity stage, consider introducing new features, line extensions, or variations that refresh the product offering and appeal to new consumer segments.
2. **Streamline products in the decline stage:** For products in the decline phase, reduce inventory, marketing spend, and production costs to minimize losses. Plan for a phased discontinuation if sales continue to decline without the possibility of revival.
3. **Invest in high-growth products:** Allocate more resources (e.g., marketing spend, supply chain capacity) to products in the growth stage to maximize their revenue potential and gain market share before the maturity phase begins.
4. **Regularly track lifecycle performance:** Continuously monitor sales, profitability, and market trends to assess where products stand in their lifecycle. This enables proactive decision-making on product enhancements, marketing adjustments, and portfolio optimization.
5. **Improve cross-functional collaboration:** Ensure that R&D, marketing, and supply chain teams are aligned throughout the product lifecycle to support timely innovation, effective marketing, and efficient inventory management.
