# 1 · Policyholder Behavior and Lapse Rate Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Policyholder Behavior and Retention]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The primary objective of this analysis is to understand policyholder behavior patterns, specifically focusing on lapse rates, to identify factors leading to lapses, assess their impact on business performance, and develop strategies to improve policyholder retention.


## 📊 Data required
1. Policy-level data, including policy start and end dates, policy type (e.g., term, whole life, universal life), premium payment frequency (monthly, quarterly, annual), and policyholder demographics (e.g., age, gender, location).
2. Lapse records, including date of lapse and reason for lapse (if available).
3. Financial data, including premium amounts and surrender values (if applicable).
4. Historical data on renewal and retention rates.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Define the lapse rate formula:
`Lapse Rate (%) = (Number of Policies Lapsed During the Period / Number of Policies In Force at Start of the Period) x 100`
2. Segment the data by grouping policyholders based on attributes such as policy type, demographics, or premium frequency. Segment lapse records by time periods (e.g., monthly, quarterly, annually).
3. Calculate lapse rates for each segment by applying the lapse rate formula to each segment to determine trends and high-risk groups.
4. Analyze trends and correlations to identify correlations between lapse rates and factors such as policy type, premium frequency, demographic factors, or payment delinquency trends.
5. Conduct predictive analysis using historical data to build predictive models (e.g., logistic regression) to identify factors most likely to contribute to lapses.
6. Benchmark lapse rates against industry standards or competitors to understand relative performance.
7. Generate visualizations, such as time-series charts, heatmaps, or scatter plots to visually represent lapse trends and segment performance.

## 📤 Output format
- Tabular format showing lapse rates by segment (e.g., age group, policy type, premium frequency).
- Charts/graphs, such as time-series lapse rate trends, bar charts by policy type, and scatter plots of correlations between lapse rates and key variables.
- Summary statistics, including overall lapse rate (%), lapse rates by major segments, and top 3 contributing factors to high lapse rates.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. High lapse rates in certain segments indicate potential issues with product fit, policyholder engagement, or affordability.
2. Correlations between lapses and demographics may suggest targeted outreach opportunities for high-risk groups (e.g., younger policyholders with lower income).
3. Trends over time, such as rising lapse rates, may signal economic challenges, competitive pressures, or dissatisfaction with the product.
4. Comparison to benchmarks reveals whether the company is underperforming relative to competitors and where corrective action is needed.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Conduct surveys or focus groups with lapsed policyholders to understand reasons for lapses.
2. Develop flexible premium payment plans to address affordability challenges.
3. Enhance customer engagement through personalized communication, reminders, and educational materials.
4. Improve the value proposition of policies by introducing additional benefits or features.
5. Leverage predictive modeling to identify at-risk policyholders early and offer retention strategies, such as discounts or loyalty rewards.
6. Strengthen training for agents and distributors to ensure proper alignment of products with customer needs.
