# KPI — Wine, Beer, and Spirits

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Consumer & Retail]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Wine, Beer & Spirits Company - Analyse]]

48 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Aging Inventory Value | This KPI measures the value of inventory that is aging or maturing. For spirits and some wines, this can represent a significant portion of a company's assets. |
| Appellation Portfolio Diversity | For wineries, this KPI measures the diversity of appellations (geographical wine regions) in a company's portfolio, which can indicate the variety of its offering and potential risk mitigation against climate conditions in specific regions. |
| Barley or Grape Contract Stability | For companies dependent on barley or grapes, this KPI measures the stability of supply contracts. It can indicate potential risks to supply and the company's relationship with growers. |
| Barrel Aging Capacity | For companies producing wines and spirits, this is a measure of how much product they can store for aging at any given time. This capacity has a direct impact on the company's potential output, especially for products that require long aging periods. |
| Barrel Aging Capacity | This KPI measures the quantity of product a company can age in barrels at any one time. It's crucial for distilleries and wineries that produce aged products, as it can limit production volumes and affect product quality. |
| Blending Consistency | This KPI is specifically for wines and spirits where blending is important for consistency. The percentage of successful blends that meet predefined criteria can be measured. |
| Bottle Aging Time | Particularly for wine and some spirits, this KPI measures the average time a product is aged in the bottle before release. Longer aging can improve quality but also ties up inventory and delays revenue. |
| Bottle Rejection Rate | This measures the number of bottles rejected due to quality issues like improper filling, faulty caps, or labeling errors. A lower rate indicates better quality control and efficiency. |
| Bottle Return Rate | This KPI is relevant for companies that offer returnable bottles. A higher rate can help companies reduce costs and meet environmental sustainability goals. |
| Breakage and Loss Rate | This measures the percentage of product lost to breakage during production, warehousing, or shipment. A lower rate indicates better operations management. |
| Cask Utilization Rate | This is a key KPI for breweries and distilleries, indicating how efficiently they are using their casks for production and aging. |
| Consumer Taste Test Performance | This KPI measures how well a company's products perform in consumer taste tests, which can be an important indicator of product quality and consumer acceptance. |
| Contract Brewing Percentage | This KPI measures the percentage of a brewery's production that is contracted out to other breweries. Higher percentages can indicate limited production capacity, lower capital costs, but potentially reduced control over quality. |
| Cost per Serving | For companies that operate tasting rooms or bars, this KPI tracks the cost of each serving sold, including both the product and associated service costs. |
| Craft Beer Sales as a Percentage of Total Beer Sales | For breweries, the percentage of sales from craft beer versus mainstream brands can reflect changing consumer tastes and the company's ability to innovate and meet market trends. |
| Distillation Efficiency | For spirits producers, this is a measure of how much alcohol is produced per unit of input. Higher efficiency can indicate better control of production processes and costs. |
| Distillation Yield | For distilleries, this KPI measures the amount of spirit produced per unit of input. It's an important indicator of production efficiency. |
| Distillery Capacity Utilization | For distilleries, this measures the actual production output as a percentage of total production capacity. Higher utilization typically means better efficiency but could also limit the ability to scale up production quickly if needed. |
| Diversity of Hop Varieties | For breweries, this KPI measures the number and diversity of hop varieties used in brewing, which can impact the flavor profiles of the beers produced. |
| Fermentation Time | This KPI tracks the average time taken to ferment the base ingredients. Prolonged fermentation times could potentially affect the production cycle and storage requirements. |
| Flavor Consistency Score | For breweries and distilleries, this measures how consistently a company can reproduce the same flavors in its products, which is critical for brand identity and customer loyalty. |
| Fruit Yield per Vine | For wineries, this KPI measures the amount of fruit each vine produces. This can help assess the productivity of the vineyard and influence decisions about planting and pruning practices. |
| Grape Ripening Consistency | This is especially important for wineries, as inconsistent ripening can affect grape quality and wine production. It can be influenced by factors such as climate change, disease, and vineyard management practices. |
| Grape Varieties Harvested | For wineries, this KPI tracks the number of different grape varieties harvested. It can indicate the diversity of a winery's product offerings and its potential to appeal to various consumer preferences. |
| Harvest Quality | This KPI is used by wine and beer producers who grow their own ingredients, measuring the quality of the harvested grapes or grains. Quality can directly influence the end product, impacting the brand reputation and pricing. |
| Label Approval Rate | In many jurisdictions, alcohol producers must get labels approved by regulatory bodies. A high rate of first-time approvals can indicate effective compliance processes. |
| Limited Edition Product Performance | Some producers release limited edition or specialty products to create brand interest or capitalize on trends. Tracking the performance of these products can provide insights into a company's brand strength and product development strategy. |
| Local Sourcing Percentage | This KPI measures the percentage of raw materials sourced locally. It can impact costs, freshness, sustainability, and local relationships. |
| Mash Efficiency | For breweries, this measures the efficiency of the mashing process, or how well the brewery extracts sugars from the grains. Higher efficiencies can mean lower ingredient costs. |
| Oenotourism Metrics | For wineries offering tours and tastings, this could include KPIs like the number of visitors, length of stay, and conversion rate of visitors to purchasers. |
| Private Label Sales Percentage | This measures the portion of a producer's sales that come from private label products (those made for retailers to sell under their own brands). A high percentage may indicate lower margins but could also indicate a strong business-to-business relationship. |
| Production Loss During Aging | For aged spirits like whisky, some product is lost to evaporation during the aging process (known as the "Angel's Share"). This loss can affect inventory levels and profitability. |
| Proprietary Yeast Strains | For breweries and distilleries, this KPI tracks the development and use of proprietary yeast strains, which can contribute to unique product profiles and potentially provide a competitive advantage. |
| Quality of Raw Material | This KPI tracks the quality of the primary raw materials (like grapes, grains, yeast, etc.) which can greatly impact the quality of the final product. It can be assessed by multiple factors, such as sugar content, acidity, or purity. |
| Ratio of Contract Brewing to In-house Production | For breweries, this KPI measures the extent to which production is outsourced (contract brewing) versus produced in-house. This can affect costs, production flexibility, and quality control. |
| Ratio of Reserve Wines | For Champagne and other sparkling wine producers, the percentage of reserve wines (older wines saved from previous years) used in the blend can influence the quality, consistency, and price of the product. |
| Ratio of Vintage to Non-Vintage Sales | For wineries, this KPI tracks the ratio of sales of vintage wines (made from grapes harvested in a particular year) to non-vintage wines (blended from multiple years). This can indicate a winery's ability to produce high-quality vintage wines and the stability of its revenue. |
| Soil Health Metrics | For vineyards and some breweries that grow their own grains, this KPI can track soil health factors like nutrient content, pH level, and microbial activity, which can impact crop health and yield. |
| Sustainable Farming Practices | This can be measured by the percentage of grapes or other raw materials sourced from farms using organic, biodynamic, or other sustainable farming methods. As consumers become more environmentally conscious, this KPI could impact a company's brand and sales. |
| Terroir Quality Assessment | This is particularly applicable for wineries, as the quality of the vineyard's terroir (the environmental conditions, especially soil and climate, in which grapes are grown) can significantly affect the quality of the wine. |
| Varietal Concentration | This KPI applies to wineries and measures the concentration of grape varietals used in wine production. A higher concentration might indicate a specialization, while a diverse variety could appeal to a broader consumer base. |
| Vintage Ratings | For wine producers, the quality of a vintage can significantly impact sales and pricing. Ratings by wine critics and consumers can serve as a KPI. |
| Vintage Yield | This is a measure of the amount of wine produced per unit of land. It is especially significant for vineyards. The yield can vary depending on the weather and other environmental conditions, which can directly impact the cost of goods sold (COGS) and profitability. |
| Visitor Metrics (for wineries/breweries/distilleries) | The number of visitors and average revenue per visitor can be key KPIs for companies that offer tastings or tours, which can be a significant revenue source. |
| Waste Ratio | This KPI measures the amount of waste produced per unit of output, including water, spent grains, and other byproducts. It's an important measure of sustainability and operational efficiency. |
| Wine Club Membership and Retention | For wineries with subscription or membership programs, this KPI tracks the number of members and how long they stay members. It can be a significant source of recurring revenue and customer loyalty. |
| Yeast Strain Diversity | For breweries, this measures the diversity of yeast strains used, which can impact the variety and uniqueness of the beers produced. |
| Yield per Hectare (for hop farms or vineyards) | This measures the production of hops or grapes per unit of land, which can be used to assess the efficiency of agricultural operations and predict future production capacity. |
