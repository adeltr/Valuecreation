# 11 · Deferred Profitability and Surplus Analysis for Long-Duration Contracts

> **Part:** [[B - Actuarial and Reserve Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the deferred profitability and surplus for long-duration life insurance contracts, ensuring that future profits are appropriately recognized, reserves are adequate, and the company meets regulatory requirements. This analysis identifies areas for financial optimization and ensures proper alignment of reserves with future liabilities.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policy-level data:** ○ Policy type (e.g., whole life, universal life, annuities). ○ Premium payment schedules and amounts. ○ Coverage amounts and guarantees.
2. **Actuarial assumptions:** ○ Mortality, lapse, and morbidity rates. ○ Discount rates and expected investment returns.
3. **Financial data:** ○ Deferred acquisition costs (DAC). ○ Present value of future premiums and claims. ○ Historical surplus levels by product type.
4. Regulatory guidelines for surplus management and profitability reporting (e.g., IFRS 17 or GAAP).
5. Industry benchmarks for surplus and profitability metrics.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate Present Value of Future Cash Flows (PVFCF)
`a. Use the formula: PVFCF = Present Value of Future Premiums - Present Value of Future Claims and Expenses`
b. Apply actuarial assumptions for discount rates, mortality, and lapses.
2. Assess Deferred Profitability a. For each policy segment, evaluate the expected profitability over the contract's duration by comparing the PVFCF to initial reserves. b. Identify periods where deferred profitability is expected to materialize.
3. Analyze Surplus Levels
`a. Calculate the surplus for each segment: Surplus = Reserves Held - PVFCF`
b. Identify segments with surplus shortfalls or excessive surpluses.

4. Segment Analysis by Policy Type a. Break down profitability and surplus by product type, payment frequency, and demographic segment.
5. **Stress Testing a. Model adverse scenarios, such as:** ■ Higher-than-expected lapse rates. ■ Lower-than-expected investment returns. ■ Adverse mortality/morbidity experience. b. Quantify the impact on deferred profitability and surplus.
6. Benchmark Against Industry Metrics a. Compare profitability and surplus levels with industry standards to evaluate competitive positioning.
7. Visualize Trends a. Generate charts showing the expected realization of deferred profits over time. b. Use heatmaps to identify high-risk segments with surplus deficiencies or negative profitability.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing PVFCF, deferred profitability, and surplus levels by policy type and segment.
- Charts showing deferred profitability realization over time for long-duration contracts.
- Stress testing results highlighting surplus vulnerabilities under adverse conditions.
- Key findings report identifying high-risk products and surplus optimization opportunities.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Positive deferred profitability indicates policies are expected to generate sufficient future cash flows to cover claims, expenses, and acquisition costs.
2. Surplus shortfalls suggest inadequate reserves, posing a risk to financial stability and compliance with regulatory standards.
3. Excessive surpluses may indicate overly conservative assumptions, potentially tying up capital unnecessarily.
4. Stress test results reveal the company’s resilience to adverse conditions and highlight areas needing reserve adjustments.

## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Regularly review and update actuarial assumptions, such as lapse, mortality, and investment rates, to reflect current trends.
2. Enhance reserve management by reallocating capital to segments with identified shortfalls.
3. Optimize product pricing and benefit structures to improve long-term profitability.
4. Implement stress testing frameworks to anticipate and prepare for adverse conditions affecting surplus and profitability.
5. Monitor regulatory changes and align reserve and surplus management practices with new standards (e.g., IFRS 17).
6. Use predictive modeling to improve accuracy in forecasting future cash flows and profitability.
7. Identify and phase out or adjust products with consistently negative deferred profitability.
