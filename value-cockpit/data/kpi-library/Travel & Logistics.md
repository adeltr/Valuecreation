# KPI — Travel & Logistics

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Travel & Hospitality]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Airline Company - Analyse]], [[Third Party Logistics Company - Analyse]]

64 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Available Air Cargo Tonne-kilometres (ACTK) | Price indicator for freight forwarding, compared to "air cargo tonne-kilometres (CTK)"; reflects air cargo capacity vs. Air cargo demand, sometimes visualized by Demand-supply ratio. |
| Available Seat Kilometers (ASK) / Available Seat Miles (ASM) | This is a measure of an airline flight's passenger carrying capacity. It's calculated by multiplying the number of seats available on each flight by the number of kilometers or miles that flight travels. This helps airlines manage supply in terms of seating capacity. |
| Average Daily Rate (ADR) | This is a key performance indicator in the hotel industry. It represents the average rental income per paid occupied room in a given time period. A higher ADR indicates a better ability to charge higher prices. |
| Average Length of Haul | This is a critical KPI for logistics and freight companies, indicating the average distance (in miles or kilometers) that freight is being transported. It can impact fuel costs, equipment utilization, and labor costs, and is a factor in pricing decisions. |
| Baggage Handling Errors | For airlines, this KPI measures the number of mishandled baggage reports per unit of passengers. Lower rates indicate higher efficiency in baggage handling, which enhances customer satisfaction. |
| Booking Conversion Rate | For online travel agencies and booking platforms, this measures the percentage of website or app visitors who make a booking. A higher rate can suggest a more effective user interface and booking process. |
| Booking Lead Time | This is crucial for companies in the travel industry. It refers to the amount of time between when a reservation is made and the actual use of the service. A longer booking lead time helps with forecasting demand and managing capacity. |
| Breakeven Load Factor | This is crucial for airlines and indicates the minimum passenger load factor an airline needs to cover its costs. It gives an idea about the company's cost structure and efficiency. |
| Carbon Footprint | This environmental KPI measures the total greenhouse gas emissions caused by a company's activities. It's increasingly important in travel and logistics due to growing environmental concerns. |
| Cargo Load Factor | This measures the percentage of available cargo capacity that is utilized. It's especially relevant for air freight and shipping companies. A higher cargo load factor means better capacity utilization and increased efficiency. |
| Churn Rate | For subscription-based travel services, this measures the proportion of contractual customers or subscribers who leave during a given period. Lower churn rates are desirable as they indicate higher customer loyalty. |
| CM2 | Contribution margin 2. |
| Cost per Available Seat Kilometer (CASK) / Cost per Available Seat Mile (CASM) | These are significant cost indicators for airlines, representing the cost incurred to offer one kilometer/mile of seating capacity. These metrics help in understanding an airline's cost efficiency. |
| Cruise Occupancy Rate | For the cruise industry, this KPI measures the percentage of available passenger capacity that's being utilized. It helps understand the demand for a cruise line's services and its capacity management effectiveness. |
| Door-to-Door Delivery Time | In logistics, this is the time taken from pick-up of a package to its delivery at the recipient's doorstep. Shorter delivery times indicate better service quality and operational efficiency. |
| Dwell Time | In logistics, dwell time refers to the time a vehicle spends at a scheduled stop without loading or unloading. Lower dwell time indicates higher operational efficiency. |
| Employee Turnover Rate | This is especially relevant for companies in this industry due to the high level of interaction between employees and customers. High turnover can be a sign of low employee satisfaction, which can impact customer service and increase costs due to hiring and training new employees. |
| Fill Rate | In logistics, the fill rate is the fraction of customer demand that is met through immediate stock availability, without backorders or lost sales. A higher fill rate signifies better inventory management. |
| First-Time Fix Rate | Specific to the maintenance aspect of airlines, cruise lines, and freight companies, this measures the percentage of maintenance issues resolved on the first attempt. A high first-time fix rate implies efficient use of resources and less downtime. |
| Freight Bill Accuracy | In logistics, this measures the percentage of freight bills that are free of errors. High accuracy reduces disputes, delays, and administrative costs. |
| Freight Tonnage | This is specific to logistics companies, particularly those in freight transportation, and often called "volume." It measures the total weight of freight transported over a specific period. An increase in this KPI can indicate higher demand for freight services.  |
| Fuel Cost as Percentage of Revenue | This KPI is highly relevant in the travel and logistics industry, given that fuel cost is a significant operating expense for these companies, especially for airlines and shipping companies. Changes in this KPI can significantly affect profitability. |
| Fuel Efficiency | For airlines and shipping companies, this measures the distance travelled per unit of fuel. Better fuel efficiency can indicate better cost management and lower environmental impact. |
| Global Average Rate (GAR) | Index for global average rate of airfreight capacity, measured in cost per kg. |
| GP (Margin) | Gross profit (margin) per kg or t (revenue doesn't count in freight forwarding). |
| Guests per Employee | For hotels and other hospitality businesses, this ratio measures productivity and service quality. A higher ratio could indicate higher productivity, but could also lead to reduced service quality if it's too high. |
| Hotel Overbooking Rate | For the hospitality industry, overbooking is a common practice to compensate for no-shows. This KPI measures the rate at which overbooking occurs. While some degree of overbooking can be profitable, excessive overbooking can lead to customer dissatisfaction. |
| Inventory Turnover | For logistics companies dealing with warehousing, this measures the number of times inventory is sold or used in a time period. It shows how effectively a company is managing its stock. |
| Landed Cost | In freight and shipping, this is the total cost of a product once it has arrived at a buyer's premises. It includes the purchase price, freight, insurance, and other costs up to the port of destination. It's crucial for logistics companies to manage these costs effectively. |
| Load Factor | This is a key metric for airlines that indicates the percentage of available seating capacity that is filled with passengers. A higher load factor means that more seats are filled, implying better efficiency and potentially higher revenues. |
| Loss Files | Number of files with no profit or loss. |
| Market Share | Important to manage global carrier relations, as well as comparison to other freight forwarding companies. |
| Negative Cost Accrual | When the cost accrual is less than the actual cost of the file and forwarder did not adjust the cost accrual. |
| Negative Revenue Accrual | When the revenue estimation / accrual is less than the invoice issued and forwarder did not adjust the revenue accrual. |
| NFD | One of the most important KPI to measure in time performance of cargo airlines = cargo and documents ready for pickup at airline (or arline's handling agent), measured in % against service level agreement. |
| Occupancy Rate | This is a critical KPI for hotels, measuring the proportion of rooms that are occupied. A higher rate is generally better and suggests strong demand, good pricing power, and effective marketing and operations. |
| On-Time In Full (OTIF) Delivery Rate | For logistics and shipping companies, OTIF measures the percentage of goods delivered on the promised date in the promised quantity. A high OTIF rate indicates reliability and efficiency. Also called "SLA Quality" - Service Level Agreement Quality. |
| On-Time Performance | This is crucial for both airlines and logistics companies. For airlines, it measures the percentage of flights departing or arriving within 15 minutes of the scheduled time. For logistics companies, it's the percentage of deliveries made on time. It is a direct indicator of operational efficiency and service quality. |
| Online Booking Rate | This KPI measures the percentage of total bookings made through online channels, which is increasingly important in the digital age. A higher online booking rate may signify successful digital marketing efforts and a tech-savvy customer base. |
| Open Files | Number of open files to control that no files are open older than eg. 12 months. |
| Operating Expense Ratio (OER) | For cruise lines and airlines, this measures operating expenses as a percentage of revenue. It's a measure of operational efficiency and profitability. |
| Order Accuracy Rate | This is a critical metric for logistics companies, measuring the percentage of orders shipped without errors (wrong item, incorrect quantity, damage, etc.). High order accuracy rate contributes to customer satisfaction and reduces costs associated with returns. |
| Passenger Complaints | This KPI tracks the number of registered complaints from passengers in the airline, rail, bus, and cruise sectors. A decrease in passenger complaints generally indicates improved service quality. |
| Passenger Revenue per Employee | In the airline industry, this measures the amount of passenger revenue generated per employee. It can help gauge employee productivity and overall operational efficiency. |
| Perfect Order Rate | This logistics KPI measures the percentage of orders that are delivered on time, complete, and undamaged. A higher perfect order rate signifies more efficient and effective logistics operations. |
| Productivity | Main KPI to measure strength of workforce, measured by closed files per time period (eg. number of closed files per FTE per month). |
| Rate of Reservations via Mobile | For the travel and hospitality sectors, this KPI tracks the percentage of total reservations made via mobile devices. An increasing rate may indicate successful mobile engagement strategies. |
| RCS | One of the most important KPI to measure quality of cargo airlines = cargo and documents received "Ready for carriage" and accepted by airline (or airline's handling agent), measured in percentage. |
| Revenue Passenger Kilometers (RPK) / Revenue Passenger Miles (RPM) | This measures the number of kilometers or miles traveled by paying passengers. It's calculated by multiplying the number of revenue-paying passengers by the distance traveled. It's crucial for airlines to monitor this as it helps them understand demand trends and capacity utilization. |
| Revenue per Occupied Room (RevPOR) | This is a variant of RevPAR, used in the hotel industry, which measures the average total revenue (including food, beverages, and other services) per occupied room, not just room revenue like RevPAR. It provides insight into a hotel's ability to upsell additional services. |
| RevPAR (Revenue Per Available Room) | This is a performance metric in the hotel industry that is calculated by dividing a hotel's total guestroom revenue by the room count and the number of days in the period being measured. It helps assess the ability to fill its available rooms at an average rate. |
| RevPASH (Revenue Per Available Seat Hour) | This KPI is commonly used in the restaurant sector of the hospitality industry. It measures the revenue generated per seat per hour, providing an understanding of how efficiently a restaurant utilizes its capacity. |
| Room Service Efficiency | For hotels, this KPI measures the average time taken to fulfill room service orders. Faster service generally leads to higher customer satisfaction. |
| Same-store Sales Growth | In the hospitality industry, particularly in hotel chains, this measures the sales growth in existing stores or properties over a certain period, excluding new ones. This helps gauge organic growth and the effectiveness of operational strategies. |
| Service Diversification | For travel companies, this KPI measures the diversity of services provided. A higher level of diversification can indicate more robust business models less susceptible to market fluctuations. |
| Ship Turnaround Time | For shipping and cruise line companies, this measures the time it takes for a ship to unload passengers or cargo, undergo maintenance, and reload for the next journey. Efficient turnaround times are crucial for maintaining schedules and improving asset utilization. |
| Slot Utilization | In logistics and port management, this refers to the number of slots used over the total slots available in a port or a warehouse over a certain period. High slot utilization reflects efficient use of resources. |
| Ticket Sales per Concert | Specific to the travel sector dealing with entertainment, this KPI gauges the average number of tickets sold per concert or event. It can indicate the popularity of events and the effectiveness of marketing campaigns. |
| Tour Operator Margin | Specific to the tour operator segment, this KPI measures the difference between the price at which a tour is sold to customers and the cost of producing the tour. It reflects pricing power and cost control in the tour operating business. |
| Transportation Cost per Unit | This is a significant KPI in logistics, measuring the cost incurred to transport each unit of a product. A lower cost indicates more efficient transportation operations. |
| Turnaround Time | For airlines, this is the time taken to unload passengers and cargo from an arriving flight, prepare the aircraft for the next flight, and load passengers and cargo. For logistics, it's the time taken to complete a process from start to finish (e.g., time taken for delivery from the point of pick-up). It's a direct indicator of operational efficiency. |
| Vehicle Utilization Rate | In transportation and logistics, this measures the proportion of a company's fleet that's being used at any given time. Higher utilization often implies more efficient use of assets. |
| Warehousing Cost per Order | This measures the warehousing costs associated with each order processed, relevant for logistics companies. It helps understand the efficiency and productivity of warehouse operations. |
| Yield | In the airline industry, yield refers to the average fare paid per kilometer, per passenger. It's a crucial measure of profitability and pricing power, particularly for cargo airlines and freight forwarding. |
