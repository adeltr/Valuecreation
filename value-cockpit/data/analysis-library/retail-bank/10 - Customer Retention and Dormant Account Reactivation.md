# 10 · Customer Retention and Dormant Account Reactivation Strategy Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Customer Behavior and Engagement]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Retail Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate retention rates and identify strategies to reduce customer churn. Additionally, to analyze dormant accounts and develop reactivation strategies to re-engage inactive customers, increasing overall account activity and profitability.


## 📊 Data required
1. Historical customer data, including account tenure and product usage patterns.
2. **Retention rates:** percentage of customers retained year over year.
3. **Dormant account data:** accounts with no activity for a specified period (e.g., 6 months).
4. Transaction history and engagement data (e.g., frequency of branch visits, online logins).
5. Customer demographics and segmentation data.
6. Feedback or survey data from dormant and churned customers.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate Retention and Churn Rates ○ Retention Rate = (Number of Customers Retained / Total Customers at Start of Period) x 100 ○ Churn Rate = 100 - Retention Rate ○ Segment these metrics by demographics, product ownership, or customer value tiers.
2. Identify Dormant Accounts ○ Define dormancy criteria (e.g., accounts with no transactions or logins in the past 6 months). ○ Segment dormant accounts by customer type (e.g., savings, checking, or credit card holders).
3. Analyze Dormancy Causes ○ Examine historical activity to identify patterns leading to dormancy (e.g., reduced transactions, high fees). ○ Use customer feedback or exit surveys to uncover reasons for inactivity.
4. **Behavioral Patterns of Retained vs. Lost Customers:** 31 ○ Compare transaction and engagement data between retained and churned customers. ○ Identify key behaviors linked to retention (e.g., regular digital banking use, multiple product ownership).
5. Dormant Account Profiling ○ Create profiles of dormant account holders based on demographic and behavioral data. ○ Determine which accounts are most likely to reactivate based on prior activity levels or product usage.
6. **Develop Retention and Reactivation Strategies:** For retention:
- Offer loyalty rewards or discounts for long-term customers.
- Implement early warning systems to identify and address at-risk customers.
For reactivation:
○ Use targeted campaigns offering incentives (e.g., fee waivers, bonus interest) for reactivation. ○ Personalize outreach using channels preferred by dormant customers (email, SMS, or phone).
7. Monitor and Test Strategy Effectiveness ○ Track retention rate improvements and reactivation campaign success rates:
`Reactivation Rate = (Number of Reactivated Accounts / Total Dormant Accounts Targeted) x 100 ○ Use A/B testing to optimize reactivation messages and offers.`


## 📤 Output format
- Retention and churn rates segmented by customer type.
- Dormant account profiles with demographics and inactivity patterns.
- Charts showing trends in retention rates and reactivation campaign performance.
- A prioritized list of actionable strategies with expected impact.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Low retention rates or high churn rates highlight issues in customer satisfaction or engagement.

2. Insights from dormant account profiling can guide resource allocation for reactivation efforts.
3. High reactivation rates in response to targeted campaigns indicate effective messaging and incentives.
4. Behavioral patterns of retained customers can serve as benchmarks for at-risk or dormant customers.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Implement proactive retention strategies, such as personalized check-ins or offers for at-risk customers.
2. Develop targeted campaigns for dormant accounts with attractive reactivation incentives.
3. Improve customer satisfaction by addressing common pain points (e.g., high fees, complex processes).
4. Promote account activity through gamification or reward programs for frequent transactions.
5. Regularly monitor churn and dormancy trends to identify emerging risks and adjust strategies accordingly.
