# KPI — Energy

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Energy & Natural Resources]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Electricity Generation Company - Analyse]]

51 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Average Energy Demand | The average amount of energy required over a specific period. This is particularly important for utility companies in understanding consumption patterns and predicting future demand. |
| Baseload Capacity | For power generation companies, the amount of continuous power that can be produced to meet basic demand. This can be a crucial indicator of a company's ability to fulfill its supply obligations. |
| Capacity Factor (for Power Generation) | This measures the actual electrical output of a power plant over a period of time compared to its maximum potential output. A higher capacity factor indicates a more efficient use of infrastructure. |
| Carbon Intensity | Given the increasing focus on climate change and the energy transition, the amount of carbon dioxide (CO2) emitted per unit of energy produced is becoming a crucial KPI. Lower carbon intensity can indicate a company's progress toward cleaner operations and its resilience in a decarbonizing world. |
| Cash Operating Costs per Barrel (or BOE) | This measures the direct cash costs associated with producing a barrel of oil equivalent (BOE), allowing investors to compare operational efficiency across companies. |
| Cycle Time | In oil and gas exploration and production, this KPI measures the time from investment decision until the start of production. Faster cycle times can indicate better project execution and quicker return on investment. |
| Days Away, Restricted or Transferred (DART) Rate | This KPI measures safety performance in the energy industry, specifically the number of incidents resulting in time away from work, restricted work activity, or job transfers per 200,000 hours worked. |
| Decline Curve Analysis | Used in the oil and gas industry, this KPI analyzes production decline rates to predict future performance and reserve life. |
| Distributed Generation Capacity | For companies in the utilities or renewable energy sectors, this KPI measures the amount of electricity generating capacity located close to the point of consumption. |
| Drilling Success Rate | For upstream oil and gas companies, the number of successful wells drilled as a percentage of total attempts can indicate the company's exploration competence. |
| Emission Rates | Particularly important in the era of increased regulation and social responsibility, this KPI measures the amount of pollutants (such as CO2, methane, etc.) emitted per unit of energy produced. |
| Energy Efficiency Ratio (EER) | Used in the heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) industry, EER measures the energy efficiency of a cooling device, defined as the ratio of the cooling capacity (in British thermal units per hour) to the power input (in watts). |
| Energy Intensity (for Energy Efficiency) | This is a measure of the energy efficiency of a nation's economy. It is calculated as units of energy per unit of GDP. Lower energy intensity often indicates a higher level of energy efficiency. |
| Energy Per Customer | For utility companies, the average amount of energy consumed per customer can provide insight into demand trends. |
| Energy Return on Investment (EROI) | This is a measure of the amount of usable energy that is produced from a given amount of input energy. It's a key measure for renewable energy sources. |
| Finding and Development (F&D) Cost | The cost incurred for discovering new reserves and bringing them to production. Lower F&D costs can indicate more efficient operations and higher profit margins. |
| Fuel Cost per MMBtu | This is the cost of fuel per million British thermal units (MMBtu). It's a critical measure for companies involved in the production and supply of energy. |
| Fuel Efficiency Rate | A measure of the amount of power generated per unit of fuel used. This can be used in various energy generation processes, with higher rates indicating more efficient use of fuel. |
| Full Cycle Costs | This includes all costs associated with finding, acquiring, developing, and producing oil and gas. It's a crucial metric to evaluate the complete costs related to oil and gas exploration and production. |
| Gas/Oil Ratio (GOR) | This is a measure of the amount of gas that comes out of a well along with each unit of oil. The ratio can be an important indicator of the well's longevity and profitability. |
| Grid Reliability (SAIDI and SAIFI) | These are used in the power sector, where System Average Interruption Duration Index (SAIDI) and System Average Interruption Frequency Index (SAIFI) measure the reliability of electricity supply. |
| Heat Rate (for Power Generation) | This is a measure of efficiency for power plants that use thermal energy. It represents the amount of thermal energy (usually measured in BTU) required to generate one kilowatt-hour (kWh) of electricity. A lower heat rate indicates a more efficient power plant. |
| Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE) | A measure of the average net present cost of electricity generation for a generating plant over its lifetime. It is a useful metric for comparing different technologies or energy sources. |
| Liquids vs. Gas Ratio | For oil and gas companies, the ratio of liquids (like oil) to gas can be significant. Because liquids generally sell at higher prices, companies with a higher liquids-to-gas ratio might have higher margins. |
| Load Factor (for Utilities) | This measures the usage of a system's total capacity. For an electrical grid, it would be the total electricity consumed divided by the total electricity that could have been consumed if all resources were fully utilized. |
| Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR) | In a high-risk industry like energy, particularly in segments like oil and gas, the LTIFR measures the number of lost time injuries occurring in a workplace per 1 million hours worked. |
| Megawatts (MW) Installed/Under Management | For power generation companies or utilities, the total capacity that they have installed or under management can be a key performance indicator. |
| Net Energy Metering (NEM) Customers | For utilities, the number of customers who are generating their own energy (through solar panels, for example) and selling it back to the grid can be a significant KPI. |
| O&M Costs per MWh | This KPI measures the operating and maintenance costs per megawatt-hour (MWh) of energy produced. It can provide insights into a power plant's efficiency and profitability. |
| Operating Netback | This is a measure of oil and gas sales revenue net of royalties, production, and transportation expenses. It represents the cash margin received for each barrel of oil or cubic feet of gas sold and can be a strong indicator of financial health. |
| Operational Efficiency | Measured by the ratio of useful output to the total input in energy production. Higher operational efficiency can lead to lower operational costs and higher profits. |
| Pipeline Capacity Utilization | For oil and gas transportation companies, the utilization rate of their pipeline capacity is a crucial KPI. High utilization can mean strong demand and higher profitability. |
| Planned Outage Factor | A measure of the planned outage time for a plant or a system of plants as a percentage of the total available time. Lower planned outage factor means more efficiency and availability. |
| Plant Availability Factor | This measures the percentage of total time that a plant is available to produce power, excluding the time that the plant is offline due to planned maintenance or outages. A higher factor indicates higher reliability and efficiency. |
| Plant Load Factor (PLF) | For power generation companies, the PLF measures the actual output of a power plant compared to the maximum it could produce. Higher PLF usually indicates better utilization and efficiency. |
| Processing Gain/Loss | For refining companies, this KPI tracks the change in volume or quality between the input (crude oil or feedstock) and the output (refined products). Processing gains can positively impact profitability. |
| Proven Reserves | The total amount of energy reserves (such as oil or gas) that a company has confirmed with reasonable certainty to be recoverable in future years from known reservoirs under existing economic and operating conditions. This can indicate the company's potential for future production. |
| Prudhoe Bay Royalty Trust (PBR) | A unique indicator for oil and gas companies, the PBR provides a measure of the net profits from oil fields. Companies with higher PBR may offer more attractive investment opportunities. |
| Refining Capacity | For companies involved in the refining sector, the total volume of crude oil they can process in a given timeframe is an important indicator of their capabilities. Greater refining capacity can translate to more revenues. |
| Refining Complexity Index | A measure of the complexity and sophistication of a refinery, with higher numbers indicating the ability to process heavier, lower-quality crude into higher-margin products. |
| Renewable Portfolio Standards (RPS) Compliance | For utilities in areas with Renewable Portfolio Standards, the percentage of their energy portfolio that comes from renewable sources can be a significant KPI. Non-compliance can result in penalties and reputational risks. |
| Reserve Life Index (RLI) | This is calculated as the total remaining proven reserves divided by the current production rate. The index provides an estimate of the lifespan of the reserve if extraction continues at the current rate. |
| Reserve Replacement Ratio (RRR) | This is the ratio of the volume of reserves added to a company's reserve base during the year to the volume of oil and gas produced during the same year. A ratio greater than 100% indicates that more reserves were added than produced, which is a positive sign for the company's future production capabilities. |
| Reserve-to-Production Ratio (R/P) | This ratio indicates the number of years that the remaining reserves would last if production rates were constant at the current level. |
| Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) in Upstream Operations | While ROCE is a common metric across industries, looking at it specifically for upstream operations (exploration and production) in energy companies can provide valuable insights into the efficiency and profitability of this core part of their business. |
| Solar Panel Efficiency | For solar energy companies, the efficiency of their solar panels, or how much of the sunlight the panel can convert into usable electricity, is a crucial KPI. |
| Spillage Rate | For oil and gas companies, the rate of unintended release of oil or other products (usually measured in barrels) can be a critical KPI, reflecting both financial losses and environmental impact. |
| Total Recordable Incident Rate (TRIR) | This safety metric measures the number of workplace injuries per 200,000 hours worked. Lower TRIRs can indicate stronger safety practices and lower risk of operational disruption. |
| Unplanned Outages | For utilities or power generation companies, the frequency and duration of unplanned outages can be a significant KPI, reflecting on the reliability of service and potential regulatory compliance issues. |
| Water Usage Efficiency | Given the intensive water usage in many energy generation processes, the ratio of energy produced to water used can be a significant KPI. |
| Wind Turbine Capacity Factor | This is used in the renewable energy sector, specifically wind energy. It is the actual power output of a turbine over a period of time as a percentage of its maximum rated power output. Higher factors indicate more productive turbines. |
