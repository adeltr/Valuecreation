# 13 · Mortality Margin and Experience Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Actuarial and Reserve Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the difference between expected and actual mortality experience, ensuring that mortality margins are sufficient to cover risks. The analysis identifies deviations, helps improve actuarial assumptions, and ensures the financial stability of life insurance products.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policyholder-level data:** age, gender, policy type, policy start date, and status (active, deceased, or lapsed).
2. **Expected mortality data:** mortality rates from the actuarial model or standard mortality tables.
3. **Actual mortality data:** observed deaths over a defined period.
4. **Policy exposure data:** total number of lives or exposure periods in the portfolio.
5. **Financial data:** premium and reserve allocations for mortality margins.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate Actual Mortality Rate Use the formula:
`Actual Mortality Rate (q_actual) = Number of Deaths / Number of Lives Exposed.`
2. Calculate Expected Mortality Rate Extract expected mortality rates (q_expected) from the mortality table or actuarial assumptions for the same cohort.
3. Determine Mortality Margin Mortality Margin = (Expected Mortality Rate - Actual Mortality Rate) / Expected Mortality Rate x 100. This measures the degree to which actual mortality differs from expected values.
4. Compare Results Across Segments ○ Segment results by age, gender, and policy type to identify variations in mortality margins. ○ Analyze high-risk cohorts where actual mortality exceeds expected values.
5. Calculate Mortality Experience Ratio Mortality Experience Ratio = Actual Deaths / Expected Deaths x 100. Ratios greater than 100% indicate mortality losses, while lower ratios suggest favorable mortality experience.
6. Trend Analysis ○ Conduct a time-series analysis to identify trends in mortality experience over time.
37 ○ Compare current margins to historical performance.
7. Benchmark Against Industry Standards Compare mortality margins and experience ratios with industry benchmarks or standard tables to determine competitive positioning.
8. Visualize Results ○ Create tables summarizing mortality experience ratios and margins across key segments. ○ Generate charts to show deviations in actual vs. expected mortality over time.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables showing mortality margins and experience ratios by age group, gender, and policy type.
- Charts displaying actual vs. expected mortality trends over time.
- Key findings highlighting segments with favorable or unfavorable mortality experience.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. A positive mortality margin indicates favorable mortality experience, where actual mortality is lower than expected, leading to profitability.
2. A negative margin or experience ratio above 100% signals higher-than-expected deaths, which can erode reserves and profitability.
3. Consistent negative margins across specific segments suggest inadequate pricing or actuarial assumptions that need adjustment.
4. Benchmark comparisons reveal whether observed mortality experience aligns with industry norms.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Update mortality assumptions regularly based on actual experience data to ensure alignment with current trends.
2. Improve underwriting practices to better screen for mortality risks at the point of sale.
3. Enhance risk selection processes for high-risk demographics or product types.
4. Develop targeted programs to improve retention and behavior in high-risk cohorts (e.g., wellness initiatives).
5. Use predictive analytics to monitor and project mortality trends, identifying early deviations.
6. Explore reinsurance strategies to mitigate exposure to segments with consistently adverse mortality experience.
