# 9 · Aircraft Leasing Strategy and Buy vs. Lease Evaluation

> **Part:** [[B - Fleet and Asset Optimization]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Airline Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Aircraft Leasing Strategy and Buy vs. Lease Evaluation is to assess the financial and operational implications of leasing versus purchasing aircraft. This analysis helps determine the most cost-effective approach to fleet acquisition and maintenance, optimizing capital expenditures and aligning with the airline’s growth and demand forecast.


## 📊 Data required
- **Current fleet composition:** owned vs. leased aircraft
- Lease terms and conditions, including lease duration, renewal options, and costs
- Purchase costs for new and used aircraft, including financing rates
- Estimated residual value of owned aircraft over time
- Maintenance and operating costs for leased vs. owned aircraft
- Aircraft depreciation rates and associated tax benefits
- Demand forecasts to determine long-term fleet requirements
- Cash flow forecasts and capital availability


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Analyze Current Fleet Composition:** Assess the current mix of owned and leased aircraft, noting the terms of existing leases, ownership durations, and any upcoming lease renewals. This provides a baseline for evaluating potential changes in fleet acquisition strategy.
2. Calculate Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)
For owned aircraft, calculate the TCO, including purchase price, maintenance, depreciation, and financing costs. Use the formula:
`TCO = Purchase Price + (Total Maintenance Costs + Depreciation - Residual Value)`
Determine the long-term financial impact of ownership, accounting for tax depreciation benefits.
3. **Calculate Total Cost of Leasing:** For leased aircraft, calculate the total cost over the lease term, including monthly lease payments and any additional operational or maintenance costs borne by the lessee. Use the formula:
`Total Lease Cost = Monthly Lease Payment x Lease Term + Maintenance Costs`
Compare this cost with the TCO for owned aircraft.
4. **Assess Flexibility Needs:** Evaluate the airline’s need for fleet flexibility based on demand forecasts and route changes. Leasing may offer more flexibility for short-term demand or route testing, while ownership can be advantageous for stable, long-term demand on established routes.
5. **Evaluate Cash Flow and Capital Availability:** Review the airline’s cash flow and capital budget. Leasing may require lower initial outlay, preserving cash for other investments, whereas purchasing requires significant upfront capital but can offer long-term savings.
6. **Consider Residual Value and Depreciation:** Project the residual value of owned aircraft over time, factoring in depreciation. For long-term ownership, high-residual-value aircraft may provide better returns on investment compared to low-residual-value aircraft, especially if resale or trade-in opportunities arise.
7. **Compare Operational Costs for Leased vs. Owned Aircraft:** Analyze operating and maintenance costs for leased versus owned aircraft. Leases may include maintenance clauses that shift costs to the lessor, reducing operational expenses, whereas ownership may entail higher maintenance costs over time.
8. **Scenario Testing for Buy vs. Lease Decisions:** Run scenarios to assess the impact of purchasing versus leasing under various market conditions (e.g., fuel price fluctuations, demand changes, interest rates). Analyze each scenario’s impact on cash flow, profitability, and flexibility.


## 📤 Output format
- **Fleet Composition Summary:** A report showing current owned and leased aircraft, including lease terms and ownership duration.
- **TCO and Lease Cost Comparison Table:** A side-by-side comparison of TCO and lease costs by aircraft type, including any maintenance or operational cost differences.
- **Flexibility and Cash Flow Analysis:** An assessment of capital requirements, cash flow impact, and flexibility needs for leasing vs. buying.
- **Scenario Analysis Results:** Charts or tables summarizing the financial and operational outcomes of different buy vs. lease strategies.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Fleet Composition Summary provides insight into the current mix and upcoming renewals, helping plan for future fleet adjustments.
- TCO and Lease Cost Comparison Table highlights the financial differences between leasing and owning, helping determine the most cost-effective option.
- Flexibility and Cash Flow Analysis shows the impact of each strategy on financial flexibility and alignment with demand forecasts.
- Scenario Analysis Results offer projections on the effects of market changes, helping choose the best option based on anticipated market conditions.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize Fleet Mix:** Balance owned and leased aircraft to align with demand forecasts, ensuring flexibility without overextending capital.
2. **Leverage Purchase Options in Lease Agreements:** Include purchase options in lease contracts, allowing the airline to acquire aircraft if demand remains stable.
3. **Prioritize High-Residual-Value Aircraft for Ownership:** Focus ownership on aircraft types with high resale value, optimizing long-term return on investment.
4. **Negotiate Lease Terms:** Secure favorable lease terms, such as lower maintenance costs or flexible renewal options, to manage costs and retain operational flexibility.
5. **Monitor Market Conditions:** Regularly assess fuel prices, interest rates, and demand trends to adjust the buy vs. lease strategy as economic conditions evolve.
