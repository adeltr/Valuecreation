# 21 · Fleet Usage Segmentation Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Underwriting & Risk Segmentation]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Understand how different types of fleet usage (e.g., commercial deliveries, ride-sharing, employee shuttles) affect claim frequency, severity, and profitability, and determine optimal segmentation strategies to underwrite each fleet segment accurately.


## 📊 Data required
- Policy and fleet details (number of vehicles, vehicle classes, primary usage)
- Historical claims data (frequency, severity, total incurred losses) segmented by fleet usage type
- Exposure data (written premium, earned premium, in-force policies) for each fleet category
- Telematics or operational data (mileage, driving hours, routes) when available
- Competitor or industry benchmarks for fleet performance across different usage segments


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify all policies categorized under fleet coverage and compile data for each usage type (e.g., delivery, rideshare, corporate transport). Organize this data to include written premium, earned premium, incurred losses, and in-force policy counts for each segment.
2. Calculate claim frequency and severity for each usage segment by applying Claim Frequency = (Number of Claims / Number of Policies) x 100 and Severity = (Total Losses / Number of Claims). Compare these metrics across segments to identify higher-risk or more profitable categories.
3. Determine the fleet usage loss ratio using Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + LAE) / Earned Premium. Track these ratios over multiple years to observe trends or fluctuations in segment performance.
4. Assess any available telematics or operational data (e.g., total miles driven, routes, driver behavior) to refine insights on driving risk. Examine whether certain usage types have higher incident rates at specific times or locations.
5. Review current underwriting guidelines to see if they differentiate adequately among varied fleet uses. Check whether premium levels or coverage requirements are aligned with observed risk and usage patterns.
6. Compare internal fleet segment data to industry benchmarks if available. Look for major differences that could indicate underpricing or overpricing in specific usage categories.

7. Identify sub-segment variations within each fleet category (e.g., short-haul vs. long-haul deliveries) if sufficient data exists. Evaluate whether a more granular segmentation approach could improve overall profitability.
8. Summarize the findings and propose refined fleet segmentation, pricing adjustments, or underwriting rule changes. Present the analysis to pricing, underwriting, and product teams for potential implementation.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables showing claim frequency, severity, and loss ratios by fleet usage segment
- Comparative charts or graphs highlighting differences in performance among segments
- A written report summarizing key findings, recommended segmentation and pricing strategies, and anticipated financial impact


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Higher-than-average claim frequency or severity in a specific usage segment suggests the need for stricter underwriting or premium surcharges.
- Low loss ratios in certain segments may indicate an opportunity to expand market share or offer competitive pricing.
- Discrepancies between operational data (e.g., actual miles driven) and rated exposure can signal premium leakage or misrepresentation.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Refine segmentation criteria based on actual driving patterns, vehicle types, and usage intensities to more accurately match premium to risk.
2. Leverage telematics solutions for real-time or near-real-time monitoring, adjusting underwriting practices based on data-driven insights.
3. Introduce targeted safety programs or driver training for high-risk fleet segments to reduce claim frequency and severity.
4. Regularly benchmark fleet data against industry norms and update pricing strategies to remain both competitive and profitable.
