# 18 · Brand Perception and Consumer Sentiment Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Marketing and Consumer Insights]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Cosmetics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Brand Perception and Consumer Sentiment Analysis is to evaluate how consumers perceive a cosmetic brand in terms of reputation, trustworthiness, product quality, and overall emotional connection. This analysis measures both qualitative and quantitative consumer sentiment across various channels (e.g., social media, customer reviews, surveys) to understand the brand’s standing in the market. The insights help the company align its branding, marketing, and product strategies with consumer expectations and identify areas for improvement.


## 📊 Data required
- Consumer feedback from surveys, focus groups, and interviews.
- Social media mentions, comments, and sentiment analysis (positive, neutral, negative).
- Customer reviews on e-commerce platforms and beauty review websites.
- Brand performance data relative to competitors (e.g., brand loyalty, advocacy, market share).
- Net Promoter Score (NPS) and customer satisfaction (CSAT) scores.
- Media coverage and influencer partnerships analysis (e.g., tone, reach, engagement).
- Historical data on brand perception shifts over time.
- Consumer demographic data (e.g., age, gender, location) for segmentation analysis.
- Brand awareness metrics (e.g., aided and unaided recall).
- Key brand attributes being evaluated (e.g., luxury, clean beauty, eco-friendly, innovative).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Gather consumer feedback data:** a. Conduct surveys and focus groups to gather in-depth qualitative feedback on how consumers perceive the brand. b. Ask consumers to describe the brand in their own words and rate it on key attributes (e.g., product quality, innovation, trustworthiness). c. Analyze customer reviews and feedback from beauty blogs, review websites, and e-commerce platforms (e.g., Sephora, Amazon) to identify recurring themes.
2. **Perform social media sentiment analysis:** a. Use social listening tools to collect data on brand mentions across social media platforms (e.g., Instagram, Twitter, TikTok). b. Categorize mentions as positive, neutral, or negative to gauge overall sentiment. c. Identify trending hashtags, keywords, and consumer concerns related to the brand.
3. **Calculate Net Promoter Score (NPS) and customer satisfaction (CSAT):** a. Measure NPS by asking consumers how likely they are to recommend the brand to others on a scale of 1-10.
61 b. Calculate CSAT by asking consumers to rate their overall satisfaction with the brand and its products. c. Use these scores to benchmark the brand’s loyalty and satisfaction levels against competitors.
4. **Analyze brand awareness and recall:** a. Conduct surveys or online polls to measure aided and unaided brand recall. Ask consumers if they recognize the brand or recall its products without prompts. b. Measure how well consumers understand the brand’s unique selling propositions (USPs) and key values (e.g., clean beauty, cruelty-free, luxury).
5. **Evaluate media and influencer impact:** a. Track media coverage and influencer partnerships, focusing on tone and sentiment (e.g., positive product reviews, neutral mentions, negative critiques). b. Analyze the reach and engagement of influencer campaigns and media features, assessing their contribution to brand perception.
6. **Benchmark against competitors:** a. Compare the brand’s perception and sentiment data with key competitors in the market. b. Identify where the brand excels (e.g., innovation, sustainability) and where it lags behind (e.g., affordability, customer service).
7. **Segment consumer data by demographics:** a. Break down brand perception data by key demographics (e.g., age, gender, location, income level) to identify differences in how various groups perceive the brand. b. Use this segmentation to refine marketing strategies for different target audiences.
8. **Track changes in brand perception over time:** a. Analyze historical data to identify shifts in brand perception, especially after new product launches, rebranding efforts, or major marketing campaigns. b. Determine whether consumer sentiment has improved, remained stable, or declined over time.
9. **Identify key drivers of positive and negative sentiment:** a. Use text analysis tools to identify key phrases or themes driving positive sentiment (e.g., "high quality," "great customer service") and negative sentiment (e.g., "too expensive," "poor packaging"). b. Categorize feedback to focus on areas that need improvement or are resonating well with consumers.
10. **Prepare a brand perception report:** a. Compile the findings into a comprehensive report, summarizing overall consumer sentiment, key strengths, and areas for improvement. b. Include actionable recommendations for enhancing brand perception based on consumer feedback and competitive insights.


## 📤 Output format
- A report summarizing overall brand perception and consumer sentiment across key platforms (e.g., social media, reviews, surveys).

- A breakdown of positive, neutral, and negative sentiment by source (e.g., social media, customer reviews, focus groups).
- A comparison chart of brand perception against key competitors, highlighting strengths and weaknesses.
- NPS and CSAT scores with benchmarks against industry averages.
- A demographic segmentation report showing differences in brand perception among target groups.
- Key drivers of positive and negative sentiment based on consumer feedback.
- Recommendations for improving brand perception, focusing on marketing, product development, or customer service improvements.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Positive brand perception:** If the majority of feedback is positive, the brand is resonating well with consumers, building trust and loyalty. Positive NPS and CSAT scores reflect high consumer satisfaction and advocacy.
- **Negative sentiment:** A high level of negative feedback (e.g., related to pricing, product quality, or customer service) indicates areas where the brand needs to improve. Negative sentiment on social media may point to specific issues (e.g., a failed product launch or marketing missteps).
- **Brand strengths:** If consumers consistently mention attributes such as "high quality," "eco-friendly," or "luxury," these are key strengths that can be further leveraged in marketing strategies.
- **Brand weaknesses:** If negative sentiment revolves around price, product performance, or accessibility, the brand may need to adjust its strategy in these areas (e.g., offer more affordable products, improve distribution).
- **Demographic insights:** If certain demographics (e.g., younger consumers or specific geographic regions) have a stronger or weaker perception of the brand, adjust marketing strategies to target these groups more effectively.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Address negative sentiment directly:** If negative feedback points to specific issues (e.g., pricing, customer service), develop actionable plans to address these concerns, such as launching more affordable product lines or improving customer service processes.
2. **Enhance brand strengths:** Use the positive aspects of consumer sentiment (e.g., product quality, eco-friendliness) as key messaging in future marketing campaigns to reinforce the brand’s strengths.
3. **Engage with consumers on social media:** Actively respond to both positive and negative feedback on social media to show consumers that the brand values their opinions and is committed to improving.
4. **Leverage influencer partnerships:** If influencer campaigns are driving positive sentiment, increase collaborations with influencers who align with the brand's values to further enhance visibility and consumer trust.

5. **Adjust messaging for specific demographics:** Use segmentation data to tailor marketing campaigns to different demographic groups, ensuring that messaging resonates with their preferences and needs.
6. **Monitor brand perception regularly:** Implement ongoing tracking of brand perception and sentiment to stay ahead of any negative trends or issues that may arise, ensuring the brand remains aligned with consumer expectations.
7. **Refine product development:** If negative feedback points to specific product issues (e.g., performance or packaging), work with product development teams to refine or reformulate products to better meet consumer expectations.
8. **Invest in brand advocacy:** Encourage satisfied customers to share their positive experiences by launching loyalty programs, referral incentives, or user-generated content campaigns to amplify positive sentiment.
