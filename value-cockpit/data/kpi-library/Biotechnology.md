# KPI — Biotechnology

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Healthcare & Life Sciences]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Biotechnology Company - Analyse]]

55 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Adaptive Trial Designs Implemented | The use of adaptive trial designs, which allow modifications during the trial based on interim results, can improve the efficiency of clinical trials. The number of such trials can be a measure of the company's adaptability and innovation in clinical development. |
| Adoption Rate of Digital Health Technologies | For patient-centric biotech companies, the rate of adopting digital health technologies like telemedicine, remote patient monitoring, and digital therapeutics can indicate their innovation capacity and patient engagement approach. |
| Advanced Therapy Medicinal Products (ATMPs) in Pipeline | ATMPs include gene therapies, tissue-engineered products, and cellular therapies. These represent cutting-edge treatments and their presence in the pipeline could indicate the company's potential for future growth. |
| Bioequivalence Success Rate | For companies developing generic or biosimilar versions of existing biologic drugs, the rate at which they successfully demonstrate bioequivalence (similar efficacy and side effects in patients) to the original can be a key performance metric. |
| Biosimilarity Index for Biosimilar Companies | For companies developing biosimilars, demonstrating a high degree of similarity to the reference biologic product in terms of safety and effectiveness is crucial. A Biosimilarity Index can be a measure of this similarity. |
| Biosimilars in Pipeline | For biotech companies specializing in biosimilars (essentially generic versions of biologic drugs), the number of biosimilars in the pipeline can be a useful KPI to assess future growth. |
| Cell Line Development Speed | For biotech companies involved in therapeutic protein production, the speed at which they can develop productive cell lines is a critical indicator of their R&D capabilities. |
| Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) as a Percentage of Revenue for Biologic Drugs | This KPI is important for companies that manufacture their own biologics, as the process is typically more complex and costly than for traditional pharmaceuticals. A lower percentage can indicate more efficient production processes. |
| Cost per Patient Acquisition | This measures how much a company spends to gain each patient for a particular therapy. Lower costs can imply more efficient marketing and sales processes. |
| Diversity of Species in Preclinical Trials | Companies that test their therapies in a diverse range of animal species before moving onto human trials might be better at predicting safety and efficacy in humans. |
| Drug Pipeline Strength and Stage | This KPI provides insight into the company's potential future earnings. It involves an examination of the number of drugs a company has under development and their stages (preclinical, clinical phases I, II, III, or registration). The further along a drug is in the pipeline, the higher its potential to generate future revenue. |
| Fermentation Efficiency | For biotech firms using microbial fermentation in their production process, the efficiency of this process can greatly impact productivity and costs. |
| Genetic Data Library Size | For companies in the genomics segment, the number of genomes sequenced and stored for research could be a critical asset, enabling more comprehensive and meaningful research. |
| Genome Sequencing Cost | In companies that specialize in gene-related research, the cost of sequencing a genome can be a key determinant of competitiveness. |
| Glycosylation Consistency for Biologic Production | In the manufacture of certain biologics, maintaining consistent glycosylation (the addition of sugars to the protein) can be a critical quality control measure. |
| Microbial Strain Efficiency | In bio-manufacturing, particularly where microbes are used to produce biologic drugs or components, the efficiency of these microbial strains can be a crucial performance indicator. |
| Microbiome Diversity (for Microbiome-focused Companies) | For companies focusing on therapies involving the human microbiome, the diversity of bacteria in their samples or products could be a key metric. |
| Number of CAR-T Therapies in Development | For companies focused on cancer immunotherapy, the number of CAR-T (Chimeric Antigen Receptor T-cell) therapies in development can indicate their potential for future growth. |
| Number of Companion Biomarkers Identified | For personalized medicine companies, the number of companion biomarkers identified (biological markers used to predict response to treatment) can be a crucial KPI. |
| Number of Contract Research Organization (CRO) Partnerships | Many biotech companies outsource portions of their research and trials to CROs. Having numerous partnerships can imply a wider reach and resources for research, but it might also bring in complexity in management. |
| Number of CRISPR-based Projects | For genomics companies, the number of projects utilizing CRISPR technology could indicate a cutting-edge research approach. |
| Number of Multi-center Trials | Multi-center trials offer larger, more diverse patient samples, which can provide more robust and generalizable data. This KPI may indicate the company's ambition and organizational capability. |
| Number of Nanoparticle-based Drugs in Development | For companies working with nanoparticle drug delivery, the number of such drugs in development can indicate the breadth of their pipeline and their potential for growth in this innovative area. |
| Number of New Molecular Entities (NMEs) in Development | NMEs are completely new substances that have not been approved before. This KPI can indicate the innovative potential of the company's research pipeline. |
| Number of Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) Platforms Utilized | For genomics-based biotech companies, the number and types of NGS platforms they utilize can indicate their technological capabilities and breadth of genomic analysis. |
| Number of Orphan Drug Designations | For biotech firms focusing on rare diseases, the number of Orphan Drug Designations they receive from regulatory agencies like FDA can be a key performance indicator. These designations can offer benefits such as reduced fees and market exclusivity. |
| Number of Precision Medicine Approvals | For companies focusing on precision medicine, the number of approved precision therapies can be an important measure of the company's success and potential future revenue. |
| Number of Probiotic Strains Developed (For Probiotic Companies) | In the probiotics sub-sector, the number of unique probiotic strains a company has developed may signal their innovative capacity and product diversity. |
| Number of Real-world Evidence Studies Conducted | Real-world evidence studies use observational data from actual patient health outcomes (rather than controlled clinical trials) to support decision-making. These studies can support product efficacy claims and are becoming more important in regulatory and reimbursement decisions. |
| Number of Synthetic Biology Applications | For companies in the synthetic biology sector, the number of applications or use cases they are developing can indicate their innovation breadth. |
| Number of Therapeutic Areas | This measures the diversity of a company's product portfolio across different diseases and conditions. A diverse portfolio can help manage risk and increase potential revenue streams. |
| Number of Tissue Engineering Projects | For companies in the regenerative medicine sector, the number of tissue engineering projects they are undertaking can provide an indication of their future growth potential. |
| Orphan Drug Status | Many biotech companies focus on rare diseases. Orphan drug status, granted by regulatory authorities for therapies developed for rare diseases, comes with various benefits such as longer exclusivity periods, which can significantly impact a company's prospects. |
| Patent Opposition Success Rate | Biotech companies often face opposition to their patents from competitors. A company's success rate in defending its patents could indicate the strength of its intellectual property portfolio. |
| Patent Portfolio Strength | Biotech companies rely on strong intellectual property rights to protect their investments in research and development. The number, breadth, and duration of a company's patents can be a strong indication of their competitive positioning and future earning potential. |
| Patient Adherence Rates | For commercial-stage biotech companies, tracking how consistently patients use their prescribed therapies can provide insights into the drug's acceptability and market performance. |
| Patient Retention Rate in Long-Term Treatment | In some biotech sectors like rare diseases, patient retention rate for a long-term treatment can be a measure of the drug's effectiveness and its acceptability to patients and physicians. |
| Percentage of Products in Pivotal Trials | Pivotal trials are the advanced stage of clinical trials (typically Phase III) that FDA or other regulators use to make approval decisions. The percentage of a company's products in this stage can be a leading indicator of near-term revenue potential. |
| Pharmacovigilance Compliance Rate | Compliance with pharmacovigilance (drug safety monitoring) requirements is a key regulatory mandate. A high compliance rate indicates good regulatory practice and reduces the risk of penalties. |
| Prevalence of Multi-Drug Resistant Organisms (MDROs) in Anti-Infective Development | For companies focused on developing anti-infective therapies, the percentage of their drug candidates effective against MDROs could be a significant KPI, given the global concern over antibiotic resistance. |
| Production Yield for Biologics | In biotech manufacturing, the production yield - the amount of usable product obtained from the production process - is crucial. High yields can improve cost-effectiveness and profitability. |
| Protein Expression Levels in Biologic Production | In biologic drug manufacturing, the level of protein expression achieved can impact the cost-effectiveness and scalability of the production process. |
| Publication Citations | The number of times a company's research is cited in peer-reviewed journals can indicate the scientific community's recognition and validation of their work, which could impact their reputation and perceived credibility. |
| Quality Adjusted Life Years (QALY) Gained per Therapy | QALY is a measure of the benefit provided by a medical intervention. The greater the QALY gain, the higher the perceived value of the treatment. |
| Rate of Biomarker Discovery | In personalized medicine and diagnostics, the rate at which a company can discover new disease biomarkers can be a key performance indicator. |
| Rate of Enrollment in Clinical Trials | The speed at which a company can fill its required slots for clinical trials can impact how quickly these trials can be completed, and therefore, how soon the product can be commercialized. |
| Regenerative Medicine Programs in Progress | For companies working in the field of regenerative medicine, the number of active programs they have can be a strong indicator of their potential for future success. |
| Revenue from Personalized Therapies | The amount of revenue a company earns from personalized or precision medicine products can indicate its success in this growing sector. |
| Scale-up Success Rate | In biotechnology, scaling up production from a lab or pilot scale to industrial scale is a major challenge. A company's success rate in doing this can indicate their manufacturing competence. |
| Success Rate of Clinical Trials | The percentage of a company's drugs that successfully pass clinical trials is a strong indication of the company's scientific capabilities. High success rates can reflect well on the company's future prospects. |
| Supply Chain Resilience | For biotech firms with complex biological supply chains, the ability to maintain supply under different stress scenarios (e.g., pandemics, trade restrictions) can be a key indicator of business stability. |
| Technological Advances in R&D | This could be measured by the number of new technologies or processes implemented in R&D. A higher rate might signify that the company is an industry leader and staying ahead of the curve. |
| Therapeutic Success Substitution Rate | This KPI refers to the success rate of a new therapy substituting an existing one in clinical trials. It is a measure of the potential of a new drug to replace existing therapies. |
| Time to Recruit for Clinical Trials | The speed at which a company can recruit the required number of participants for a clinical trial can affect how quickly that trial can begin and, consequently, the overall development timeline. |
| Utilization of Personalized Medicine Approaches | The use of genetic or other biomarker data to customize treatments to individual patients is becoming more prevalent in biotech. Companies utilizing personalized medicine approaches could be seen as more innovative and patient-centered. |
