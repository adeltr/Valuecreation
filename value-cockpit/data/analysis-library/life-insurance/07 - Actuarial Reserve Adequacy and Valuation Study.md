# 7 · Actuarial Reserve Adequacy and Valuation Study

> **Part:** [[B - Actuarial and Reserve Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the adequacy and accuracy of actuarial reserves for life insurance policies, ensuring that liabilities are fully covered and aligned with regulatory requirements. This analysis ensures financial stability and supports effective risk management.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policy-level data, including:** ○ Policy type (e.g., term, whole life, universal life). ○ Coverage amounts and premium payment details.
2. **Actuarial assumptions, such as:** ○ Mortality and morbidity rates. ○ Lapse rates and policy renewal rates. ○ Discount rates and investment returns.
3. **Reserve valuation data, including:** ○ Current reserves by policy and overall portfolio. ○ Reserve calculation methodology (e.g., gross premium reserves, net premium reserves).
4. **Financial and regulatory data:** ○ Solvency standards and guidelines. ○ Benchmark reserve levels for similar companies or policies.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Compile Policy Data a. Aggregate data on policy liabilities by type, coverage amounts, and duration. b. Group policies into cohorts (e.g., by duration or type) for analysis.
2. Review Current Reserve Calculation Methodology a. Identify reserve types (e.g., gross premium, net premium, unearned premium reserves) and assumptions used. b. Ensure methodologies comply with regulatory frameworks (e.g., IFRS 17, GAAP, Solvency II).
3. Validate Actuarial Assumptions a. Compare current mortality, lapse, and morbidity assumptions with recent experience studies.
20 b. Assess the appropriateness of discount rates used against prevailing interest rates and investment returns.
4. Perform Reserve Adequacy Testing a. Use cash flow projection models to test reserve adequacy under various scenarios: ■ Baseline Scenario: Based on current assumptions. ■ Stress Scenarios: Higher-than-expected lapse rates, adverse mortality/morbidity, or lower investment returns. b. Calculate reserve shortfalls or surpluses for each scenario.
5. Assess Reserve Sufficiency a. Compare calculated reserves to actual reserves held. b. Identify gaps or excesses, highlighting policies or cohorts with insufficient reserves.
6. Benchmark Against Industry Standards a. Compare reserve levels and methodologies to industry averages or competitors.
7. Generate Visualizations a. Create tables and charts summarizing reserve adequacy by policy type and scenario. b. Use heatmaps to identify high-risk cohorts or reserve shortfalls.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables showing reserve adequacy by policy type, cohort, and scenario.
- Stress test results highlighting reserve sufficiency under different assumptions.
- Charts comparing actual reserves vs. required reserves.
- Key findings identifying underfunded reserves and potential risks.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Adequate reserves indicate that the company can meet its future obligations under normal and stress scenarios.
2. Reserve shortfalls highlight underfunded liabilities that may expose the company to solvency risks.
3. Surpluses may suggest overly conservative assumptions, potentially tying up capital that could be used elsewhere.
4. Misalignment with industry benchmarks may indicate either competitive advantages (higher reserves) or vulnerabilities (lower reserves).

## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Update actuarial assumptions regularly based on recent experience studies and market trends.
2. Enhance scenario analysis capabilities to identify potential reserve inadequacies under extreme conditions.
3. Improve alignment of reserve methodologies with regulatory requirements and best practices (e.g., implementing IFRS 17 standards).
4. Use predictive modeling to refine assumptions for lapse rates, mortality, and morbidity.
5. Increase capital reserves or adjust reinsurance strategies to mitigate risks from underfunded liabilities.
6. Conduct regular audits of reserve calculations to ensure accuracy and transparency.
