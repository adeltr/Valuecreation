# 1 · Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Finance & Revenue]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) analysis is to determine the direct costs associated with delivering a SaaS product to customers. COGS typically includes costs related to hosting, customer support, third-party integrations, and other direct expenses necessary to provide the service. Understanding COGS is critical for determining gross margins and the overall proﬁtability of the SaaS business.


## 📊 Data required
1. Hosting and infrastructure costs (e.g., cloud services, servers)
2. Customer support costs (e.g., salaries, support software)
3. Software licenses or third-party integrations (e.g., APIs, payment gateways)
4. Data storage and bandwidth costs
5. Direct labor costs (e.g., salaries of technical support or operations staW)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Determine the time period for analysis:** Choose a period (e.g., monthly or quarterly) to analyze the COGS. SaaS companies typically calculate COGS monthly or quarterly for more granular tracking.
2. **Gather all direct costs:** Collect all direct costs associated with delivering the SaaS product. This will include:
o    Hosting and infrastructure costs: Expenses for cloud hosting (e.g., AWS, Google Cloud) and any servers required to run the software.
o    Customer support costs: Include the salaries of support staW, customer service software, and any outsourced support services.
o    Third-party software costs: If your SaaS product uses third-party APIs, payment processors, or other software that incurs a cost, include these fees.
o    Data storage and bandwidth costs: Costs related to storing customer data and providing network bandwidth for customer usage.
5 o    Direct labor costs: Include the salaries of any technical staW directly involved in keeping the platform operational (e.g., site reliability engineers, operations staW).
3. **Sum up all direct costs:** Add together all the direct costs gathered to calculate the total Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) for the selected period.
COGS = Hosting Costs + Customer Support Costs + Third-Party Software Costs + Data Storage Costs + Direct Labor Costs For example, if your direct costs in a quarter were:
o    Hosting Costs: $20,000 o    Customer Support Costs: $10,000 o    Third-Party Software Costs: $5,000 o    Data Storage Costs: $3,000 o    Direct Labor Costs: $7,000 Then, COGS for that quarter would be:
`COGS = $20,000 + $10,000 + $5,000 + $3,000 + $7,000 = $45,000`


## 📤 Output format
The output is a dollar ﬁgure representing the total cost of goods sold for the chosen period. In the example above, the output would be:
Cost of Goods Sold: $45,000


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   High COGS: A high COGS relative to revenue indicates that the company’s costs for providing the service are too high, which may negatively affect gross margins. It could suggest inefficiencies in infrastructure, costly customer support operations, or reliance on expensive third-party services.
•   Low COGS: A lower COGS means that the company is able to deliver its service at a lower direct cost, which improves gross margins and increases proﬁtability. A company with a low COGS can scale more efficiently.
The analysis of COGS should be combined with gross margins analysis to ensure that the company is earning enough revenue to cover the cost of providing the product while generating sufficient proﬁt.

## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize hosting and infrastructure costs:** Explore cloud service cost-saving measures like auto-scaling, reserved instances, or switching to more cost-efficient providers.
2. **Improve customer support eKiciency:** Use automation or AI-driven customer support tools (e.g., chatbots, self-service portals) to reduce the number of support tickets that require human intervention, cutting support- related costs.
3. **Negotiate better rates with third-party providers:** Work with third-party software providers to negotiate volume discounts, or consider developing in-house solutions to replace expensive third-party services.
4. **Optimize data storage and bandwidth usage:** Employ data compression techniques and optimize bandwidth usage to lower storage and data transfer costs.
5. **Review direct labor eKiciency:** Ensure that the operations and support teams are operating efficiently. Consider introducing performance metrics or process improvements that could reduce the amount of labor needed to maintain the platform.
