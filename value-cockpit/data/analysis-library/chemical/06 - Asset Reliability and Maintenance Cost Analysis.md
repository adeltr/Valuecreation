# 6 · Asset Reliability and Maintenance Cost Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Production & Process Optimization]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Chemical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to evaluate the reliability of key production assets and to assess maintenance costs associated with keeping these assets operational. By understanding asset reliability and maintenance expenses, a company can improve equipment uptime, reduce unplanned downtime, and optimize the cost-effectiveness of maintenance programs.


## 📊 Data required
1.   Historical downtime data for each major asset
2.   Maintenance costs (labor, parts, materials) for each asset
3.   Asset age, usage hours, and maintenance history
4.   Failure frequency and types of failures for each asset
5.   Production output losses due to asset downtime
6.   Industry benchmarks for asset reliability and maintenance costs


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate Asset Reliability (Mean Time Between Failures - MTBF):
`MTBF = Total Operating Time / Number of Failures`
For example, if a machine operates 1,000 hours with 5 failures, then:
`MTBF = 1,000 / 5 = 200 hours`
2. Calculate Mean Time to Repair (MTTR):
`MTTR = Total Downtime / Number of Repairs`
For example, if the total downtime over the period was 50 hours across 10 repairs, then:
`MTTR = 50 / 10 = 5 hours`
3. Determine Maintenance Cost per Unit of Production:
`Maintenance Cost per Unit = Total Maintenance Cost / Production Output`
For example, if total maintenance cost is $50,000 and production output is 10,000 tons, then:
`Maintenance Cost per Unit = 50,000 / 10,000 = $5 per ton`
4. Assess Downtime Impact on Production Output:
Calculate the production output lost due to downtime, which can highlight the financial impact of asset unreliability.

`Lost Output (tons) = (Downtime Hours x Average Production Rate)`
For example, if downtime totaled 100 hours and production is 10 tons per hour, then:
`Lost Output = 100 x 10 = 1,000 tons`
5. Benchmark Asset Reliability and Maintenance Costs Against Industry Standards:
Compare MTBF, MTTR, and maintenance cost per unit to industry benchmarks to assess whether the facility’s assets are operating at expected reliability and cost efficiency.
6. Identify High-Cost and Low-Reliability Assets:
Rank assets by maintenance cost and reliability (MTBF) to identify equipment that incurs high maintenance costs or frequent breakdowns. Focus on these assets for targeted improvements.


## 📤 Output format
1.   MTBF and MTTR for each asset (in hours)
2.   Maintenance Cost per Unit of Production (e.g., dollars per ton)
3.   Downtime Impact on Production Output (tons lost)
4.   Table of high-cost, low-reliability assets for priority focus


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High MTBF and low MTTR:** Indicate reliable assets that require minimal downtime for repairs.
- **High maintenance cost per unit of production:** May indicate inefficient maintenance spending or over-reliance on costly repairs. This metric should ideally align with industry averages.
- **Downtime impact on production output:** High output losses due to downtime suggest a need to improve maintenance strategies to reduce unplanned downtime.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Implement Predictive Maintenance:** Use sensors and data analytics to predict when maintenance is needed, reducing unplanned downtime and optimizing maintenance schedules.
2. **Enhance Preventive Maintenance Programs:** Conduct regular preventive maintenance to reduce the frequency of breakdowns and extend asset lifecycles.
3. **Invest in Asset Upgrades or Replacements:** Identify and replace assets with consistently low MTBF and high maintenance costs to reduce long-term maintenance expenses.
4. **Optimize Spare Parts Management:** Ensure critical spare parts are readily available to minimize MTTR when breakdowns occur.
