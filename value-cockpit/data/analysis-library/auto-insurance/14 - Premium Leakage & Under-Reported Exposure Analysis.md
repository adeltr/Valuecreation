# 14 · Premium Leakage & Under-Reported Exposure Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Underwriting & Risk Segmentation]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Identify areas where policyholders or agents may under-report driver information, vehicle usage, or other risk factors leading to inadequate premium collection, and determine strategies to recapture lost revenue and align premiums with actual exposure.


## 📊 Data required
- Policy information detailing driver counts, vehicle usage (personal, commercial, rideshare), and annual mileage
- Claims data segmented by discrepancies in reported vs. actual exposure (e.g., undisclosed drivers, misrepresented garaging address)
- Third-party data sources (DMV records, credit checks, telematics, vehicle registration databases)
- Audit or inspection reports highlighting inconsistencies between reported and actual exposure
- Industry or competitor benchmarks on typical leakage rates and detection methods


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Compile policy data and identify key exposure fields such as number of drivers, vehicle usage type, and garaging location. Compare reported information to any available third-party data (e.g., DMV or registration records) to detect mismatches.
2. Calculate the frequency of discrepancies by using Discrepancy Rate = (Number of Policies with Under-Reported Exposures / Total Policies) x 100. Categorize discrepancies by type (e.g., unreported drivers, incorrect mileage, false address).
3. Analyze claim outcomes for policies with detected under-reporting to see if these accounts have disproportionately high loss ratios or severity. Use Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + LAE) / Earned Premium, comparing under-reporters to accurately reported segments.
4. Estimate premium leakage by projecting the additional premium that would have been charged if exposures were reported accurately. Reference rating tables or underwriting guidelines to calculate potential revenue differences.
32
5. Investigate patterns or indicators that might predict under-reporting, such as certain vehicle types, policyholder demographics, or coverage limits. Look for trends that can help refine underwriting or detection algorithms.
6. Benchmark leakage rates against industry or competitor data to see if your company’s under-reporting rate is comparatively high or low. Determine if differences result from lenient underwriting, lack of verification, or other factors.
7. Evaluate current controls and validation processes (e.g., driver history checks, address verification tools). Identify any gaps that allow misrepresentation to pass unchecked.
8. Document findings and propose remediation such as enhanced underwriting procedures, regular data cross-checks, or targeted audits of high-risk segments. Share results with underwriting, pricing, and claims teams to foster collective improvement.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing discrepancy types, frequency, and estimated premium impact
- Graphs depicting the difference in claim severity or loss ratios between accurate and under-reported policies
- Written report detailing key findings, recommended process enhancements, and potential financial benefits


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A high discrepancy rate often indicates significant premium leakage and suggests a need for stronger validation measures.
- Policies with under-reported exposures typically reveal higher-than-expected claims cost, illustrating the direct impact on profitability.
- Industry benchmarks can help determine whether your company’s leakage rate is abnormally high and inform where to focus remediation.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Implement automated data cross-checks, pulling from public or private databases (e.g., driver’s license records, telematics).
2. Strengthen agent training and incentives to ensure thorough risk disclosure and reduce intentional misrepresentation.
3. Conduct periodic audits or inspections of policies flagged for potential under-reporting based on predictive analytics.

4. Introduce policyholder self-service tools allowing easy updates of vehicle usage or driver information, reducing accidental misreporting.
