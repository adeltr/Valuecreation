# 11 · Supply Chain, Inventory, and Working Capital Efficiency Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Finance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Chemical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to assess the efficiency of the supply chain, inventory management, and working capital utilization. By understanding how effectively these areas are managed, a company can reduce inventory costs, improve cash flow, and enhance responsiveness to market demand, ultimately optimizing the entire production and distribution pipeline.


## 📊 Data required
1.   Inventory levels (raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods)
2.   Inventory turnover rate
3.   Days inventory outstanding (DIO)
4.   Supply chain lead times for key materials
5.   Working capital metrics, including accounts payable (AP) and accounts receivable (AR)
6.   Cost of capital and carrying costs for inventory
7.   Industry benchmarks for inventory turnover and working capital ratios


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Inventory Turnover Rate:**
`Inventory Turnover Rate = Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) / Average Inventory`
For example, if COGS is $500,000 and average inventory is $100,000:
`Inventory Turnover Rate = 500,000 / 100,000 = 5`
2. Calculate Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO)
`DIO = (Average Inventory / COGS) x 365`
This metric indicates the average number of days inventory is held before being sold or used in production. For example, if average inventory is $100,000 and COGS is $500,000:
`DIO = (100,000 / 500,000) x 365 = 73 days`
3. **Analyze Working Capital Metrics:** How to Analyze a Chemicals Company Days Payable Outstanding (DPO): Measures the average number of days the company takes to pay its suppliers.
`DPO = (Accounts Payable / COGS) x 365`
Days Sales Outstanding (DSO): Measures the average number of days it takes to collect payment after a sale.
`DSO = (Accounts Receivable / Total Sales) x 365`
Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC): Represents the time taken to convert investments in inventory and other resources into cash flows from sales.
`CCC = DIO + DSO - DPO`
4. **Evaluate Supply Chain Lead Times:** Track lead times for critical raw materials and components. Identify bottlenecks and analyze the reliability of suppliers by reviewing any discrepancies between actual and promised lead times.
5. **Calculate Cost of Inventory Carrying:**
`Inventory Carrying Cost = Average Inventory x Carrying Cost Rate`
For example, if the carrying cost rate is 20% and average inventory is $100,000:
`Inventory Carrying Cost = 100,000 x 0.20 = $20,000 per year`
6. **Benchmark Against Industry Standards:** Compare the company’s inventory turnover, DIO, CCC, and other working capital metrics to industry standards. Inefficiencies relative to benchmarks may suggest excess inventory, slow payment collections, or opportunities to negotiate better supplier terms.


## 📤 Output format
1. Inventory Turnover Rate, Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO), Days Payable Outstanding (DPO), and Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)
2. Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) and Inventory Carrying Cost
3. Table or chart of supply chain lead times and variability
4. Benchmark comparison for inventory and working capital efficiency metrics


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High inventory turnover and low DIO:** Suggest that inventory is managed efficiently, with minimal holding time and responsive demand fulfillment.

- **Low inventory turnover or high DIO:** Indicates potential overstocking or inefficient inventory usage, tying up working capital.
- **High Cash Conversion Cycle:** Signals a slower working capital cycle, meaning cash is tied up for longer periods, reducing liquidity.
- **Long lead times or high variability:** Suggest supply chain bottlenecks, which can lead to production delays and stockouts.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize Inventory Levels:** Implement demand forecasting to better align inventory levels with actual demand, reducing overstock and stockouts.
2. **Negotiate Better Supplier Terms:** Extend DPO by negotiating longer payment terms with suppliers, freeing up working capital.
3. **Improve Receivables Management:** Shorten DSO by tightening credit terms or offering incentives for early payments to accelerate cash inflow.
4. **Reduce Lead Times and Increase Supplier Reliability:** Strengthen relationships with key suppliers and explore multiple sourcing options to reduce lead times and improve supply chain flexibility.
5. **Implement Just-in-Time (JIT) Inventory:** Minimize inventory holding by synchronizing production schedules closely with supply deliveries, reducing carrying costs and freeing up cash.
