# 15 · Product Mix Profitability and Market Positioning Evaluation

> **Part:** [[C - Profitability and Product Performance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the profitability of a life insurance company’s product mix and assess its positioning in the market relative to competitors. The analysis identifies high-performing products, underperforming segments, and opportunities to optimize product offerings for improved financial performance and market competitiveness.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Product-level financial data:** ○ Premium revenue ○ Claims paid ○ Acquisition costs (e.g., commissions, marketing) ○ Maintenance expenses (e.g., administrative costs) ○ Profit margins (actual and expected)
2. **Policy-level data:** ○ Product type (e.g., term life, whole life, annuities) ○ Policy duration and size ○ Lapse rates and persistency rates
3. **Customer segmentation data:** demographics (age, income, location)
4. **Market data:** competitor product offerings, pricing strategies, and positioning.
5. **Economic data:** interest rates, inflation, and investment returns affecting product profitability.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate Product Profitability ○ Use the formula:
`Product Profitability = Premium Revenue - (Claims Paid + Acquisition Costs + Maintenance Expenses)`
○ Determine profit margins as a percentage of premium revenue for each product type.
2. Segment Profitability by Product ○ Break down profit margins by product category (e.g., term life, whole life, universal life, annuities). ○ Identify products with the highest and lowest margins.
3. Analyze Contribution to Overall Profitability ○ Calculate the contribution of each product to the company’s total profit:
`Contribution (%) = (Product Profit / Total Profit) x 100`
○ Identify products driving profitability and those eroding margins.

4. Evaluate Market Positioning ○ Compare the company’s product offerings, pricing, and profitability with competitors. ○ Assess whether the company competes on price, features, or unique benefits. ○ Analyze gaps in the market where demand exceeds supply.
5. Analyze Customer Segments ○ Identify which customer segments are purchasing specific products and how profitable they are. ○ Evaluate whether there is alignment between profitable products and high-growth customer segments.
6. Conduct Sensitivity Testing ○ Test the impact of changes in key assumptions (e.g., lapse rates, interest rates, expense inflation) on product profitability.
7. Visualize Findings ○ Create tables summarizing product profitability and market positioning. ○ Generate bar charts showing contribution to total profit by product. ○ Use scatter plots to compare profitability and market competitiveness across products.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing profitability, profit margins, and contribution for each product category
- Competitive analysis tables highlighting differences in pricing, positioning, and features
- Charts illustrating product profitability, including high vs. low performers
- Key findings identifying opportunities to improve product mix and competitive positioning


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Products with high profitability and strong market positioning are strengths and should be prioritized for growth.
2. Products with low margins or negative profitability indicate cost inefficiencies, mispricing, or lack of market demand.
3. Gaps in product offerings compared to competitors present opportunities for new product development or repositioning.
4. High growth potential in specific customer segments highlights areas to focus acquisition and marketing efforts.

5. Sensitivity testing reveals vulnerabilities in product profitability under changing economic conditions or policyholder behavior.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Optimize or phase out underperforming products while enhancing profitable ones.
2. Adjust product pricing to ensure alignment with risk, claims experience, and acquisition costs.
3. Develop innovative products tailored to high-growth customer segments and unmet market needs.
4. Strengthen cost management strategies to reduce acquisition and maintenance expenses for low-margin products.
5. Improve distribution efficiency by aligning high-performing products with effective channels (e.g., agents, bancassurance, direct sales).
6. Enhance marketing efforts for competitive products to capture market share and boost profitability.
7. Monitor competitor offerings and pricing regularly to adjust positioning and remain competitive.
