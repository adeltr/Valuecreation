# 6 · Policy Retention Rate Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Underwriting]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Policy Retention Rate Analysis is to measure the percentage of policies renewed within a given period, indicating customer loyalty, satisfaction, and product value. High retention rates generally contribute to stable revenue and reduced acquisition costs, while low rates may suggest underlying service or pricing issues.


## 📊 Data required
1. Number of policies at the beginning of the period
2. Number of policies renewed during the period
3. Segment data, if analyzing retention by product line, customer type, or region


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the Period:** Select the analysis period (e.g., monthly, quarterly, or annually) based on business needs and data availability.
2. **Calculate Policy Retention Rate:** Use the formula:
`Policy Retention Rate = (Number of Policies Renewed / Number of Policies at Start of Period) x 100`
3. **Segment the Data:** If data is available, calculate retention rates by segment (e.g., by product line, customer demographic, or region) to understand which areas have higher or lower retention rates.
4. **Track Retention Over Time:** Calculate the retention rate over multiple periods to identify trends and see if retention is improving, stable, or declining.
5. **Compile Results:** Present results in a table or chart, showing retention rates by period and segment, if applicable, for clear visual comparison.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Period (e.g., Q1 2023)
- Number of Policies at Start of Period
- Number of Policies Renewed
- Retention Rate (%)
For additional insights, a line chart can display retention rate trends over time, and a bar chart can show retention by segment.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Retention Rate:** Indicates customer loyalty, satisfaction, or competitive pricing. Generally favorable as it contributes to revenue stability and lowers acquisition costs.
- **Low Retention Rate:** Suggests potential issues, such as customer dissatisfaction, competitive pricing pressures, or lack of product fit.
Benchmarking against industry averages or historical retention rates can provide context to determine if the company’s retention is strong or if there’s room for improvement.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Enhance Customer Engagement Implement proactive customer service and engagement strategies, such as reaching out before renewal, to improve customer loyalty.
2. Review Pricing and Competitiveness Regularly assess pricing strategies to ensure premiums remain competitive without sacrificing profitability.
3. Offer Loyalty Programs or Discounts Consider offering loyalty incentives, such as discounts for long-term customers, to encourage renewals.
4. Analyze Reasons for Non-Renewal Survey customers who do not renew to identify common reasons and adjust service or product offerings accordingly.
5. Segment-Specific Retention Initiatives In segments with lower retention rates, tailor specific retention strategies that address unique needs, such as personalized communications or segment-specific incentives.
6. Use Data Analytics to Predict Churn Use predictive modeling to identify customers at risk of non-renewal and implement targeted retention efforts.
