# 23 · Carbon Credits and Regenerative Agriculture Sequestration Opportunity

> **Part:** [[C - Environmental Sustainability & Resource]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Agriculture Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine the farm’s potential to sequester carbon through regenerative practices and assess opportunities to monetize these efforts via carbon credits or related environmental markets.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical land-use and farming practices
- Soil organic carbon (SOC) baseline measurements
- Current and planned regenerative practices (cover crops, reduced tillage, agroforestry)
- Climate data (temperature, precipitation)
- Crop and pasture yields
- Carbon credit program or market requirements (verification protocols, methodologies)
- Economic data (costs of implementing regenerative practices, potential credit revenue)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Establish baseline carbon stock. Collect soil samples (e.g., 0–30 cm depth) across representative fields. Send samples to a certified lab to measure soil organic carbon. Record land-use history to contextualize results.
Step 2: Inventory regenerative practices. List all current practices that enhance carbon sequestration (cover cropping, no-till, rotation grazing, agroforestry). Estimate the acreage and intensity of these practices.
Step 3: Identify applicable carbon credit standards or programs. Research various protocols (Verra, Gold Standard, regional government programs) to determine eligibility criteria and monitoring requirements.
Step 4: Model potential carbon sequestration. Use approved calculators or software (COMET-Farm, Cool Farm Tool) to project sequestration rates under different regenerative scenarios over multiple years.
51 Step 5: Evaluate verification and monitoring needs. Review data collection protocols, soil sampling frequency, and third-party auditing requirements. Determine the resources and timeline needed for verification.
Step 6: Perform a financial feasibility analysis. Compare the cost of implementing regenerative practices (equipment changes, labor, inputs) to potential revenues from carbon credit sales. Include any co-benefits like improved soil health or reduced input costs.
Step 7: Summarize findings and recommend an implementation path. Consolidate carbon sequestration projections, credit opportunities, and financial returns. Outline a step-by-step plan to adopt regenerative practices and enroll in a suitable carbon program.


## 📤 Output format
- A soil carbon baseline table or chart detailing current SOC levels in different fields
- Modeled projections for future carbon sequestration under various regenerative scenarios
- A short report comparing carbon credit programs and potential revenues
- A recommended implementation roadmap, including verification steps and milestones


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Higher projected carbon sequestration and cost-effective verification processes indicate strong potential for generating carbon credits.
- Close alignment with program requirements and robust data collection improves the likelihood of successful enrollment in carbon markets.
- Co-benefits (improved soil fertility, water retention) can further justify the adoption of regenerative agriculture beyond carbon credit revenue.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Begin a rigorous soil sampling and record-keeping program to establish and maintain SOC baselines.
2. Expand regenerative practices (cover crops, reduced tillage, compost applications) that have proven carbon sequestration potential.
3. Engage with reputable carbon credit verifiers to understand documentation, auditing, and reporting requirements.
52
4. Use modeling tools to continuously update and refine carbon sequestration projections as practices evolve.
5. Explore grant funding or cost-sharing opportunities to offset upfront investment in regenerative methods.
6. Collaborate with industry peers or cooperatives to share best practices and reduce verification costs.
