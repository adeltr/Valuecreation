# 28 · Inbound-to-Manufacturing (I2M) & JIT Delivery Capability Analysis

> **Part:** [[E - Value-Added & Specialized Services]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Third Party Logistics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine how effectively the 3PL supports inbound logistics to manufacturing plants, focusing on Just-in-Time (JIT) delivery performance, inventory reduction, and alignment with production schedules.


## 📊 Data required
- Inbound shipment data (origin, volume, frequency) and associated lead times
- Production schedules and forecast data from manufacturing clients
- Inventory levels at manufacturing sites (raw materials, components)
- Transportation resources and carrier partnerships for inbound flows
- Performance metrics (on-time delivery to plant, line stoppages, expedited shipments)
- Demand variability and seasonality patterns


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Map Inbound Supply Chains:** Identify origins, consolidation points, and transportation modes used to deliver materials to manufacturing plants. Document how closely inbound shipments align with production cycles.
2. **Examine JIT Delivery Schedules:** Compare scheduled arrival windows against actual delivery times. Calculate on-time performance and identify patterns of early or late arrivals.
3. **Assess Inventory Strategy:** Review the target inventory levels at manufacturing sites and measure actual levels. Determine how much buffer stock is on hand to protect against delivery variances.
4. **Analyze Disruptions & Line Stoppages:** Investigate any production halts or slowdowns caused by inbound delays or shortages. Note the frequency, impact on throughput, and costs associated with recovery (e.g., expedited freight).
5. **Review Carrier & Routing Optimization:** Evaluate how shipments are planned, routed, and consolidated to meet JIT requirements. Check if load planning tools or collaborative programs are in place to reduce transit times and variability.

6. **Evaluate Communication & Visibility Tools:** Determine if real-time tracking and proactive alert systems are in use. Analyze how quickly issues are flagged and resolved to avoid manufacturing delays.
7. **Summarize Findings & Provide Recommendations:** Highlight critical gaps in on-time performance, inventory alignment, and communication processes. Suggest improvements to scheduling, routing, and technology adoption.


## 📤 Output format
- A flow diagram or timeline showing inbound logistics steps, from supplier dispatch to plant delivery
- A performance scorecard comparing scheduled vs. actual delivery times and associated KPI trends (on-time percentage, line stoppages)
- An inventory analysis detailing average on-hand days, buffer targets, and variance from plan
- A recommendation report outlining specific actions to enhance JIT compliance and reduce production risks


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High On-Time Delivery with Low Inventory:** Indicates strong alignment between inbound flows and production needs, minimizing holding costs.
- **Frequent Line Stoppages or Expedites:** Suggests inconsistent delivery performance, likely requiring tighter control over carrier schedules or better communication.
- **Excessive Buffer Stock:** Points to overcompensation for unreliable inbound flows, tying up capital and storage space.
- **Weak Visibility or Late Issue Detection:** Implies a need for more robust tracking and exception management tools to prevent supply disruptions.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Enhance Supply Chain Collaboration:** Develop stronger ties and data-sharing protocols with suppliers and carriers, ensuring alignment on schedules and priorities.
2. **Implement Advanced Routing & Scheduling Tools:** Use TMS or specialized algorithms to optimize inbound shipments, reduce transit variability, and align deliveries with production slots.

3. **Adopt Real-Time Tracking & Alerts:** Deploy IoT or GPS solutions that offer real-time shipment visibility, sending proactive alerts if delays threaten JIT requirements.
4. **Refine Inventory Policies:** Adjust buffer stock levels based on improved reliability; continuously evaluate whether JIT performance allows for lower safety stocks.
5. **Regularly Audit Performance & Root Causes:** Conduct periodic reviews of missed deliveries, expedite triggers, and line stoppages to drive ongoing improvements in inbound-to-manufacturing operations.
