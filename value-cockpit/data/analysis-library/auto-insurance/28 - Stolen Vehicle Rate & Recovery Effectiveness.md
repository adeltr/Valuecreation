# 28 · Stolen Vehicle Rate & Recovery Effectiveness

> **Part:** [[C - Claims & Loss Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine the frequency of vehicle thefts within the portfolio, analyze recovery rates and associated claim costs, and identify strategies to reduce theft incidents and improve stolen vehicle recovery outcomes.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Historical claims data for stolen vehicles, including final loss amounts
- ​ Vehicle recovery records indicating which vehicles were found, condition, and recovery timelines
- ​ Policy information (vehicle make/model/year, location, security features)
- ​ Law enforcement or external databases on theft rates by region
- ​ Costs associated with vehicle recovery efforts (e.g., private investigators, GPS technology)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Collect stolen vehicle claims data over a chosen time period (e.g., 3–5 years). Include theft date, location, policy details, recovery status, and final claim settlement amounts. 2.​ Calculate the stolen vehicle rate using Stolen Vehicle Rate = (Number of Stolen Vehicle Claims / Total Insured Vehicles) x 100. Segment these results by factors like vehicle make/model, geographic region, and presence of anti-theft devices. 3.​ Assess recovery effectiveness by calculating Recovery Rate = (Number of Recovered Vehicles / Number of Stolen Vehicles) x 100 and noting the average days to recovery. Compare recovery rates across different security features, policyholder segments, and regions. 4.​ Analyze claim severity by comparing the total loss amount for unrecovered vehicles vs. those recovered but damaged. Calculate average claim payout for stolen vehicles in each category to gauge cost differences. 5.​ Identify high-risk vehicles and hotspots by examining patterns in theft incidents (e.g., certain models or locations with elevated theft rates). Investigate whether premium surcharges or underwriting adjustments are warranted for these segments. 6.​ Review existing measures such as policy discounts for anti-theft devices, partnerships with GPS tracking services, or educational campaigns for insureds.

Highlight any major discrepancies that may suggest an opportunity for improvement. 8.​ Document findings and propose enhancements such as targeted underwriting for high-risk vehicles, expanded use of telematics or tracking, or improved relationships with law enforcement. Present these recommendations to underwriting, claims, and product teams for decision-making.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ Tables or charts illustrating stolen vehicle frequency, segmented by region, vehicle model, and presence of anti-theft features
- ​ Recovery statistics, including timelines and final claim settlement amounts
- ​ A written summary highlighting key findings, cost implications, and recommended strategies for theft prevention and recovery improvement


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ A high stolen vehicle rate in specific regions or for particular makes/models signals a need for tighter underwriting guidelines or premium surcharges.
- ​ Low recovery rates indicate potential deficiencies in anti-theft measures, collaboration with law enforcement, or policyholder education.
- ​ Significantly higher claim severity for unrecovered vehicles underscores the financial impact of theft on overall loss ratios.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Offer or mandate enhanced security measures (e.g., GPS trackers, immobilizers) in high-theft regions or for frequently targeted vehicle models. 2.​ Provide policyholder education on safe parking practices, anti-theft device usage, and timely reporting of theft incidents. 3.​ Collaborate with law enforcement and vehicle tracking services to streamline the recovery process. 4.​ Adjust underwriting and rating strategies to reflect theft risk, incentivizing safer vehicle choices and adoption of security features.
