# 22 · In‐Room Dining Profitability and Utilization

> **Part:** [[D - Operations & Hospitality Services]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Assess the cost, revenue, and usage patterns of room service, finding ways to boost orders, control expenses, and maintain service quality.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical in‐room dining orders by daypart (breakfast, lunch, dinner, late‐night).
- Revenue figures tied to each order, including any service or delivery fees.
- Direct costs (food, beverage) associated with in‐room dining items.
- Labor costs and overhead (staffing, equipment, delivery logistics).
- Guest feedback regarding food quality, delivery time, and overall satisfaction.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Collect order and revenue data. Gather the number of in‐room dining orders placed over a chosen timeframe, along with total sales revenue generated.
2. Break down orders by daypart or meal period. Understand peak demand times (e.g., breakfast vs. dinner) to align staffing and menu availability.
3. Calculate direct costs. Determine food and beverage costs (COGS) for each menu item served through in‐room dining.
4. Include labor and overhead. Factor in delivery staff time, kitchen labor (if separate from restaurant labor), and any delivery trolleys or equipment maintenance.
5. **Compute profitability metrics. ○ Contribution Margin per order:** (Menu Price - Food & Beverage Cost) ○ Net Profit Margin: [(Total In‐Room Dining Revenue - (COGS + Labor + Overhead)) / Total In‐Room Dining Revenue] x 100
6. Analyze guest feedback. Correlate satisfaction or complaint rates with daypart, menu items, or delivery time.
7. Identify usage patterns. Pinpoint common order times, average check size, and repeat usage by loyalty members or certain guest segments.
8. Review menu performance. Determine which items are most/least popular and compare profitability across the in‐room dining menu.


## 📤 Output format
1. **A table showing:** ○ Daypart or meal period ○ Number of orders 48 ○ Revenue ○ Food & Beverage Cost ○ Labor & Overhead Costs ○ Net Profit Margin (%)
2. Charts or graphs illustrating order volume and revenue by daypart, plus profitability trends over time.
3. Narrative highlighting key findings and any anomalies in guest feedback or ordering patterns.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High demand but low profit may suggest labor or overhead inefficiencies, or underpricing of certain menu items.
- Low demand but high margin items might still be valuable if they align with guest preferences (e.g., premium in‐room dining options).
- Repeated guest complaints about delivery speed or menu variety could be driving down usage and revenues.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Streamline operations by adjusting staffing to match peak order times and minimize wait times.
2. Introduce high‐margin menu items or bundles that appeal to guests at various dayparts (e.g., quick breakfast, late‐night snack).
3. Enhance communication with guests (e.g., estimated delivery time) to improve satisfaction and reduce complaints.
4. Offer promotions or loyalty incentives for in‐room dining to encourage trial and repeat orders.
5. Periodically review and update the in‐room dining menu to keep it fresh, relevant, and profitable.
