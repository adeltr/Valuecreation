# KPI — Real Estate & Construction

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Real Estate & Construction]]

53 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Absorption Rate | In the context of commercial real estate, absorption rate is a measure of the rate at which available spaces are sold or leased in a specific real estate market during a specific time period. It's useful in estimating how long it will take for existing space to be 'absorbed,' or filled. |
| Average Construction Time | This measures the average time taken to complete construction projects. Shorter times can suggest efficient project management, while longer times may flag potential inefficiencies or problems. |
| Average Revenue per Lease | In the rental sector, this KPI calculates the average revenue generated from each lease, providing a benchmark to evaluate performance and compare properties. |
| Average Time to Lease | This metric measures the average length of time it takes from when a property becomes vacant until it is leased again. A shorter time period is usually preferable as it indicates a more effective leasing process. |
| Bids per Project | In construction, this KPI shows the number of bids received per project, which can be a measure of competitiveness and market interest. |
| Building Efficiency Ratio | This ratio calculates the total rentable square footage divided by the gross square footage of a building. A higher ratio suggests better space utilization and potentially higher income generation capacity. |
| Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate) | This is used in real estate to indicate the return on an investment property. It is calculated as the annual net operating income divided by the property purchase price or market value. The cap rate allows comparisons between different types of property and market conditions. |
| Change Orders as a Percentage of Construction Cost | Change orders refer to any changes in the original construction plans, such as design modifications or additional features. If this percentage is high, it might indicate poor planning or project management. |
| Change Orders as a Percentage of Contract Value | In construction, this KPI tracks the value of change orders as a proportion of the overall contract value. A high percentage can indicate project instability or potential profitability issues. |
| Construction Cost Per Square Foot | This metric indicates how much it costs to build a particular project on a per square foot basis. It helps compare the cost efficiency of different projects or companies. |
| Construction Rework Costs | This KPI measures the costs associated with correcting or redoing construction tasks. Lower rework costs can indicate better initial work quality and can contribute to better overall project profitability. |
| Cost Variance (CV) | Used mainly in project management, CV is a measure of the cost performance of a project. It's the difference between the budgeted cost of work performed (BCWP) and the actual cost of work performed (ACWP). |
| Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) | Although used across industries, in real estate and construction, DSO measures the average number of days that receivables (like rental income) remain outstanding before they are collected. Shorter DSO usually indicates that tenants are paying their rent promptly. |
| Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) | This ratio compares a property's annual net operating income to its annual mortgage debt service, thus measuring the property's ability to cover its debt obligations. |
| Energy Consumption per Square Foot | For sustainability-focused companies or properties, this KPI measures the amount of energy consumed per square foot. Lower figures can indicate better energy efficiency. |
| Gross Rent Multiplier (GRM) | This metric gives a rough measure of the value of an investment property by taking the price of the property and dividing it by its gross rental income. A lower GRM generally suggests a more favorable investment. |
| Land to Development Cost Ratio | For developers, this ratio compares the cost of acquiring the land to the cost of developing it. Lower ratios may suggest higher profitability. |
| Lease Commencement Rate | For commercial real estate, this measures the speed at which a property or space moves from being leased to the lease actually commencing. |
| Lease Expiry Profile | This measures the timeline of when leases will expire. Companies with a balanced lease expiry profile will likely face less volatility in rental income. |
| Lease Renewal Rate | This KPI tracks the rate at which existing tenants choose to renew their leases. A higher renewal rate can imply tenant satisfaction and predictability of cash flow. |
| Leasing Spread | The difference between the rental rate of new leases and the rental rate of expiring leases for the same space. Positive leasing spreads imply rising rental prices. |
| Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratio | For companies involved in real estate financing, the LTV ratio compares the amount of a loan to the appraised value of the property. A lower LTV generally indicates lower risk. |
| Loan-to-Value Ratio (LTV) | This ratio compares the loan amount to the appraised value of the property. It's an essential measure of the risk associated with the loan. A lower LTV suggests less default risk. |
| Maintenance Costs as a Percentage of Revenue | This metric provides insight into the relative cost of maintaining properties compared to the income they generate. If this percentage is high, it could indicate inefficiency in maintenance activities or potentially problematic properties. |
| Net Present Value (NPV) of Property Developments | This financial indicator is used to estimate the profitability of an investment or project. Positive NPV indicates that the projected earnings are expected to result in a net gain, compared to the cost of investment. |
| Percentage of Green Buildings | For construction and development companies focused on sustainable building, this KPI measures the proportion of their properties that meet certain environmental or sustainability standards. |
| Percentage of Pre-leased Units | For property development companies, this KPI measures the percentage of units leased before the project's completion. A high percentage may indicate strong demand for the project. |
| Percentage of Project Completion | For construction companies, this KPI indicates the progress of ongoing projects. Tracking this can help identify any potential delays or issues early on. |
| Percentage of Rent Collections | This KPI tracks the percentage of total potential rent that is successfully collected in a period. It provides insights into tenant reliability and the effectiveness of rent collection efforts. |
| Percentage of Rented vs. Owned Assets | For businesses that both own and rent properties, this ratio offers insight into the balance between rented and owned assets. It can be a strategic decision based on factors like liquidity, control, and risk. |
| Percentage of Revenue from Property Management Services | For diversified real estate companies, this KPI provides insight into the relative importance of property management in their business model. |
| Percentage of Sustainable Materials Used | For construction firms focused on green building, this KPI measures the proportion of materials used that are sustainable or environmentally friendly. This can be important for the company's environmental impact and for attracting certain customers or investors. |
| Profitability Index (PI) | Used in capital budgeting, PI is the ratio of payoff to investment of a proposed project. It is a useful economic measure that can aid in selecting the most profitable projects. |
| Project Slippage | This measures the difference between the planned completion date and the actual completion date of a construction project. Regular slippages can indicate potential issues with project management and execution. |
| Property Operating Income | This measures the income generated from a property after deducting all operating expenses (excluding financing and depreciation). Higher operating income can indicate efficient property management and promising profitability. |
| Property Turnover Rate | This metric shows the rate at which properties or units become vacant. A high turnover rate may suggest issues with tenant satisfaction, property management, or market conditions. |
| Quality Control Fail Rate | This measures the percentage of construction projects or tasks that fail quality control checks on the first inspection. A lower fail rate can suggest higher quality workmanship and may decrease rework costs. |
| Rate of Return on Investment in Real Estate (ROI) | This KPI, often used by investors and real estate firms, measures the percentage return on a particular real estate investment. |
| Ratio of Commercial to Residential Properties | For diversified real estate companies, this ratio provides insights into the balance between commercial and residential properties in their portfolio. The optimal mix may depend on market conditions and strategic goals. |
| Rent Growth Rate | This KPI shows the annual percentage increase in rent. A positive rent growth rate may indicate a desirable area or strong property management and provides a signal for potential increases in revenue. |
| Rent vs. Local Median | For rental property owners or managers, this KPI measures how a property's rental rates compare to the local median. If the rent is higher, the property might offer superior features or be in a more desirable location. |
| Residential or Commercial Units Sold | For real estate development companies, this KPI tracks the number of units sold in a given period, reflecting sales performance and market demand. |
| Retention Cost per Tenant | This is a measure of how much a property management company spends to keep an existing tenant. This could include maintenance, incentives, and other costs. Lower costs can indicate efficient management. |
| Revenue per Available Room (RevPAR) | In the hotel real estate sector, RevPAR measures the average revenue generated per available room. It's a key performance measure of how well a hotel is filling its rooms and at what average rate. |
| Sales per Square Foot | For retail property owners, this KPI measures the average revenue generated per square foot of retail space. It's a measure of the property's revenue-generating efficiency. |
| Square Footage Under Management | In property management, this KPI measures the total square footage managed by the firm or real estate manager. It can reflect the scale and capacity of the management firm. |
| Tenant Acquisition Cost | For rental property businesses, this KPI measures the total cost involved in acquiring a new tenant, including marketing and administration expenses. Lower costs are generally preferable. |
| Tenant Retention Rate | The percentage of tenants who renew their leases at the end of the lease term. Higher tenant retention rates often indicate tenant satisfaction and reduce costs associated with tenant turnover. |
| Total Construction Cost Variance | This KPI measures the difference between the estimated costs of construction and the actual costs. Minimizing this variance can be key to profitability for construction companies. |
| Total Revenue Backlog | For construction companies, this represents the total value of projects that have been contracted but not yet completed or billed. It can provide insights into future revenue streams. |
| Vacancy Duration | This KPI tracks the average time that rental units remain vacant between tenants. Shorter durations can indicate an efficient leasing process and reduce lost rental income. |
| Vacancy Rate | This represents the number of vacant properties or units as a percentage of the total properties or units. A high vacancy rate may indicate a less desirable area or poor management, affecting the potential rental income. |
| Work-in-Progress (WIP) to Billings Ratio | This ratio provides a measure of uncompleted work compared to work that has been billed. A high WIP to Billings ratio might indicate potential cash flow problems if a lot of work is being completed but not yet billed. |
