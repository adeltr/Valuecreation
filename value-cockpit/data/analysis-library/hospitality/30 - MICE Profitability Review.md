# 30 · MICE Profitability Review

> **Part:** [[G - Events & Alternative Offerings]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Examine revenue, costs, and booking patterns within the Meetings, Incentives, Conferences, and Exhibitions segment to enhance overall profitability and market share.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical MICE booking data (types of events, attendance, room nights associated, F&B revenue).
- Revenue breakdown from MICE events (room revenue, banquet/catering, audiovisual, other ancillary services).
- Cost of sales (labor, F&B costs, event setup, equipment rentals).
- Occupancy data during MICE events compared to non‐MICE periods.
- Booking window and lead time for different types of MICE events.
- Feedback from event organizers and attendees (satisfaction scores, repeat business, referrals).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Collect all MICE‐related bookings and revenue data for your chosen period (monthly, quarterly, or annually). Categorize events by type (meetings, incentive trips, conferences, exhibitions).
2. Break down revenue streams from MICE events, separating out room revenue, F&B, AV/equipment, and any other fees (parking, venue rental, etc.).
3. **Calculate direct costs specific to MICE events:** staffing (banquet servers, event managers), F&B cost of goods, equipment rentals, and any outsourced services.
4. Determine departmental or event‐level profit margins by comparing total MICE revenue to direct and allocated overhead costs (e.g., share of utilities, cleaning, administrative expenses).
5. Analyze event attendance and room block pickup to see if actual room nights match the initial block commitment. Note discrepancies that may affect revenue forecasts.
6. Review booking windows for MICE events (e.g., corporate conferences may book months ahead, while smaller meetings might be short‐lead), then align this data with your revenue management strategies.
7. Correlate MICE activity with occupancy patterns. Investigate whether MICE events are filling low‐demand periods or cannibalizing higher‐rate transient business.

8. Assess organizer and attendee feedback to identify strengths and areas to improve (service quality, technology offerings, space layout, catering options).
9. Compare performance with internal or external benchmarks (past years, competitive set).


## 📤 Output format
1. **A spreadsheet or table summarizing:** ○ Event Type ○ Attendance and Room Pickup ○ Total MICE Revenue ○ Direct Costs (F&B, staffing, AV) ○ Net Profit or Margin (%)
2. Charts illustrating MICE revenue contribution over time, segmentation by event type, and profit margins across different event categories.
3. A narrative discussing key findings, recurring challenges, and potential areas of growth or cost savings.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High profit margins on certain event types suggest you can focus on marketing similar events or upselling related services.
- Low pickup on contracted room blocks may indicate inaccurate forecasting or insufficiently enforced contractual obligations.
- Significant overhead costs or labor spikes for MICE events can reduce net margins, signaling a need for more efficient staffing or renegotiated vendor contracts.
- Positive organizer feedback and repeat bookings suggest strong service quality and an opportunity to nurture long‐term partnerships.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Optimize pricing for meeting spaces and packages based on demand patterns, offering peak‐ vs. off‐peak event rates.
2. Refine labor scheduling to align with event needs and reduce idle staff hours.
3. Enhance service offerings (e.g., advanced AV, hybrid event technology, tailored F&B menus) to justify higher margins.
4. Negotiate favorable supplier or vendor contracts for AV equipment, décor, or outsourced event services.
5. Focus on relationship building with corporate clients and event planners to encourage repeat business and referrals.

6. Monitor pickup and post‐event data to refine future contracts, reduce attrition risk, and forecast revenue more accurately.
