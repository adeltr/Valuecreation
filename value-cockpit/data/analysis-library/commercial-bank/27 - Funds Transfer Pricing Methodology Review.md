# 27 · Funds Transfer Pricing Methodology Review

> **Part:** [[D - Capital, Liquidity, and Funding]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Commercial Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
Ensure the bank’s funds transfer pricing (FTP) framework accurately allocates funding costs and benefits across business units to optimize profitability and risk management.


## 📊 Data required
- Current FTP policies and procedures
- Breakdown of asset and liability balances (e.g., loan portfolios, deposits, wholesale funding)
- Interest rates, spreads, and cost of funds data
- Historical market rates and yield curves for reference
- FTP results by product line or business unit
- Peer or industry benchmarks (if available)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Review the bank’s existing FTP policy, including how transfer rates are determined for various asset and liability products, noting whether a matched-maturity approach or a pool-based approach is used.
Step 2: Gather data on actual funding sources, costs, and durations, ensuring alignment between the FTP assumptions (e.g., reference rates, liquidity premiums) and real market conditions.
Step 3: Verify the allocation of FTP charges and credits to business units or product lines, assessing whether they accurately reflect each unit’s contribution to or use of liquidity and capital.
Step 4: Compare current FTP outcomes (e.g., business unit profitability or cost attribution) with alternative methodologies or benchmarks to identify potential gaps or distortions in pricing.
Step 5: Conduct scenario analysis or sensitivity testing on key parameters (e.g., interest rate fluctuations, credit spreads, liquidity costs) to determine how changes in market conditions might affect FTP results.
59 Step 6: Summarize findings in a report, highlighting any misalignment between FTP assumptions and the bank’s actual risk profile or funding structure, and propose improvements to methodology or governance.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables and charts showing FTP rates by product, business unit, and maturity profile
- Comparative analysis of actual funding costs versus allocated FTP charges
- Written summary detailing methodological strengths, identified gaps, and recommended adjustments


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- If FTP charges do not accurately reflect true funding costs or liquidity risk, certain product lines may appear artificially profitable or unprofitable.
- Large deviations from peer or market benchmarks may indicate mispricing that could distort internal performance evaluations.
- Robust methodologies typically incorporate dynamic market inputs and align closely with actual liquidity and interest rate risks.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Review and update FTP policies regularly to ensure alignment with current market rates, liquidity premiums, and the bank’s funding mix.
2. Enhance governance over FTP processes by establishing independent oversight, regular validation, and transparent communication with business units.
3. Integrate scenario analysis and stress testing into the FTP framework to capture potential fluctuations in interest rates and liquidity conditions.
4. Adopt a more granular or matched-maturity approach for products with distinct liquidity profiles, such as long-term loans or seasonal deposits.
5. Benchmark against industry peers or best practices to stay competitive and ensure fairness in internal cost and profit allocation.
