# 22 · Dedicated vs. Shared Logistics Solutions Comparison

> **Part:** [[D - Commercial & Contractual Structures]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Third Party Logistics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Compare the benefits, trade-offs, and cost implications of operating dedicated logistics solutions (exclusive assets and facilities for one customer) versus shared (multi-client) logistics solutions.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical shipment volumes and variability per customer
- Current capacity usage and asset utilization rates
- Detailed cost breakdowns for dedicated vs. shared scenarios (labor, overhead, capital)
- Service-level requirements (delivery windows, handling specifications)
- Forecasted growth and demand patterns
- Pricing models for both dedicated and shared operations


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define Operational Scenarios:** Identify the parameters for dedicated logistics (e.g., exclusive fleet, dedicated warehouse space) and shared logistics (e.g., multi-client warehouse, pooled transport).
2. **Calculate Cost Structures:** Break down fixed costs (facility lease, equipment, core staffing) and variable costs (fuel, handling, packaging) for both scenarios. Determine cost per unit shipped or per pallet to compare.
3. **Assess Service Levels & Requirements:** Evaluate how dedicated vs. shared setups meet customer-specific service metrics (on-time performance, special handling). Check if unique processes or high-touch services favor dedicated resources.
4. **Analyze Scalability & Flexibility:** Measure how each option adapts to volume surges or seasonal demand fluctuations. Consider whether shared logistics offers better flexibility via capacity pooling.
5. **Compare Asset Utilization Rates:** Look at fleet usage, warehouse occupancy, and labor utilization in dedicated vs. shared arrangements. Identify opportunities for improved asset turnover in a shared environment.

6. **Evaluate Risk & Control Factors:** Determine if dedicated models provide better operational control and brand protection vs. potential operational synergies and cost-sharing in a multi-client facility.
7. **Forecast Long-Term Benefits & Drawbacks:** Project future volumes and costs to understand how each approach scales over time. Incorporate sensitivity analysis for demand volatility or new market expansions.


## 📤 Output format
- A cost comparison table displaying per-unit or per-pallet expense under dedicated vs. shared solutions
- A summary chart illustrating asset utilization, service level performance, and operational risks
- A scenario analysis showing potential outcomes under varying demand conditions (low, average, peak)
- A recommendation report highlighting which model is most advantageous, with rationale based on cost, service, and flexibility


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Lower Cost Per Unit in Shared Model:** Suggests economies of scale and flexible capacity utilization, appealing when volumes are variable.
- **Higher Service Control in Dedicated Model:** Reflects exclusive operational focus on one customer’s needs, beneficial for complex or time-sensitive operations.
- **Better Scalability in Shared Environments:** Indicates the ability to handle demand spikes without large capital outlays for additional capacity.
- **Strong Brand Control and Customization in Dedicated Setups:** Highlights personalized customer experiences, though often at a higher operational cost.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Conduct Volume Forecasting & Sensitivity Analyses:** Project both stable and peak scenarios to determine cost advantages of dedicated vs. shared solutions.
2. **Negotiate Flexible Shared Space or Capacity:** Create contingency clauses in shared logistics contracts to secure additional capacity during peak periods at favorable rates.

3. **Evaluate Hybrid Approaches:** Combine dedicated elements (e.g., select warehouses) with shared resources (transport fleets) to balance cost savings and service control.
4. **Implement Rigorous Performance Tracking:** Monitor key metrics (cost per unit, on-time delivery) regularly to ensure chosen models meet evolving needs.
5. **Review Periodically:** Revisit the dedicated vs. shared decision as market conditions, volumes, and customer requirements change over time.
