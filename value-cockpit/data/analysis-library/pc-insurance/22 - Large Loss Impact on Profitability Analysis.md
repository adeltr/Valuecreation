# 22 · Large Loss Impact on Profitability Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Claims Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Large Loss Impact on Profitability Analysis is to assess the effect of significant losses on overall profitability, helping the company understand its exposure to high-cost claims and the adequacy of risk mitigation strategies. This analysis provides insights into how large losses influence financial performance and informs adjustments in pricing, reinsurance, and underwriting practices.


## 📊 Data required
1. Claims data, identifying large losses (typically claims exceeding a certain threshold, e.g., top 5% by cost)
2. Total claims paid, segmented by large losses vs. other claims
3. Total earned premiums for the analysis period
4. Large loss thresholds for defining high-severity claims


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define Large Loss Threshold:** Determine a threshold for large losses, such as the top 5% of claim amounts or a fixed amount (e.g., claims over $100,000). This threshold should align with the company’s financial context and risk tolerance.
2. **Segment Claims Data by Large Losses and Regular Claims:** Divide claims into two categories: large losses (above the threshold) and regular claims (below the threshold).
3. **Calculate Total Claims Paid and Large Loss Amounts:** Sum the total claims paid for both large losses and regular claims.
4. **Calculate Large Loss Ratio:** Use the following formula to determine the portion of claims costs due to large losses:
`Large Loss Ratio = (Total Large Loss Amount / Total Claims Paid) x 100`
This ratio indicates the percentage of total claims costs attributable to large losses.
5. **Calculate Impact on Overall Profitability:** To assess the impact on profitability, calculate the loss ratio with and without large losses:
- Loss Ratio (Including Large Losses) = (Total Claims Paid / Total Earned Premiums) x 100
- Loss Ratio (Excluding Large Losses) = ((Total Claims Paid - Total Large Loss Amount) / Total Earned Premiums) x 100 Compare these two ratios to quantify the impact of large losses on profitability.
6. **Track Large Loss Trends Over Time:** Analyze large loss impact over multiple periods (e.g., quarterly, annually) to detect any trends or shifts in the frequency or cost of large losses.
7. **Compile Results:** Present the results in a table with total claims, large losses, large loss ratios, and loss ratios with and without large losses for comparison.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Period (e.g., Q1 2023, Q2 2023)
- Total Claims Paid ($)
- Large Loss Amount ($)
- Large Loss Ratio (% of total claims)
- Loss Ratio (Including Large Losses) (%)
- Loss Ratio (Excluding Large Losses) (%)
A line or bar chart comparing loss ratios with and without large losses over time can illustrate the impact visually.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Large Loss Ratio:** Indicates that a significant portion of claims costs is driven by large losses, which may expose the company to volatility in profitability.
- **Significant Difference Between Loss Ratios (With and Without Large Losses):** Highlights the impact of large losses on profitability, indicating a need for reinsurance or adjustments in underwriting for high-severity risks.
- **Trends in Large Loss Frequency or Cost:** Rising trends may suggest an increasing risk of large losses, necessitating proactive risk management strategies.
Benchmarking large loss ratios against industry standards can provide further insight into whether large loss exposure is typical or excessive for the company’s market position.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Enhance Reinsurance Coverage Consider increasing reinsurance coverage specifically for large losses to reduce the direct financial impact on the company.
2. Refine Underwriting Guidelines for High-Severity Risks Strengthen underwriting standards for segments with a high frequency of large losses to better manage risk exposure.
3. Adjust Pricing to Reflect Large Loss Risk Incorporate large loss trends into pricing models to ensure premiums adequately cover potential high-cost claims.
4. Implement Targeted Risk Mitigation Programs Work with policyholders in high-risk segments to implement preventative measures that reduce the likelihood of large losses.
5. Monitor Large Loss Claims by Segment Regularly track large loss claims by product line or customer segment to identify any areas requiring additional attention or adjusted risk limits.
6. Use Predictive Modeling to Identify Potential Large Losses Apply predictive analytics to detect claims with a high likelihood of developing into large losses, enabling early intervention and management.
