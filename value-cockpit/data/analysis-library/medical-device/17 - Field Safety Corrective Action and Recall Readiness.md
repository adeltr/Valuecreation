# 17 · Field Safety Corrective Action and Recall Readiness Evaluation

> **Part:** [[B - Clinical & Post-Market]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Medical Device Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Assess a medical device company’s preparedness to execute field safety corrective actions (FSCAs), including product recalls, when device-related safety risks or regulatory noncompliances emerge. Verify that processes exist for timely and effective risk mitigation to safeguard patients and comply with regulations.


## 📊 Data required
- Current standard operating procedures (SOPs) for field safety notices, corrective actions, and recalls
- Past recall or FSCA documentation, including root cause analyses and corrective/preventive actions (CAPAs)
- Complaint handling records and adverse event reports that may have triggered past recalls or near-miss situations
- Quality Management System (QMS) documentation related to risk management, product traceability, and communication plans
- Regulatory guidelines or notifications regarding product safety and recall requirements (for example, FDA 21 CFR Part 7, EU MDR obligations)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Review existing recall and FSCA policies. Verify that SOPs detail how to identify, escalate, and manage potential field actions and that roles and responsibilities are clearly outlined.
2. Evaluate traceability systems. Confirm the company can accurately locate products in the supply chain and identify affected batches or serial numbers.
3. Examine internal communication channels. Assess how swiftly potential device issues are brought to the attention of decision-makers to initiate a FSCA if needed.
4. Check external communication plans. Determine how effectively the company notifies regulatory authorities, healthcare professionals, and patients in the event of a recall or field action.
5. Analyze past recall experiences. Review root cause analyses, timelines, and CAPA effectiveness to see if previous lessons have been integrated into current processes.

6. Assess risk management and documentation. Ensure that risk assessments for post-market issues are documented and regularly updated, with clear criteria for initiating FSCA or recall.
7. Conduct a readiness drill or simulation. Run a mock recall scenario to test the efficiency and clarity of SOPs, documentation retrieval, and stakeholder notifications.
8. Summarize findings and propose improvements. Highlight strengths in readiness, identify procedural gaps, and recommend training or system enhancements.


## 📤 Output format
- A gap analysis report mapping current FSCA and recall procedures against regulatory requirements
- A risk-based action plan with priority levels, assigned owners, and deadlines for remedial measures
- A written summary of the recall readiness drill findings (if performed) and recommendations for process refinement
- An executive-level overview highlighting the overall state of recall preparedness


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A robust FSCA program, with high traceability and swift decision-making, indicates strong recall readiness and patient safety oversight.
- Persistent process gaps, delays in communication, or poor record-keeping increase regulatory and litigation risks in the event of a serious device issue.
- Recurring problems in past recalls may indicate that the company has not fully integrated lessons learned into its QMS.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
- Implement an integrated tracking system to enhance product traceability and reduce time to identify affected units.
- Conduct regular training and scenario-based drills to ensure staff understand recall procedures and responsibilities.
- Maintain a predefined communication plan to rapidly notify regulators, healthcare providers, and patients when a safety issue arises.
- Continuously monitor complaint data, adverse events, and emerging product risks to catch potential recall triggers early.
- Engage in periodic third-party audits to validate the efficacy of FSCA and recall processes, incorporating external best practices.
