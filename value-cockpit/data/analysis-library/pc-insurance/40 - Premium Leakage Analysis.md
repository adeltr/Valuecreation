# 40 · Premium Leakage Analysis

> **Part:** [[E - Operations]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Premium Leakage Analysis is to identify areas where potential revenue is lost due to undercharging, mispricing, or other inefficiencies in premium collection. By analyzing premium leakage, the company can improve pricing accuracy, reduce revenue loss, and ensure premiums align with risk exposure.


## 📊 Data required
1. Policy premiums and risk factors (e.g., coverage limits, location, insured value)
2. Claims history and severity per policy type
3. Underwriting and rating guidelines
4. Premium adjustments, discounts, and endorsements
5. Historical audit data on premium collection (optional)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Identify Risk Factors that Influence Premiums:** Review key risk factors used in underwriting and pricing for each policy type, such as location, insured value, and coverage limits. This forms the baseline for assessing whether premiums align with risk.
2. **Calculate Expected Premiums Based on Risk Exposure:** Using underwriting guidelines, calculate the expected premium for each policy based on the associated risk factors. Compare these expected premiums to actual premiums collected:
`Premium Leakage = Expected Premium - Actual Premium Collected`
3. **Assess Discounts and Adjustments:** Analyze any discounts or endorsements applied to policies to identify potential overuse or errors. Discounts that do not align with risk factors may contribute to premium leakage.
4. **Evaluate Claims Ratios by Segment:** Compare the loss ratio (claims paid as a percentage of earned premiums) across segments to identify any underpriced areas:
`Loss Ratio (%) = (Claims Paid / Earned Premiums) x 100`
High loss ratios may indicate underpricing or premium leakage, especially if claims costs consistently exceed collected premiums.
5. **Conduct a Premium Audit:** Perform a sample audit of policy premiums to check for discrepancies between expected and actual premiums. This can highlight common causes of leakage, such as miscalculated premiums or underwriting errors.
6. **Analyze Leakage by Policy Type and Segment:** Segment premium leakage by policy type, customer demographic, or geographic region to identify areas where leakage is most prevalent. This allows for targeted interventions.
7. **Compile Results:** Summarize findings in a table, showing expected premiums, actual premiums, and premium leakage for each policy type or segment.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Policy Type or Segment (e.g., auto, property)
- Expected Premium ($)
- Actual Premium Collected ($)
- Premium Leakage ($)
- Loss Ratio (%)
- Discounts Applied (%)
A bar chart comparing expected versus actual premiums by policy type can help illustrate premium leakage across segments.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Premium Leakage:** Indicates significant under-collection, which may result from mispricing, underwriting errors, or excessive discounts.
- **Low or No Premium Leakage:** Suggests that premiums align well with risk factors, indicating effective pricing and underwriting practices.
- **High Loss Ratio with Premium Leakage:** Suggests underpricing relative to risk, potentially requiring adjustments to align premiums with actual exposure.
Benchmarking against industry standards can provide additional context for assessing whether the company's premium leakage levels are typical or excessive.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Enhance Underwriting Accuracy Implement more rigorous underwriting practices to ensure premiums reflect the actual risk profile, reducing the chance of mispricing.
2. Optimize Rating and Pricing Models Refine pricing models to better incorporate risk factors, ensuring that expected premiums accurately reflect exposure.
3. Audit Discounts and Adjustments Regularly Review discount policies and premium adjustments periodically to prevent overuse or misapplication, aligning discounts more closely with risk factors.
4. Conduct Regular Premium Audits Perform periodic audits of policy premiums to identify discrepancies and address leakage proactively.
5. Use Predictive Analytics for Pricing Optimization Leverage predictive analytics to model and adjust premiums based on claims data and emerging trends, minimizing leakage due to outdated pricing models.
6. Train Underwriters on Premium Leakage Risks Provide training for underwriters to recognize and prevent common causes of premium leakage, fostering a focus on accuracy.
7. Set Leakage Reduction Targets and Monitor Progress Establish targets for reducing premium leakage and monitor results over time, adjusting strategies as necessary to meet revenue goals.
