# 12 · Product Texture Change Over Time (Stability Testing)

> **Part:** [[A - Product and Research & Development (R&D)]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Cosmetics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Product Texture Change Over Time (Stability Testing) analysis is to evaluate how a cosmetic product's texture holds up under various storage conditions and over its intended shelf life. This analysis ensures that the product remains consistent in texture, viscosity, and feel, and does not separate, harden, or degrade over time, ensuring consumer satisfaction and product effectiveness.


## 📊 Data required
- Product formulation details (e.g., emulsifiers, thickeners, preservatives).
- Initial product texture data (viscosity, spreadability, smoothness).
- Stability test results under various conditions (temperature, humidity, light).
- Results from accelerated aging tests (e.g., freeze-thaw cycles).
- Consumer feedback on texture experience during product use.
- Packaging information (e.g., air-tightness, material compatibility with the formulation).
- Shelf-life data (expiration testing, degradation trends).
- Industry benchmarks for texture stability.
- Microbiological testing results (e.g., microbial growth under different storage conditions).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define initial texture parameters:** a. Measure the initial texture of the product immediately after production, using standard texture analysis methods (e.g., viscosity testing, rheology measurements). b. Record data on key texture characteristics such as spreadability, consistency, and smoothness.
2. **Conduct accelerated stability tests:** a. Store product samples under varying temperature and humidity conditions, simulating extended storage and potential consumer environments. b. Use accelerated aging techniques, such as subjecting the product to multiple freeze-thaw cycles, to simulate long-term use. c. Track texture changes over time at intervals (e.g., 1 month, 3 months, 6 months).
3. **Evaluate viscosity and texture consistency:** a. Measure changes in product viscosity using a viscometer or rheometer after different storage periods. b. Compare viscosity data to the initial baseline to determine if the product has thickened, thinned, or separated over time. c. Check for signs of phase separation (e.g., oil and water separation in emulsions) or granulation.
4. **Analyze temperature impact on texture:** a. Store samples at different temperature settings (e.g., 5°C, 25°C, 40°C) to observe the effect on product texture.
41 b. Document texture changes (hardening, melting, or separation) that occur under extreme temperatures.
5.   **Perform packaging compatibility testing:** a. Evaluate the product’s packaging to ensure it protects the product from external conditions that may affect texture (e.g., air exposure, light exposure). b. Assess whether the packaging material reacts with the formulation, causing any texture degradation.
6.   **Microbiological testing:** a. Conduct microbiological stability tests to ensure that microbial contamination does not affect the product’s texture over time, particularly for products stored under higher humidity or after exposure to air. b. Ensure that preservatives in the formulation are effective in maintaining product integrity.
7.   **Collect consumer feedback on texture:** a. Gather feedback from consumers over time regarding the feel, spreadability, and application of the product. b. Look for complaints about changes in texture, especially for long-term users of the product.
8.   **Compare with industry benchmarks:** a. Benchmark the product’s texture stability against competitor products in similar categories (e.g., creams, gels, serums). b. Assess whether the product’s texture stability meets or exceeds industry standards for long-term storage.
9.   **Review shelf-life data:** a. Use the data from accelerated stability tests to estimate the product’s shelf life. b. Identify the point at which the texture changes beyond acceptable limits and determine the optimal expiration period.


## 📤 Output format
- A report detailing texture changes over time under different storage conditions, including viscosity measurements and visual observations.
- A table showing viscosity changes at various time intervals and temperatures.
- Graphs illustrating the impact of different environmental conditions (e.g., temperature, humidity) on product texture.
- A summary of consumer feedback related to texture stability and application experience.
- A stability comparison report showing how the product’s texture holds up compared to competitors.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Consistent texture over time:** If the product maintains its original texture (e.g., smoothness, viscosity) over its shelf life under different conditions, this indicates strong stability and high product quality.
42
- **Texture degradation:** Significant changes in texture, such as thickening, thinning, or phase separation, suggest that the product may not be stable under certain conditions. This may require reformulation or improved packaging to enhance stability.
- **Packaging incompatibility:** If texture degradation occurs due to packaging issues (e.g., air or light exposure), consider switching to more protective or compatible packaging materials.
- **Microbial contamination:** If microbial growth affects texture, the product may require stronger preservatives or better packaging to ensure safety and stability.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Reformulate to improve stability:** If texture issues arise under certain conditions, adjust the formulation by adding or adjusting stabilizers, thickeners, or emulsifiers to improve consistency over time.
2. **Use advanced packaging:** If air or light exposure affects texture, consider using air-tight, opaque, or UV-protective packaging to prevent environmental degradation.
3. **Enhance preservative systems:** For products that show texture changes due to microbial contamination, reformulate with stronger preservatives or antimicrobial agents, particularly for products exposed to air or stored in high humidity.
4. **Improve temperature stability:** If the product is sensitive to temperature extremes, reformulate it with ingredients that maintain texture integrity at high or low temperatures. Alternatively, update the product label to recommend proper storage conditions.
5. **Monitor consumer feedback:** Continuously gather consumer feedback post-launch to ensure that the texture remains consistent throughout the product’s shelf life. Use this data to make ongoing improvements.
6. **Increase shelf-life testing:** Extend testing periods to better understand the long-term stability of the product, ensuring that it remains usable well past its intended shelf life.
7. **Benchmark regularly against competitors:** Regularly compare your product’s texture stability to that of competitors and industry standards to maintain or improve market competitiveness.
