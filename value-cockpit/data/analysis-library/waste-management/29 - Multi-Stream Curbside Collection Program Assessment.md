# 29 · Multi-Stream Curbside Collection Program Assessment

> **Part:** [[E - Fleet & Collection Optimization]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Waste Management Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the effectiveness and efficiency of a multi-stream curbside collection program (where recyclables, organics, and waste are separated at the source), identify opportunities to improve collection processes, and increase overall diversion rates.


## 📊 Data required
- Collection data (tonnage, frequency) for each stream (e.g., paper, plastics, organics, general waste)
- Participation rates and set-out rates (how many households properly sort their waste)
- Contamination levels by stream (e.g., non-recyclables in recycling bin)
- Operational costs (labor, fuel, vehicle types, maintenance)
- Route details (mileage, number of stops, time per stop)
- Disposal and processing costs (landfill tipping fees, recycling facility gate fees, composting charges)
- Commodity prices or market values for each recyclable stream


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Collect and review current program data Gather information on how many households or businesses participate, frequency of pickups, tonnage collected by stream, and any available quality metrics (e.g., contamination rates).
2. Segment waste streams and evaluate diversion Determine the share of each material stream in the total collected waste. Identify the portion that is successfully diverted (recycled, composted) versus landfilled.
3. Calculate contamination and capture rates Assess the extent of non-target materials in each stream (contamination) and the percentage of targeted materials actually recovered in the correct bin (capture rate).
Equation:
`Capture Rate (%) = ((Amount of Targeted Material Correctly Collected) / (Total Amount of Targeted Material in Waste Stream)) x 100`

4. Analyze operational efficiency Examine collection routes for potential redundancies or inefficiencies. Compare labor, vehicle usage, and fuel costs for multi-stream vs. single-stream or dual-stream systems.
5. Assess financial performance Factor in disposal costs saved through diversion and revenue from selling recyclable materials. Weigh these benefits against the added complexity or cost of multiple collection trucks or bins.
6. Investigate community engagement and education Review outreach efforts and educational programs (e.g., bin labeling, public campaigns). Determine if low participation or high contamination correlates with insufficient guidance.
7. Develop recommendations for improvement Highlight changes in collection frequency, bin design, routing, or public education to increase participation and reduce contamination.


## 📤 Output format
- A summary report detailing program participation, tonnages per stream, contamination rates, and diversion outcomes
- Tables or charts comparing multi-stream vs. alternative collection methods (costs, diversion rates, operational metrics)
- Maps of collection routes indicating potential overlaps or under-served areas
- Recommendations for operational refinements (e.g., new routing software, targeted education)


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High contamination rates in a multi-stream system may signal inadequate education or enforcement; low capture rates can indicate poor sorting compliance.
- Comparing operational costs to diversion benefits reveals whether the multi-stream program is cost-competitive or more expensive than alternatives.
- Community attitudes and participation levels strongly influence the success of multi-stream programs.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Strengthen community outreach with clear labeling, consistent messaging, and feedback on proper sorting practices.

2. Employ route optimization tools to minimize travel distances and streamline separate collections for each stream.
3. Adjust bin sizes or collection frequencies to match the volume of each stream, ensuring efficient use of vehicle capacity.
4. Conduct regular audits and feedback loops (e.g., tagging bins with errors) to reduce contamination over time.
5. Explore incentive or reward systems (e.g., pay-as-you-throw, recycling rebates) to encourage better source separation and higher participation.
