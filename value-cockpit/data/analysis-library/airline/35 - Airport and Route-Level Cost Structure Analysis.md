# 35 · Airport and Route-Level Cost Structure Analysis

> **Part:** [[G - Cost Management and Financial Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Airline Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Airport and Route-Level Cost Structure Analysis is to evaluate and manage the costs associated with specific airports and routes, enabling the airline to identify high-cost areas and implement cost-saving measures. This analysis aims to enhance route profitability and optimize resource allocation by understanding the cost structure at each airport and across routes.


## 📊 Data required
- Operating costs by airport and route, including landing fees, parking fees, gate fees, and terminal usage fees
- Variable costs by route, such as fuel, crew costs, maintenance, and ground handling
- Passenger-related costs, including security, baggage handling, and customer service by airport
- Revenue data by route and load factors per route and cabin class
- Seasonal and demand-based cost fluctuations for each airport
- Competitor data on costs and pricing on similar routes
- Historical data on delays, cancellations, and other route-specific operational disruptions


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Operating Costs by Airport and Route:** Break down operating costs at each airport and across all routes. Include landing, parking, gate, and terminal fees, as well as any variable costs incurred by route. Use the formula:
`Total Operating Cost per Route = Landing Fees + Gate Fees + Fuel`
Cost + Crew Costs + Ground Handling + Other Route-Specific Costs Identify routes and airports with the highest costs, providing a baseline for cost optimization.
2. **Assess Revenue and Load Factor by Route:** Review revenue per route and calculate load factors for each cabin class. Identify high-cost, low-revenue routes that may require adjustment in pricing, frequency, or capacity to improve profitability.
3. **Evaluate Cost per Available Seat Mile (CASM) by Route and Airport\:** Calculate the CASM for each route, using:
`CASM = Total Operating Cost per Route / Available Seat Miles (ASM)`
This metric helps identify cost-efficiency by route, highlighting routes with high CASM that may need cost management efforts.
4. **Analyze Seasonal and Demand-Based Cost Fluctuations:** Review how costs fluctuate seasonally at each airport and route. High-demand periods may increase fees for parking, gate access, or staffing. Assess whether adjusting flight schedules can minimize costs during peak periods or shift to more favorable times.
5. **Benchmark Costs Against Competitor Routes and Airports:** Compare costs per route and per airport against competitors operating on similar routes. Identify cost advantages or disadvantages, which can highlight areas for negotiation with airports or ground handling providers to reduce costs.
6. **Identify High-Cost Airports and Routes for Optimization:** Highlight airports and routes with recurring high costs or limited profitability. Focus on those with high gate fees, variable costs, or high CASM, suggesting these routes for frequency reductions, schedule adjustments, or alternative airport options.
7. **Run Scenario Testing for Cost Reduction Strategies:** Test cost-saving strategies, such as reducing flight frequency, using smaller aircraft on low-demand routes, or negotiating lower airport fees. Measure potential savings and profitability impacts from each scenario to guide decision-making.


## 📤 Output format
- **Operating Cost Summary by Airport and Route:** A table listing operating costs segmented by airport and route, identifying high-cost areas and potential cost-saving opportunities.
- **Revenue and Load Factor Report by Route:** A report showing revenue and load factors per route, highlighting routes with profitability challenges.
- **Cost per Available Seat Mile (CASM) Analysis:** A breakdown of CASM by route, pinpointing routes with high CASM that need attention.
- **Seasonal Cost Fluctuation Summary:** A report on seasonal and demand-based cost changes by route, suggesting potential schedule adjustments.
- **Competitor Benchmarking on Route Costs:** A comparison of the airline’s route costs with competitor data on similar routes.
- **Scenario Analysis Results for Cost Reduction Strategies:** Charts or tables showing projected savings and profitability changes from proposed cost reduction strategies.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Operating Cost Summary by Airport and Route identifies high-cost routes and airports, guiding efforts for cost management and negotiations.
- Revenue and Load Factor Report by Route shows route profitability, helping assess which routes may need pricing adjustments or reduced frequency.
- CASM Analysis highlights routes with high per-seat costs, suggesting areas for operational or cost structure adjustments.
- Seasonal Cost Fluctuation Summary indicates routes with seasonal cost increases, supporting adjustments to schedules or capacity during peak-cost periods.
- Competitor Benchmarking on Route Costs provides insights into the airline’s cost structure relative to competitors, helping negotiate lower fees or adjust pricing.
- Scenario Analysis Results for Cost Reduction Strategies forecast potential financial impacts from different strategies, supporting data-driven decision-making.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Negotiate Lower Fees at High-Cost Airports:** Work with airports to reduce landing, gate, or terminal fees on high-cost routes, potentially through volume agreements or preferred partnerships.
2. **Adjust Flight Schedules to Avoid Peak Costs:** Shift flight times to off-peak periods to minimize costs associated with peak demand, reducing expenses for gate fees, parking, and staffing.
3. **Reduce Frequency or Capacity on Low-Demand Routes:** Cut flight frequency or switch to smaller aircraft on low-demand routes, lowering overall route costs while maintaining essential services.
4. **Optimize CASM with Efficient Fleet Utilization:** Use fuel-efficient or right-sized aircraft on high CASM routes to reduce per-seat costs and improve profitability.
5. **Explore Alternative Airports:** Where feasible, consider using secondary airports with lower fees for cost-intensive routes, balancing cost savings with passenger convenience and demand.
