# 35 · Lease Financing and Equipment Finance Review

> **Part:** [[H - Specialized and Industry-Specific Solutions]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Commercial Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine how effectively the bank manages and expands its lease and equipment finance portfolio to generate profitable returns while controlling associated credit and operational risks.


## 📊 Data required
- Portfolio breakdown by asset type (e.g., vehicles, machinery, technology equipment)
- Lease structures (operating vs. finance leases), rates, and terms
- Credit risk metrics (default rates, loss given default, delinquencies)
- Residual value assumptions and realized values
- Operational costs (e.g., servicing, asset recovery, insurance)
- Industry and economic trends affecting the value of leased assets


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Segment the current lease financing and equipment finance portfolio by asset type, lease structure, and industry sector to identify concentration risks and high- performing segments.
Step 2: Collect credit performance data (default rates, delinquencies, recoveries) and compare these metrics against both internal targets and peer benchmarks, looking for underperforming segments that may require tighter underwriting or additional risk controls.
Step 3: Review residual value assumptions in both new lease originations and the existing portfolio, evaluating how closely actual realized values match projected amounts upon lease maturity or early termination.
Step 4: Calculate profitability metrics for each sub-segment (e.g., net interest margin, fee income, net returns after loss provisions) to determine how each product or asset category contributes to overall performance.
75 Step 5: Analyze operational processes, including lease documentation, servicing, billing, and asset recovery workflows, identifying inefficiencies, manual touchpoints, or process gaps that could inflate costs or delay recoveries.
Step 6: Incorporate external market data (e.g., used equipment demand, changes in technology or environmental regulations) to forecast potential shifts in residual values and asset life cycles, informing both pricing strategies and risk management.


## 📤 Output format
- Tabular summaries listing portfolio composition, delinquency data, and realized residuals by asset class
- Charts showing historical trends in lease volumes, credit losses, and net returns
- Written assessment highlighting underperforming segments, emerging risks, and recommendations for product or process improvements


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Steady or improving credit performance indicates effective underwriting and risk management, while rising delinquencies or defaults suggest heightened credit risk or market volatility.
- Large gaps between projected and realized residual values may signal inaccurate asset valuations or evolving market conditions requiring more frequent reviews.
- Healthy profitability and stable residual performance typically point to a well- managed leasing program that aligns with customer demand and market trends.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Update residual value models and pricing assumptions regularly to reflect the latest market data and technological shifts.
2. Enhance credit underwriting criteria and monitoring practices for higher-risk asset segments or industries.
3. Streamline servicing and asset recovery processes through automation or vendor partnerships to reduce operational costs.
4. Build specialized teams or hire industry experts for specific equipment categories, improving asset valuation and portfolio management.
5. Diversify lease offerings (e.g., flexible lease terms, buyback guarantees) to stay competitive and address a broader range of customer needs.
