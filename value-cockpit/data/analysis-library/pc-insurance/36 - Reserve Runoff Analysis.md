# 36 · Reserve Runoff Analysis

> **Part:** [[D - Finance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Reserve Runoff Analysis is to assess how accurately the company’s reserves for outstanding claims are managed over time. By comparing initial reserve estimates with actual claim settlements, this analysis helps evaluate the adequacy of reserves, identify trends in over- or under-reserving, and refine reserve-setting practices for future claims.


## 📊 Data required
1. Initial reserves set at the time claims are reported
2. Adjusted reserves over time for each claim
3. Final settled amounts for each claim
4. Claim type or product line (optional, for segment analysis)
5. Historical reserve data over multiple periods


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Collect Initial and Subsequent Reserve Data:** Gather data on the initial reserve amounts and any adjustments made in each period until the claim is fully settled.
2. **Calculate Reserve Runoff for Each Claim:** Determine the reserve runoff for each claim by comparing the initial reserve to the actual settled amount:
`Reserve Runoff = Initial Reserve - Final Settlement Amount`
Positive values indicate over-reserving (excess reserve), while negative values indicate under-reserving (reserve shortfall).
3. **Aggregate Reserve Runoff by Period:** Sum the reserve runoff for all claims within each period (e.g., quarterly, annually) to get a view of reserve adequacy trends over time.
4. **Calculate Reserve Runoff Ratio:** For each period, calculate the reserve runoff ratio, indicating the percentage difference between initial reserves and final settlements:
`Reserve Runoff Ratio (%) = (Total Reserve Runoff / Total Initial Reserves) x 100`
A ratio close to zero suggests accurate reserve estimates; positive ratios indicate over-reserving, and negative ratios suggest under-reserving.
5. Analyze Trends by Claim Type or Product Line (Optional)
If applicable, segment the runoff analysis by claim type or product line to identify any areas with consistent over- or under-reserving patterns.
6. **Track Reserve Runoff Over Multiple Periods:** Analyze the reserve runoff and runoff ratio over multiple periods to detect patterns in reserve adequacy, helping improve future reserve-setting practices.
7. **Compile Results:** Organize findings in a table displaying initial reserves, adjusted reserves, final settlements, and runoff ratios for each period and claim type.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Period (e.g., Q1 2023, Q2 2023)
- Total Initial Reserves ($)
- Total Adjusted Reserves ($)
- Total Final Settled Amounts ($)
- Reserve Runoff ($)
- Reserve Runoff Ratio (%)
A line chart showing reserve runoff ratios over time can help visualize trends and pinpoint periods with significant over- or under-reserving.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Reserve Runoff Ratio Near 0%:** Indicates accurate reserve setting, suggesting the reserves closely match actual claim costs.
- **Positive Reserve Runoff Ratio (Over-Reserving):** Implies that reserves are consistently higher than actual claim settlements, potentially tying up excess capital.
- **Negative Reserve Runoff Ratio (Under-Reserving):** Indicates that reserves are consistently lower than actual settlements, which may signal the need for more conservative reserve estimates.
Benchmarking against industry standards or historical data can provide further context for assessing reserve adequacy.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Refine Reserve-Setting Practices Adjust reserve-setting methodologies to better align initial estimates with expected claim costs, based on past reserve runoff trends.
2. Monitor and Adjust for Claims Severity Trends Regularly review trends in claims severity to inform reserve estimates, especially in high-variance segments.
3. Implement Actuarial Models for Accurate Projections Use actuarial models that incorporate historical claims data and loss development factors to improve reserve accuracy.
4. Conduct Regular Reserve Reviews Perform periodic reserve adequacy reviews to identify any necessary adjustments in reserve-setting assumptions and practices.
5. Segment Analysis for Targeted Improvements Focus on specific claim types or product lines with consistent reserve runoff issues, refining reserve-setting guidelines accordingly.
6. Incorporate Economic and Environmental Factors Adjust reserves for external factors, like inflation or natural disasters, that may affect claims costs, ensuring adequate coverage for changing risk levels.
7. Benchmark Reserve Runoff Performance Compare the company’s reserve runoff ratios to industry averages, using these insights to enhance reserve-setting practices and improve accuracy over time.
