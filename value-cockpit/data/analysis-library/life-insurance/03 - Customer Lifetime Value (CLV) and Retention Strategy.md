# 3 · Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) and Retention Strategy Evaluation

> **Part:** [[A - Policyholder Behavior and Retention]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To calculate the lifetime value of a policyholder and evaluate the effectiveness of retention strategies in maximizing CLV. This analysis helps in identifying high-value customers, optimizing customer acquisition and retention efforts, and prioritizing resources to enhance profitability.


## 📊 Data required
1. Policyholder data, including demographics, policy type, premium payment frequency, and average policy tenure.
2. Historical revenue data, including premiums collected, renewal rates, and additional revenue from riders or upsells.
3. Retention cost data, including costs of retention campaigns, loyalty programs, and customer service.
4. Churn and lapse rate data for different segments of policyholders.
5. Industry benchmarks for CLV and retention strategies.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate the Average Customer Lifetime
`○ Use the formula: Average Lifetime = 1 / Churn Rate ○ Churn Rate = (Number of Customers Lost During the Period / Total Customers at Start of the Period) x 100.`
2. Determine Average Annual Revenue per Customer (AARPC) ○ Sum all premiums and additional revenue generated from riders or upsells for a policyholder segment and divide by the number of active policyholders in that segment.
3. Estimate Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)
`○ Use the formula: CLV = AARPC x Average Lifetime - Retention Cost per Customer`
4. Segment the Data ○ Analyze CLV by policyholder demographics, policy type, premium payment frequency, and distribution channels. ○ Segment CLV calculations for new customers vs. retained customers.
5. Analyze Retention Strategies ○ Review costs and ROI of retention campaigns (e.g., loyalty discounts, personalized communication). ○ Measure the impact of retention strategies on renewal rates and CLV.

6. Identify High-Value Customer Segments ○ Identify the top-performing segments with the highest CLV and evaluate the retention strategies applied to these segments.
7. Benchmark Against Industry Standards ○ Compare your CLV and retention strategy effectiveness with industry benchmarks to identify competitive gaps.
8. Visualize CLV and Strategy ROI ○ Create visualizations, such as bar charts for CLV by segment and ROI of retention campaigns, to communicate insights effectively.


## 📤 Output format
- CLV calculations by segment, summarized in a table.
- ROI of retention strategies, displayed in comparative bar charts.
- Heatmaps showing CLV distribution across demographics and policy types.
- Key findings report identifying the top 3-5 factors contributing to high CLV.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Higher CLV in specific segments indicates these customers are the most profitable and worth prioritizing for retention efforts.
2. Low CLV in certain segments suggests inefficiencies in retention strategies, product offerings, or customer engagement.
3. A high retention cost relative to CLV signals the need to optimize resource allocation and improve cost efficiency.
4. Comparison to industry benchmarks reveals how well the company is performing relative to competitors.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Use predictive analytics to identify at-risk high-value customers and implement proactive retention strategies.
2. Enhance loyalty programs or offer value-added services to increase customer engagement and renewals.
3. Personalize customer communication based on preferences and behaviors to strengthen relationships.
4. Optimize acquisition efforts to focus on customer segments with high potential CLV.
5. Evaluate and refine retention campaigns to ensure a positive ROI and alignment with high-value segments.
6. Conduct regular feedback surveys to identify areas of dissatisfaction and improve customer experience.
