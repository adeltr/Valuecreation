# 39 · Global Trade Flow Analysis for Raw Materials and Finished Products

> **Part:** [[G - Supply Chain]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Chemical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to assess the flow of raw materials and finished products across global markets, identifying key trade routes, dependencies, and potential risks. By understanding global trade flows, the company can optimize logistics, manage costs, and mitigate risks associated with trade disruptions, tariffs, and geopolitical factors.


## 📊 Data required
1.   Origin and destination data for raw materials and finished products
2.   Trade routes and transportation modes used for each flow
3.   Volume, frequency, and cost data associated with each trade route
4.   Import/export tariffs, trade agreements, and regulatory requirements by country
5.   Lead times and transit times for each route
6.   Geopolitical risks and recent disruptions impacting trade routes
7.   Industry benchmarks for trade flow efficiency and costs


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Map Key Trade Routes for Raw Materials and Finished Products Identify and map the primary trade routes used for sourcing raw materials and distributing finished products. Document the transportation modes (e.g., sea, air, rail) and entry/exit points in each country, providing a visual overview of the supply chain’s global footprint.
2. Analyze Volume, Frequency, and Cost of Each Trade Route Calculate the volume and frequency of shipments for each trade route and transportation mode. Summarize the associated costs, including freight, insurance, and handling fees. This provides insight into high-volume, high-cost routes that may benefit from optimization.
3. Evaluate Import/Export Tariffs and Trade Agreements Review tariffs, trade agreements, and other regulatory requirements that affect each trade route. Trade agreements can offer cost advantages, while high tariffs on certain routes may suggest opportunities to explore alternative sourcing or distribution strategies.
4. Assess Lead Times and Transit Times by Route Measure the lead time and transit time for each route from supplier to production site or from production site to customer. Identify routes with prolonged transit times or frequent delays, which may indicate a need for alternative routes or expedited shipping options.
5. Identify Geopolitical Risks and Trade Disruption Points Analyze potential risks associated with each trade route, including geopolitical tensions, natural disaster zones, or regions prone to regulatory changes. Identify routes that may How to Analyze a Chemicals Company be vulnerable to sudden disruptions and consider alternative pathways or contingency plans.
6. Benchmark Trade Flow Efficiency and Costs Against Industry Standards Compare the company’s trade flow costs, transit times, and efficiency with industry benchmarks. Higher-than-average costs or transit times may indicate inefficiencies, while shorter times and lower costs suggest competitive advantages in logistics.
7. Develop Recommendations for Trade Flow Optimization Based on the analysis, identify opportunities for improving trade flow efficiency, such as optimizing transportation modes, leveraging free trade agreements, or implementing just-in-time shipping. Recommend adjustments that enhance resilience, reduce costs, and minimize delays.


## 📤 Output format
1.   Visual map of trade routes for raw materials and finished products
2.   Summary of trade volume, frequency, and costs by route
3.   Overview of tariffs, trade agreements, and regulatory factors affecting each route
4.   Lead time and transit time analysis by trade route
5.   Geopolitical risk assessment for each trade route
6.   Benchmark comparison of trade flow efficiency and costs
7.   Recommendations for trade flow optimization and risk mitigation


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High-volume, high-cost routes:** Suggest that these routes may benefit from optimization efforts or cost-saving initiatives, such as alternative transport modes or consolidation.
- **Routes with significant geopolitical risks:** Indicate a need for contingency planning and potential exploration of safer, alternative routes.
- **High tariffs or regulatory barriers:** Suggest potential savings by leveraging free trade agreements or adjusting sourcing and distribution strategies.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize Transportation Modes and Consolidate Shipments:** For high-volume routes, consider consolidating shipments or using cost-effective modes to reduce per-unit costs and improve logistics efficiency.
2. **Leverage Trade Agreements and Duty-Free Zones:** Where possible, adjust sourcing and distribution to utilize free trade agreements and duty-free zones to minimize tariff costs and improve profitability.
3. **Establish Contingency Plans for High-Risk Routes:** Develop alternative routes or back-up suppliers for routes with high geopolitical risk, ensuring continuity during disruptions.
4. **Invest in Just-in-Time Shipping:** Where feasible, implement just-in-time (JIT) shipping for finished products to minimize warehousing needs and reduce holding costs.
