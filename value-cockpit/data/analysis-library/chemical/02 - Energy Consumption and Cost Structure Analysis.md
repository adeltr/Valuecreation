# 2 · Energy Consumption and Cost Structure Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Production & Process Optimization]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Chemical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to assess energy consumption patterns within the production process and to evaluate the cost structure related to energy usage. This analysis helps in identifying opportunities to reduce energy costs, improve sustainability, and optimize energy-intensive processes, directly impacting the company’s bottom line and environmental footprint.


## 📊 Data required
1.   Total energy consumed (kffh, GJ, or equivalent) during the production period
2.   Energy costs (cost per kffh, GJ, etc.)
3.   Breakdown of energy consumption by process (e.g., heating, cooling, machinery)
4.   Production output during the same period (tons or units)
5.   Operational hours during the period
6.   Benchmark data on energy usage and costs for similar facilities or industry standards


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate Total Energy Consumption Per Unit of Production:
Use the following formula to standardize energy usage:
`Energy Consumption per Unit = Total Energy Consumed / Production Output`
For example, if a facility uses 500,000 kffh to produce 10,000 tons, then:
`Energy Consumption per Unit = 500,000 / 10,000 = 50 kffh per ton`
2. Determine Energy Cost per Unit of Production:
Multiply the energy consumption per unit by the cost per unit of energy to get the energy cost per unit of production:
`Energy Cost per Unit = Energy Consumption per Unit x Energy Cost per kffh`
Continuing with the example, if energy costs $0.10 per kffh, then:
`Energy Cost per Unit = 50 x 0.10 = $5 per ton`
3. Analyze Energy Breakdown by Process:
Examine energy use by each major production process or equipment type to identify energy-intensive steps. This can involve tracking energy consumption specifically allocated to processes like heating, cooling, or chemical reactions.

4. Benchmark Against Industry Standards:
Compare the energy consumption and cost per unit to industry standards or benchmarks. High energy consumption relative to peers can indicate inefficiencies or outdated equipment that may benefit from upgrades.
5. Identify Patterns and Seasonality:
Review historical data to spot any trends, seasonal variations, or anomalies in energy consumption, which can reveal specific times when energy usage spikes and provide insight into seasonal efficiencies or deficiencies.


## 📤 Output format
1. Table of energy consumption metrics (e.g., energy per ton, cost per ton)
2. Energy cost breakdown by process (e.g., heating, cooling)
3. Benchmark comparison table or chart, showing facility metrics vs. industry standards


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High energy consumption per unit:** Indicates possible inefficiencies or areas where the production process could be optimized.
- **High energy costs per unit:** May signal an opportunity for renegotiating energy rates, investing in energy-efficient equipment, or using alternative energy sources.
- **Comparisons to benchmarks:** If energy use per ton is higher than industry standards, this indicates room for improvement in energy efficiency.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Upgrade to Energy-Efficient Equipment:** Invest in equipment or machinery that operates more efficiently or with reduced energy consumption.
2. **Optimize Process Timing and Scheduling:** Run energy-intensive processes during off-peak energy cost times, where possible, to reduce overall costs.
3. **Implement Energy Recovery Systems:** Utilize waste heat recovery or other energy capture technologies to recycle energy back into the production process.
4. **Regularly Maintain and Calibrate Equipment:** Ensure that machinery operates at peak efficiency, as unmaintained equipment can consume more energy over time.
5. **Explore Renewable Energy Sources:** Consider solar, wind, or other renewable options, if feasible, to offset costs and reduce reliance on conventional energy.
