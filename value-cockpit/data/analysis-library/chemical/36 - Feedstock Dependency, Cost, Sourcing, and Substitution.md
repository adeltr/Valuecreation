# 36 · Feedstock Dependency, Cost, Sourcing, and Substitution Strategy Analysis

> **Part:** [[F - Research & Development (R&D)]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Chemical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to evaluate the company’s dependency on key feedstocks, assess feedstock costs and sourcing strategies, and explore substitution options. By understanding dependency and substitution possibilities, the company can reduce supply chain risk, control costs, and adapt to market or regulatory changes effectively.


## 📊 Data required
1. List of primary feedstocks, quantities used, and costs
2. Dependency levels for each feedstock, including single-sourced or multi-sourced materials
3. Historical and forecasted price data for feedstocks
4. Supplier contracts and terms, including exclusivity, pricing, and lead times
5. Alternative feedstock options and associated costs
6. Quality and performance implications of feedstock substitutions
7. Environmental and regulatory factors influencing feedstock selection
8. Industry benchmarks for feedstock costs, dependency levels, and substitution strategies


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify Key Feedstocks and Assess Dependency Levels List all key feedstocks used in production, noting whether they are single-sourced, multi-sourced, or critical to the manufacturing process. Feedstocks with high dependency levels (e.g., single-sourced materials) pose a higher risk and should be prioritized in the analysis.
2. Analyze Feedstock Cost Trends and Volatility Review historical and projected cost trends for each feedstock, assessing average price volatility. Feedstocks with high price volatility can significantly impact production costs, making it essential to evaluate options for cost stabilization, such as long-term contracts or hedging strategies.
3. Evaluate Sourcing Strategy and Supplier Agreements Examine sourcing strategies and supplier agreements for each feedstock, including contract terms, lead times, and reliability. Assess whether each feedstock is sourced from multiple suppliers or regions to reduce risks associated with dependency on a single source.
4. Identify and Assess Alternative Feedstock Options Explore alternative feedstocks that could replace high-risk or high-cost materials. Evaluate the cost, availability, and quality differences of each substitute, as well as any potential impacts on production processes or product quality.
5. Consider Environmental and Regulatory Factors Assess any environmental or regulatory factors that influence feedstock selection, such How to Analyze a Chemicals Company as sustainability requirements, emissions limits, or hazardous material restrictions. If certain feedstocks are subject to regulation, substitutions may provide a more sustainable or compliant option.
6. Analyze the Feasibility of Substitution Evaluate the technical and economic feasibility of each potential substitution. Assess how substitutes would affect production processes, product performance, customer satisfaction, and overall cost structure. Where feasible, consider testing or piloting substitutes before making large-scale changes.
7. Benchmark Against Industry Standards for Feedstock Sourcing and Substitution Compare the company’s feedstock dependency levels, costs, and sourcing strategies with industry benchmarks. Benchmarking highlights whether the company’s costs are competitive and if there are best practices in the industry that could guide feedstock substitution.


## 📤 Output format
1. Feedstock dependency summary, showing single-sourced vs. multi-sourced materials and critical dependencies
2. Cost trend analysis for each feedstock, with historical volatility and projected costs
3. Supplier and sourcing strategy overview, including contract terms and geographic diversification
4. Alternative feedstock options with cost, quality, and performance comparisons
5. Regulatory and environmental compliance review for each feedstock
6. Benchmark comparison for dependency levels, cost control, and substitution strategies


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High dependency with limited sourcing options:** Indicates a need for diversification or development of alternative sourcing strategies to mitigate supply risk.
- **High cost volatility for essential feedstocks:** Suggests exploring long-term contracts, hedging strategies, or substitutes to stabilize costs.
- **Regulatory or environmental constraints:** Suggest that substitutions may be required to ensure compliance or improve sustainability, particularly if more sustainable alternatives are available.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Diversify Supplier Base:** Identify and qualify additional suppliers for high-dependency feedstocks to reduce reliance on single sources and ensure supply chain resilience.
2. **Implement Cost Hedging or Long-term Contracts:** Secure fixed pricing through long-term contracts or use hedging strategies for feedstocks with high price volatility to control costs.
3. **Invest in Feedstock Substitution R&D:** Allocate R&D resources to investigate and test viable substitutes for high-risk or high-cost feedstocks, ensuring they meet quality and performance standards.
