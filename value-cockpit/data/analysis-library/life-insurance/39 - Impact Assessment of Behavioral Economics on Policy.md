# 39 · Impact Assessment of Behavioral Economics on Policy Uptake and Retention

> **Part:** [[I - Customer Experience and Digital Transformation]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate how behavioral economics principles influence policyholder decisions regarding policy uptake and retention. The analysis identifies behavioral biases, decision triggers, and strategies that insurers can leverage to improve acquisition, persistency rates, and customer lifetime value.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policy-level data:** ○ Policy uptake rates, lapse rates, and persistency trends ○ Premium amounts, policy types, and customer demographics
2. **Customer behavior data:** ○ Response rates to pricing, incentives, and communication campaigns ○ Feedback from surveys or focus groups on purchasing decisions and reasons for lapses
3. **Communication data:** ○ Types, frequency, and content of communication with policyholders
4. **Financial data:** ○ Acquisition costs, retention costs, and lifetime value (LTV) per policyholder
5. **Benchmark data:** ○ Behavioral economics applications in the insurance industry


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify Behavioral Economics Factors a. Analyze customer behavior to identify common behavioral principles that impact decisions, such as: ■ Loss aversion: Customers prioritize avoiding loss (e.g., missed benefits from not renewing). ■ Status quo bias: Customers default to inaction, delaying purchases or renewals. ■ Framing effect: How the presentation of choices impacts decisions (e.g., “Save 10% today” vs. “Pay full price later”). ■ Hyperbolic discounting: Preference for immediate benefits over long-term rewards.
2. Segment Customer Data a. Categorize policyholders based on demographics, behavior, and policy types to identify segments most affected by behavioral biases.
114 b. Compare uptake and retention patterns across these segments.
3. Analyze Policy Uptake Trends a. Measure how pricing, incentives, and messaging influence uptake:
`Policy Uptake Rate (%) = (Number of New Policies Sold / Total Prospects Contacted) x 100 b. Test the impact of behavioral nudges, such as limited-time offers, bundled benefits, or simplified product explanations.`
4. Evaluate Retention and Lapse Trends a. Calculate persistency and lapse rates:
`Persistency Rate (%) = (Active Policies / Policies at Start of Period) x 100 Lapse Rate (%) = (Policies Terminated / Total Policies) x 100 b. Correlate lapses with factors such as communication frequency, incentives, or decision fatigue.`
5. **Test Behavioral Interventions a. Evaluate the impact of interventions such as:** ■ Default options: Offering automatic policy renewals or bundling products. ■ Framing: Highlighting the benefits of retention (e.g., savings, protection continuity). ■ Incentives: Rewards for early renewals or multi-policy adoption. ■ Simplification: Clear, jargon-free communication and easy policy processes.
6. Conduct A/B Testing a. Test different behavioral approaches on small customer groups: ■ Group A: Default renewal enabled vs. Group B: Manual renewal. ■ Group A: Framed messages highlighting benefits vs. Group B: Standard communication. b. Measure the differences in policy uptake, persistency rates, and response rates.
7. **Measure Financial Impact a. Quantify changes in:** ■ Policy acquisition costs ■ Retention costs ■ Lifetime value (LTV) of policyholders after implementing behavioral strategies.
8. Benchmark Against Industry Standards a. Compare policy uptake, lapse, and retention rates with benchmarks for insurers using behavioral economics strategies.
9. **Visualize Results:** 115 a. Present tables summarizing uptake, lapse, and persistency rates by segment and behavioral intervention. b. Use charts to compare the effectiveness of various behavioral nudges in improving outcomes.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables showing policy uptake, persistency, and lapse rates segmented by customer behavior and intervention type
- Charts illustrating the impact of behavioral strategies on policyholder decisions
- Key findings highlighting effective behavioral nudges and their financial impact


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Higher policy uptake and persistency rates indicate that behavioral interventions effectively address customer biases.
2. Low uptake or high lapse rates suggest gaps in communication, incentive design, or product presentation.
3. Significant improvements in key metrics, such as persistency rates and customer LTV, highlight the success of targeted behavioral strategies.
4. A/B testing results reveal which behavioral nudges have the greatest impact on specific customer segments.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Implement default renewal options to reduce policy lapses caused by decision inertia.
2. Use framing techniques to emphasize the benefits of retention and the costs of policy lapses.
3. Introduce incentives, such as premium discounts or loyalty rewards, for early renewals or policy conversions.
4. Simplify product explanations, communication, and processes to improve customer understanding and decision-making.
5. Personalize messages and offers based on customer segments to address specific biases or preferences.
6. Conduct continuous A/B testing to refine behavioral strategies and measure their impact.
7. Leverage behavioral insights to enhance digital tools, such as self-service portals and reminder systems, for improved customer engagement.
