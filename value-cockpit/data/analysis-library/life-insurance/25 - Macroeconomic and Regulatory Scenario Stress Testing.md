# 25 · Macroeconomic and Regulatory Scenario Stress Testing Analysis

> **Part:** [[D - Capital and Solvency Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the financial resilience of a life insurance company under adverse macroeconomic and regulatory conditions. This analysis identifies vulnerabilities, measures the impact on capital and solvency, and ensures readiness to meet regulatory requirements.


## 📊 Data required
- **Financial data:** balance sheet details, available capital, reserves, assets, and liabilities
- **Risk exposure data:** market risk, credit risk, insurance risk, and operational risk
- **Policy-level data:** premium revenue, claims, lapse rates, and persistency trends
- **Actuarial assumptions:** mortality rates, morbidity rates, lapse rates, and discount rates
- **Macroeconomic inputs:** interest rate shocks, equity market declines, inflation, and GDP changes
- **Regulatory scenarios:** capital requirement changes, solvency thresholds, and stress testing guidelines


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Define stress scenarios for macroeconomic and regulatory shocks. Examples include: ○ Interest rate shocks: increase or decrease in interest rates by 100–200 basis points. ○ Market downturns: declines in equity markets or bond values by 20%–30%. ○ Inflation spikes: rising claims expenses and administrative costs. ○ Regulatory tightening: increased capital requirements or stricter solvency thresholds.
2. Establish the baseline financial position. Measure the company’s solvency ratio, capital reserves, and financial metrics under normal conditions.
3. **Apply stress scenarios to financial components:** ○ Adjust asset values to reflect market downturns. ○ Increase liabilities to simulate prolonged low-interest rates or higher claims reserves. ○ Model increased expenses and claims under inflation scenarios. ○ Simulate higher lapse rates during economic distress.
71
4. Calculate solvency and capital adequacy post-stress. Use the formulas:
`Solvency Ratio (%) = (Available Capital / Required Capital) x 100 Capital Buffer = Available Capital - Required Capital`
5. Segment results by risk type to identify key vulnerabilities. Assess the impact on market risk, credit risk, insurance risk, and operational risk.
6. Benchmark post-stress results against regulatory thresholds, such as Solvency II or local solvency requirements, to assess compliance.
7. Perform trend analysis to compare results with historical stress tests and monitor changes in risk exposure.
8. **Visualize results to highlight key impacts. Use:** ○ Tables summarizing solvency ratios and capital adequacy under each stress scenario. ○ Charts showing changes in solvency ratios and available capital before and after stress testing.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables displaying solvency ratios, capital reserves, and financial metrics under baseline and stress conditions
- Charts illustrating the impact of adverse macroeconomic and regulatory scenarios on capital adequacy
- Key findings identifying financial vulnerabilities and risk sensitivities


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. A solvency ratio above 100% under all scenarios indicates financial resilience and regulatory compliance.
2. A significant decline in solvency ratios highlights vulnerability to macroeconomic shocks or regulatory changes.
3. High sensitivity to specific risks, such as interest rate movements or claims inflation, signals areas requiring targeted mitigation.
4. Failure to meet regulatory solvency thresholds under stressed conditions indicates capital inadequacy and potential regulatory concerns.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Increase capital reserves to build buffers against adverse macroeconomic conditions.
2. Diversify investments to reduce exposure to interest rate volatility and market downturns.
3. Optimize reinsurance strategies to transfer risks, such as mortality, morbidity, or longevity.

4. Improve asset-liability management (ALM) to align investment strategies with liability durations.
5. Strengthen cost controls to mitigate the impact of inflation on expenses and claims reserves.
6. Conduct regular internal stress testing to anticipate capital shortfalls and take preemptive corrective actions.
7. Monitor regulatory changes to ensure compliance and proactively adjust capital allocation strategies.
