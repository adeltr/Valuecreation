# 1 · Capacity Utilization Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Operations]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Manufacturing Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Capacity Utilization Analysis is to measure how effectively a manufacturing company is using its production capacity. It helps determine whether the company is operating efficiently, underutilizing resources, or overutilizing capacity, which could lead to potential inefficiencies.


## 📊 Data required
- **Actual Production Output:** The number of units produced in a given period (e.g., day, week, month).
- **Maximum Production Capacity:** The maximum number of units that could be produced with current equipment and resources in the same period.
- **Time Period:** The specific time frame for the analysis (e.g., monthly, quarterly).
- **Shift Schedules:** Total available work hours in a period based on labor and machine availability.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Gather Data:** ○ Collect actual production output for the period under review (e.g., daily or monthly). ○ Determine the maximum production capacity for the same period, based on machine throughput and available labor.
2. **Calculate Capacity Utilization:** Capacity Utilization (%) = (Actual Production Output / Maximum Production Capacity) x 100 For example, if a factory produced 8,000 units in a month and its maximum capacity is 10,000 units: Capacity Utilization = (8,000 / 10,000) x 100 = 80%
3. **Optional:** Break Down by Machine or Line: ○ If applicable, repeat this analysis for individual production lines or machines to identify differences in utilization across the facility.
4. **Track Over Time:** ○ Conduct this analysis regularly (e.g., monthly or quarterly) to identify trends in capacity utilization.


## 📤 Output format
6
- A table that includes actual output, maximum capacity, and capacity utilization percentage for each production line or machine.
- A graph (line chart or bar chart) showing capacity utilization over time.
- A summary of overall capacity utilization for the entire facility.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **85% to 95% Capacity Utilization:** This range is typically considered optimal. It indicates that resources are being used efficiently while allowing some flexibility for demand changes or maintenance.
- **Below 80% Capacity Utilization:** Indicates underutilization, where resources are not being fully leveraged. This could be a sign of inefficiencies, low demand, or excess capacity.
- **Above 95% Capacity Utilization:** This may suggest overutilization, which can lead to equipment wear, increased downtime, and limited ability to respond to changes in demand or emergencies.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Increase Demand or Production Volume:** ○ Boost sales and marketing efforts to increase product demand, leading to higher production volumes. ○ Explore new market opportunities or diversify product offerings to drive up production.
2. **Optimize Shift Schedules:** ○ Adjust labor or shift patterns to better align production with available capacity.
3. **Improve Operational Efficiency:** ○ Implement lean manufacturing principles to eliminate waste and streamline processes. ○ Reduce machine downtime by enhancing maintenance practices or adopting predictive maintenance technology.
4. **Upgrade Equipment:** ○ If equipment limitations cause low utilization, invest in equipment upgrades or replacements to increase capacity.
5. **Balance Workloads Across Production Lines:** ○ Redistribute work to ensure more even utilization of production lines, preventing bottlenecks or underuse.
6. Reduce Downtime:
7 ○ Analyze the root causes of downtime and take corrective actions, such as optimizing maintenance schedules or speeding up changeovers.
