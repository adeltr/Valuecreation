# 13 · Construction & Demolition (C&D) Waste Diversion and Recycling Efficiency

> **Part:** [[B - Recycling & Materials Recovery]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Waste Management Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To assess the effectiveness of C&D waste diversion programs, measure recycling efficiency, and identify strategies to reduce landfill disposal costs while capitalizing on the value of recoverable materials such as metals, wood, concrete, and drywall.


## 📊 Data required
- C&D waste generation data (tons or cubic yards)
- C&D waste composition by material type (wood, concrete, metals, drywall, etc.)
- Information on local recycling or diversion facilities (capacity, material acceptance, tipping fees)
- Transportation and handling costs for each waste stream
- Historical or benchmark diversion rates for C&D projects
- Applicable regulations or green building standards (e.g., LEED requirements)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify sources of C&D waste Break down project activities (e.g., demolition, renovation) and track the volume and type of debris generated at each stage.
2. Conduct a waste audit or materials inventory Perform on-site or off-site sorting to quantify each material type (wood, metal, masonry, drywall) and determine overall composition.
3. Evaluate disposal and recycling routes Document the existing methods for waste handling (e.g., landfill, specialized recycling facilities). Gather data on tipping fees, transportation costs, and material revenue potential.
4. Calculate diversion rate Quantify the fraction of total C&D waste that is diverted from landfill through recycling or reuse.
Equation:
`Diversion Rate (%) = ((C&D Waste Diverted) / (Total C&D Waste Generated)) x 100`

5. Compare against benchmarks or targets Assess current diversion performance relative to historical rates, industry standards, or specific certification goals (e.g., LEED prerequisites).
6. Analyze cost-benefit of improved diversion Estimate savings from reduced landfill tipping fees and potential revenue from recycling markets. Factor in any additional sorting or transportation costs.
7. Develop improvement plan Identify practical measures (e.g., on-site separation, improved contractor training, partnerships with recyclers) to enhance diversion rates and lower costs.


## 📤 Output format
- A table summarizing the volume of each material category, disposal routes, and associated costs
- Graphs or charts comparing actual diversion rates to benchmarks or industry standards
- A financial analysis showing potential savings or revenue increases from enhanced recycling
- Recommendations for operational, contractual, or technological interventions to improve C&D waste management


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A higher diversion rate indicates more materials are being recovered, reducing both environmental impact and disposal expenses.
- Low diversion rates may point to inadequate sorting infrastructure, insufficient staff training, or limited recycling market access.
- Comparing cost and revenue data clarifies the financial impact of investing in better C&D waste diversion practices.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Implement on-site sorting or source separation to reduce contamination and increase material recovery value.
2. Establish partnerships with specialized recyclers or reuse organizations for materials like wood, concrete, and metals.
3. Provide ongoing training or incentives for contractors and subcontractors to prioritize waste reduction and recycling.
4. Incorporate diversion requirements into project bids and contracts to ensure accountability throughout the supply chain.

5. Leverage green building certifications and local regulations to secure funding, tax benefits, or recognition for high diversion performance.
