# 6 · Booking Window Patterns Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Revenue & Pricing Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Examine how far in advance guests typically book, allowing you to refine pricing strategies, marketing campaigns, and inventory management.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical booking data indicating the date each reservation was made and the arrival date.
- Occupancy data by date.
- Pricing or rate information over the same period.
- Segment data (if available) to differentiate between business, leisure, group, etc.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather historical booking data. Compile reservation records with both the booking date and arrival date over a chosen time frame.
2. Calculate the booking window for each reservation. Subtract the booking date from the arrival date to determine the number of days between reservation and arrival.
3. Categorize booking windows. Group reservations by lead time ranges (e.g., 0-7 days, 8-14 days, 15-30 days, 31-60 days, 60+ days).
4. Analyze trends across different periods. Compare booking window patterns by month, season, or year to identify any fluctuations.
5. Correlate booking windows with ADR and occupancy. Determine if shorter lead times correlate with higher or lower rates, and if longer lead times produce higher occupancy.
6. Split results by segment (if applicable). For example, identify if business travelers typically book within a shorter window than leisure guests.


## 📤 Output format
- A table displaying booking window categories, number of reservations, share of total bookings, and associated ADR.
- Charts or graphs (e.g., bar chart) showing the distribution of booking windows over time.
- Written commentary linking booking windows to revenue metrics and occupancy.

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A higher proportion of short-lead bookings may indicate last-minute business or leisure travelers who are less price sensitive or are driven by urgent needs.
- Long-lead bookings suggest more time to optimize pricing and can help forecast occupancy more accurately.
- Changes in booking patterns may signal shifts in traveler behavior or market conditions.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Adjust rate strategies to capitalize on last-minute bookings (e.g., yield premium rates for short lead times).
2. Launch early-bird promotions or discounts to secure bookings far in advance.
3. Monitor booking window trends weekly or monthly to quickly adapt pricing and marketing.
4. Segment offers by lead time, tailoring promotions to suit both short- and long-lead travelers.
