# 7 · Scripted vs. Unscripted Content Mix Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Content Strategy & Portfolio]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Media & Entertainment Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine the optimal balance between scripted (e.g., dramas, comedies, feature films) and unscripted (e.g., reality TV, documentaries, talk shows) content in order to maximize audience appeal, budget efficiency, and overall ROI.


## 📊 Data required
1.​ Current content library classified into scripted vs. unscripted 2.​ Production costs and marketing spend for each type of content 3.​ Revenue and engagement metrics (ratings, viewership, streaming hours, ad revenue) 4.​ Audience demographic data and preferences 5.​ Competitive programming schedules for relevant markets


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Classify and catalog all current content. Separate the existing slate into scripted and unscripted categories, noting genre, target audience, and release platforms. 2.​ Gather performance metrics. Collect data on production costs, marketing spend, and revenues (ad revenue, subscription growth, licensing) tied to each show or title. 3.​ Calculate profitability by category. Use a formula such as: Profit Margin (%) = (Total Revenue - Total Costs) / Total Revenue x 100 to compare profitability of scripted vs. unscripted. 4.​ Assess viewership trends and demographics. Determine which segments of the audience are most engaged by scripted vs. unscripted content, using ratings, streaming metrics, and social media data. 5.​ Benchmark against competitors. Analyze competitor strategies and performance in the same genres or formats to understand market saturation and audience demand. 6.​ Perform scenario modeling. Simulate various content mix ratios (e.g., 60% scripted / 40% unscripted) and forecast the potential impact on revenues, budgets, and audience growth. 7.​ Propose the optimal content mix. Based on results, recommend a ratio of scripted to unscripted that delivers the best balance of profitability, audience reach, and brand positioning.

## 📤 Output format
- ​ A dashboard or spreadsheet showing profitability and audience metrics by scripted vs. unscripted categories
- ​ Scenario-based models illustrating how shifts in content mix might affect revenue and audience engagement
- ​ A brief written report summarizing key findings and recommendations for content strategy


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ High profitability in one category may signal the need to invest more in that area; conversely, lower profitability might indicate over-saturation or misalignment with the audience
- ​ Engagement metrics by demographic groups reveal which types of content resonate with specific audience segments
- ​ Comparing scripted vs. unscripted performance can guide resource allocation and scheduling decisions


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Refine content development pipelines, ensuring both scripted and unscripted projects have clearly defined audience targets 2.​ Adjust marketing strategies to effectively promote each type of content to its most responsive demographic group 3.​ Explore co-production or talent partnerships in unscripted formats if production costs are consistently lower and ROI is favorable 4.​ Regularly review competitor schedules and consumer trends to identify emerging unscripted sub-genres or niche scripted storytelling opportunities 5.​ Continuously track performance data, adapting the content mix to shifting audience preferences and market conditions.
