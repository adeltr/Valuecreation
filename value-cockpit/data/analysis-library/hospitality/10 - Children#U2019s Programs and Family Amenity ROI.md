# 10 · Children’s Programs and Family Amenity ROI

> **Part:** [[A - Revenue & Pricing Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine the financial return and guest value of children’s programs and family amenities, guiding you on how to best cater to family travelers.


## 📊 Data required
- Records of participation in children’s programs (e.g., number of kids enrolled, frequency of use).
- Revenue generated from family‐related services (e.g., program fees, extended stay packages).
- Costs associated with running each program (labor, materials, licensing, etc.).
- Guest satisfaction scores or feedback specific to family amenities.
- Room and ancillary revenue comparisons between family travelers and other segments.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify all children’s programs and family amenities to be evaluated. List services such as kids’ clubs, babysitting, play areas, family‐friendly dining options.
2. Collect participation and revenue data. Gather enrollment figures, total program fees, and any incremental spend (e.g., food, merchandise) attributed to families.
3. Calculate total costs. Include staffing, supplies, maintenance, and marketing expenses for each program.
4. Determine ROI for each program. Use a formula such as:
`ROI (%) = [(Revenue from Program – Total Costs) / Total Costs] x 100`
5. Analyze guest satisfaction scores or feedback. Correlate positive reviews and repeat visits with the availability and quality of family amenities.
6. Compare performance across different seasons or promotional periods. Note whether family amenities are more utilized during holidays or school breaks.
7. Benchmark against industry standards if available. Compare your children’s program metrics to similar properties or brand norms.

## 📤 Output format
- A table listing each family amenity or program, total costs, revenue, ROI percentage, and satisfaction metrics.
- Charts or graphs depicting the contribution of these programs to overall property revenue and guest satisfaction.
- Narrative explanation highlighting top‐performing programs, improvement areas, and seasonal trends.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High ROI indicates a successful program that brings in more revenue than its operational cost.
- Low or negative ROI may still be valuable if it significantly boosts overall guest satisfaction and loyalty.
- Seasonal variations in utilization can guide scheduling, staffing, and promotional planning.
- Positive feedback from families can lead to repeat bookings, referrals, and enhanced brand reputation.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Tailor programs to different age groups to appeal to a broader range of families.
2. Introduce family packages or bundles that include program fees, meals, and activities.
3. Train staff to deliver a high‐quality, engaging experience for children, improving guest satisfaction.
4. Consider partnering with local attractions or children’s entertainment providers to expand offerings.
5. Leverage marketing channels focusing on family‐centric travel segments to drive awareness and bookings.
