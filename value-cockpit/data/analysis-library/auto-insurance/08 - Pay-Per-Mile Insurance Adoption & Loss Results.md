# 8 · Pay-Per-Mile Insurance Adoption & Loss Results

> **Part:** [[A - Pricing & Product Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate the adoption rate, usage patterns, and profitability of pay-per-mile insurance policies to determine if mileage-based pricing accurately reflects risk and fosters sustainable growth.


## 📊 Data required
- Policy-level data on pay-per-mile adoption
- Earned premium, incurred losses, and total miles driven for pay-per-mile policies
- Policy retention rates and renewal data specific to pay-per-mile customers
- Market or competitor benchmarks on pay-per-mile adoption and loss trends


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify all policies under the pay-per-mile program. Gather detailed information on effective policy dates, mileage readings, earned premiums, and incurred losses.
2. Calculate the adoption rate by using Adoption Rate = (Number of Pay-Per-Mile Policies / Total Policies) x 100. Compare this rate over time to understand growth trends.
3. Compute the average miles driven per pay-per-mile policy to see how usage levels vary by customer segments. Segment this data by factors like vehicle type or driver demographics to find patterns.
4. Determine pay-per-mile claim frequency and severity by using Claim Frequency = (Number of Claims / Total Pay-Per-Mile Policies) x 100 and Average Claim Severity = (Total Losses / Number of Claims). Evaluate if mileage has a direct correlation to claim likelihood or cost.
5. Calculate a pay-per-mile loss ratio using Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + Loss Adjustment Expenses) / Earned Premium for pay-per-mile policies. Compare against traditional auto policies to gauge profitability differences.
6. Assess retention and renewal rates among pay-per-mile customers to see if the program attracts long-term, lower-risk drivers. Note whether heavy-mileage drivers remain in the program or switch to standard coverage.
7. Benchmark against competitor data if available to gauge your relative performance. Identify whether your adoption or loss results deviate significantly from the broader market.

8. Document findings and recommendations such as adjusting per-mile rates or introducing tiered mileage bands. Present your analysis to product, pricing, and underwriting teams for final approval and implementation planning.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables showing pay-per-mile adoption rates, claim frequency, severity, and loss ratios over time
- Comparative charts of pay-per-mile vs. standard policy performance metrics
- Written summary outlining key findings, recommended adjustments, and estimated financial impact


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A significantly lower loss ratio among pay-per-mile customers may signal that low-mileage drivers represent a safer segment, confirming the viability of mileage-based pricing.
- High loss ratios or unfavorable claim severity among pay-per-mile customers could indicate misalignment in the pricing structure, especially if heavy-mileage drivers are not accurately segmented.
- Retention and renewal patterns provide insight into whether pay-per-mile policies truly attract and retain low-risk customers or if they encourage churn among high-mileage drivers seeking cheaper alternatives.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Refine mileage-based rate tiers to better match usage segments and reduce adverse selection.
2. Introduce or enhance telematics solutions that more accurately capture driving patterns.
3. Offer incentives or discounts for consistently low-mileage or safe-driving customers.
4. Periodically review competitor offerings and market data to keep pricing and coverage options competitive and aligned with real-world risk.
