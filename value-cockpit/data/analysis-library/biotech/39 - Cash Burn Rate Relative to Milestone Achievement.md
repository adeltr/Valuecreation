# 39 · Cash Burn Rate Relative to Milestone Achievement

> **Part:** [[E - Finance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Biotechnology Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to evaluate a biotechnology company’s cash management by comparing how efficiently its cash reserves are being used to achieve key milestones (e.g., clinical trial progress, regulatory submissions, or product launches). This involves assessing the company's monthly cash burn rate and aligning it with significant milestones to ensure that sufficient financial resources are available to complete critical objectives before additional funding is required. The analysis helps determine the company’s operational efficiency and long-term sustainability.


## 📊 Data required
- Financial statements (cash flow, income statement, and balance sheet)
- Current cash reserves and liquidity position
- Monthly and quarterly cash burn rate (how fast the company spends cash)
- List and timeline of key milestones (e.g., clinical trial phases, regulatory approvals, product launches)
- Historical data on past fundraising rounds, including amounts and timing
- Competitive benchmarks on cash burn rates and milestones in similar biotech firms
- Budget allocation across different functions (R&D, clinical trials, marketing, etc.)
- Any external factors that could affect cash flow or milestone progress (e.g., regulatory delays, clinical trial risks)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate the current cash burn rate:
The cash burn rate measures how quickly the company is using its cash reserves. To calculate:
`Cash Burn Rate = (Cash Balance at Beginning of Period - Cash Balance at End of Period) / Number of Months in the Period`
Alternatively, for companies without significant revenue:
`Cash Burn Rate = Total Monthly Operating Expenses`
This calculation provides insight into how fast the company is spending its cash each month.
2. Identify key milestones:
- List the company’s critical milestones, such as completion of clinical trials (Phase 1, 2, 3), regulatory submissions (IND, NDA, BLA), product launches, or securing strategic partnerships.
- Understand the timeline for each milestone and the associated costs required to reach them.
154
3. Project cash runway:
The cash runway tells you how long the company can operate before running out of cash, based on its burn rate:
`Cash Runway = Current Cash Reserves / Monthly Cash Burn Rate`
For example, if the company has $10 million in cash and a monthly burn rate of $1 million, the runway is 10 months.
4. Compare cash runway with milestone timelines:
- Determine whether the company has enough cash to achieve key milestones by comparing the cash runway with the projected timelines for milestone achievement. This ensures that critical milestones can be reached before cash reserves run out.
- If a company’s runway is shorter than the time required to achieve significant milestones, it may need to raise additional funding before those milestones are completed.
5. Analyze historical milestone achievement:
Review how well the company has achieved past milestones relative to its spending. Has the company historically met its milestone deadlines and budgets, or have there been delays and cost overruns? Understanding the efficiency of cash utilization provides insight into future projections.
6. Benchmark against industry peers:
- Compare the company’s cash burn rate and milestone timelines with those of competitors in the same industry. Determine if the company’s spending and milestone progress are in line with industry standards.
- Use competitive data to evaluate whether the company is spending appropriately or if it needs to optimize its operations.
7. Evaluate future funding needs:
- Assess whether the company will need to raise additional funds to reach upcoming milestones. If so, determine the timing and magnitude of future fundraising efforts.
- Ensure the company has a clear path for securing capital, either through equity, debt, or partnerships, and evaluate whether future fundraising aligns with upcoming value-creation events (e.g., positive clinical trial results).
8. Review operational efficiency:
- Examine how the company allocates cash between core activities (such as R&D or clinical trials) versus non-core activities (such as administrative costs). Determine whether there are inefficiencies in spending that can be optimized.
155
- A high proportion of spending on non-critical areas may indicate opportunities for cost-saving measures.
9. Incorporate risk factors and contingencies:
- Identify external risk factors that could affect cash burn or delay milestone achievement, such as clinical trial setbacks, regulatory issues, or market changes.
- Perform a sensitivity analysis to evaluate how different scenarios (e.g., faster cash burn or delayed milestones) could impact the company’s financial health.
10. Provide recommendations:
- Based on the analysis, provide actionable recommendations on how to manage the company’s cash more effectively. This might include adjusting spending, optimizing R&D allocation, or raising capital sooner than planned.
- If additional fundraising is needed, suggest the best timing based on upcoming milestones or value-driving events.


## 📤 Output format
- **Cash burn rate and milestone alignment report:** A detailed report comparing the company’s current cash burn rate with the timeline for achieving key milestones. It includes an analysis of the cash runway and recommendations for optimizing financial resources.
- **Funding needs and strategy report:** A report assessing whether and when the company will need additional capital, including strategies for structuring future fundraising efforts.
- **Operational efficiency assessment:** A report highlighting how well the company uses its cash to achieve milestones, identifying potential areas for improving spending efficiency.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Strong cash management:** If the company’s cash burn rate is aligned with milestone timelines and its cash reserves are sufficient to reach critical milestones, the company is managing its finances well. Efficient cash usage relative to achieving value-driving milestones indicates solid operational efficiency.
- **Moderate cash management:** If the company is close to achieving milestones but may run out of cash shortly before completion, the company should consider raising funds or cutting costs to extend its runway.
- **Weak cash management:** If the company’s cash burn rate is high and milestones are significantly delayed, it faces financial risk. In this case, the company should take immediate action to optimize spending or seek additional funding.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
156
1. **Optimize spending:** Focus on prioritizing cash flow to critical activities, such as advancing R&D or clinical trials, while reducing or deferring non-essential expenses.
2. **Extend cash runway:** Explore ways to extend the cash runway, such as by renegotiating contracts or delaying non-essential spending.
3. **Align milestones with cash flow:** Reassess the timing of cash outflows and milestone deadlines to ensure alignment, potentially delaying less critical milestones if cash flow is tight.
4. **Raise capital strategically:** Plan fundraising efforts around key milestones (such as clinical trial readouts) to secure funding when the company is in a strong position.
5. **Monitor burn rate regularly:** Regularly track cash burn and milestone progress to ensure the company stays on course.
