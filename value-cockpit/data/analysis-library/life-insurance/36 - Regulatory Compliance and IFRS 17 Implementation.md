# 36 · Regulatory Compliance and IFRS 17 Implementation Readiness Review

> **Part:** [[H - Regulatory Compliance and Financial Reporting]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To assess the company's readiness for IFRS 17 implementation and overall regulatory compliance. This analysis identifies gaps in systems, processes, and reporting frameworks while ensuring alignment with accounting standards and regulatory requirements.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Financial data:** ○ Current reserves, profit margins, cash flows, and liabilities under existing accounting standards ○ Impact assessments of IFRS 17 transition on profit recognition and balance sheet
2. **Actuarial data:** ○ Discount rates, risk adjustment calculations, and contractual service margins (CSM) ○ Expected and actual cash flow data for life insurance contracts
3. **Systems and process data:** ○ Accounting systems, data management frameworks, and reporting tools ○ Status of IFRS 17 implementation projects
4. **Compliance documentation:** ○ Regulatory guidelines on IFRS 17 ○ Current internal policies for financial reporting
5. **Benchmark data:** ○ Industry readiness benchmarks for IFRS 17 adoption


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Understand Current Compliance Status a. Review existing financial reporting processes and compare them to IFRS 17 requirements. b. Identify key changes in accounting treatment for life insurance contracts, including the measurement of liabilities, CSM, and revenue recognition.
2. Evaluate IFRS 17 Transition Impact a. Calculate the impact of IFRS 17 on financial statements:
104 ■ Recalculate insurance liabilities using discount rates and risk adjustments under IFRS 17. ■ Assess the changes in profit emergence and income statement volatility. b. Measure the effect on equity, reserves, and profitability.
3. Assess Systems and Data Readiness a. Review the readiness of accounting systems to handle IFRS 17 requirements, including: ■ Cash flow tracking for contracts ■ Calculation of risk adjustments and CSM ■ Reporting frameworks for general measurement and variable fee approaches b. Identify any data gaps or systems requiring upgrades.
4. Conduct Compliance Gap Analysis a. Compare current reporting practices with IFRS 17 requirements: ■ Measurement models: general measurement, premium allocation approach, or variable fee approach ■ Disclosure requirements for financial statements ■ Risk adjustment and CSM calculations
5. Evaluate Internal Processes and Governance a. Assess whether actuarial, finance, and IT teams are integrated to support IFRS 17 compliance. b. Review governance frameworks for data accuracy, validation, and auditability.
6. Stress Test Reporting Processes a. Conduct mock reporting runs under IFRS 17 to identify gaps in timelines, data flow, and outputs. b. Analyze the accuracy of financial statements under stress scenarios.
7. Benchmark IFRS 17 Readiness a. Compare the company’s implementation status, processes, and financial impacts to industry peers.
8. Visualize Results a. Summarize the impact on financial metrics, compliance gaps, and systems readiness in tables. b. Use charts to highlight the transition effect on balance sheets, income statements, and reporting timelines.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing IFRS 17 impacts on reserves, equity, and profit recognition
- Compliance gap analysis results detailing areas needing improvement 105
- Charts illustrating financial metric changes pre- and post-IFRS 17 implementation
- Key findings on system readiness, governance gaps, and process alignment


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Significant differences in liability measurements and profit emergence highlight the financial impact of IFRS 17 adoption.
2. Gaps in systems readiness and data availability indicate areas requiring immediate attention to meet reporting deadlines.
3. Poor alignment between actuarial and finance teams signals governance and process issues.
4. Benchmark comparisons reveal whether the company is lagging behind peers in readiness and implementation progress.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Upgrade accounting and data management systems to handle IFRS 17 calculations and reporting requirements.
2. Conduct regular mock runs of IFRS 17-compliant financial statements to identify and resolve gaps.
3. Strengthen governance frameworks to integrate actuarial, finance, and IT teams for smooth implementation.
4. Train internal teams on IFRS 17 requirements to build expertise and improve reporting accuracy.
5. Engage external consultants to validate readiness and provide guidance on bridging compliance gaps.
6. Monitor regulatory updates and industry best practices to ensure ongoing alignment with IFRS 17 standards.
7. Establish a robust internal audit process to validate data accuracy, calculations, and compliance readiness.
