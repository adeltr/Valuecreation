# 20 · Compassionate Use Program Strategy

> **Part:** [[C - Operations & Supply Chain]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Pharmaceutical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of developing a Compassionate Use Program Strategy is to establish a clear framework for providing investigational drugs to patients who have no other treatment options and do not qualify for clinical trials. Compassionate Use Programs (CUPs), also known as "named patient programs" or "early access programs," allow patients with life-threatening or serious illnesses to access therapies that are still in development. The strategy should assess the operational, regulatory, and ethical considerations of offering compassionate use, balancing the needs of patients with the company’s broader drug development goals.


## 📊 Data required
- List of investigational drugs eligible for compassionate use and their current development stages.
- Regulatory guidelines governing compassionate use in key markets (e.g., FDA’s Compassionate Use Program, EMA’s Compassionate Use Program).
- Criteria for patient eligibility (e.g., life-threatening conditions, no alternative treatments, non-eligibility for clinical trials).
- Infrastructure for drug manufacturing, distribution, and administration.
- Legal, financial, and ethical considerations for providing drugs outside of clinical trials.
- Historical data on the number of compassionate use requests, approvals, and patient outcomes.
- Clinical data from compassionate use patients (safety, efficacy, adverse events).
- Impact on clinical development timelines and regulatory submissions.
- Stakeholder perspectives, including patient advocacy groups, healthcare providers, and regulators.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define Eligibility Criteria for Compassionate Use:** ○ Establish clear eligibility criteria for patients seeking access to investigational drugs through compassionate use. These criteria should align with regulatory guidelines and ensure that compassionate use is offered only to patients with serious or life-threatening conditions who have exhausted all other treatment options and cannot participate in clinical trials. ○ Common eligibility requirements may include: ■ Serious or life-threatening illness. ■ No comparable or satisfactory alternative treatments available. ■ Ineligibility for ongoing clinical trials. ■ Reasonable expectation that the investigational drug may provide a therapeutic benefit.
2. Evaluate Regulatory Guidelines and Compliance:
77 ○   Review the regulatory framework for compassionate use in key markets such as the U.S. (FDA), Europe (EMA), and other regions where the company operates. Ensure that the program complies with all relevant regulations, including requirements for patient consent, reporting of adverse events, and submission of compassionate use data to regulatory agencies. ○ Consider the potential impact of the Right to Try legislation in the U.S., which allows patients to bypass some FDA oversight in seeking investigational drugs.
3.   **Assess Operational and Logistical Feasibility:** ○ Determine whether the company has the operational capacity to manufacture, store, and distribute the investigational drug outside of its clinical trial infrastructure. Evaluate whether sufficient quantities of the drug can be produced without impacting ongoing clinical trials or delaying commercial manufacturing. ○ Consider the logistical challenges of providing the drug to patients in different geographic regions, including cold chain logistics (for temperature-sensitive drugs), legal requirements, and distribution networks. ○ Assess whether the company can support compassionate use requests across multiple regions or if the program should be limited to specific countries.
4.   **Establish a Process for Managing Compassionate Use Requests:** ○ Develop a clear process for receiving, evaluating, and responding to compassionate use requests from healthcare providers. This process should include: ■ A system for reviewing individual patient cases. ■ Documentation requirements for healthcare providers requesting compassionate use (e.g., medical history, justification for the request). ■ A timeline for responding to requests, with clear guidelines for approval or rejection. ○ Ensure that the compassionate use request process is transparent, efficient, and aligned with regulatory timelines to avoid delays that could impact patient outcomes.
5.   **Analyze the Financial and Legal Implications:** ○ Evaluate the financial costs of offering drugs through compassionate use, including manufacturing, distribution, monitoring, and administrative costs. Use the following formula to calculate the Cost per Compassionate Use Patient:

In many cases, compassionate use drugs are provided free of charge, but this may not be feasible for all companies or drugs. ○ Review the legal implications of offering investigational drugs outside of clinical trials, including potential liability in the event of adverse events or failure to achieve the desired outcomes. Ensure that the company’s legal team is involved in crafting the program’s framework to mitigate legal risks.`
6.   Assess the Impact on Clinical Development and Regulatory Approval:
78 ○  Analyze how providing the drug through compassionate use may affect the ongoing clinical development of the drug. This includes: ■ The potential impact on drug supply for clinical trials. ■ Whether data from compassionate use can be used to support regulatory submissions or post-marketing commitments. ○ Consider the regulatory risk that adverse events in compassionate use patients could delay regulatory approvals or require additional studies.
7. **Monitor Patient Outcomes and Adverse Events:** ○ Develop a system for monitoring patient outcomes in the compassionate use program, including tracking adverse events, drug efficacy, and overall survival rates. This data should be collected in compliance with regulatory guidelines and may be used to support the drug’s broader development. ○ Ensure that healthcare providers administering the drug report all relevant safety data to the company, as this will be critical for ongoing safety monitoring and compliance with regulatory obligations.
8. **Engage with Stakeholders and Build Relationships:** ○ Collaborate with healthcare providers, patient advocacy groups, and regulatory bodies to ensure that the compassionate use program is aligned with patient needs and broader healthcare priorities. ○ Engage with patient advocacy organizations to communicate the company’s compassionate use policies and ensure transparency in how patients can access investigational therapies. ○ Build strong relationships with key opinion leaders (KOLs) in the medical community to ensure that the compassionate use program is clinically justified and meets ethical standards.
9. **Develop a Communication and Education Strategy:** ○ Create a clear communication plan to inform healthcare providers, patients, and caregivers about the compassionate use program, its eligibility criteria, and the application process. This should include educational materials that explain the risks and benefits of accessing investigational drugs outside of clinical trials. ○ Ensure that communication with patients is compassionate and empathetic, as they are often facing critical health challenges.
10. Evaluate Program Success and Impact:
- Regularly review the program’s performance, including patient outcomes, program costs, and the overall impact on the company’s reputation and clinical development. Consider conducting periodic evaluations to assess whether the program is meeting its objectives and addressing unmet medical needs.
- Adjust the compassionate use program as needed based on feedback from healthcare providers, regulatory bodies, and patients.


## 📤 Output format
The output of the Compassionate Use Program Strategy analysis typically includes a comprehensive summary of the program’s structure, operational feasibility, and potential 79 challenges. For example:
- **Eligibility and Operational Feasibility:** The company’s compassionate use program will be limited to its lead oncology drug, which is currently in Phase III trials. Eligibility criteria include patients with advanced-stage cancer who are not eligible for clinical trials and have no other treatment options. The drug will be provided free of charge to patients in the U.S. and EU, where the company has established distribution networks. A compassionate use request process will be developed with a target response time of two weeks from submission.
- **Financial and Legal Considerations:** The estimated cost of providing the drug through compassionate use is $40,000 per patient, with an anticipated enrollment of 100 patients in the first year. Legal counsel has reviewed the program’s framework, and liability protections have been put in place to mitigate the risk of adverse events. The program will not impact ongoing clinical trials, as manufacturing capacity is sufficient to meet both trial and compassionate use demands.
- **Patient Outcomes and Data Collection:** The company will implement a safety monitoring system to track patient outcomes and report adverse events to the FDA and EMA. Data collected from compassionate use patients will not be used to support regulatory submissions, but it will contribute to post-market safety surveillance once the drug is approved.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Program Impact:** If the compassionate use program effectively meets the needs of patients with serious or life-threatening conditions while maintaining compliance with regulatory requirements and minimizing legal risks, it is likely to have a positive impact on the company’s reputation and clinical development timeline.
- **Moderate Program Impact:** If the program meets patient needs but faces challenges related to costs, logistics, or adverse events, adjustments may be necessary to improve efficiency and reduce risks. Continued stakeholder engagement and operational improvements can help enhance the program’s impact.
- **Low Program Impact:** If the compassionate use program leads to significant operational disruptions, high costs, or adverse regulatory outcomes, the company should consider revising its approach or limiting the scope of the program to reduce risks.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Streamline the Compassionate Use Request Process:** Ensure that the process for receiving and evaluating compassionate use requests is efficient and transparent. Automating parts of the process or establishing a dedicated team to manage requests can reduce delays and improve patient access.
2. **Optimize Drug Supply and Distribution:** Work with manufacturing and logistics teams to ensure that sufficient quantities of the drug are available for compassionate use without impacting clinical trial timelines or commercial manufacturing plans. Consider 80 leveraging third-party logistics providers for global distribution.
3.   **Enhance Data Collection and Monitoring:** Strengthen the systems used to collect safety and efficacy data from compassionate use patients. This data can be valuable for post-marketing surveillance and understanding how the drug performs in real-world settings.
4.   **Engage with Regulators and Healthcare Providers:** Collaborate closely with regulatory agencies and healthcare providers to ensure that the compassionate use program is aligned with medical and ethical standards. Regular communication with regulators can help address any concerns and improve program compliance.
5.   **Communicate Clearly with Patients and Advocacy Groups:** Provide clear, compassionate communication to patients, caregivers, and advocacy groups about the compassionate use program, its eligibility criteria, and its risks and benefits. Transparency is key to building trust and ensuring that patients fully understand their options.
6.   **Monitor Program Costs and Adjust as Needed:** Regularly assess the financial impact of the compassionate use program and make adjustments to ensure that it remains sustainable. This may involve limiting the scope of the program or finding ways to reduce operational costs.
