# 5 · Glass Coverage & Zero-Deductible Claim Impact

> **Part:** [[A - Pricing & Product Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate the frequency, severity, and financial impact of glass coverage claims—especially those with zero deductible—and determine if existing rates and coverage terms are appropriately aligned with actual loss experience.


## 📊 Data required
- Policy-level data indicating glass coverage election and deductible structure
- Historical glass claims (frequency, severity, total incurred losses)
- Repair vs. replacement breakdown (e.g., windshield chip repair vs. full windshield replacement)
- Relevant loss adjustment expenses
- State regulations or mandates (in some jurisdictions, zero-deductible glass coverage may be required)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather policy data indicating glass coverage details such as deductibles, limits, and election rates. Include the number of claims, total incurred losses, and repair/replacement costs from the past 3–5 years.
2. Calculate glass claim frequency by dividing the number of glass claims by the total number of insured vehicles and converting to a percentage. Evaluate trends over time to see if frequency is rising or falling.
3. Assess glass claim severity by determining the average cost per claim for both repair and replacement. Compare these figures to industry benchmarks or competitor data if available.
4. Identify the proportion of zero-deductible claims and calculate Zero-Deductible Loss Ratio = (Incurred Glass Losses + LAE for zero-deductible claims) / Earned Premium for zero-deductible coverage. Check if this ratio is within acceptable profitability targets.
5. Segment the data by vehicle type, geographic region, and driver demographics to find groups with higher claim frequency or severity. Note any potential correlations with extreme weather, road conditions, or driving habits.
6. Examine repair vs. replacement ratios to see if cost-saving opportunities exist (e.g., encouraging chip repairs over full replacements). Evaluate if existing claim protocols or vendor partnerships are effectively minimizing claim severity.
7. Analyze the impact on overall premiums and rate adequacy by considering whether losses from zero-deductible policies require higher premiums or policy 14 adjustments. Compare current pricing to actual cost trends to pinpoint areas of under- or over-pricing.
8. Document conclusions and recommendations such as revising coverage options, adjusting deductibles, or establishing partnerships with preferred glass repair services. Present these findings to decision-makers for implementation.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts showing claim frequency and severity trends over time, broken out by zero-deductible vs. standard deductible
- Segmented analysis by geography, vehicle type, and demographic profile
- Written summary of key findings, recommendations on coverage/pricing adjustments, and projected financial impact


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High glass claim frequency signals increased likelihood of environmental or road condition factors.
- A high ratio of full replacements over repairs indicates cost escalations that might be controllable through vendor relationships or customer education.
- Zero-deductible coverage with escalating claim costs may require adjustments to premium or deductible structures to remain profitable.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Encourage chip repairs over full replacements through claim process design, educational materials, or incentives.
2. Adjust pricing or deductibles for glass coverage to align with actual loss costs and market conditions.
3. Partner with preferred glass repair vendors to obtain favorable rates and streamline the claim process.
4. Monitor state regulations closely to ensure compliance while optimizing product offerings and profitability.
