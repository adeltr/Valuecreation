# KPI — Media & Entertainment

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Media & Entertainment]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Media & Entertainment Company - Analyse]]

47 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Ad-Blocker Impact | This KPI measures the percentage of users employing ad-blockers, which can significantly impact revenue for platforms relying on ad revenues. |
| Audience Demographics | Understanding who is consuming content can help companies target their offerings more effectively. Metrics might include age, gender, geographical location, and more. |
| Average Ticket Price | For movie theaters and live performance venues, this KPI can provide insight into pricing strategy and audience willingness to pay. |
| Binge Rate | For streaming platforms, tracking the rate at which viewers consume multiple episodes of a series in one session can provide insights into content engagement and quality. |
| Box Office Revenue | For movie studios, box office revenue is a crucial measure of a film's success. It's important to consider both domestic and international box office revenue. |
| Click-Through Rate (CTR) | Particularly relevant to digital media platforms, CTR measures how often people who view an advertisement end up clicking on it. It indicates the effectiveness of the ads in capturing viewer interest. |
| Content Library Size and Growth | This assesses the total number of shows, movies, songs, or other content available on the platform, and how quickly it's growing. A large and growing library could attract and retain more users. |
| Content Lifespan | This measures the average period a piece of content continues to generate significant viewership or engagement. Longer content lifespan can indicate timeless appeal and potential for long-term revenue. |
| Content Production Time | The time taken from concept to release of new content can indicate efficiency of operations. |
| Content Release Frequency | This measures the number of new releases (movies, shows, episodes, games, etc.) within a specified timeframe. Frequent releases can help maintain audience interest and engagement. |
| Conversion Rate of Recommendations | For platforms using recommendation algorithms (Netflix, Amazon Prime Video, Spotify), this KPI tracks how often users consume recommended content. It can gauge the effectiveness of the recommendation system. |
| Cost per Thousand Impressions (CPM) | Common in digital advertising, CPM measures the cost an advertiser pays for one thousand views or impressions of its advertisement. Lower CPM can indicate more cost-effective advertising. |
| Digital Conversion Rate | In the context of online entertainment platforms, this KPI measures the rate at which users become paying customers after using a free trial or a free version. |
| Distribution Breadth | Measures the reach of a company's content across different platforms and geographies. Broader distribution can lead to higher viewership and revenues. |
| Game Daily Active Users (DAUs) and Monthly Active Users (MAUs) | For gaming companies, tracking the number of daily and monthly active users can help gauge the game's popularity and stickiness. |
| In-app Purchase Revenue | For gaming and app-based platforms, revenue generated from in-app purchases can be a significant income stream and can indicate the success of monetization strategies. |
| Licensing Revenue | Revenue earned from licensing content to other platforms or in other markets can be a significant source of income for content creators. |
| Live Event Attendance | For businesses focused on live performances (music festivals, theater), event attendance can indicate popularity and customer appeal. |
| Merchandise Sales | This applies to media entities that sell branded merchandise. High merchandise sales can reflect strong brand affinity and can be a significant revenue source. |
| Mobile vs. Desktop Engagement | Depending on the platform, comparing engagement on mobile versus desktop can help optimize content delivery and marketing strategies. |
| Pay-Per-View (PPV) Sales | For sports broadcasters and some special entertainment events, the number of PPV sales can give a clear indication of the popularity and demand for specific events or matches. |
| Peak Concurrent Users | For digital platforms and multiplayer online games, this metric measures the maximum number of users active on the platform or game simultaneously. It can indicate system performance, popularity, and engagement. |
| Percentage of Original Content | For platforms producing their own content, this measures the proportion of original content to licensed content. A high percentage may indicate a competitive advantage, as original content can be a differentiator and draw in viewers. |
| Player Retention (Day 1, Day 7, Day 30, etc.) | For the gaming industry, tracking how many players return to the game after specific periods (e.g., after one day, one week, one month) can provide insights into the game's ability to keep players engaged over time. |
| Pre-Orders | For movie tickets, video games, books, and other forms of media, the volume of pre-orders can serve as an early indicator of demand and success. |
| Premium Subscription Conversion | For platforms with freemium models, this KPI tracks the percentage of free users who upgrade to a premium, paid version. |
| Program Ratings | In broadcasting, programs' rating points are often used as a proxy for popularity and audience size. |
| Ratio of Active to Registered Users | This KPI helps assess how many users who sign up for a platform actually use it. A higher ratio indicates better user activation. |
| Readership/Subscribership Growth | For traditional and digital publishing platforms, growth in readership or subscribership can indicate successful content strategies and market demand. |
| Retention Viewing | In the broadcasting industry, this measures the proportion of the audience at the beginning of a program that is still viewing at the end. High retention viewing can indicate compelling content and loyal viewership. |
| Retransmission Fees | For traditional broadcasters, retransmission fees paid by cable and satellite companies can be a significant revenue source. |
| Royalty and Licensing Costs | These are costs incurred by a media company to use copyrighted content. In the music streaming industry, for example, these costs can be a significant portion of revenue. Low royalty and licensing costs can indicate strong negotiating power and impact profitability. |
| Screen Occupancy Rate | For the movie theater industry, this rate measures the proportion of total seats sold to the total seats available. A higher rate can indicate successful scheduling and film selection strategies. |
| Season Pass Sales | For industries like theme parks or sports entertainment, the number of season passes sold can provide insight into customer loyalty and stable revenue streams. |
| Sequel Success Rate | In the film and video game industry, the success rate of sequels can indicate the strength of a franchise. |
| Social Media Followers and Engagement | Number of followers, likes, shares, comments, etc., across social media platforms can indicate brand recognition and popularity. |
| Sponsorship Revenue | For media entities that host events or shows, revenue from sponsorships can be a crucial income stream. The growth or decline of sponsorship revenue can indicate the perceived value of association with the brand. |
| Subscriber Growth Rate | For companies offering subscription-based services (e.g., Netflix, Spotify), this KPI helps track the rate at which the subscriber base is increasing. A high growth rate could indicate successful marketing strategies and high product demand. |
| Ticket Sales (Gross and Net) | For live entertainment or movie theater companies, ticket sales can provide insight into audience demand and the success of specific shows, concerts, or movies. |
| Time Spent per Session | Measures how long, on average, a user spends during a single interaction with a media platform or service. This can indicate user engagement and content relevance. |
| Usage of Second-Screen Services | The use of additional devices (smartphones, tablets) during the viewing of television or online content can indicate audience engagement and offer additional advertising opportunities. |
| User Retention Rate | For digital platforms and subscription-based services, tracking the percentage of users who continue to use the service over a certain period can provide insights into user loyalty and satisfaction. |
| User-generated Content | For platforms that allow user-generated content (UGC), tracking the amount and popularity of UGC can provide insights into user engagement and community strength. |
| Video Completion Rate | For digital video platforms (YouTube, OTT platforms), the percentage of videos watched to completion can indicate the quality and relevance of the content to its audience. |
| Viewer or User Engagement Metrics | This could include factors like average session duration, average views per user, and likes or shares per user, depending on the platform. These metrics help gauge how actively and frequently users interact with the content, which can influence advertising revenue and user retention. |
| Viewer Participation Rate | For media that encourage viewer participation (e.g., voting in talent shows, comments on social media), this rate can indicate engagement levels. |
| Viewer Ratings and Reviews | These are especially important for TV shows, movies, and digital content. High ratings and positive reviews can drive viewership, popularity, and potentially, revenue. |
