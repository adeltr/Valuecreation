# 31 · Reinsurance Optimization & Ceded Risk Evaluation

> **Part:** [[D - Financial Performance & Reserving]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine whether current reinsurance programs effectively balance retained vs. ceded risk in a way that optimizes capital usage, protects against catastrophic losses, and maintains acceptable profit margins.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Historical loss data (frequency, severity, large-loss indicators)
- ​ Current and past reinsurance contracts, including coverage terms, limits, attachment points, and premium costs
- ​ Exposure data by line of business (earned premium, policy counts, geographic concentration)
- ​ Catastrophe modeling outputs (if applicable)
- ​ Industry benchmarks or competitor data on reinsurance structures and pricing


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Collect and organize historical loss data to identify patterns and potential large-loss exposures. Examine whether certain segments or geographic areas drive a disproportionate share of losses. 2.​ Review existing reinsurance contracts to determine coverage types, attachment points, and limits. Compare these terms to historical losses to see how often reinsurance layers would have been triggered. 3.​ Calculate net retained losses under current reinsurance structures. Estimate retained losses after ceded amounts to understand the ultimate financial impact on the primary insurer. 4.​ Determine the cost of reinsurance by analyzing ceded premium as a percentage of total premium. Examine whether the benefits of transferred risk justify the expenses, considering both frequent and catastrophic event scenarios. 5.​ Utilize catastrophe modeling (if applicable) to simulate potential large-scale events. Estimate how reinsurance programs would respond under different severity levels and if additional capacity or alternative structures are required. 6.​ Benchmark against industry peers if data is available. Compare reinsurance spend, attachment levels, and retention strategies to identify under- or over-reinsurance. 7.​ Conduct scenario testing with alternative reinsurance structures, such as different retentions or quota-share vs. excess-of-loss deals. Evaluate the impact on net losses, capital adequacy, and overall profitability.
67 8.​ Document recommended changes to reinsurance layers, limits, or structures. Present final findings to executive and finance teams, highlighting financial projections for each proposed alternative.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ Tables or charts showing current vs. proposed reinsurance layers, corresponding retentions, and costs
- ​ Graphs or tables illustrating net retained losses under various catastrophe or large-loss scenarios
- ​ Written summary detailing key insights, recommended modifications, and projected financial outcomes


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ Excessive reinsurance costs might erode underwriting profit, whereas inadequate reinsurance could expose the insurer to catastrophic risk.
- ​ Gaps between large-loss trends and current attachment points suggest the need for adjusted layers or additional treaty types.
- ​ Benchmark comparisons help identify whether your organization is overpaying or underinsuring relative to peers.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Regularly reassess loss trends and catastrophe models to ensure attachment points and limits remain appropriate. 2.​ Negotiate more favorable contract terms or explore alternative reinsurance solutions (e.g., collateralized reinsurance, ILS). 3.​ Align reinsurance strategy with capital management objectives, potentially retaining more risk if capital levels permit. 4.​ Use dynamic scenario testing to adapt reinsurance structures in response to emerging risks or market changes.
