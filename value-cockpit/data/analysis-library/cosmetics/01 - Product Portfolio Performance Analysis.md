# 1 · Product Portfolio Performance Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Product and Research & Development (R&D)]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Cosmetics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Product Portfolio Performance Analysis is to evaluate the effectiveness and market performance of a cosmetics company's product range. This analysis helps to determine which products are driving sales, which are underperforming, and where there are gaps or redundancies in the portfolio. It also assesses whether the current product mix aligns with market trends and consumer demand.


## 📊 Data required
- Sales data by product (revenue, units sold, profit margin).
- Product launch dates and lifecycle stages.
- Market share by product category (if available).
- Customer feedback and ratings.
- Marketing spend per product.
- Inventory turnover rates by product.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Collect product-level sales data.
Gather data on revenue, units sold, and profit margins for each product within the portfolio. Organize this data by category (e.g., skincare, makeup, hair care).
2. Categorize products by lifecycle stage.
Determine whether each product is in its launch, growth, maturity, or decline phase based on the time since introduction and sales trends over time.
3. Evaluate profitability.
For each product, calculate its profit contribution:
`Product Profit = (Product Revenue - Product Cost)`
Rank the products from most profitable to least profitable.
4. Assess market performance.
Compare each product's sales performance against industry benchmarks and competitors (if available). This helps identify strong or weak performers.
5. Identify gaps and overlaps.
6 Look at product categories and subcategories (e.g., foundation, moisturizer) to determine if there are market gaps (unmet needs) or product overlaps (redundant offerings).
6. Analyze customer feedback.
Review product reviews, ratings, and return rates to assess consumer sentiment and identify areas for improvement.
7. Calculate inventory turnover.
Use the formula:
`Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory`
This shows how frequently each product is sold and replaced, highlighting slow-moving inventory.
8. Examine marketing effectiveness.
Compare marketing spend per product to its sales performance. This will help to determine if the marketing investment is yielding sufficient returns.
9. Visualize performance.
Create a performance matrix, plotting product profitability on one axis and market performance on the other (e.g., Boston Consulting Group (BCG) matrix: stars, cash cows, question marks, dogs).


## 📤 Output format
- A product performance matrix (e.g., BCG matrix).
- A table summarizing sales, profit margins, and inventory turnover per product.
- A report summarizing key findings, including top-performing products, underperforming products, and portfolio gaps or overlaps.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High profit and high market performance:** These are "star" products. The company should continue investing in them.
- **High profit but low market performance:** These are "cash cows," generating profits but potentially at risk of market decline. Maintain but monitor closely.
- **Low profit but high market performance:** These products may require cost reduction strategies or pricing adjustments to become profitable.
- **Low profit and low market performance:** These are candidates for discontinuation or a significant revamp to become viable.
- **Inventory turnover:** Products with low inventory turnover may indicate overstocking or low demand, signaling a need for reevaluation.

## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Refine the product mix:** Focus on high-performing categories and eliminate or restructure underperforming products.
2. **Increase R&D investment in growth areas:** For products in the "question mark" category, invest in R&D to drive innovation and boost market share.
3. **Optimize pricing and marketing:** Reevaluate pricing strategies for low-profit but high-market-share products. Adjust marketing spend based on return on investment (ROI).
4. **Reduce costs for low-margin products:** Explore ways to reduce production or supply chain costs to increase profitability for lower-margin products.
5. **Introduce new products to fill gaps:** If there are missing categories that align with current beauty trends (e.g., waterless beauty or clean beauty), launch new products to meet consumer demand.
