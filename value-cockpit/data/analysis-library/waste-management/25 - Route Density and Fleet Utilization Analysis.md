# 25 · Route Density and Fleet Utilization Analysis

> **Part:** [[E - Fleet & Collection Optimization]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Waste Management Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the efficiency of waste collection routes, gauge the utilization of the vehicle fleet, and identify opportunities to reduce operational costs, improve service levels, and minimize environmental impacts.


## 📊 Data required
- Fleet information (number of vehicles, capacity, fuel type, maintenance records)
- Daily or weekly route data (mileage, stops, total waste collected)
- GPS tracking or telematics data (vehicle locations, idle times, driving speeds)
- Operational costs (fuel, labor, vehicle maintenance)
- Customer density and service area maps
- Historical service times and on-time collection rates
- Local traffic patterns and constraints (road closures, congestion hotspots)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Map current routes and service areas Gather existing route maps and use GPS or telematics data to visualize the geographic coverage. Identify overlapping or inefficiently covered regions.
2. Calculate route density and collection efficiency Compare the total weight or volume of waste collected to the miles traveled and time spent on each route.
Equation:
`Route Density (tons/mile) = (Total Waste Collected on Route (tons)) / (Total Miles Driven on Route)`
3. Evaluate fleet utilization Assess the ratio of actual vehicle operating hours or capacity usage against the total available. Note any consistent underutilization or overcapacity issues.
4. Analyze cost drivers Break down operational costs by route and vehicle, focusing on fuel consumption, labor 72 hours, and maintenance expenses. Identify high-cost routes or vehicles with excessive downtime.
5. Identify optimization opportunities Pinpoint routes with overlapping coverage or low route density. Investigate rerouting, consolidation, or adjusting service frequencies to enhance efficiency.
6. Conduct scenario modeling Use route optimization software or manual route analysis to test potential improvements (e.g., cluster-based assignments, dynamic routing). Compare projected outcomes in terms of mileage, labor, and fuel savings.
7. Develop implementation plan Choose the best routing strategies or schedule changes, factoring in any constraints (customer service agreements, traffic patterns). Outline training needs and communication plans for drivers or dispatchers.


## 📤 Output format
- A report summarizing current route density, fleet utilization rates, and key cost metrics
- Maps or GIS visuals highlighting route overlaps or inefficiencies
- Tables or charts showing scenario comparisons (e.g., baseline vs. optimized routes) in terms of distance, hours, and costs
- An action plan with recommended route changes, vehicle allocations, and associated cost/benefit estimates


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Higher route density indicates more efficient collection (less mileage per ton of waste collected), potentially reducing fuel and labor costs.
- Consistent underutilization may suggest excess fleet capacity or misaligned scheduling.
- Excessive idling or wasted mileage highlights training needs or the potential benefit of route optimization software.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Implement route optimization tools (or work with specialists) to redesign inefficient routes and consolidate low-density service areas.
2. Adjust fleet size and vehicle types to match actual service requirements, considering factors such as urban vs. rural routes.

4. Provide driver training on efficient driving techniques and proper vehicle operation to reduce fuel consumption and maintenance costs.
