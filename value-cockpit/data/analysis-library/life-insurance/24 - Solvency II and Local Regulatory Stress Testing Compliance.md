# 24 · Solvency II and Local Regulatory Stress Testing Compliance Review

> **Part:** [[D - Capital and Solvency Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To assess compliance with Solvency II and local regulatory stress testing requirements. This ensures capital adequacy, identifies financial vulnerabilities under stress scenarios, and supports regulatory alignment.


## 📊 Data required
- **Financial data:** total assets, liabilities, capital reserves, and solvency ratios
- **Risk exposure data:** credit risk, market risk, insurance risk, and operational risk
- **Capital structure details:** available capital levels and required capital under Solvency II frameworks
- **Economic scenarios:** interest rate shocks, market downturns, mortality/longevity shocks, and lapse risks
- Historical stress testing results and solvency performance
- Local regulatory benchmarks for capital and stress testing


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Review Solvency II compliance across its three pillars:** ○ Pillar 1: Quantitative capital requirements (SCR and MCR). ○ Pillar 2: Governance, risk management, and internal controls. ○ Pillar 3: Public disclosures and transparency.
2. **Conduct regulatory stress testing using required scenarios:** ○ Market risk: equity market declines, interest rate shocks, and bond value changes. ○ Insurance risk: mortality/longevity shocks, lapse risk increases, and morbidity deviations. ○ Credit risk: increased default rates for assets in the portfolio.
3. **Calculate the Solvency Capital Requirement (SCR):** ○ Aggregate capital needs for credit risk, market risk, insurance risk, and operational risk.
`○ Formula: SCR = Credit Risk Capital + Market Risk Capital + Insurance Risk Capital + Operational Risk Capital.`
4. **Measure Minimum Capital Requirement (MCR):** ○ Determine the minimum threshold of capital needed to meet regulatory compliance. ○ Ensure available capital is greater than the MCR.
5. Evaluate Solvency Ratios:
68
`○ Solvency Ratio = (Available Capital / SCR) x 100. ○ Compare against regulatory minimums and identify gaps.`
6. **Segment Risk Contributions:** ○ Analyze the share of each risk type contributing to the total SCR. ○ Identify the largest contributors to capital strain.
7. **Conduct stress testing to model adverse scenarios:** ○ Market downturns (e.g., 30% equity loss). ○ Higher-than-expected mortality or morbidity claims. ○ Increased lapse rates. ○ Measure the impact of these scenarios on solvency ratios and capital adequacy.
8. Benchmark results against local regulatory requirements and peer companies. ○ Identify gaps in compliance and opportunities to improve.
9. **Visualize results with clear outputs:** ○ Tables summarizing SCR, MCR, solvency ratios, and stress test results. ○ Charts displaying trends in solvency ratios before and after stress testing.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing solvency ratios, SCR, and MCR results under baseline and stress scenarios
- Charts showing trends in solvency ratios and capital adequacy over time
- Stress testing outcomes illustrating risk sensitivities and vulnerabilities
- Key findings highlighting compliance issues, capital risks, and areas for improvement


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. A solvency ratio above 100% under all scenarios indicates sufficient capital to meet regulatory requirements.
2. Solvency ratios below the SCR threshold signal potential capital shortfalls and risks of regulatory breaches.
3. Gaps between Solvency II compliance and local stress testing requirements highlight areas for improvement in modeling or capital allocation.
4. High contributions from specific risks (e.g., market risk or lapse rates) indicate vulnerabilities requiring mitigation.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Increase capital reserves to strengthen solvency ratios and comply with SCR and MCR requirements.
2. Diversify investment portfolios to minimize exposure to market and credit risks.

3. Enhance reinsurance arrangements to transfer underwriting risks and improve capital efficiency.
4. Improve risk management frameworks to monitor emerging risks and ensure alignment with regulatory standards.
5. Conduct frequent stress testing to proactively identify capital weaknesses and address them.
6. Streamline governance processes to meet Pillar 2 requirements and strengthen internal controls.
7. Regularly benchmark capital performance and stress testing outcomes against peers and industry best practices.
