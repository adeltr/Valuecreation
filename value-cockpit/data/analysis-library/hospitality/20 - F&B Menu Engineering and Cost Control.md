# 20 · F&B Menu Engineering and Cost Control

> **Part:** [[D - Operations & Hospitality Services]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine which menu items drive the most profit and how to strategically price or redesign the menu to increase revenue and minimize waste.


## 📊 Data required
- Itemized sales data (quantity sold, revenue) for each menu item over a specific period.
- Food cost data for each item (ingredient costs, portion sizes).
- Menu item pricing information.
- Kitchen production times, if relevant, to understand operational impact.
- Guest feedback or survey data regarding menu preferences (if available).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Compile sales data for each menu item. Gather the number of units sold and total revenue generated for each item within your chosen timeframe.
2. Calculate food cost per menu item. Sum the cost of ingredients per standard recipe portion to find each item’s direct cost.
3. Determine contribution margin (CM) of each item. Contribution Margin = (Menu Price - Food Cost)
4. Calculate popularity. Express how often an item is sold relative to total items sold.
5. Categorize items using a Menu Engineering matrix. Classify items as Stars (high CM, high popularity), Plow Horses (low CM, high popularity), Puzzles (high CM, low popularity), or Dogs (low CM, low popularity).
6. Identify opportunities to adjust pricing or portion sizes. Based on each item’s profitability and popularity, decide whether to raise the price, modify the recipe, or remove the item.
7. Review kitchen impact. Consider preparation complexity and time when evaluating items to keep or remove.
8. Track changes over time. If you adjust prices or recipes, monitor subsequent sales data and food cost for ongoing optimization.


## 📤 Output format
- A table or spreadsheet listing each menu item, total sales volume, revenue, food cost, contribution margin, and popularity percentage.

- A Menu Engineering matrix or chart plotting items based on popularity (x‐axis) vs. contribution margin (y‐axis).
- Written summary outlining which items are Stars, Plow Horses, Puzzles, and Dogs, with recommendations for each category.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Stars:** High profit, high demand items. Keep them prominent on the menu; consider minor price adjustments if demand is strong.
- **Plow Horses:** Popular but low profit. Look to improve margin via slightly higher pricing or refined recipes without reducing perceived value.
- **Puzzles:** High margin but low popularity. Consider re‐positioning or promoting these items, or adjusting price or recipe to increase appeal.
- **Dogs:** Low margin, low popularity. Often candidates for removal unless they serve a strategic purpose.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Redesign the menu layout to highlight Star items and upsell them.
2. Adjust recipe costs by negotiating supplier contracts or substituting ingredients to improve margins on Plow Horses.
3. Promote Puzzle items with specials, staff recommendations, or new plating concepts.
4. Remove or replace Dog items unless they are essential to the brand identity or guest experience.
5. Continuously monitor food costs and sales data to refine the menu engineering process over time.
