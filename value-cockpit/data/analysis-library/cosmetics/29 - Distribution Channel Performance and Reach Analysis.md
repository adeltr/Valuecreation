# 29 · Distribution Channel Performance and Reach Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Sales and Distribution]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Cosmetics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Distribution Channel Performance and Reach Analysis is to evaluate how effectively the brand’s products are distributed across various channels, such as brick-and-mortar retail stores, e-commerce platforms, and direct-to-consumer (DTC) initiatives. This analysis assesses the sales performance, geographic reach, and consumer engagement in each channel to identify strengths, weaknesses, and opportunities for optimization. The insights help the company ensure that its distribution strategy maximizes market penetration, sales, and customer satisfaction.


## 📊 Data required
- Sales data segmented by distribution channel (e.g., retail, e-commerce, DTC).
- Geographic reach and coverage data for each channel (e.g., regions or countries where products are available).
- Customer acquisition and retention data by channel.
- Channel-specific marketing and promotional spend (e.g., retailer promotions, online ads).
- Inventory turnover and stock availability data for each channel.
- Channel profitability and cost-to-serve data (e.g., distribution costs, margin comparisons).
- Consumer behavior data by channel (e.g., purchasing patterns, preferences, average order value).
- Competitor analysis on channel strategies (e.g., exclusive retail partnerships, omnichannel approaches).
- Consumer feedback related to purchasing experience across different channels (e.g., delivery speed, availability, convenience).
- Channel-specific return rates and customer service performance data.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Collect and segment sales data by channel:** a. Break down sales performance by distribution channel (e.g., brick-and-mortar, e-commerce, DTC) over a defined period. b. Analyze which channels drive the most revenue and whether certain channels outperform others during specific promotions or product launches.
2. **Analyze geographic reach and coverage:** a. Map the geographic distribution of products for each channel, identifying which regions or countries have high or low penetration. b. Evaluate whether key markets are being underserved by certain channels or whether geographic expansion opportunities exist.
105
3. **Evaluate customer acquisition and retention by channel:** a. Analyze customer acquisition rates and retention metrics for each distribution channel. b. Identify which channels bring in the most new customers and which have the highest retention or repeat purchase rates.
4. **Review channel-specific marketing spend and performance:** a. Track marketing and promotional spending by channel (e.g., digital ads for e-commerce, in-store promotions for retailers) and compare it to the revenue generated. b. Evaluate the ROI of marketing investments by channel to determine whether certain channels deliver better returns.
5. **Assess inventory turnover and stock availability:** a. Review inventory turnover rates for each channel, assessing how quickly products move through the supply chain and whether there are stockout or overstock issues. b. Analyze how well each channel manages stock availability, especially for high-demand products or during peak sales periods.
6. **Analyze channel profitability and cost-to-serve:** a. Calculate the profitability of each channel by comparing revenue to distribution costs, including shipping, handling, and retail margins. b. Evaluate whether some channels are more expensive to serve than others and whether those costs are justified by higher sales or customer loyalty.
7. **Review consumer behavior by channel:** a. Analyze purchasing patterns in each channel, including the average order value (AOV), frequency of purchases, and product preferences. b. Identify any differences in consumer behavior between online and in-store shopping (e.g., impulse buys in-store vs. planned purchases online).
8. **Conduct a competitor analysis of distribution strategies:** a. Review how key competitors structure their distribution strategies, such as exclusive partnerships with retailers, focus on e-commerce, or omnichannel approaches. b. Benchmark the brand’s performance against competitors in each channel to identify areas for improvement.
9. **Collect consumer feedback on purchasing experience:** a. Gather feedback from consumers on their purchasing experience across different channels, focusing on factors like delivery speed, product availability, and ease of shopping. b. Identify whether certain channels deliver a better overall customer experience and contribute to higher satisfaction and loyalty.
10. **Evaluate return rates and customer service performance:** a. Analyze return rates by channel to assess whether certain distribution methods lead to higher return volumes (e.g., online returns vs. in-store returns). b. Evaluate customer service performance across channels, focusing on response times, issue resolution, and overall satisfaction.


## 📤 Output format
- A report summarizing sales performance by channel, comparing revenue and growth across different distribution methods.

- A geographic reach and coverage map showing regions where products are available and identifying gaps in distribution.
- A customer acquisition and retention analysis by channel, highlighting the channels that drive the most engagement and loyalty.
- A marketing ROI report, comparing the effectiveness of marketing spend across different channels.
- An inventory turnover and stock availability analysis, including insights into stockout or overstock issues in each channel.
- A profitability and cost-to-serve comparison by channel, identifying which distribution methods are the most profitable.
- A summary of consumer purchasing behavior by channel, including differences in preferences, AOV, and shopping frequency.
- A competitor benchmarking report comparing distribution strategies and performance.
- A consumer feedback analysis showing how purchasing experiences vary across different channels.
- Recommendations for optimizing distribution strategies based on the findings, including channel-specific improvements.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High sales performance in certain channels:** If specific channels (e.g., e-commerce) outperform others in terms of sales, the company may want to prioritize those channels or increase investment in marketing and logistics to further boost performance.
- **Geographic gaps in distribution:** If certain regions are underserved, the company can explore expanding its reach through new retail partnerships, e-commerce platforms, or improved distribution networks to capture more market share in those areas.
- **Channel profitability and cost challenges:** If some channels are less profitable or more costly to serve, the company should evaluate whether the channel is worth continuing or if cost efficiencies can be implemented to improve margins.
- **Consumer preferences for certain channels:** If consumers prefer shopping through specific channels (e.g., DTC for personalized experiences or brick-and-mortar for immediacy), the company should align its product availability and marketing efforts with these preferences to drive engagement and loyalty.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize high-performing channels:** Focus investment and resources on the channels that are driving the highest sales and customer engagement, whether through improved marketing, better inventory management, or expanded geographic reach.
2. **Address underperforming channels:** If certain channels are underperforming, investigate the root cause (e.g., stock issues, poor customer experience, inadequate marketing) and make improvements to boost performance or reduce costs.
3. **Expand geographic reach:** Identify underserved regions or countries and develop a strategy to expand distribution in those areas, whether through new retail partnerships or increased e-commerce presence.
107
4. **Improve channel profitability:** Analyze distribution costs and identify areas where efficiencies can be gained, such as through better inventory management, streamlined shipping processes, or improved supplier negotiations.
5. **Tailor marketing strategies to each channel:** Ensure that marketing efforts are aligned with the strengths of each channel. For example, use digital ads and influencer marketing for e-commerce channels, while focusing on in-store promotions for retail partners.
6. **Leverage consumer feedback:** Use consumer feedback to improve the shopping experience in each channel, such as addressing issues with delivery speed, stock availability, or return policies.
7. **Adopt an omnichannel strategy:** Integrate the brand’s online and offline channels to provide a seamless shopping experience. For example, allow customers to buy online and pick up in-store or make returns in physical stores for online purchases.
8. **Monitor competitor performance:** Continuously benchmark the brand’s distribution strategy against competitors to stay ahead of market trends and identify potential areas for competitive advantage.
