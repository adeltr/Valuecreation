# 35 · Portfolio Diversification and Correlation Analysis

> **Part:** [[D - Finance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Portfolio Diversification and Correlation Analysis is to assess the degree of diversification within the company’s investment portfolio and examine how different assets correlate with one another. This analysis helps identify areas of over-concentration or high correlation, ensuring the portfolio’s resilience against market volatility and improving risk-adjusted returns.


## 📊 Data required
1. Portfolio holdings, segmented by asset classes (e.g., equities, bonds, real estate) and individual investments
2. Historical returns for each asset or asset class
3. Correlation coefficients between assets or asset classes
4. Industry benchmarks for diversification (optional)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Identify Key Asset Classes and Holdings:** Segment the portfolio into primary asset classes (e.g., equities, fixed income, real estate) and identify individual holdings within each class.
2. **Calculate Proportion of Each Asset Class in the Portfolio:** For each asset class, calculate its weight as a percentage of the total portfolio value:
`Weight of Asset Class = (Value of Asset Class / Total Portfolio Value) x 100`
This determines the portfolio’s concentration within each asset class.
3. **Assess Diversification Ratio:** Calculate the diversification ratio by comparing the number of unique assets or asset classes in the portfolio to the portfolio’s total value. A high diversification ratio suggests better spread of investments.
4. **Calculate Correlation Coefficients Between Asset Classes:** Determine the correlation between returns of different asset classes. Correlation coefficients range from -1 to 1:
- 1 indicates a perfect positive correlation (assets move together)
- -1 indicates a perfect negative correlation (assets move in opposite directions)
- 0 indicates no correlation (independent movement)
5. **Calculate Weighted Portfolio Correlation:** To assess the portfolio’s overall diversification, calculate the weighted average correlation based on the correlation between each pair of asset classes and their weights in the portfolio. Lower overall correlation suggests better diversification.
6. **Analyze Concentration and Correlation Risks:** Identify areas of over-concentration (e.g., high weights in a single asset class or sector) and high correlation, which can increase portfolio risk during market downturns.
7. **Compile Results:** Summarize findings in a table displaying asset weights, correlation coefficients, and the portfolio’s overall diversification ratio.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Asset Class or Investment
- Weight in Portfolio (%)
- Correlation Coefficient with Other Classes
- Diversification Ratio (overall)
A correlation heatmap or matrix can visually show correlations between asset classes, and a pie chart of asset weights can illustrate concentration.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Diversification with Low Correlation:** Indicates that the portfolio is well-spread across asset classes that do not move together, reducing risk exposure.
- **High Concentration in Certain Asset Classes:** Suggests that the portfolio may be overly exposed to specific sectors, which could lead to greater losses during adverse market events.
- **High Correlation Between Assets:** Implies that assets may move together, reducing diversification benefits and increasing portfolio volatility during market downturns.
Benchmarking against industry norms can provide additional context for determining if the portfolio’s diversification is adequate.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Increase Allocation to Low-Correlation Assets Add assets with low or negative correlations to core holdings, such as commodities or alternative investments, to reduce portfolio-wide correlation.
2. Reduce Concentration in Highly Correlated Assets Diversify by reducing allocation to highly correlated assets, particularly within volatile asset classes, to improve portfolio stability.
3. Consider Geographic and Sector Diversification Spread investments across different geographic regions or industry sectors to reduce exposure to single-country or industry risks.
4. Rebalance the Portfolio Regularly Periodically adjust asset allocations to maintain diversification targets as market conditions change, ensuring alignment with the company’s risk tolerance.
5. Use Hedging Strategies for High-Correlation Assets Implement hedging strategies for asset classes or investments with high positive correlations to offset potential downturns.
6. Monitor Economic and Market Trends Stay informed on economic shifts or market cycles that could impact correlations between asset classes, enabling proactive adjustments in the portfolio.
7. Incorporate Diversification Ratios into Investment Strategy Set diversification goals based on a targeted diversification ratio, aligning investment selections to achieve and maintain these targets over time.
