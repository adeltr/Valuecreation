# 15 · Residual Market & Assigned Risk Pool Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Underwriting & Risk Segmentation]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate the composition, performance, and financial impact of policies placed in residual or assigned risk pools, and determine whether current underwriting, pricing, and servicing strategies effectively manage this often higher-risk segment.


## 📊 Data required
- Policies categorized under residual market or assigned risk pools (written premium, earned premium, number of policies)
- Claims data (frequency, severity, incurred losses, loss adjustment expenses) for these high-risk segments
- Underwriting guidelines and acceptance criteria for standard vs. residual market placements
- Historical loss ratios and claim trends specific to assigned risk business
- Industry or competitor benchmarks on residual market share and profitability


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify all policies currently in the residual market or assigned risk pool, and extract premium, loss, and policyholder information. Note the drivers’ risk profiles, including violations or insurance history gaps.
2. Calculate claim frequency and severity for this segment. Use Claim Frequency = (Number of Claims / Number of Policies) x 100 and Severity = (Total Incurred Losses / Number of Claims). Compare these metrics to standard market business.
3. Determine the loss ratio for assigned risk policies using Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + LAE) / Earned Premium. Assess whether the pool’s profitability (or lack thereof) aligns with internal targets and industry norms.
4. Review underwriting guidelines to identify how risks are designated as residual or assigned risk. Look for opportunities to transition borderline cases to the standard market with appropriate pricing or discounts for improved driving behavior.
5. Analyze historical trends to see if the assigned risk pool’s size is growing or shrinking. Correlate changes with external factors such as economic shifts, regulatory changes, or competitor practices.
6. Benchmark against industry data to gauge your assigned risk share relative to peers. A higher-than-average share might suggest opportunities to reduce or 35 reclassify risks, while a lower share may indicate overly strict underwriting in the standard market.
7. Assess servicing and claims management procedures to see if specialized handling of high-risk cases can mitigate losses. Identify whether an elevated level of driver coaching, telematics, or claim triage would be beneficial.
8. Document findings and propose strategic actions such as refining underwriting guidelines, introducing graduated coverage for improving risks, or adjusting rates in assigned risk segments. Present results to underwriting, pricing, and risk management teams.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables or charts showing assigned risk pool claim frequency, severity, and loss ratios compared to standard market segments
- Written summary highlighting key risk drivers, trends, and potential underwriting/pricing adjustments
- Recommendations for transitioning or managing residual market policies to improve overall portfolio profitability


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A consistently high assigned risk loss ratio suggests the need for enhanced pricing or risk management strategies to compensate for higher risk.
- Growth in assigned risk policies may reflect changes in underwriting standards or broader market conditions, necessitating a review of acceptance criteria.
- Low conversion from assigned risk to standard policies could indicate missed opportunities for upgrading customers who have improved their driving records or reduced violations.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Strengthen underwriting protocols to ensure that only truly uninsurable or extremely high-risk drivers end up in assigned risk pools.
2. Use targeted driver improvement programs or telematics for assigned risk customers to reduce claim frequency and facilitate eventual migration to standard coverage.
3. Collaborate with regulators to align assigned risk rates and policy structures with actual loss experience.
4. Continuously monitor the size and performance of the assigned risk segment and make data-driven adjustments to underwriting and pricing practices.
