# 13 · Shelf Space Allocation and Optimization

> **Part:** [[B - Operations]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Retail Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Shelf Space Allocation and Optimization is to determine the most e9ective way to allocate shelf space in a retail store to maximize sales, product visibility, and proﬁtability. This analysis aims to ensure that high-demand and high-margin products are given optimal space while minimizing stockouts and excess inventory of low-performing products.


## 📊 Data required
•   Sales data by product and category (units sold, revenue).
•   Gross proﬁt margin per product.
•   Inventory turnover rate by product.
•   Shelf space measurements (linear feet or square feet per category/product).
•   Foot tra9ic data (optional for high-tra9ic zones).
•   Planogram data (optional for current product placement).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Collect sales and inventory data for each product:** Gather the sales data (units sold, revenue) and inventory turnover rate for each product in the store. This will provide insight into the demand for each product and its contribution to overall proﬁtability.
2. **Measure current shelf space allocation:** Record the current amount of shelf space (linear feet or square feet) allocated to each product or category. This can be based on physical shelf measurements or from existing planograms.
3. **Calculate sales per square foot of shelf space:** To evaluate the performance of each product relative to its shelf space, calculate the sales per square foot (or linear foot) of shelf space:
`Sales per Square Foot = Total Sales / Total Shelf Space This metric provides a measure of how e9iciently the allocated space is generating revenue.`
4. **Identify high-margin and high-turnover products:** Analyze the gross proﬁt margin and inventory turnover rate for each product. High-margin products generate more proﬁt per sale, while high-turnover products sell quickly and require more frequent replenishment. Inventory Turnover Rate = (Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory)
5. **Optimize shelf space based on product performance:** Reallocate shelf space to prioritize high-margin, high-turnover products. Products that generate higher sales per square foot and have higher proﬁt margins should be given more prominent or larger shelf space. Use the following equation to adjust shelf space allocation:
`Optimal Shelf Space = (Product Sales / Total Store Sales) x Total Available Shelf Space This formula helps allocate space proportionally based on the contribution of each product to total sales.`
6. **Consider customer foot traMic:** If foot tra9ic data is available, analyze high-tra9ic zones in the store and place high-margin or popular products in these areas to maximize visibility. Products with lower performance can be placed in less prominent areas.
7. **Test and adjust the planogram:** Once shelf space is reallocated, implement a new planogram and monitor sales performance. Continuously track sales and inventory data to identify whether the adjustments have improved performance. If necessary, reﬁne the space allocation further based on updated data.
Potential complications that can arise with this analysis •   Seasonal ﬂuctuations: Sales performance for certain products may vary seasonally, which can lead to over- or under-allocation of shelf space if seasonality is not taken into account.
•   Limited shelf space: If there is insu9icient shelf space, trade-o9s must be made between high-margin and high-turnover products, which may complicate optimization decisions.
•   Inventory issues: Products with high demand but frequent stockouts may skew the analysis, leading to incorrect space allocation if stockouts are not accounted for.
•   Inconsistent foot traMic patterns: Changes in customer foot tra9ic due to promotions, store layout changes, or external factors can impact the e9ectiveness of shelf space allocation.


## 📤 Output format
The output typically includes a comparison of current vs. optimized shelf space allocation, sales per square foot, and recommendations for reallocation. The results can be presented in tables, charts, or updated planograms.
Example output:
•   Product A:
o   Current shelf space: 5 linear feet o   Sales per square foot: $400 o   Recommended shelf space: 7 linear feet •   Product B:
o   Current shelf space: 8 linear feet o   Sales per square foot: $200 o   Recommended shelf space: 5 linear feet


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   High sales per square foot: Products with high sales per square foot are making e9icient use of their shelf space and may warrant more space or better placement.
•   Low sales per square foot: Products with low sales per square foot may need to be reduced in shelf space or moved to less prominent areas unless there is a strategic reason for keeping them.
•   High-margin products in high-traMic zones: Placing high-margin products in high-tra9ic areas can boost proﬁtability by increasing exposure to customers.
•   Stockout-prone products: If high-demand products frequently experience stockouts, reallocating shelf space to allow for more inventory can reduce missed sales opportunities.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Reallocate shelf space regularly:** Conduct frequent reviews of sales performance and adjust shelf space allocations to match product performance and customer demand.
2. **Optimize placement in high-traMic areas:** Use customer foot tra9ic data to place high- performing products in prominent areas to maximize visibility and sales.
3. **Balance high-margin and high-turnover products:** Ensure that both high-margin and fast- moving products are given su9icient shelf space, with priority given to those that generate the most revenue or proﬁt.
4. **Reduce space for underperforming products:** Identify products with low sales per square foot and consider reducing their shelf space or replacing them with higher-performing items.
5. **Address stockout issues:** For high-demand products that frequently stock out, allocate additional shelf space or adjust inventory replenishment to ensure products are always available.
6. **Use data-driven planogram adjustments:** Continuously monitor sales, inventory, and foot tra9ic data to make data-driven decisions about shelf space allocation and product placement.
7. **Consider promotional areas:** Allocate temporary shelf space for promotions or seasonal items to maximize sales during peak periods while maintaining ﬂexibility in the regular shelf space allocation.
