# 5 · Deferred Revenue Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Finance & Revenue]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Deferred Revenue Analysis is to assess the revenue that a SaaS company has received in advance for services it has not yet delivered. Deferred revenue is a liability on the balance sheet because it represents an obligation to provide services or products in the future. This analysis helps a SaaS company track its future obligations and recognize revenue at the appropriate time, which is crucial for accurate ﬁnancial reporting and cash ﬂow forecasting.


## 📊 Data required
1. Customer payments received in advance for subscriptions or services
2. Length of the subscription period (monthly, quarterly, annually)
3. Revenue recognized over time for those subscriptions
4. Start and end dates of each contract


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Determine the period for analysis:** Select the time period for the analysis (monthly, quarterly, or annually). Deferred revenue is usually tracked and updated on a monthly basis.
2. **Collect customer payments received in advance:** Identify all payments that have been received in advance for services that have not yet been provided. This could include annual subscription payments, upfront payments for multi- year contracts, or any prepayments for services.
3. **Determine the length of the subscription period:** For each customer payment, identify the subscription or service period over which the revenue will be recognized. For example, if a customer pays $12,000 for a 12-month subscription, revenue is recognized at $1,000 per month.
4. **Calculate Deferred Revenue for each customer:** Use the following formula to calculate deferred revenue:
`Deferred Revenue = Total Payment Received - Revenue Recognized to Date`
For example, if a customer pays $12,000 upfront for a 12-month subscription and two months of service have already been delivered, the deferred revenue is:
`Deferred Revenue = $12,000 - ($1,000 x 2) = $10,000`

5. **Sum all deferred revenue:** Add the deferred revenue from all customers to calculate the total deferred revenue for the period. This amount will be reported as a liability on the balance sheet until the services are fully provided.


## 📤 Output format
The output is typically a dollar ﬁgure representing the total deferred revenue, which is reported as a liability on the company’s balance sheet. For example:
Total Deferred Revenue: $500,000


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   High Deferred Revenue: A high deferred revenue ﬁgure indicates that the company has received signiﬁcant prepayments for future services, which can be a positive sign of customer trust and cash ﬂow. However, this also means the company has a large future obligation to deliver services, and it cannot yet recognize this revenue as proﬁt.
•   Low Deferred Revenue: A low deferred revenue balance could mean that most revenue is recognized on a shorter-term basis (e.g., monthly billing) or that there are fewer long-term contracts. While this may simplify ﬁnancial reporting, it may also indicate less predictability in future revenue streams.
Deferred revenue should be analyzed in conjunction with recognized revenue to ensure that the company is accurately reporting its ﬁnancial performance and fulﬁlling its obligations to customers.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Encourage longer-term contracts:** Offering discounts or incentives for annual or multi-year contracts can increase deferred revenue and improve cash ﬂow predictability.
2. **Automate revenue recognition:** Implement software to automate the calculation and recognition of deferred revenue. This ensures that revenue is recognized consistently and in line with the subscription or service period.
3. **Monitor cash ﬂow vs. deferred revenue:** Carefully track cash ﬂow alongside deferred revenue. Although deferred revenue improves short-term cash ﬂow, the company must be prepared to deliver services over time without counting the revenue as proﬁt until it's earned.

4. **Align sales and ﬁnance teams:** Ensure that the sales and ﬁnance teams are aligned in understanding how different payment terms and contract structures impact deferred revenue and cash ﬂow. This alignment can help in crafting contracts that balance revenue recognition and customer needs.
5. **OKer ﬂexible payment options:** For customers who may be hesitant to commit to long-term contracts, offer ﬂexible payment options (e.g., quarterly or semi-annual payments) that still increase deferred revenue without requiring full upfront payment.
