# 17 · Guaranteed Universal Life (GUL) and Indexed Universal Life (IUL) Product Performance Review

> **Part:** [[C - Profitability and Product Performance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the financial performance and profitability of Guaranteed Universal Life (GUL) and Indexed Universal Life (IUL) products. This analysis identifies drivers of product performance, examines customer behavior, and assesses areas for improvement in pricing, investment strategies, and product design.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Product-level data:** ○ Premiums (planned and flexible) collected by policy type (GUL vs. IUL). ○ Death benefit payouts, surrender values, and lapse rates. ○ Expense data (acquisition, administration, and claims expenses).
2. **Policyholder data:** ○ Demographics (age, income, gender, location). ○ Policy duration, payment frequency, and persistency rates.
3. **Interest crediting and investment data:** ○ IUL crediting rates tied to market indices (e.g., S&P 500). ○ Minimum guarantees for both GUL and IUL products.
4. **Actuarial assumptions:** ○ Mortality rates, lapse rates, and interest rate assumptions.
5. **Benchmark data:** ○ Competitive GUL and IUL product performance metrics (pricing, crediting rates, fees).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Segment Product Performance a. Separate GUL and IUL policies to evaluate performance individually. b. Analyze profitability at a cohort level based on age, premium structure, and duration.
2. Calculate Policy Profitability a. Use the formula:
`Product Profitability = Premium Revenue - (Claims Paid + Acquisition Costs + Maintenance Costs)`
b. For IUL products, factor in interest crediting rates and investment-linked components.
3. **Analyze Persistency and Lapse Rates:** 48 a. Measure persistency rates over time (e.g., 13-month, 25-month, and 37-month persistency). b. Identify segments with high lapse rates and assess the causes (e.g., affordability, market performance).
4. Evaluate Investment Performance for IUL a. Compare interest crediting rates applied to IUL policies with market index performance (e.g., S&P 500). b. Assess policyholder value derived from minimum guarantees vs. actual credited interest.
5. Stress Test Under Key Scenarios a. Conduct sensitivity analysis for both products: ■ GUL: Impact of changes in lapse rates, mortality assumptions, and discount rates on profitability. ■ IUL: Impact of market volatility and index underperformance on crediting rates and reserve adequacy.
6. Assess Risk Exposure a. For GUL, measure exposure to low lapse rates, which increase long-term liabilities. b. For IUL, evaluate risk associated with volatile interest crediting and customer behavior.
7. Benchmark Against Competitors a. Compare pricing, features, guarantees, and investment returns with competitive GUL and IUL offerings.
8. Visualize Results a. Use tables to summarize product-level profitability, persistency rates, and investment performance. b. Generate charts showing trends in interest crediting rates for IUL and lapse performance for GUL.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing profitability metrics, claims payouts, and expense ratios for GUL and IUL products
- Charts illustrating IUL crediting rate performance vs. benchmarks (e.g., index returns)
- Persistency rate tables highlighting lapse trends over time
- Stress testing results showing sensitivity of profitability to changes in assumptions
- Key findings summarizing product performance, risk exposure, and opportunities for improvement 49


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. High persistency rates and strong profitability indicate that products are meeting policyholder needs and aligned with pricing assumptions.
2. High lapse rates in GUL policies may signal affordability issues or inadequate value perception by policyholders.
3. For IUL, lower-than-expected crediting rates relative to market benchmarks may lead to dissatisfaction and reduced policyholder retention.
4. Stress test results reveal financial vulnerabilities under adverse mortality, lapse, or market conditions.
5. Benchmark comparisons highlight areas where the company may be lagging competitors in pricing, guarantees, or investment returns.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Improve communication and education for policyholders to clarify product benefits, particularly for guarantees in GUL and crediting rates in IUL.
2. Optimize investment strategies for IUL portfolios to ensure competitive and stable crediting rates while managing downside risks.
3. Adjust pricing and premium structures to improve alignment with policyholder behavior and financial assumptions.
4. Implement targeted retention strategies for high-risk lapse segments through loyalty programs, personalized communication, or payment flexibility.
5. Monitor competitor products regularly to refine guarantees, features, and pricing to maintain market competitiveness.
6. Conduct regular experience studies to validate actuarial assumptions for mortality, lapse rates, and expense trends.
