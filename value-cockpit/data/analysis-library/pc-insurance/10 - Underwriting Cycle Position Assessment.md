# 10 · Underwriting Cycle Position Assessment

> **Part:** [[A - Underwriting]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Underwriting Cycle Position Assessment is to determine the current phase of the underwriting cycle (soft, hard, or neutral market) and understand its impact on profitability, pricing strategies, and underwriting practices. By assessing the cycle’s position, a company can make informed adjustments to pricing, underwriting standards, and risk appetite to align with market conditions.


## 📊 Data required
1. Historical combined ratios over several years (industry-wide and/or company-specific)
2. Premium growth rates over time
3. Claim frequency and severity trends over time
4. Market conditions (e.g., economic factors, reinsurance availability, regulatory changes)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Define the Underwriting Cycle Phases Typically, the underwriting cycle has two main phases: ○ Hard Market: Characterized by high premiums, stricter underwriting standards, reduced capacity, and lower claim frequency. ○ Soft Market: Characterized by lower premiums, relaxed underwriting standards, increased capacity, and often higher claim frequency.
2. Analyze Combined Ratios Over Time Examine combined ratio trends to identify shifts in underwriting profitability: ○ Rising Combined Ratios often indicate a softening market, as competition drives lower premiums and more relaxed underwriting standards. ○ Declining Combined Ratios suggest a hardening market, with stricter underwriting and higher premiums.
3. Evaluate Premium Growth Trends Analyze premium growth rates over time. A rapid growth rate may suggest a soft market with aggressive underwriting, while slower growth or stabilization often indicates a hard market.
4. Assess Claims Frequency and Severity Trends Identify changes in claim frequency and severity. Higher claim frequency and severity are often associated with soft markets, while lower levels typically correspond to hard markets due to tightened underwriting.
5. **Consider Market Factors Review external factors affecting the cycle, such as:** ○ Economic conditions (e.g., inflation, interest rates) ○ Reinsurance availability and costs ○ Regulatory changes impacting pricing or coverage terms
6. Determine Current Cycle Position Based on the combined ratio trends, premium growth, claims trends, and market conditions, determine the current underwriting cycle phase (hard, soft, or neutral).
7. Compile Results Document findings in a summary report, including the identified cycle phase and the supporting data trends for easy reference.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a report or table with the following sections:
- Combined Ratio Trends (by year)
- Premium Growth Rates (by year)
- Claims Frequency and Severity Trends (by year)
- Current Cycle Position (soft, hard, or neutral)
- Summary of Key Market Factors A line chart showing the combined ratio over time can visually illustrate shifts in the underwriting cycle.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Soft Market:** Indicates increased competition, lower premiums, and relaxed underwriting. It’s generally less profitable, so the company should focus on maintaining discipline and avoiding underpricing.
- **Hard Market:** Suggests higher premiums, stricter underwriting standards, and reduced competition. This is typically a more profitable phase, allowing the company to optimize rates and improve loss ratios.
- **Neutral Market:** Falls between soft and hard markets. Companies should continue monitoring trends and adjust their strategies as market conditions evolve.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Adjust Pricing Based on Market Position In a hard market, capitalize on higher premiums. In a soft market, focus on maintaining pricing discipline to avoid underpricing.
2. Refine Underwriting Standards Tighten underwriting guidelines during a soft market to avoid taking on excessive risk. Relax standards cautiously in a hard market, if profitable opportunities arise.
3. Monitor Market Conditions Regularly Continuously track combined ratios, premium growth, and claims trends to detect shifts early and adapt pricing or underwriting strategies accordingly.
4. Leverage Reinsurance Strategically During a soft market, consider higher levels of reinsurance to mitigate risk. In a hard market, review reinsurance needs to balance cost and risk transfer.
5. Optimize Capital Allocation Allocate capital toward high-performing segments or regions, especially in a hard market, to maximize returns.
6. Plan for Future Cycle Shifts Develop strategies to prepare for the next cycle phase. For instance, build reserves during a hard market to manage potential losses during a future soft market.
