# 18 · Supply Chain Analytics & Reporting Maturity Assessment

> **Part:** [[C - Technology & Systems]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Third Party Logistics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine the 3PL’s analytics capabilities and reporting sophistication across various supply chain functions, identifying gaps in data quality, tool adoption, and decision-making processes.


## 📊 Data required
- Current reporting systems (BI tools, dashboards, custom spreadsheets)
- Data sources (ERP, WMS, TMS, CRM) and integration methods
- Key performance indicators (KPIs) currently tracked (on-time delivery, cost per shipment, fill rate, etc.)
- Frequency and format of reports (daily, weekly, monthly)
- User access and adoption rates (who uses the reports, how often)
- Data governance policies and procedures


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Inventory Existing Analytics & Reporting Tools:** List all platforms and methods used to capture, analyze, and distribute supply chain data. Include formal BI solutions and informal tools (spreadsheets, email reports).
2. **Evaluate Data Sources & Quality:** Identify all data inputs, how they are integrated, and assess their reliability. Note any manual data entry points or reconciliation efforts that might introduce errors.
3. **Map Current KPIs & Metrics:** Compile the metrics being tracked across operations (transportation, warehousing, customer service). Check if metrics are well-defined, consistent, and aligned with business goals.
4. **Assess Reporting Frequency & Formats:** Determine how often reports are generated and in what format (dashboards, static PDFs, Excel). Look for redundancies or overly manual reporting processes.
5. **Gauge User Adoption & Decision-Making Impact:** Interview stakeholders to see how reports influence daily operations or strategic initiatives. Identify any challenges in interpreting or acting on the data.
6. **Determine Analytical Depth:** Investigate if advanced analytics techniques (predictive modeling, machine learning) are in use. Note whether analysis is predominantly descriptive (historic) or prescriptive (forward-looking).
7. **Compare to Industry Best Practices:** Benchmark the organization’s analytics maturity against industry standards or peer companies. Highlight strengths and weaknesses in data governance, tools, or processes.

## 📤 Output format
- A maturity model or scorecard showing the organization’s analytics capabilities on a scale (e.g., Basic, Intermediate, Advanced)
- A mapping of existing reports, their data sources, and the user groups reliant on them
- A gap analysis table identifying critical missing KPIs or inconsistent data definitions
- A recommendations document outlining short- and long-term improvements to analytics platforms and reporting processes


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Maturity:** Indicates robust, scalable analytics infrastructure, with real-time reporting, predictive capabilities, and strong data governance.
- **Moderate Maturity:** Suggests a decent foundation, but limited advanced analytics or inconsistent data quality.
- **Low Maturity:** Reflects reliance on manual processes, siloed data, and limited visibility, resulting in slower or less-informed decision-making.
- **High User Adoption:** Shows that data insights are integral to operations and strategic planning.
- **Low User Adoption:** May suggest reports are not intuitive, relevant, or timely enough to support effective decision-making.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Consolidate Data Sources:** Implement a data warehouse or integration platform to reduce siloed information and provide a single source of truth.
2. **Enhance BI Tools & Dashboards:** Invest in modern analytics solutions that allow real-time reporting, self-service dashboards, and ad hoc querying.
3. **Implement Data Governance Policies:** Define clear guidelines for data ownership, quality standards, and maintenance procedures.
4. **Expand Advanced Analytics Use Cases:** Explore predictive and prescriptive analytics, leveraging machine learning where it can add value to forecasting or route optimization.
5. **Promote a Data-Driven Culture:** Provide training and support for business users, ensuring they understand how to interpret metrics and use data to inform decisions.
