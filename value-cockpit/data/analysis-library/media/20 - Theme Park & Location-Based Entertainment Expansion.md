# 20 · Theme Park & Location-Based Entertainment Expansion Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Live & Experiential Entertainment]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Media & Entertainment Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate the potential for expanding into or enhancing theme parks, branded attractions, and other location-based entertainment offerings. Determine the financial viability, brand alignment, and long-term strategic benefits of such expansions.


## 📊 Data required
1. Existing theme park or location-based entertainment assets (attendance figures, revenue, operating costs)
2. Market research on tourism trends, competitor attractions, and consumer behavior
3. Demographic data on target audiences (families, thrill-seekers, cultural tourists)
4. Potential site locations, infrastructure costs, and regulatory considerations (zoning, permits)
5. Brand/IP portfolio suitability for rides, immersive experiences, and themed environments


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Compile historical performance data. If already operating theme parks or attractions, gather metrics on attendance, per capita spend, operational costs, and overall profitability.
2. Assess brand/IP compatibility. Review your catalog of IPs (films, TV shows, characters) to identify those with strong fan bases or high experiential potential. Consider how each IP can translate into rides, live shows, or interactive zones.
3. Conduct market feasibility studies. Analyze the local or regional tourism market, competitor offerings, and consumer spending habits to estimate potential visitor volume. Factor in seasonality and economic conditions.
4. Model capital investment and ROI. Calculate up-front infrastructure and construction costs for new rides or park expansions. Estimate ongoing operational costs (staff, utilities, maintenance) and forecast revenue using formulas like: ROI (%) = (Projected Revenue - Total Costs) / Total Costs x 100.
5. Evaluate location and regulatory environment. Examine site accessibility (transportation, proximity to key population centers), and identify local permitting or environmental hurdles that may affect timelines and budgets.

6. Develop a phased expansion plan. Prioritize attractions or experiences that offer the highest ROI or strategic brand impact first. Plan phased rollouts to manage capital expenditure and maintain public excitement.
7. Create a marketing and partnership strategy. Collaborate with travel agencies, local governments, and corporate sponsors. Develop cross-promotional packages (e.g., film releases tied to new park attractions).


## 📤 Output format
- A financial model or spreadsheet detailing capital expenditure, projected attendance, operating costs, and ROI
- A slide presentation or report summarizing location assessments, IP alignment, and strategic recommendations for expansion
- A roadmap outlining phased construction timelines, marketing campaigns, and projected capacity milestones


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Strong demand indicators (high projected attendance, positive tourist trends, brand synergy) support further investment in themed experiences
- Low or marginal ROI projections may signal the need to scale back initial scope, explore alternative sites, or refine creative concepts
- Public reception and repeat visitation rates are crucial for evaluating the long-term sustainability of any theme park or location-based venture


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Partner with established park operators or regional developers to leverage their expertise in construction, operations, and local regulations
2. Bundle experiences (hotel stays, VIP tours, themed dining) to increase guest spending and enhance brand immersion
3. Diversify attractions to cater to multiple demographics (families, thrill-seekers, pop-culture fans) and spread visitation across different seasons
4. Offer compelling annual pass programs and loyalty incentives to encourage repeat visits and stable cash flow
5. Continuously evolve the experience with seasonal events, new ride technology, and IP-based refreshes to maintain excitement and drive return traffic.
