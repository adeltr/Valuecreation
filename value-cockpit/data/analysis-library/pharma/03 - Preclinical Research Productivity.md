# 3 · Preclinical Research Productivity

> **Part:** [[A - Research & Development]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Pharmaceutical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Preclinical Research Productivity analysis is to evaluate how efficiently a pharmaceutical company’s research teams are identifying, optimizing, and advancing potential drug candidates from discovery into clinical trials. This analysis focuses on the effectiveness of preclinical research investments, innovation output, and scientific risk mitigation strategies in the early stages of drug development.


## 📊 Data required
- Number of drug candidates identified in a given time period (e.g., annually).
- Number of drug candidates advancing from discovery into preclinical studies.
- Number of preclinical candidates advancing into Phase I clinical trials.
- Preclinical research spending, including both direct costs (laboratories, trials) and indirect costs (infrastructure, overhead).
- Time duration from target identification to entry into preclinical testing.
- Scientific publications, patents filed, or technology licensing deals related to preclinical discoveries.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Data Collection:** ○ Gather historical data on the number of drug candidates entering preclinical research each year. ○ Collect information on preclinical success rates, measured as the number of drug candidates that successfully transition to clinical trials (Phase I). ○ Obtain preclinical research expenditure for the evaluation period. ○ Track time spent per drug candidate in the preclinical stage, from target identification to IND (Investigational New Drug) filing.
2. **Calculate Preclinical Success Rate:** ○ Use the following equation to calculate the success rate:
`Preclinical Success Rate = (Number of Candidates Entering Phase I / Number of Candidates in Preclinical Research) x 100 ○ This percentage shows the rate at which preclinical candidates are successfully progressing to clinical trials.`
3. **Evaluate Productivity Metrics:** ○ Research Efficiency (Cost per Candidate): ■ This metric calculates the financial efficiency of preclinical research by determining the cost per drug candidate successfully transitioned to clinical trials:
`Cost per Candidate = Preclinical Research Spending / Number of Candidates Entering Phase I`
12 ■ A lower value indicates higher productivity, as the company is advancing more candidates at a lower cost. ○ Time to Preclinical Advancement: ■ This measures how quickly a company is able to advance candidates through preclinical research:
`Time to Preclinical Advancement = Total Preclinical Duration (in months or years) / Number of Candidates Entering Phase I ■ Shorter time frames indicate higher research efficiency and faster drug development timelines.`
4. **Pipeline Output Analysis:** ○ Compare the number of successful preclinical candidates entering Phase I against the company’s historical performance and industry benchmarks. ○ Assess whether the output of viable candidates is increasing or stagnating over time, and correlate this with changes in R&D investment or strategic focus areas.
5. **Scientific Impact Assessment:** ○ Quantify the number of publications, patents, or significant technology licenses that result from preclinical research efforts. These outputs can serve as indirect indicators of the company’s research productivity and innovation potential.

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Preclinical Success Rate:** A higher success rate (>20%) indicates that the company’s early-stage drug discovery and candidate optimization processes are effective, with a strong likelihood of identifying viable candidates for clinical development.
13
- **Low Cost per Candidate:** A lower cost per candidate transitioning to Phase I suggests that the company is efficiently utilizing its research resources, minimizing waste, and generating high-value candidates with fewer financial inputs.
- **Shorter Time to Preclinical Advancement:** Companies that can advance candidates faster (within 2-3 years) demonstrate agility in their drug development process, which may lead to earlier market entry and competitive advantages.
- **Strong Scientific Output:** A high volume of patents and publications points to a robust innovation pipeline, where the company is generating novel intellectual property and scientific advancements that can be leveraged for future drug development.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Invest in Automation and AI-driven Discovery Tools:** Implement advanced technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning, and high-throughput screening tools to accelerate drug discovery and preclinical testing, reducing time and increasing productivity.
2. **Strengthen Collaboration with External Partners:** Collaborate with academic institutions, biotech firms, or contract research organizations (CROs) to bring in specialized expertise, share costs, and enhance research output in preclinical stages.
3. **Focus on Predictive Biomarkers:** Develop and validate predictive biomarkers during preclinical research to improve the selection of candidates most likely to succeed in clinical trials, thus increasing the success rate.
4. **Optimize Resource Allocation:** Continuously monitor R&D spending efficiency by prioritizing high-potential projects and divesting from those with poor preclinical performance, ensuring that resources are focused on the most promising candidates.
5. **Enhance Scientific Talent and Expertise:** Build or recruit top-tier scientific talent with expertise in key areas like molecular biology, chemistry, and pharmacology to improve research productivity and reduce the time taken to advance drug candidates.
