# 17 · Mortgage Loan-to-Value (LTV) and Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Product and Portfolio Performance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Retail Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
To assess the risk profile of the mortgage portfolio by analyzing Loan-to-Value (LTV) and Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratios. This analysis identifies high-risk loans and ensures the portfolio is aligned with prudent lending standards.


## 📊 Data required
1. Mortgage loan data (loan amount, property value at origination, outstanding balance).
2. Updated property valuations.
3. Borrower income data.
4. Loan repayment history (e.g., delinquency or default rates).
5. Portfolio segmentation data (e.g., geographic location, loan type).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratios ○ Calculate the current LTV for each loan:
`Current LTV = (Outstanding Loan Balance / Current Property Value) x 100 ○ Segment loans by LTV ranges (e.g., ≤50%, 51–80%, >80%). ○ Identify loans with high LTV ratios (>80%), indicating increased default risk.`
2. Analyze Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratios ○ Calculate DTI for each borrower:
`DTI = (Total Monthly Debt Payments / Monthly Gross Income) x 100 ○ Segment borrowers by DTI thresholds (e.g., ≤35%, 36–50%, >50%). ○ Highlight borrowers with DTI ratios exceeding standard limits (e.g., >43%).`
3. Evaluate Risk Correlations ○ Assess the relationship between high LTV/DTI ratios and delinquency or default rates. ○ Identify geographic regions or borrower profiles with higher risk levels.
4. Monitor Trends in LTV and DTI ○ Track changes in LTV and DTI ratios over time due to property value fluctuations, loan repayments, or changes in borrower income. ○ Highlight trends in market conditions (e.g., declining property values increasing LTV ratios).
5. **Perform Stress Testing:** 50 ○ Simulate scenarios such as property value declines or income reductions to evaluate impacts on LTV and DTI ratios. ○ Assess changes in risk levels under adverse conditions.
6. Segment Portfolio by Risk Levels ○ Classify loans into risk categories based on combined LTV and DTI thresholds (e.g., Low, Moderate, High). ○ Prioritize high-risk loans for closer monitoring or intervention.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing the distribution of LTV and DTI ratios across the portfolio.
- Charts illustrating trends in LTV and DTI over time.
- Risk heatmaps showing geographic concentrations of high LTV/DTI loans.
- Stress testing results indicating projected risk changes under adverse scenarios.
- A report summarizing insights and actionable recommendations for risk management.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. High LTV ratios (>80%) indicate greater exposure to property market fluctuations, increasing default risk.
2. High DTI ratios (>43%) signal potential borrower stress and reduced repayment capacity.
3. Correlations between high LTV/DTI ratios and delinquency rates suggest areas needing intervention.
4. Trends showing rising LTV or DTI ratios may require adjustments in lending policies or risk mitigation strategies.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Tighten lending criteria for high-risk borrowers, such as reducing maximum allowable LTV and DTI thresholds.
2. Monitor property valuations regularly and adjust risk profiles accordingly.
3. Offer refinancing or restructuring options to borrowers with high DTI ratios to improve repayment capacity.
4. Introduce loan insurance products to mitigate risks associated with high LTV loans.
5. Use predictive analytics to proactively identify loans at risk of default based on LTV and DTI trends.
