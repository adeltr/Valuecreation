# KPI — Agriculture

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Agriculture & Food]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Agriculture Company - Analyse]]

56 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Age of Orchard/Vineyard | For businesses in the fruit and wine industry, the age of the orchards or vineyards can affect both the quantity and quality of production. Older trees/vines can sometimes produce fewer, but higher quality fruits. |
| Agricultural Worker Turnover Rate | This KPI measures how often workers leave the company. High turnover can suggest issues with pay, conditions, or morale, while low turnover can indicate a stable, satisfied workforce. |
| Animal Breeding Success Rate | For operations involved in breeding livestock, the success rate of their breeding program is a key performance indicator. |
| Aquaculture Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR) | Similar to FCR in livestock, this measures the efficiency with which fish or shrimp convert feed into body mass. |
| Aquaculture Survival Rate | In aquaculture, the survival rate of the fish or other aquatic organisms is a key performance indicator. High mortality rates can suggest issues with disease, water quality, or other environmental factors. |
| Biomass Production | For companies engaged in forestry or biofuel production, the amount of biomass (organic material used for fuel) produced can be an essential KPI. |
| Brix Degree | For fruit and vegetable growers, Brix degree, a measure of the sugar content in an aqueous solution, can be a critical indicator of produce quality. Higher Brix degrees often signify better taste and overall quality. |
| Cage-Free or Free-Range Percentage | In the poultry and egg industry, this KPI shows the percentage of animals raised in cage-free or free-range conditions. This could be a significant factor in meeting consumer demand for ethically raised products. |
| Carbon Footprint | In an era of growing environmental concerns, this measures the total greenhouse gas emissions caused directly and indirectly by a company's activities. This KPI can indicate how 'green' the agriculture company's operations are, which could influence its public image, regulatory compliance, and potential for growth in a market increasingly prioritizing sustainability. |
| Carbon Sequestration Rate | This measures the rate at which a farm's practices enable the capture and storage of atmospheric carbon dioxide, an important aspect of sustainable farming. |
| Cold Storage Capacity | For industries that require storage of produce, the amount of cold storage available can be a critical KPI. Limited cold storage could lead to spoilage and wasted product. |
| Cover Crop Usage | The use of cover crops can improve soil health and reduce the need for fertilizers. This KPI measures the percentage of a farm's total land where cover crops are used. |
| Crop Diversity Index | This measures the range of different crops a farm produces. A higher index can make a farm more resilient against disease or adverse weather affecting a single crop. |
| Crop Insurance Coverage | The percentage of crops or livestock insured against weather events or other losses can provide insight into a farming operation's risk management strategies. |
| Crop Rotation Frequency | This metric measures how often a company rotates its crops, a practice often used to improve soil health and prevent pest and disease buildup. A higher frequency might indicate a commitment to sustainable farming practices. |
| Crop Yield per Acre | This is the amount of crop produced per unit of land. It is one of the most critical KPIs as it reflects the efficiency of the land use and the effectiveness of farming practices. Higher crop yield can indicate advanced technologies and optimized farming strategies. |
| Days to Maturity for Crops | This is the number of days it takes from planting a crop until it is ready to be harvested. Shorter maturity times can enable quicker crop turnover and potentially increase annual yield. |
| Disease and Pest Resistance | This indicates the resilience of a company's crops or livestock to diseases and pests. A higher resistance can point to strong genetics, careful biosecurity measures, and overall healthy conditions. |
| Fallow Land Ratio | This is the percentage of a farm's total land that is left fallow (unused) in any given year. Leaving land fallow can improve soil health, but also represents land that is not directly productive. |
| Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR) | This is particularly relevant to livestock and aquaculture businesses. It measures the efficiency with which the livestock or fish convert feed into body mass. Lower FCR means better efficiency and lower costs. |
| Fertilizer Efficiency | This metric shows how much crop yield is produced per unit of fertilizer used. It reflects the effectiveness of a company's fertilizer application techniques and the efficiency of their crop varieties. Higher efficiency is generally better. |
| Fish Stock Health | For fisheries and aquaculture operations, the health and diversity of the fish stock are crucial for ongoing productivity and sustainability. |
| Forest Stewardship Council (FSC) Certification | For businesses in the forestry industry, having FSC certification can be an important KPI, as it signals to customers that the wood products come from responsibly managed forests. |
| Grazing Capacity | For ranches and other businesses in the livestock industry, this measures the amount of livestock that a given parcel of land can support, generally in terms of the number of head per acre. |
| Harvest-to-Sale Time | This KPI measures the time it takes from when a crop is harvested or an animal is ready for slaughter until it is sold. Shorter times can indicate efficient supply chains and strong demand for products, while longer times can indicate potential inefficiencies or weak demand. |
| Honey Yield per Hive | For apiaries and other businesses involved in honey production, this KPI measures the amount of honey produced per hive. |
| Hybridization Success Rate | For companies that are creating new hybrid plants, the success rate of their hybridization efforts is a key performance indicator. |
| Input Cost per Acre or per Animal | This measures how much a company is spending on inputs like seeds, fertilizer, feed, water, and other items necessary to produce their crops or livestock. Lower input costs can signify efficient purchasing, good relationships with suppliers, or success in minimizing waste. |
| Irrigation Efficiency | This KPI measures the ratio of the amount of water used for irrigation that is effectively taken up by crops versus the total water applied. Higher efficiency reduces water waste, lowers costs, and improves sustainability. |
| Livestock Growth Rates | For businesses focusing on livestock, the rate at which animals grow or produce is key. This includes measures such as poultry weight gain, milk production per cow, or eggs per hen. Fast growth rates can indicate efficient operations and good animal health. |
| Manure Management Practices | This KPI can assess how effectively a livestock company manages the manure produced by its animals, which can have significant environmental impacts and may be subject to regulation. |
| Milk Yield per Cow | In dairy farming, the average milk yield per cow is a crucial productivity indicator. Higher yields may reflect better feed efficiency, genetics, and animal care. |
| Nutrient Management Efficiency | This measures how efficiently a farm manages its use of nutrients, such as those provided through fertilizers. High efficiency can reduce costs and environmental impact. |
| Organic Matter Content in Soil | This KPI indicates the percentage of organic material in the soil, which can affect its fertility and the health and productivity of crops. |
| Pesticide Use | The amount and type of pesticides used can be a key performance indicator, especially for companies seeking to minimize their environmental impact or produce organic crops. |
| Plant Density | For certain high-yield crops, the density of plantings can affect overall productivity. There's often a balance to be struck between crowding (which can limit growth) and under-utilization of available land. |
| Pollination Success Rate | For businesses focusing on crops dependent on pollination, the rate of successful pollination can significantly impact yields. |
| Post-Harvest Loss | This metric measures the amount of product lost post-harvest due to factors like spoilage, pests, or logistical issues. Lower loss percentages indicate better handling, storage, and distribution practices. |
| Protein Content | For grain producers, the protein content of the grain is a critical KPI. This is especially important for wheat used in bread-making, where higher protein content often means higher quality. |
| Rate of Genetic Gain | In seed production and animal breeding, this KPI measures the rate at which beneficial traits are increased in the population over time through selective breeding or genetic modification. |
| Rate of Organic Production | This KPI measures the percentage of a farm's produce that is grown organically. This could be significant in assessing a company's positioning in the high-value organic market. |
| Return on Investment (ROI) for Precision Farming Techniques | Precision farming involves using advanced technology like GPS and IoT devices to manage crops more effectively. This KPI measures the ROI for these techniques, helping assess whether the technology investment is paying off. |
| Robotic Milking Adoption Rate | In the dairy industry, the percentage of milking performed by automated or robotic systems is a KPI that can indicate efficiency, modernization, and labor savings. |
| Ruminant Methane Emissions | For livestock operations, particularly those involving ruminant animals like cows, the amount of methane emitted is an important environmental KPI. |
| Seasonality of Production | This KPI measures how much a company's production varies with the seasons. Depending on the specific agriculture segment, some level of seasonality may be normal, but significant swings could increase business risk. |
| Seed Germination Rate | This indicates the percentage of seeds that successfully germinate and develop into healthy plants. A high rate may suggest quality seeds and planting methods, and could predict a successful harvest. |
| Shell Strength (for poultry farms) | For egg producers, the strength of eggshells can be an indicator of the overall health of their flocks and the quality of their feed. |
| Soil Health Index | This metric assesses the quality and health of the soil used for farming. It could incorporate measures of nutrient levels, organic matter content, pH level, and other key soil characteristics. Healthy soil can lead to higher crop yields and better long-term sustainability. |
| Sustainable Certification Rate | This KPI measures the percentage of a farm's products that meet specific standards for sustainable agriculture and have been certified as such by a recognized organization. It's becoming increasingly important as consumers and regulators demand more sustainable farming practices. |
| Tilling Efficiency | For crops that require tilling, the efficiency of the tilling process can affect both costs and yields. |
| Traceability Rate | This measures the ability to trace a product from the field to the consumer. In an era where consumers are increasingly interested in the source of their food, this can be an important KPI for agriculture businesses. |
| Turbidity Levels | For aquaculture, the measure of water clarity (turbidity) can indicate the health of the aquatic environment, affecting the survival and growth of the species being farmed. |
| Vineyard Yield per Acre | This KPI is essential for winemakers. It represents the amount of grape produced per acre. Higher yields aren't always better, as they can sometimes result in lower-quality wine. |
| Water Usage per Crop Unit or per Animal | Water is a crucial resource in agriculture, and its efficient use is increasingly important given climate change concerns. This KPI measures how much water a company uses per unit of output, with lower usage potentially indicating more sustainable and efficient practices. |
| Weed Infestation Levels | High levels of weed infestation can lower crop yields and indicate issues with a company's weed management practices. |
| Worker Safety Incidents | The frequency of accidents or safety incidents can indicate how well a company is ensuring the safety of its workforce. A lower rate is usually better. |
