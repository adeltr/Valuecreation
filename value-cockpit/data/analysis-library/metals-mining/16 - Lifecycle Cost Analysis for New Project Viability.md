# 16 · Lifecycle Cost Analysis for New Project Viability

> **Part:** [[B - Operational Efficiency and Cost Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Metals & Mining Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to estimate the total costs associated with a mining project over its lifecycle, from initial exploration to mine closure and reclamation. This analysis helps determine the economic viability of a new project and supports investment decision-making.


## 📊 Data required
- Exploration and development costs.
- Capital expenditure (CapEx) for infrastructure, equipment, and facilities.
- Operating expenditure (OpEx) for mining, processing, transportation, and overhead.
- Closure and reclamation costs.
- Revenue estimates based on ore grades, production rates, and commodity prices.
- Project timeline and mine life projections.
- Discount rate for Net Present Value (NPV) calculations.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Estimate Initial Exploration and Development Costs:** ○ Gather data on geological surveys, drilling, feasibility studies, and permitting expenses. ○ Include costs for environmental and regulatory compliance.
2. **Calculate Capital Expenditure (CapEx):** ○ Estimate costs for building infrastructure (e.g., roads, power lines, processing plants). ○ Include equipment procurement and installation expenses.
3. **Estimate Operating Expenditure (OpEx):** ○ Calculate recurring costs for mining, processing, maintenance, and administration. ○ Include transportation and logistics expenses.
4. **Project Closure and Reclamation Costs:** ○ Estimate costs for environmental reclamation, decommissioning infrastructure, and compliance with closure regulations.
5. **Forecast Revenues:** ○ Use projected production rates, ore grades, recovery rates, and commodity prices to estimate annual revenues.

6. **Calculate Lifecycle Costs:** ○ Sum all costs (CapEx, OpEx, and closure costs) over the project’s timeline. ○ Use the formula:
`Total Lifecycle Cost = CapEx + (OpEx x Mine Life) + Closure Costs`
7. **Determine Net Present Value (NPV):** ○ Discount future cash flows using the formula:
`NPV = Σ [(Revenue - OpEx) / (1 + Discount Rate)^Year] - CapEx - Closure`
Costs ○ A positive NPV indicates economic viability.
8. **Perform Sensitivity Analysis:** ○ Test project viability under different scenarios (e.g., changes in commodity prices, production rates, or cost estimates).
9. **Compare Against Alternatives:** ○ Benchmark the lifecycle costs and NPV against similar projects to assess competitiveness.


## 📤 Output format
- Lifecycle Cost Summary Table with breakdowns for CapEx, OpEx, and closure costs.
- Revenue and Cash Flow Projection Chart illustrating annual revenues and costs.
- Sensitivity Analysis Graph showing NPV under varying scenarios.
- Narrative Report summarizing findings, risks, and recommendations for decision-making.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Positive NPV:** Indicates the project is economically viable under current assumptions.
- **Negative NPV:** Suggests the project is not viable without cost reductions or revenue enhancements.
- **High Lifecycle Costs Relative to Revenues:** Points to potential inefficiencies or high-risk factors.
- **Stable Sensitivity Results:** Reflects resilience to market and operational uncertainties.


## 🚀 Steps to improve

1. Optimize mine design and production planning to minimize CapEx and OpEx.
2. Negotiate better terms with suppliers and contractors to reduce initial and recurring costs.
3. Leverage advanced technology to enhance operational efficiency and recovery rates.
4. Explore renewable energy sources or energy efficiency programs to reduce operating costs.
5. Identify opportunities for by-product recovery to increase revenues.
6. Perform phased project implementation to manage financial risks and optimize cash flows.
7. Regularly review and update cost and revenue projections to reflect market and operational changes.
