# 8 · Mortality and Longevity Risk Trend Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Actuarial and Reserve Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate trends in mortality and longevity risks to ensure the company’s pricing, reserves, and risk management strategies align with emerging demographic patterns. This analysis identifies shifts in life expectancy, mortality rates, and their implications on financial and actuarial assumptions.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Historical mortality and longevity data:** ○ Policyholder mortality data segmented by demographics (e.g., age, gender, location). ○ Mortality tables (industry-standard and internal).
2. Policy-level data, including coverage type, term duration, and payout amounts.
3. **Economic and healthcare data:** ○ Life expectancy trends (e.g., from government or industry reports). ○ Trends in healthcare advancements and disease prevalence.
4. Actuarial assumptions related to mortality and longevity.
5. Benchmark mortality and longevity trends from industry peers.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Aggregate Mortality Data ○ Collect historical policyholder mortality data. ○ Segment data by demographics (e.g., age, gender) and policy type to identify variations in mortality trends.
2. Analyze Longevity Trends ○ Compare historical life expectancy trends for policyholders against broader population data (e.g., national life expectancy reports). ○ Assess changes in longevity assumptions over time, factoring in medical advancements or lifestyle changes.
3. Compare Actual vs. Expected Mortality ○ Calculate the ratio of actual deaths to expected deaths using the formula:
`Mortality Ratio = (Actual Deaths / Expected Deaths) x 100 ○ Identify deviations across policy cohorts or demographic groups.`
4. Evaluate Mortality and Longevity Risk Factors ○ Assess the impact of external factors (e.g., pandemics, aging populations, medical innovations) on mortality and longevity trends.
5. **Review Actuarial Assumptions:** 23 ○ Compare current mortality and longevity assumptions to recent experience data. ○ Update mortality tables if trends indicate significant changes in risk exposure.
6. Conduct Scenario Analysis ○ Develop stress scenarios to test the financial impact of extreme changes in mortality (e.g., unexpected pandemics) or longevity (e.g., further life expectancy increases).
7. Benchmark Against Industry Trends ○ Compare company-specific mortality and longevity trends with industry benchmarks to identify potential gaps or opportunities.
8. Visualize Results ○ Create trend charts to show changes in mortality ratios over time. ○ Use heatmaps to highlight high-risk policy cohorts based on mortality deviations.


## 📤 Output format
- Mortality ratio tables by demographic segment and policy type.
- Longevity trend charts comparing policyholder life expectancy to national trends.
- Scenario analysis reports showing financial impact under various mortality and longevity assumptions.
- Key findings highlighting high-risk segments and assumptions requiring adjustment.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Increasing life expectancy trends may indicate greater longevity risk, requiring adjustments to reserves, premiums, or policy terms.
2. High mortality ratios in specific segments could signal underpricing of certain policies or inadequate underwriting practices.
3. Deviations from industry mortality and longevity benchmarks may point to unique company-specific risks or opportunities.
4. Stress test results provide insights into the company’s resilience to unexpected mortality or longevity shocks.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Update mortality and longevity assumptions regularly to reflect emerging trends and ensure pricing accuracy.
2. Enhance underwriting practices to account for changing mortality risks in high-risk segments.
