# 2 · Reserve Replacement Rate and Resource Conversion Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Resource and Reserve Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Metals & Mining Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to assess a mining company’s ability to replace depleted reserves through exploration, development, or acquisitions and to evaluate the effectiveness of converting resources into economically viable reserves. This ensures long-term sustainability of operations.


## 📊 Data required
- Annual production data (ore quantity and grade extracted).
- Reserve additions (through exploration, development, or acquisitions).
- Resource-to-reserve conversion data (historical and projected).
- Exploration expenditures and results.
- Resource classification data (Measured, Indicated, Inferred).
- Reserve and resource reporting standards (e.g., JORC, NI 43-101).


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Reserve Depletion:** ○ Determine the quantity of ore extracted in the analysis period (e.g., one year). ○ Record the corresponding decrease in reserve estimates.
2. **Track Reserve Additions:** ○ Quantify new reserves added through exploration, reclassification of resources, or acquisitions. ○ Separate data by source: greenfield exploration, brownfield expansion, or external purchases.
3. **Calculate Reserve Replacement Rate (RRR):** ○ Use the formula:
`RRR = (Reserves Added / Reserves Depleted) x 100 ○ An RRR above 100% indicates reserves are being replenished faster than they are depleted.`
4. **Analyze Resource Conversion Efficiency:** ○ Track the conversion of Measured and Indicated resources into reserves. ○ Calculate the Resource Conversion Rate using the formula:
`Resource Conversion Rate = (Reserves Added from Resource Conversion / Total Measured and Indicated Resources) x 100`
5. Evaluate Exploration Success:
9 ○ Compare exploration expenditures to reserves added to calculate the Discovery Cost:
`Discovery Cost = Exploration Expenditures / Reserves Added`
6. **Perform Trend Analysis:** ○ Analyze historical trends in RRR and Resource Conversion Rates to identify patterns and potential risks.
7. **Benchmark Against Industry Standards:** ○ Compare the company’s RRR and conversion efficiency with peer companies to gauge competitiveness.
8. **Conduct Sensitivity Analysis:** ○ Assess how changes in exploration budget, commodity prices, or geological factors impact reserve replacement and conversion efficiency.


## 📤 Output format
- Reserve Replacement Rate Table with annual depletion, additions, and calculated RRR.
- Resource Conversion Efficiency Chart showing the proportion of resources converted to reserves over time.
- Discovery Cost Summary highlighting exploration spending efficiency.
- Trend Graphs for RRR and conversion rates across multiple years.
- Narrative Report summarizing findings, risks, and recommendations.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High RRR (>100%):** Indicates successful reserve replenishment, ensuring sustainability.
- **Low RRR (<100%):** Suggests the company may face future production constraints without intervention.
- **High Conversion Rate:** Reflects effective exploration and resource development practices.
- **High Discovery Costs:** May indicate inefficiencies in exploration or unfavorable geological conditions.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Prioritize high-potential exploration targets using advanced geophysical and geochemical techniques.
2. Increase investment in brownfield projects near existing operations for lower-risk reserve additions.
