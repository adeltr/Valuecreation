# 9 · In‐Room Amenities Cost‐Benefit Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Revenue & Pricing Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Weigh the costs of providing in-room amenities against their impact on guest satisfaction and room revenue, ensuring a profitable and appealing amenity selection.


## 📊 Data required
- List of all in‐room amenities provided, with associated costs (purchase, restocking, maintenance).
- Usage data or estimates (e.g., average consumption of toiletries, mini‐bar usage).
- Guest satisfaction or feedback ratings regarding in‐room amenities.
- Room rates or relevant operational revenue figures for comparison.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Collect data on in‐room amenities usage. Gather historical usage rates, restock frequency, and any waste or breakage data.
2. Calculate total cost per amenity. Include procurement costs, restocking, and any related labor (e.g., housekeeping hours).
3. Estimate revenue contribution or value enhancement. If certain amenities are tied to premium room categories or rate premiums, factor that in.
4. Review guest satisfaction scores or feedback. Identify which amenities guests explicitly mention as value‐adds vs. those rarely noted.
5. Determine cost‐to‐benefit ratios. Compare the total cost of each amenity to its perceived or actual revenue/satisfaction contribution.
6. Identify potential changes in amenity offerings. Look for low‐usage items with high costs, or items that significantly boost guest satisfaction at minimal expense.


## 📤 Output format
- A table listing each in‐room amenity, cost per occupied room, usage frequency, and any associated incremental revenue or satisfaction metric.
- Charts or graphs illustrating the cost‐benefit ratio of each amenity.
- Written commentary highlighting top‐performing and underperforming amenities.

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High‐cost amenities that show minimal usage and low impact on satisfaction may warrant removal or cost reduction strategies.
- Amenities that contribute positively to guest satisfaction and justify a higher room rate can improve overall profitability and loyalty.
- Balancing guest expectations with operational costs is key to maximizing net benefit.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Conduct regular reviews of amenity offerings, removing or replacing underutilized items.
2. Negotiate supplier contracts or bulk purchasing agreements to lower procurement costs.
3. Consider “by request” or “on‐demand” models for low‐usage amenities to reduce waste.
4. Communicate the value of upgraded amenities (e.g., premium coffee, eco‐friendly toiletries) to enhance perceived quality and support premium pricing.
