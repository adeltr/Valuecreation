# 25 · Co-packing and Contract Manufacturing Evaluation

> **Part:** [[C - Operations]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Co-packing and Contract Manufacturing Evaluation in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to assess the effectiveness, cost efficiency, and reliability of external manufacturing and packaging partners. Co-packing (third-party packaging) and contract manufacturing (outsourced production) allow CPG companies to expand production capacity, reduce operational complexity, and increase flexibility. This analysis helps CPG companies evaluate whether their co-packing and contract manufacturing arrangements are delivering on key performance metrics such as cost, quality, service levels, and scalability.


## 📊 Data required
1. Cost data for co-packing and contract manufacturing agreements (e.g., production costs, packaging costs, labor costs)
2. Production lead times and reliability of co-packing and contract manufacturing partners
3. Product quality metrics, including defect rates, consistency, and compliance with specifications
4. Service level performance, including on-time delivery rates and fulfillment accuracy
5. Capacity utilization data for co-packers and contract manufacturers
6. Supply chain and logistics data (e.g., shipping times, handling costs, location of co-packing/contract manufacturing facilities)
7. Contract terms and agreements, including pricing, volume commitments, and service level agreements (SLAs)
8. Internal versus outsourced production cost comparisons (e.g., in-house manufacturing costs vs. co-packing/contract manufacturing)

9. Supplier performance data, including responsiveness, flexibility, and issue resolution
10. Competitor benchmarking data on the use of co-packing and contract manufacturing


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the objectives of the evaluation:** Establish clear goals for the evaluation of co-packing and contract manufacturing partners. Objectives may include reducing production costs, increasing flexibility and scalability, improving product quality, and ensuring reliable service levels. For example, a goal could be to lower production costs by 10% while maintaining or improving product quality and delivery times.
2. **Gather cost data for co-packing and contract manufacturing:** Collect detailed cost data for all co-packing and contract manufacturing activities, including production, labor, and packaging costs. Compare these costs to in-house production costs (if applicable) to assess the financial viability of outsourcing. The formula for cost savings from outsourcing is:
`Cost Savings (%) = ((In-house Costs - Outsourced Costs) / In-house Costs) x 100 For example, if in-house production costs $500,000 and outsourcing costs $400,000: Cost Savings = (($500,000 - $400,000) / $500,000) x 100 = 20%`
3. **Assess production lead times and reliability:** Evaluate the production lead times and reliability of co-packers and contract manufacturers. Long or inconsistent lead times can disrupt supply chain operations and lead to stockouts or missed deadlines. Track on-time delivery rates and lead time variability for each partner. The formula for on-time delivery rate is:
`On-time Delivery Rate (%) = (Number of On-time Deliveries / Total Number of`
146 Deliveries) x 100 For example, if 95 out of 100 deliveries were on time:
`On-time Delivery Rate = (95 / 100) x 100 = 95% A high on-time delivery rate indicates reliable service from the co-packing or contract manufacturing partner.`
4. **Evaluate product quality and consistency:** Review product quality metrics, including defect rates, consistency, and compliance with product specifications. High defect rates or quality issues can damage brand reputation and lead to costly recalls or rework. Calculate defect rates using the formula:
`Defect Rate (%) = (Number of Defective Units / Total Units Produced) x 100 For example, if 500 units out of 50,000 produced were defective: Defect Rate = (500 / 50,000) x 100 = 1% Consistency in product quality across batches is critical to maintaining consumer trust and brand integrity.`
5. **Analyze capacity utilization:** Determine the capacity utilization of your co-packing and contract manufacturing partners. Capacity utilization measures how efficiently a co-packer or manufacturer uses its available resources to meet production demands. If a partner’s capacity is underutilized, it may result in higher per-unit costs or delays in scaling production. The formula for capacity utilization is:
`Capacity Utilization (%) = (Actual Output / Maximum Output Capacity) x 100 For example, if a co-packer produces 80,000 units but has the capacity to produce 100,000 units: Capacity Utilization = (80,000 / 100,000) x 100 = 80% High capacity utilization indicates efficient use of production resources, while low utilization may signal inefficiency.`
6. **Compare internal vs. outsourced production costs:** Analyze the total costs of outsourcing production versus producing in-house. This should include a comparison of labor, materials, overhead, and logistics costs for both options.
147 Outsourcing may offer cost savings in terms of reduced overhead and flexibility, but in-house production can provide greater control over quality and lead times.
7. **Review service level agreements (SLAs) and contract terms:** Assess the service level agreements (SLAs) and contractual terms with your co-packing and contract manufacturing partners. SLAs typically define key performance metrics such as on-time delivery, product quality, and volume commitments. Ensure that your partners are meeting these SLAs consistently. If there are gaps in performance, consider renegotiating the terms or switching partners.
8. **Evaluate supplier responsiveness and issue resolution:** Track how well co-packing and contract manufacturing partners respond to issues such as production delays, quality problems, or changes in demand. Timely communication and responsiveness are critical for resolving issues and minimizing disruptions to the supply chain.
9. **Analyze supply chain and logistics costs:** Review supply chain and logistics costs associated with co-packing and contract manufacturing, including shipping times and transportation costs. Location is key—partners that are closer to your distribution centers or key markets may offer cost savings through reduced shipping times and costs.
10. **Benchmark against competitors:** Compare your co-packing and contract manufacturing strategies with industry benchmarks and competitor practices. This can provide insight into whether your current partners are competitive in terms of cost, quality, and flexibility. If competitors are using more efficient or cost-effective partners, it may be time to reevaluate your own arrangements.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Co-packing and Contract Manufacturing Evaluation typically includes:

- A breakdown of production, packaging, and labor costs for each co-packing and contract manufacturing partner.
- On-time delivery rates and lead time performance for each partner.
- Product quality metrics, including defect rates and consistency data.
- Capacity utilization analysis for co-packers and manufacturers, identifying over- or underutilized capacity.
- Comparison of internal versus outsourced production costs, including labor, materials, and overhead.

- Recommendations for optimizing outsourcing relationships, renegotiating contracts, or switching partners.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High cost savings with reliable quality and delivery:** If outsourcing to co-packers or contract manufacturers provides significant cost savings without sacrificing quality or lead times, these partnerships are likely valuable. Continue to work with these partners while exploring opportunities for further optimization.
- **High defect rates or inconsistent quality:** If product quality is frequently compromised, it may be necessary to renegotiate terms with the partner, increase quality control measures, or switch to a more reliable supplier. Poor quality can lead to brand damage and higher costs in the long run.
149
- **Low capacity utilization with high costs:** If co-packing or manufacturing partners are operating at low capacity but incurring high costs, there may be inefficiencies in the production process. Consider consolidating production with higher-capacity partners or adjusting order volumes to better match the partner’s capabilities.
- **Delays and missed delivery deadlines:** If partners consistently fail to meet on-time delivery expectations, it can lead to stockouts and supply chain disruptions. In this case, consider renegotiating SLAs, investing in better logistics, or finding alternative partners.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize partner selection:** Regularly evaluate and compare co-packing and contract manufacturing partners based on cost, quality, lead times, and service levels. Maintain relationships with top-performing partners while seeking alternatives for underperformers.
2. **Increase quality control measures:** Work closely with partners to ensure that they meet product quality standards. Implement quality control checks and audits to identify issues early and avoid large-scale defects or recalls.
3. **Negotiate better contract terms:** Review SLAs and contract terms regularly to ensure that partners are meeting performance expectations. Renegotiate terms as needed to reflect changes in production volume, quality standards, or lead time requirements.
4. **Improve supply chain efficiency:** Reduce logistics costs by working with co-packers and manufacturers located closer to distribution centers or key markets. This can shorten shipping times and reduce transportation expenses.
5. **Leverage data for continuous improvement:** Continuously track and analyze key metrics such as costs, defect rates, capacity utilization, and delivery times.
