# 38 · Distribution Channel Performance Analysis

> **Part:** [[E - Operations]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Distribution Channel Performance Analysis is to evaluate the effectiveness and profitability of each distribution channel (e.g., brokers, direct sales, online platforms) used to acquire policies. This analysis helps identify high-performing channels, assess cost efficiency, and support decisions on resource allocation, optimizing the company’s overall distribution strategy.


## 📊 Data required
1. Policies sold by each distribution channel
2. Premiums earned by channel
3. Acquisition costs for each channel (e.g., commissions, advertising expenses)
4. Claims data segmented by channel
5. Policy retention rates by channel
6. Customer demographics by channel (optional, for deeper insights)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Policy Count and Premiums by Channel:** Determine the total number of policies sold and premiums earned for each distribution channel:
`Premiums per Channel = Sum of Premiums for All Policies in Channel`
This indicates the volume of business each channel contributes.
2. **Calculate Acquisition Cost per Channel:** Assess acquisition costs, such as commissions and marketing expenses, associated with each channel. Calculate the acquisition cost ratio:
`Acquisition Cost Ratio (%) = (Total Acquisition Cost for Channel / Premiums for Channel) x 100`
This shows how much it costs to acquire each dollar of premium through a given channel.
3. **Calculate Loss Ratio by Channel:** Determine claims costs as a percentage of premiums for each channel to assess claims experience and profitability:
`Loss Ratio (%) = (Claims Paid in Channel / Premiums for Channel) x 100`
A lower loss ratio indicates better claims performance.
4. **Calculate Policy Retention Rate by Channel:** Evaluate the retention rate for each channel, which measures customer loyalty and stability:
`Retention Rate (%) = (Renewed Policies in Channel / Total Policies in Channel) x 100`
Higher retention rates suggest stronger customer loyalty within a channel.
5. **Assess Combined Ratio for Overall Channel Profitability:** Calculate the combined ratio, which incorporates both claims costs and acquisition expenses:
`Combined Ratio (%) = Loss Ratio + Acquisition Cost Ratio`
A combined ratio below 100% indicates profitability for the channel, while a ratio above 100% suggests potential losses.
6. Analyze Customer Demographics and Preferences by Channel (Optional)
Segment customers by demographics within each channel to understand which channels attract specific customer segments, supporting targeted marketing and resource allocation.
7. **Compile Results:** Summarize the findings in a table showing key performance indicators (KPIs) like premiums, acquisition cost ratio, loss ratio, retention rate, and combined ratio for each channel.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Distribution Channel (e.g., broker, direct, online)
- Premiums Earned ($)
- Acquisition Cost Ratio (%)
- Loss Ratio (%)
- Retention Rate (%)
- Combined Ratio (%)
A bar chart comparing combined ratios or a pie chart showing premium distribution by channel can help visualize channel performance.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **Low Acquisition Cost Ratio:** Indicates cost-effective customer acquisition, which is especially desirable in high-volume channels.
- **Low Loss Ratio:** Suggests favorable claims experience, making the channel more profitable.
- **High Retention Rate:** Implies strong customer loyalty, which is beneficial for sustainable long-term profitability.
- **Combined Ratio Below 100%:** Indicates profitable operations for the channel, as premiums cover both acquisition and claims costs.
Benchmarking results against industry standards or historical performance can provide further insights into each channel’s relative effectiveness.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Allocate Resources to High-Performing Channels Focus marketing and sales efforts on channels with low combined ratios and high retention rates, maximizing return on investment.
2. Optimize Acquisition Costs in High-Cost Channels Review commission structures, marketing expenses, or operational processes to reduce acquisition costs in channels with high acquisition cost ratios.
3. Enhance Retention Strategies for Low-Retention Channels Implement targeted retention efforts, such as personalized customer outreach or loyalty programs, in channels with lower retention rates.
4. Adjust Underwriting Standards for High-Loss Channels Tighten underwriting criteria or pricing in channels with high loss ratios to better align premiums with risk.
5. Utilize Data to Personalize Channel Marketing Use customer demographic data to tailor marketing strategies for each channel, improving customer targeting and acquisition efficiency.
6. Encourage Digital Transformation for Cost Efficiency Invest in digital or online channels, which often have lower acquisition costs and appeal to younger, tech-savvy customers.
7. Benchmark Channel Performance Regularly Conduct regular performance analyses to adapt strategies as market dynamics and customer preferences change, ensuring the distribution strategy remains efficient and effective.
