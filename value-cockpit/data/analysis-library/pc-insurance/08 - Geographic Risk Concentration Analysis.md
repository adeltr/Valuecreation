# 8 · Geographic Risk Concentration Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Underwriting]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Geographic Risk Concentration Analysis is to evaluate the distribution of policies and associated risks across different geographic regions. This analysis helps assess the company’s exposure to regional risks, such as natural disasters, economic downturns, or regulatory changes, and informs decisions about diversification to reduce vulnerability.


## 📊 Data required
1. Number of policies by geographic region (e.g., state, county, or city)
2. Total insured value or policy exposure in each region
3. Claims data by geographic region (optional, for a deeper risk assessment)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define Geographic Regions:** Choose the level of granularity for the regions (e.g., by state, county, city) based on data availability and business needs.
2. **Calculate Total Exposure by Region:** Sum the total insured value or exposure amount in each region. This is the aggregate value of all policies within that region.
3. **Calculate the Percentage of Total Exposure per Region:** For each region, calculate the exposure concentration as a percentage of the company’s total exposure:
`Exposure Percentage per Region = (Total Exposure in Region / Total Exposure Across All Regions) x 100`
4. **Identify High-Concentration Regions:** Identify regions with a high percentage of total exposure, which may indicate potential over-concentration and increased vulnerability to region-specific risks.
5. Analyze Claims Data (Optional)
If available, examine claims data by region to identify high-risk areas with frequent or severe claims, adding another layer to risk assessment.
6. **Compile Results:** Organize the findings in a table or chart to visualize exposure distribution across regions and identify areas with significant concentration.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table showing:
- Region (e.g., state, county)
- Total Exposure ($)
- Exposure Percentage (% of total exposure)
A heatmap can also be useful to visualize geographic concentration, with regions shaded according to their exposure levels.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High-Concentration Regions:** Regions with a high percentage of total exposure indicate a concentrated risk. If these regions are prone to specific hazards (e.g., hurricanes, earthquakes), the company may face significant losses in adverse events.
- **Low-Concentration Regions:** These regions have limited impact on overall risk. However, they may present growth opportunities for geographic diversification.
Benchmarking against industry standards or comparing concentration over time can help assess if the geographic concentration is increasing or well-balanced.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Increase Geographic Diversification Focus on expanding business in underrepresented or lower-risk regions to distribute exposure more evenly.
2. Utilize Reinsurance Consider reinsurance options to mitigate risks in high-concentration areas, particularly those prone to natural disasters.
3. Adjust Underwriting Standards by Region Strengthen underwriting criteria in high-risk areas or set higher premiums to account for region-specific risks.
4. Implement Regional Risk Mitigation Programs Work with policyholders in high-concentration regions to implement risk reduction strategies, such as flood protection or fire prevention measures.
5. Review Geographic Risk Concentration Periodically Regularly conduct this analysis to monitor changes in geographic exposure and adjust strategies as needed.
6. Incorporate Climate and Environmental Data Use external data on climate risk, economic stability, or regulatory trends to enhance understanding of regional risks.
