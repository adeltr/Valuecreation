# 2 · Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) Growth Rate

> **Part:** [[A - Finance & Revenue]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) Growth Rate analysis is to measure the percentage increase (or decrease) in MRR over a period of time. MRR represents the predictable and recurring revenue that a SaaS company generates from its customers each month. Understanding the MRR Growth Rate helps a SaaS company track its revenue expansion, customer acquisition, retention, and overall business health.


## 📊 Data required
1. MRR at the start of the month
2. MRR at the end of the month
3. MRR from new customers acquired during the month
4. MRR lost from churned customers
5. MRR expansion from existing customers (e.g., upgrades or cross-sells)
6. MRR contraction from existing customers (e.g., downgrades)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Determine the MRR at the start and end of the month:
o    Starting MRR: The MRR at the beginning of the month, including recurring subscription fees from all customers.
o    Ending MRR: The MRR at the end of the month, after factoring in new customers, churned customers, upgrades, and downgrades.
2. **Calculate MRR from new customers:** Identify the MRR added during the month from newly acquired customers. This value should only include revenue from customers who signed up within the month.
3. **Calculate MRR lost from churned customers:** Identify the MRR lost from customers who canceled their subscriptions (churn) during the month. This value reﬂects the negative impact on recurring revenue.
4. **Calculate MRR expansion:** Identify the MRR gained from existing customers who upgraded their subscriptions, purchased additional services, or expanded their usage within the month.
8
5. **Calculate MRR contraction:** Identify the MRR lost from existing customers who downgraded their subscriptions, reducing the amount they are paying monthly.
6. **Calculate the Net MRR Growth Rate:** Use the following formula to calculate the Net MRR Growth Rate:
`MRR Growth Rate (%) = [(Ending MRR - Starting MRR) / Starting MRR] x 100`
For example, if your MRR at the start of the month was $100,000, and by the end of the month, your MRR increased to $110,000, your growth rate would be:
`MRR Growth Rate (%) = [($110,000 - $100,000) / $100,000] x 100 = 10%`


## 📤 Output format
The output is typically expressed as a percentage increase (or decrease) in MRR from one month to the next. In the example above, the output would be:
Monthly Recurring Revenue Growth Rate: 10%


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   Positive MRR Growth Rate: A positive MRR Growth Rate indicates that the company is acquiring new customers or expanding revenue from existing customers faster than it is losing revenue through churn or downgrades. A strong growth rate is a healthy indicator of business expansion.
•   Negative MRR Growth Rate: A negative MRR Growth Rate suggests that the company is losing revenue, either through churn, downgrades, or insufficient customer acquisition. This could signal issues in customer retention, product-market ﬁt, or ineffective sales efforts.
The MRR Growth Rate is a leading indicator of the company’s overall ﬁnancial health, as it reﬂects the steady, predictable revenue stream that fuels the business’s growth.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Increase customer acquisition eKorts:** Boost marketing and sales initiatives to acquire more new customers, which will directly increase MRR. Focus on targeted campaigns that attract higher-value customers.
2. **Improve customer retention:** Reduce churn by improving customer support, product quality, and customer success strategies. Engaging customers early and frequently helps ensure satisfaction and reduces the likelihood of cancellations.
9
3. **Encourage upsells and cross-sells:** Drive MRR expansion by offering additional products or features to existing customers. Implementing a well-designed upgrade path or encouraging customers to move to higher- tier plans can increase MRR.
4. **Reduce customer downgrades:** Analyze why customers are downgrading and address underlying issues, such as product complexity or price sensitivity. Offering ﬂexible pricing plans or product conﬁgurations may reduce contraction.
5. **Monitor customer feedback:** Regularly collect feedback from customers to understand their pain points and opportunities for improving the product. A better product can increase customer satisfaction and drive upsells.
