# 32 · Layered Reinsurance Utilization Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Risk Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Layered Reinsurance Utilization Analysis is to assess the effectiveness of the company’s layered reinsurance structure in managing different levels of risk exposure. This analysis evaluates how efficiently each layer of reinsurance coverage (e.g., primary, excess, catastrophe) is utilized, identifying opportunities to optimize reinsurance costs while maintaining adequate protection against losses.


## 📊 Data required
1. Reinsurance structure details, including layers, limits, and retention levels
2. Claims data segmented by layers, showing how often each layer is utilized
3. Reinsurance premiums paid for each layer
4. Reinsurance recoveries by layer
5. Exposure data by product line or region (optional, for refined insights)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Identify Reinsurance Layers and Coverage Limits:** Define the reinsurance layers in place (e.g., primary, excess, catastrophe) along with retention and limit amounts for each layer. This provides a framework for assessing how claims are distributed across layers.
2. **Segment Claims by Reinsurance Layer:** Organize claims data by reinsurance layer to determine the frequency and amount of claims falling within each layer’s coverage range.
3. **Calculate Utilization Rate of Each Layer:** For each layer, calculate the utilization rate, indicating how often it was triggered:
`Utilization Rate = (Number of Claims Triggering Layer / Total Claims) x 100`
This helps assess if each layer is appropriately aligned with the company’s claims experience.
4. **Calculate Reinsurance Recovery Ratio for Each Layer:** Assess the cost-effectiveness of each layer by calculating the recovery ratio:
`Reinsurance Recovery Ratio (%) = (Total Recoveries from Layer / Reinsurance Premium Paid for Layer) x 100`
A recovery ratio over 100% indicates that the layer is cost-effective, as recoveries exceed premiums paid.
5. Analyze Layer Effectiveness by Product Line or Region (Optional)
If applicable, further segment the utilization and recovery analysis by product line or region. This helps pinpoint specific areas where reinsurance layers are under- or over-utilized.
6. **Assess Layer Gaps or Redundancies:** Identify any layers that are consistently underutilized or over-utilized, suggesting potential gaps in coverage or unnecessary reinsurance costs. Analyze if any adjustments (e.g., raising limits, adding layers) could improve cost-efficiency or coverage alignment.
7. **Compile Results:** Summarize findings in a table showing reinsurance utilization, recovery ratios, and any identified gaps or redundancies by layer.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Reinsurance Layer (e.g., primary, excess, catastrophe)
- Retention Limit ($)
- Coverage Limit ($)
- Utilization Rate (%)
- Reinsurance Recovery Ratio (%)
- Suggested Adjustments (if applicable)
A line or bar chart illustrating utilization and recovery ratios by layer can provide additional insights into reinsurance effectiveness.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Utilization Rate and Recovery Ratio:** Indicates that the layer is frequently used and cost-effective, suggesting appropriate alignment with claims experience.
- **Low Utilization Rate but High Recovery Ratio:** Implies that the layer is seldom used but still financially efficient when triggered, typically suitable for catastrophe layers.
- **High Utilization with Low Recovery Ratio:** May indicate a layer is over-utilized relative to its cost, suggesting a need for restructuring or adjusting retention and limits.
Benchmarking utilization and recovery ratios against industry standards can provide additional context for determining the effectiveness of the layered structure.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Adjust Retention Levels to Optimize Layer Efficiency Increase or decrease retention levels based on claims frequency for each layer to enhance cost-efficiency and ensure layers align with actual risk exposure.
2. Consider Adding or Removing Layers If gaps or redundancies are identified, explore the feasibility of adding new layers (e.g., an additional catastrophe layer) or removing underutilized ones to improve alignment.
3. Refine Product Line or Regional Reinsurance Allocations Tailor reinsurance allocations for high-risk regions or product lines with unique risk characteristics to maximize the effectiveness of each layer.
4. Negotiate Reinsurance Terms for Cost Efficiency Work with reinsurers to negotiate terms, especially for layers with low recovery ratios, to optimize premium costs relative to anticipated recoveries.
5. Regularly Review and Adjust Reinsurance Structure Conduct periodic reviews of the layered structure and claims data to ensure reinsurance remains aligned with evolving risk profiles and market conditions.
6. Monitor Emerging Risks to Update Layer Requirements Stay informed on new risk trends (e.g., climate risk) that could affect claims patterns, allowing for proactive adjustments to layer limits and reinsurance strategies.
7. Use Catastrophe Models for Improved Layer Planning Integrate catastrophe models to simulate potential large-loss events and adjust reinsurance layering to manage catastrophic risks more effectively.
