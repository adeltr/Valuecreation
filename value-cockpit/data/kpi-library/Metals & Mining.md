# KPI — Metals & Mining

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Energy & Natural Resources]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Metals & Mining Company - Analyse]]

49 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Average Realized Price (ARP) | This is the average price per unit (such as ounce for gold, pound for copper) that the company sold its product for. This helps to understand the financial realization of the company compared to the spot or benchmark prices. |
| Blast Efficiency | For mining operations that require blasting, this measures the volume of rock broken per unit of explosive used. Higher efficiency can lead to lower operational costs. |
| Break-Even Stripping Ratio | This is the stripping ratio at which a mining operation becomes economically viable. It's important for understanding the profitability threshold of a mining project. |
| Carbon Emissions per Ton of Ore | This is a sustainability KPI measuring the amount of carbon dioxide released for each ton of ore extracted. Lower emissions per ton represent more environmentally friendly operations. |
| Cash Cost or All-In Sustaining Cost (AISC) | These are measures of direct production costs per unit of production. AISC includes costs associated with sustaining current production levels for the long term. These are crucial metrics for understanding the profitability of mining operations. |
| Commodity Price Sensitivity | This measures the potential impact of changes in commodity prices on the company's profitability. |
| Compliance Costs | Costs associated with meeting environmental, safety, and other regulations. These costs can have a significant impact on the profitability of mining companies. |
| Concentration Ratio | Used in the concentration process of mineral extraction, this ratio gives the amount of the desired mineral in the concentrate to the amount in the original ore. |
| Cost per Blast | For mining operations that involve blasting, this KPI measures the cost incurred for each blast. It can help determine the efficiency and cost-effectiveness of the blasting operation. |
| Cost per ton | This is a common measure in the mining industry and can reflect the total costs associated with extracting and processing each ton of ore. |
| Crushing Efficiency | The efficiency of the crushing process can be measured by the amount of ore crushed per unit of time or energy used. Higher efficiency can lead to lower operational costs. |
| Decommissioning and Restoration Provisions | These are the funds set aside or costs estimated for the restoration of mining sites after operations are completed. |
| Dilution Rate | The percentage of non-ore material present in the ore that is mined. Lower dilution rates are preferred as they indicate higher-quality mining. |
| Drilling Success Rate | This measures the percentage of drilling activities that resulted in the discovery of mineral deposits. A higher rate would indicate more efficient and successful exploration. |
| Environmental Impact Metrics | These could include CO2 emissions per ton of ore processed, water usage per ton of ore processed, or land disturbed per ton of ore extracted. As environmental concerns become increasingly important to investors, these metrics can provide insight into a company's environmental stewardship and potential liability exposure. |
| Exploration Success Ratio | This is the proportion of exploration attempts that result in the discovery of commercially viable deposits. A higher ratio indicates more effective exploration strategies. |
| Froth Flotation Efficiency | Specific to the processing of certain types of ores, this measures the efficiency of separating valuable minerals from the ore using froth flotation techniques. |
| Grade Quality | This refers to the concentration of the desired mineral within the ore. Higher-grade ores can often be mined and processed more economically. |
| Heap Leach Pad Size | For mines using heap leaching, the size of the leach pad can give an idea about the scale of operations and potential production volumes. |
| Land Rehabilitation Percentage | Post-mining, this is the percentage of land successfully rehabilitated or restored for other uses. It's a critical measure of a mining company's environmental responsibility and future reclamation liabilities. |
| Lateral Development Rate | In underground mining, this measures the speed of creating horizontal passages (drifts, levels, ramps) for access, ventilation, or transportation purposes. Faster development can lead to quicker access to reserves. |
| Leach Recovery Rate | In heap leaching (common for gold and copper), this measures the proportion of valuable metal extracted from the ore during the leaching process. |
| Load and Haul Efficiency | This measures the amount of material moved (loaded and transported) per unit of time or resources, indicating the efficiency of the excavation process. |
| Metallurgical Recovery Rates | Specific to certain types of mining, this measures the percentage of valuable metal that can be physically separated and recovered from the ore during processing. |
| Mill Throughput | This is the amount of material passed through the mill (where the ore is processed) in a given period. It's a measure of the processing capacity and efficiency of a mining operation. |
| Mine Life | The estimated number of years a mine can continue operations at the current rate of extraction. |
| Mine Safety – Accident / Injury Rate | This is a measure of safety in the company's operations. It is calculated as the number of accidents or injuries per number of hours worked. A lower rate is preferable and reflects better on the company's commitment to safety. |
| Mineral Exploration Success Rate | The number of exploration projects that successfully identify economically viable mineral deposits as a percentage of total exploration projects. |
| Mineral Reserve Life Index (MRLI) | This is calculated by dividing the total reserves by the annual production. This KPI gives an indication of how long the mine can operate at the current production rate without further exploration. |
| Minerals Extraction Efficiency (MEE) | This measures the percentage of desired minerals successfully extracted from the ore during the beneficiation process. Higher MEE signifies more efficient and profitable operations. |
| Mining Fleet Efficiency | The operational efficiency of the mining fleet, which could be measured in terms of fuel efficiency, payload efficiency, or uptime/downtime. |
| Mining Process Efficiency | This might be measured by ore processed per man-hour, production volume per energy used, or other similar ratios reflecting the efficiency of the mining operation. |
| Noise and Vibrations Levels | Measuring the levels of noise and vibrations at mining sites can give an indication of the potential impact on local communities and environments. |
| Off-Grade Material Ratio | This is the ratio of material that doesn't meet the minimum grade requirements to the total material extracted. A lower ratio implies more efficient operations and better resource utilization. |
| Ore Milled | This refers to the amount of ore processed in a period. It helps to gauge the operational capacity of the company's processing facilities. |
| Ore Reserve Replacement Ratio (ORRR) | This ratio measures the amount of discovered mineral reserves to the amount of mineral depletion during a given period. A high ORRR indicates a company is adding reserves at a faster pace than it is depleting them. This is a critical measure of the longevity of a company's operations. |
| Ore to Waste Ratio | This ratio gives the amount of waste generated for every unit of ore extracted. A lower ratio is preferable from both cost and environmental perspectives. |
| Percentage of Recycled Metal | For mining companies that also engage in recycling, this measures the percentage of total output that comes from recycled materials. It's a sustainability indicator and could be a source of cost savings. |
| Rate of Drilling | In exploration phases, the speed at which drilling is completed can be an important measure of efficiency. |
| Ratio of Reserves to Production (R/P ratio) | This represents the number of years a mine can continue to operate at its current production rate using its proven reserves only. |
| Reclamation Costs per Ton | This refers to the cost associated with restoring a site after mining is completed. It's calculated on a per-ton basis and is relevant to a company's long-term environmental liabilities. |
| Recovery Rate | This is the percentage of valuable metal in the ore that is recovered in the processing of the ore. A higher recovery rate indicates greater efficiency in the processing operations. |
| Royalty Rate | The percentage of revenue or profit paid to the land or mineral rights owner. High royalty rates can impact profitability. |
| Smelter Availability | For companies with smelting operations, this measures the percentage of time a smelter is operational versus total time. High availability means less downtime and higher productivity. |
| Smelting and Refining Yield | For companies involved in these downstream processes, the yield represents the percentage of finished metal obtained from the input feed. |
| Stripping Ratio | This is the ratio of the volume of overburden (or waste material) required to be handled to extract a ton of ore. The lower the ratio, the less waste material needs to be moved, hence, the operation is more efficient. |
| Tailings Produced | Tailings are the waste material left over after mining and processing. Tracking the amount of tailings produced can shed light on environmental impact and potential future liabilities. |
| Unit Cost of Production (UCP) | Calculated by dividing the total production cost by the total units produced. It helps analysts understand how efficiently the company is using its resources to produce each unit of output. |
| Water Quality Index | This measures the quality of water bodies around the mining site before and after mining operations. It's important for evaluating the environmental impact of mining activities. |
