# 37 · In-Network vs. Out-of-Network Utilization Rate Analysis

> **Part:** [[G - Access to Care and Integration]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Health Payor]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the utilization rates of in-network versus out-of-network providers, identifying trends and factors that lead members to seek out-of-network care. This analysis aims to improve network adequacy, reduce out-of-network costs, and enhance member satisfaction by ensuring adequate access to preferred providers within the network.


## 📊 Data required
1. Claims data segmented by in-network and out-of-network utilization, including service type (e.g., primary care, specialty, emergency) and associated costs
2. Member demographics, including age, geographic location, and insurance plan type, to analyze utilization patterns across different groups
3. Network adequacy data, including provider availability and wait times for in-network services by specialty and region
4. Member feedback on reasons for out-of-network utilization, such as limited access, provider preference, or emergency situations
5. Benchmark data on in-network vs. out-of-network utilization rates within similar health plans


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate In-Network and Out-of-Network Utilization Rates:** Determine the percentage of claims attributed to in-network and out-of-network services, segmented by service type (e.g., primary care, specialty care, emergency).
2. **Analyze Costs Associated with Out-of-Network Care:** Calculate the average cost per claim for out-of-network services compared to in-network services to assess the financial impact of out-of-network utilization.
3. **Segment Data by Demographics and Location:** Analyze utilization patterns by member demographics, geographic region, and insurance plan type to identify groups with higher out-of-network usage rates.
4. **Assess Network Adequacy:** Review network adequacy data, including provider availability, wait times, and geographic distribution of in-network providers, to determine if limited access is driving out-of-network utilization.
5. **Gather Member Feedback on Out-of-Network Utilization:** Collect feedback from members regarding their reasons for using out-of-network services, focusing on barriers to in-network access or specific provider preferences.

6. **Benchmark Utilization Against Industry Standards:** Compare in-network and out-of-network utilization rates with industry benchmarks to identify any deviations from standard practice.
7. **Identify Key Drivers of Out-of-Network Utilization:** Based on findings, determine the primary reasons for out-of-network utilization, such as limited access to certain specialties, geographic gaps, or member preference for specific providers.


## 📤 Output format
- Summary table showing in-network and out-of-network utilization rates, costs, and utilization by service type and demographic
- Graphs illustrating trends in in-network vs. out-of-network utilization over time and by geographic region
- Benchmark comparison chart for in-network and out-of-network utilization rates
- Summary report with key findings, reasons for out-of-network usage, and recommendations for enhancing network adequacy


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Out-of-Network Utilization with High Costs:** Indicates a potential gap in network adequacy or member satisfaction, suggesting a need for network expansion or improved provider access.
- **Low Out-of-Network Utilization with Member Satisfaction:** Suggests that the network meets member needs effectively, supporting cost containment and satisfaction.
- **Regional or Demographic Discrepancies:** Variations in out-of-network usage across different regions or demographic groups may indicate access issues or specific provider preferences that should be addressed.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Expand Provider Network in High-Utilization Areas:** Add in-network providers in regions or specialties with high out-of-network usage to improve member access and reduce out-of-network costs.
2. **Enhance Member Education on In-Network Options:** Provide members with clear, accessible information on in-network providers and help them find providers that meet their preferences, especially for common specialties.
3. **Implement Cost-Sharing Incentives:** Encourage members to use in-network services by adjusting cost-sharing structures, such as lower copays or deductibles for in-network providers.

4. **Regularly Monitor Network Adequacy:** Continuously evaluate network adequacy metrics, including geographic coverage, provider availability, and wait times, to proactively address potential access gaps.
5. **Streamline Referral and Authorization Processes:** Simplify referral processes to make in-network care more accessible, especially for specialties where members are frequently going out-of-network.
