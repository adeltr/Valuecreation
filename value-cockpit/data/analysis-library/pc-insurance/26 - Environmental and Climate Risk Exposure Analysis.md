# 26 · Environmental and Climate Risk Exposure Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Risk Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Environmental and Climate Risk Exposure Analysis is to assess the company’s exposure to environmental and climate-related risks, such as floods, wildfires, hurricanes, and rising sea levels. This analysis helps identify regions and policy types vulnerable to climate risks, supporting risk mitigation strategies, pricing adjustments, and portfolio diversification.


## 📊 Data required
1. Geographic location of policies, including address or ZIP code
2. Total insured value (TIV) for each policy
3. Climate and environmental hazard data for each region (e.g., flood zones, wildfire risk maps)
4. Historical claims data related to climate events
5. Environmental risk forecasts or climate change projections (optional, for long-term planning)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Identify Climate and Environmental Hazard Zones:** Use environmental hazard data (e.g., FEMA flood maps, wildfire risk maps) to define high-risk areas for each type of climate risk, such as flood zones, wildfire-prone areas, hurricane paths, and areas susceptible to sea-level rise.
2. **Map Policies to Hazard Zones:** Determine the overlap between the company’s policy locations and identified hazard zones. Calculate the total insured value (TIV) in each high-risk zone to estimate the potential exposure.
3. **Calculate Exposure Concentration by Climate Risk Type:** For each type of climate risk, calculate the exposure concentration as a percentage of total TIV:
`Exposure Concentration (%) = (Total TIV in Hazard Zone / Total TIV in Portfolio) x 100`
This provides insight into the degree of exposure to specific climate risks.
4. **Analyze Historical Claims Related to Climate Events:** Review claims data for past climate-related losses by type (e.g., flood, hurricane, wildfire) to identify regions with high historical claims frequency and severity, highlighting areas where climate risks have previously materialized.
5. **Estimate Potential Losses Under Various Scenarios:** Use environmental risk forecasts or climate change models to estimate potential losses for different climate scenarios, such as a 1-in-100-year flood event or projected sea-level rise over the next decade.
6. **Evaluate Insurance Coverage and Policy Limits in High-Risk Areas:** Assess whether the policy limits and coverage types are adequate in high-risk areas, considering the potential for increased claims due to climate-related events.
7. **Compile Results:** Summarize the results in a table showing the TIV, exposure concentration, historical climate-related claims, and potential loss estimates by climate risk type and region.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Climate Risk Type (e.g., flood, wildfire)
- Region (e.g., coastal, inland forest)
- Total Insured Value (TIV) in Hazard Zone ($)
- Exposure Concentration (% of portfolio TIV)
- Historical Climate Claims ($ and frequency)
- Potential Loss Estimates under Climate Scenarios ($)
A map visualizing TIV distribution in high-risk areas and a bar chart comparing exposure concentrations by climate risk type can provide a clear overview.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Exposure Concentration in Hazard Zones:** Indicates significant vulnerability to specific climate risks, which may necessitate adjustments to pricing, underwriting, or reinsurance strategies.
- **High Historical Climate Claims:** Suggests regions where climate events have consistently led to claims, highlighting the need for risk management and possibly stricter underwriting.
- **Rising Potential Loss Estimates:** Implies increasing risk due to climate change projections, suggesting a need for proactive measures to limit exposure or adjust reinsurance.
Comparing results to industry benchmarks can help contextualize the company’s climate risk exposure relative to peers.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Enhance Geographic Diversification Reduce concentration in high-risk areas by expanding coverage in regions with lower climate risk exposure, balancing the portfolio.
2. Implement Climate Risk-Based Pricing Adjustments Adjust premiums for policies in high-risk areas to better reflect the potential for climate-related losses, ensuring sustainability of risk-prone segments.
3. Strengthen Underwriting Standards in High-Risk Regions Refine underwriting guidelines to limit exposure in regions with significant climate risk or to require additional mitigation measures.
4. Promote Risk Mitigation Programs for Policyholders Encourage or incentivize policyholders in high-risk areas to adopt preventive measures (e.g., flood-proofing, fire-resistant materials) to reduce claims frequency and severity.
5. Integrate Catastrophe Models with Climate Change Scenarios Use climate-adjusted catastrophe models that incorporate future climate change projections, allowing for more accurate risk assessments and strategic planning.
6. Adjust Reinsurance Programs for Climate Exposure Align reinsurance coverage with the company’s climate risk profile, ensuring adequate protection for areas susceptible to severe climate events.
7. Monitor Climate and Environmental Trends Regularly Keep updated on climate science developments and adjust exposure management strategies based on new insights or changes in environmental risk patterns.
