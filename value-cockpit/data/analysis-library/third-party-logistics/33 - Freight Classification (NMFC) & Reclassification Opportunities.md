# 33 · Freight Classification (NMFC) & Reclassification Opportunities

> **Part:** [[F - Trade & Regulatory Compliance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Third Party Logistics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine whether current National Motor Freight Classification (NMFC) assignments for various products are accurate, and identify opportunities to reclassify items or optimize packaging to reduce transportation costs without compromising service levels.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Current NMFC codes and descriptions for each SKU or product category
- ​ Historical shipment data (weights, dimensions, densities)
- ​ Freight invoices and cost breakdowns by commodity class
- ​ Product handling requirements (fragility, hazardous materials, special packaging)
- ​ Carrier contracts or rate agreements linked to specific classifications
- ​ Any documented accessorial charges or classification-based surcharges


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Collect Product & Shipment Data:​ Gather product dimensions, weights, and packaging details for each SKU. Compile historical shipment records to see how items have been classified and billed. 2.​ Compare Actual Characteristics to NMFC Descriptions:​ Review the official NMFC classification guidelines. Match your product specifications (density, stowability, handling) to the most fitting classification. 3.​ Identify Potential Misclassifications:​ Look for discrepancies where product density or handling requirements suggest a different, potentially lower-cost classification. Note any commonly confused or ambiguous categories. 4.​ Calculate Cost Impact of Reclassification:​ Estimate potential savings by applying the revised classification to past shipping volumes. Factor in carrier contracts, accessorial charges, and any minimum charges. 5.​ Validate with Carriers & Adjust Contracts:​ Present your proposed reclassifications or packaging optimizations to carriers. Negotiate if necessary to confirm that new classifications will be honored or if additional documentation is required. 6.​ Implement Tracking & Update SOPs:​ Update standard operating procedures for packaging, labeling, and 83 documentation to ensure consistent use of the correct NMFC class. Monitor ongoing shipments to verify cost savings. 7.​ Review Regularly:​ Schedule periodic reviews of product classifications to account for product changes, new packaging, or updated NMFC guidelines.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ A comparative table showing current vs. proposed NMFC classifications, with associated freight rates
- ​ A cost savings analysis illustrating the financial impact of each reclassification opportunity
- ​ A summary document highlighting packaging or product handling modifications required to qualify for lower classifications
- ​ An updated SOP or checklist ensuring consistent application of revised classifications


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **​ High Frequency of Misclassifications:** Indicates potential for immediate cost savings if corrected.
- **​ Significant Differences in Density or Handling Requirements vs. Current Class:** Suggests that shipments have been overclassified (or underclassified) leading to suboptimal rates.
- **​ Minimal Cost Impact from Reclassification:** May mean your classifications are already accurate or that further negotiation with carriers is needed to unlock savings.
- **​ Carrier Pushback or Denials:** Could signal either strict adherence to NMFC guidelines or the need for more detailed documentation to support a revised class.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Conduct Regular Classification Audits: Schedule periodic checks of product dimensions, weight, and packaging to keep NMFC codes accurate over time. 2.​ Optimize Packaging: Reduce dimensions, improve density, or redesign packaging to qualify for more favorable classes. 3.​ Train Staff on NMFC Guidelines: Ensure those responsible for product data entry or shipping documentation understand the classification system and its impact on costs.
