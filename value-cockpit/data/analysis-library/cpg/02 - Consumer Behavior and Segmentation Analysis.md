# 2 · Consumer Behavior and Segmentation Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Marketing]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Consumer Behavior and Segmentation Analysis is to understand the key behaviors, preferences, and purchasing patterns of different consumer groups within a consumer packaged goods (CPG) company’s market. This analysis helps identify distinct customer segments, uncover consumer needs, and develop tailored marketing and product strategies for each segment. By segmenting consumers, a company can more effectively target and serve its customers, increasing customer satisfaction, loyalty, and overall sales.


## 📊 Data required
1. Demographic data (e.g., age, gender, income, education level)
2. Psychographic data (e.g., values, interests, lifestyle, attitudes)
3. Behavioral data (e.g., purchase frequency, product usage, shopping channel preferences)
4. Geographic data (e.g., region, urban vs. rural, country)
5. Consumer feedback and satisfaction data (e.g., survey responses, reviews, social media sentiment)
6. Purchase history (e.g., products bought, time of purchase, average order value)
7. Brand loyalty metrics (e.g., repeat purchase rates, loyalty program participation)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the objective of segmentation:** Clearly identify the goals for segmenting consumers, such as identifying new growth opportunities, developing targeted marketing campaigns, or optimizing product offerings.
10
2. **Collect relevant consumer data:** Gather demographic, psychographic, behavioral, and geographic data on existing and potential consumers. This data can be obtained through customer surveys, CRM systems, third-party data providers, social media, and transaction records.
3. **Segment the consumer base:** Use data-driven methods to create meaningful consumer segments. Common segmentation techniques include: ○ Demographic segmentation: Group consumers by factors such as age, income, gender, education, and family size. ○ Psychographic segmentation: Categorize consumers by lifestyle, values, interests, and opinions. ○ Behavioral segmentation: Identify patterns such as purchasing frequency, brand loyalty, and product usage. ○ Geographic segmentation: Divide consumers by location, considering regional or local market differences.
4. Segmentation can also be hybrid, combining multiple factors for more nuanced groupings.
5. **Cluster analysis for consumer segmentation:** Use clustering algorithms (e.g., k-means clustering) to identify groups of consumers with similar attributes. The number of clusters (segments) can be determined based on the variability in the data. For each cluster, analyze the key traits that differentiate them from others.
6. **Develop consumer personas for each segment:** Once the clusters are identified, create detailed consumer personas for each segment. These personas should include key characteristics, motivations, purchasing behaviors, and preferred communication channels. For example, "Tech-Savvy Millennial Moms" or "Value-Conscious Suburban Families."
7. **Calculate segment contribution to revenue:** Analyze the contribution of each segment to overall company revenue. The formula for Segment Contribution can be calculated as:
`Segment Contribution = (Segment Revenue / Total Revenue) x 100`
For example, if the "Urban Professionals" segment generated $30 million in 11 revenue, and the total company revenue is $150 million:
`Segment Contribution = ($30 million / $150 million) x 100 = 20%`
8. **Analyze consumer behavior within each segment:** Evaluate key behaviors and purchasing patterns within each segment, such as: ○ Frequency of purchase (e.g., weekly, monthly, seasonally) ○ Average basket size or order value ○ Brand loyalty vs. switching behavior ○ Preferred shopping channels (e.g., in-store, online, mobile)
9. **Identify needs and pain points:** Through consumer surveys or feedback, identify the unique needs, preferences, and pain points of each segment. This insight can help inform product development, marketing messages, and customer service improvements.
10. **Evaluate trends and changes over time:** Monitor changes in consumer behavior and segment sizes over time. For example, assess whether a particular segment is growing or shrinking in size or if their purchasing preferences are shifting.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Consumer Behavior and Segmentation Analysis should include:
- A detailed breakdown of each identified consumer segment, including demographic, psychographic, and behavioral profiles.
- Data visualizations such as pie charts or bar graphs representing the size and contribution of each segment to total revenue.
- Key insights into the needs, preferences, and purchase behaviors of each segment.
- Recommendations for marketing strategies, product offerings, and communication channels based on segment analysis.

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High revenue, high engagement segments:** These are the core consumer groups that drive the most value for the company. Strategies should focus on retaining and growing these segments by offering tailored products, promotions, and marketing messages.
- **High potential, low engagement segments:** These segments may not yet contribute significantly to revenue but have potential for growth. Consider investing in targeted marketing campaigns or new product offerings to engage these consumers.
- **Low revenue, high engagement segments:** These are loyal customers who may not yet contribute significantly to revenue but are highly engaged with the brand. Increasing average order value or encouraging more frequent purchases may be ways to maximize value from this group.
- **Low revenue, low engagement segments:** These segments may not align well with the company’s core value proposition and could be deprioritized or targeted with specific, low-cost campaigns.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Refine marketing messages for each segment:** Tailor marketing campaigns to address the specific needs, preferences, and pain points of each segment. Use the most effective communication channels (e.g., social media, email, in-store promotions) based on segment behavior.
2. **Develop personalized product offerings:** Create new products or customize existing ones to meet the unique demands of high-value consumer segments. For example, offering smaller package sizes for value-conscious shoppers or premium options for more affluent customers.
13
3. **Optimize pricing and promotions:** Adjust pricing and promotional strategies to better align with consumer segments. For instance, loyalty programs could be introduced for frequent buyers, while occasional buyers could be targeted with seasonal discounts.
4. **Improve customer retention:** For high-potential, low-engagement segments, consider investing in loyalty programs or personalized experiences to boost engagement and repeat purchases.
5. **Monitor segment shifts over time:** Track changes in the size, behavior, and purchasing power of each segment to adjust marketing and product strategies as needed. Consumer behavior can evolve due to external factors such as economic conditions or cultural trends.
