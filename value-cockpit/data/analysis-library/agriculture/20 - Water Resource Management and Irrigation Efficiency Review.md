# 20 · Water Resource Management and Irrigation Efficiency Review

> **Part:** [[C - Environmental Sustainability & Resource]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Agriculture Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate on-farm water usage, infrastructure, and irrigation practices to optimize water resource management, minimize waste, and enhance crop yields and sustainability.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Current irrigation methods (drip, sprinkler, flood, etc.)
- ​ Water sources (groundwater, surface water, rainwater harvesting)
- ​ Historical water usage data (volumes, timing)
- ​ Crop water requirements based on species and growth stages
- ​ Soil moisture and infiltration rates
- ​ Local climate and rainfall patterns
- ​ Irrigation equipment performance and maintenance records
- ​ Costs associated with water access and irrigation infrastructure


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Document current water usage and sources.​ Record volumes drawn from each source, noting any seasonal variations or restrictions.
Step 2: Assess irrigation methods.​ Identify which systems (sprinkler, drip, flood) are used per field or crop. Evaluate each method’s typical efficiency rates based on manufacturer data and field conditions.
Step 3: Compare actual water usage to crop requirements.​ Gather data on each crop’s evapotranspiration and soil moisture needs. Check for over- or under-irrigation by comparing recorded usage with scientifically recommended levels.
Step 4: Evaluate infrastructure and maintenance.​ Inspect pumps, pipes, valves, and emitters for leaks or wear. Check flow rates and pressure consistency in different sections of the field.
Step 5: Check soil moisture monitoring practices.​ Review whether soil sensors or manual probes are used, and how frequently. Determine if real-time data is utilized for irrigation scheduling.
45 Step 6: Analyze cost implications.​ Calculate total water-related expenditures, including pumping costs, equipment depreciation, and water access fees if applicable.
Step 7: Summarize findings and recommend improvements.​ Outline any inefficiencies, such as leakages or outdated systems. Suggest technical upgrades, scheduling changes, or precision irrigation tools based on the findings.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ A table summarizing water usage per field/crop, actual vs. recommended irrigation volumes, and associated costs
- ​ A concise report highlighting infrastructure status, irrigation efficiencies, and maintenance needs
- ​ Recommendations for system upgrades, alternative irrigation methods, or water scheduling software


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ Significant discrepancies between recommended and actual water usage suggest inefficient or unsustainable practices.
- ​ High irrigation costs may indicate outdated infrastructure, poor maintenance, or inefficient water use.
- ​ Consistent soil moisture levels within target ranges usually indicate well-managed irrigation strategies.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Conduct regular leak detection and repair to prevent water loss. 2.​ Upgrade to drip or micro-sprinkler systems in areas prone to over-irrigation. 3.​ Employ soil moisture sensors or digital tools for data-driven irrigation scheduling. 4.​ Optimize field layout (e.g., leveling, zoning) to improve water distribution. 5.​ Recycle or reuse water where feasible (tailwater recovery systems, rainwater harvesting). 6.​ Provide training to staff on efficient watering techniques and equipment upkeep.
