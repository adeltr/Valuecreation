# 19 · Living Benefits Utilization and Impact on Profitability Study

> **Part:** [[C - Profitability and Product Performance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the utilization of living benefits (e.g., accelerated death benefits, chronic illness coverage, and long-term care riders) and assess their impact on the profitability of life insurance products. The analysis helps identify trends in claims usage, financial impacts, and opportunities to optimize product design and pricing.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policy-level data:** ○ Policies with living benefits (e.g., critical illness, long-term care, accelerated death benefit). ○ Policyholder demographics (age, gender, location, health status). ○ Premium amounts and policy duration.
2. **Claims data:** ○ Number of claims filed for living benefits. ○ Claims paid, claim severity, and average time to claim.
3. **Actuarial assumptions:** ○ Utilization rates (expected usage of living benefits). ○ Mortality and morbidity rates. ○ Discount rates for present value calculations.
4. **Expense data:** ○ Acquisition and maintenance costs associated with living benefits. ○ Administrative costs for processing claims.
5. **Benchmark data:** ○ Industry utilization rates for living benefits. ○ Competitor product offerings and pricing.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Segment Policies by Living Benefit Type ○ Group policies into categories based on living benefits offered (e.g., accelerated death benefit, chronic illness). ○ Further segment by policyholder age, gender, and health profile.
2. Calculate Utilization Rates ○ Use the formula:
`Utilization Rate (%) = (Number of Living Benefit Claims Filed / Total Policies with Living Benefits) x 100`
3. Measure Financial Impact ○ Calculate total claims payouts for living benefits.
54 ○ Assess profitability by comparing claims and expenses against premium revenue:
`Profitability = Premium Revenue - (Claims Paid + Expenses)`
○ Identify the contribution of living benefit claims to overall product profitability.
4. Compare Expected vs. Actual Utilization ○ Compare observed utilization rates with actuarial assumptions to identify deviations. ○ Calculate the deviation percentage:
`Deviation (%) = ((Actual Utilization - Expected Utilization) / Expected Utilization) x 100`
5. Analyze Impact on Mortality and Reserves ○ Assess how living benefit claims impact future death benefit payouts and reserves. ○ Calculate reserve adjustments due to accelerated claims payments.
6. Conduct Sensitivity Analysis ○ Model the impact of changes in utilization rates, morbidity trends, and claim severity on profitability. ○ Example: Increase utilization rates by 10% and measure the effect on claims and reserves.
7. Benchmark Against Competitors ○ Compare utilization rates, claims experience, and product design with competitors to identify gaps or competitive advantages.
8. Visualize Results ○ Use tables to summarize utilization rates, claims paid, and profitability across benefit types and segments. ○ Generate charts showing trends in utilization rates, claim amounts, and deviations from assumptions.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing utilization rates, claims paid, and profitability by living benefit type
- Charts showing trends in utilization rates and financial impact
- Sensitivity analysis results showing profitability changes under various utilization scenarios
- Key findings identifying deviations from assumptions and high-impact areas for improvement 55


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Higher-than-expected utilization rates indicate underestimation of claims, which can reduce profitability and strain reserves.
2. Low utilization rates may suggest that living benefits are not fully understood or valued by policyholders.
3. Significant deviations between expected and actual utilization highlight a need to revise actuarial assumptions.
4. High claims severity or administrative costs erode profitability and may require adjustments to pricing or claims processes.
5. Competitive benchmarking reveals opportunities to improve product features or align pricing with market standards.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Update actuarial assumptions for living benefits based on recent utilization trends and claims experience.
2. Improve customer education and communication to increase awareness of living benefits, ensuring policyholders use them effectively.
3. Optimize underwriting practices to manage risks associated with living benefit claims, particularly for chronic illness or long-term care riders.
4. Adjust pricing for products with higher-than-expected living benefit utilization to maintain profitability.
5. Streamline claims processes to reduce administrative costs and enhance policyholder satisfaction.
6. Conduct regular experience studies to monitor deviations from assumptions and recalibrate reserve requirements.
7. Benchmark against competitor products to identify opportunities for innovation, such as flexible living benefit options or bundled riders.
