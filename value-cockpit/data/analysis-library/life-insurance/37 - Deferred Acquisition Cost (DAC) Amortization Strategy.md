# 37 · Deferred Acquisition Cost (DAC) Amortization Strategy Analysis

> **Part:** [[H - Regulatory Compliance and Financial Reporting]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the company's deferred acquisition cost (DAC) amortization practices to ensure alignment with profitability, regulatory requirements, and financial reporting standards. This analysis identifies opportunities to optimize DAC amortization for improved financial performance and capital efficiency.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policy-level data:** ○ Acquisition costs (e.g., commissions, underwriting costs, marketing expenses) incurred at policy inception ○ Premiums collected, policy duration, and projected cash flows
2. **Financial data:** ○ Current DAC balance, amortization schedules, and remaining unamortized DAC ○ Revenue and expense recognition under existing amortization practices
3. **Actuarial assumptions:** ○ Policy lapse rates, mortality rates, persistency rates, and discount rates
4. **Benchmark data:** ○ Industry DAC amortization rates and methods (e.g., straight-line, revenue-based, or present value of expected profits) ○ Regulatory requirements for DAC treatment (e.g., IFRS 17, GAAP)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate the Current DAC Balance and Amortization Schedule a. Summarize acquisition costs deferred at policy inception and track the DAC balance over time. b. Review the amortization method used: ■ Straight-line amortization: costs are spread evenly over the policy’s duration. ■ Revenue-based amortization: amortization aligns with expected premium revenue. ■ Present value of expected profits (PVEP): amortization reflects future profit emergence.
2. Evaluate Amortization Impact on Profitability a. Compare the amortization schedule with premium revenue and profit emergence:

b. Analyze whether the timing of amortization aligns with revenue recognition to avoid front-loading expenses.
3. Assess Key Assumptions and Sensitivity a. Review assumptions for lapse rates, mortality, and persistency that drive DAC amortization schedules. b. Conduct sensitivity analysis to model the impact of higher-than-expected lapse rates or changes in persistency on unamortized DAC:
`Impact on DAC = Change in Assumption x Remaining Unamortized`

4. Analyze DAC Write-Off Risks a. Identify policies with high DAC balances relative to projected future revenue. b. Measure the risk of DAC write-offs in case of policy lapses, low persistency, or profitability deviations.
5. Benchmark DAC Practices a. Compare the company’s DAC amortization methods and schedules with industry standards and regulatory guidelines. b. Identify gaps where amortization practices may overstate profitability or conflict with regulatory standards (e.g., IFRS 17).
6. Measure Financial Impact of Alternative Strategies a. Test alternative amortization methods, such as shifting from straight-line to revenue-based amortization, to evaluate: ■ Profit recognition timing ■ Alignment with cash flow patterns and expected profits ■ Impact on capital and financial statements
7. Visualize Results a. Summarize current and alternative amortization schedules in tables. b. Use charts to show the DAC balance, amortization trends, and revenue alignment over time.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables showing current DAC balance, amortization schedules, and sensitivity to assumptions
- Charts illustrating the alignment between DAC amortization, revenue recognition, and profit emergence
- Comparison of alternative strategies and their impact on financial metrics
- Key findings identifying inefficiencies, write-off risks, and optimization opportunities 108


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. DAC amortization aligned with revenue recognition ensures that acquisition costs are appropriately matched with profitability.
2. High amortization ratios early in a policy’s life can signal front-loaded expenses that may reduce short-term profitability.
3. Sensitivity analysis highlights the impact of lapses and persistency changes on unamortized DAC, identifying write-off risks.
4. Misalignment between DAC amortization and cash flows can overstate or understate financial performance.
5. Benchmark comparisons provide insights into industry-standard practices and regulatory compliance.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Align DAC amortization schedules with expected premium revenue or profit emergence to improve financial accuracy.
2. Regularly review and update key assumptions (e.g., lapse rates, persistency) to reflect actual experience and avoid surprises.
3. Adopt more dynamic amortization methods, such as PVEP, to align with future cash flows and profits.
4. Monitor policies with high DAC balances and proactively manage lapses or persistency to minimize write-offs.
5. Benchmark amortization practices against regulatory guidelines (e.g., IFRS 17) and industry standards to ensure compliance.
6. Conduct sensitivity testing regularly to measure the financial impact of changes in assumptions and adjust strategies accordingly.
7. Improve expense management to reduce acquisition costs and limit DAC exposure over time.
