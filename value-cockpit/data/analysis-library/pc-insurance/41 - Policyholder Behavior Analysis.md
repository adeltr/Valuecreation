# 41 · Policyholder Behavior Analysis

> **Part:** [[F - Sales & Marketing]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Policyholder Behavior Analysis is to understand patterns in policyholder actions, such as renewals, lapses, claims filing, and engagement with policy terms. This analysis helps the company anticipate customer needs, improve retention, adjust pricing, and tailor products based on behavior trends.


## 📊 Data required
1. Policy renewal rates
2. Lapse rates (policies not renewed or canceled)
3. Claims frequency and severity by policy type
4. Demographic data on policyholders (age, location, policy type)
5. Policy adjustments (e.g., endorsements, changes in coverage limits)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Calculate Renewal and Lapse Rates:** Determine the renewal and lapse rates for each policy type, which show policyholder loyalty and retention:
- Renewal Rate (%) = (Number of Renewed Policies / Total Policies up for Renewal) x 100
- Lapse Rate (%) = (Number of Lapsed Policies / Total Policies up for Renewal) x 100 These metrics help gauge overall policyholder satisfaction and retention by policy type or demographic group.
2. **Analyze Claims Frequency and Severity by Policy Type:** Examine the average number and severity of claims filed by policyholders. Segment by policy type to identify trends in claims behavior:
`Claims Frequency = (Total Claims Filed / Total Policies)`
High claims frequency and severity could indicate risky behavior among certain policyholder groups or highlight policies that may need premium adjustments.
3. **Segment Policyholders by Demographics:** Divide policyholders into groups based on demographics like age, location, and policy type to see how behavior patterns vary. This allows for tailored product recommendations and pricing adjustments.
4. **Identify Common Policy Adjustments and Endorsements:** Track changes made by policyholders to their policies, such as adjustments to coverage limits or additional endorsements. Frequent changes may indicate evolving needs or dissatisfaction with initial coverage.
5. **Evaluate Lapse and Claim Patterns Together:** Examine any correlation between lapse rates and claim frequency. High lapse rates combined with high claims frequency could signal potential adverse selection, where higher-risk policyholders are more likely to drop or file claims.
6. **Monitor Long-Term Trends in Policyholder Behavior:** Track behavior over multiple periods (e.g., quarterly, annually) to observe any emerging trends in policyholder actions, such as increased lapses in specific segments or rising interest in certain coverage options.
7. **Compile Results:** Summarize findings in a table showing renewal rates, lapse rates, claims frequency, and common policy changes for each policy type and demographic group.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Policy Type or Segment (e.g., auto, property)
- Renewal Rate (%)
- Lapse Rate (%)
- Claims Frequency (claims per policy)
- Average Claims Severity ($)
- Common Policy Adjustments (e.g., coverage increases, endorsements)
A bar chart showing renewal and lapse rates by policy type or a line chart of claims frequency trends over time can illustrate patterns in policyholder behavior.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Renewal Rate and Low Lapse Rate:** Indicates strong customer loyalty and satisfaction, often associated with well-tailored products and effective customer engagement.
- **Low Renewal Rate and High Lapse Rate:** Suggests potential dissatisfaction or lack of engagement, highlighting the need for targeted retention strategies.
- **High Claims Frequency and Severity:** May indicate a need for pricing adjustments to better reflect risk or signal policy types attracting higher-risk customers.
Comparing results to industry averages or historical data helps contextualize behavior trends within a broader market framework.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Develop Targeted Retention Strategies Use renewal and lapse data to implement retention campaigns for segments with lower renewal rates, such as offering incentives for renewal or improved customer service.
2. Adjust Premiums for High-Claims Segments Reassess pricing for segments with high claims frequency or severity to ensure that premiums reflect the associated risk.
3. Offer Customized Product Enhancements Use insights from policy adjustments to introduce product enhancements, such as flexible coverage options or added endorsements that match policyholder preferences.
4. Educate Policyholders on Coverage Benefits Provide clear information on policy benefits and coverage options to enhance customer understanding and satisfaction, reducing lapses.
5. Tailor Marketing and Engagement by Demographic Segment Use demographic insights to create personalized marketing campaigns, targeting segments more likely to renew or less likely to lapse.
6. Implement Proactive Claims Management For policyholders with high claims frequency, consider proactive claims management, such as loss prevention education or claims tracking tools to improve their experience.
7. Benchmark Policyholder Behavior Against Industry Data Compare renewal, lapse, and claims trends with industry standards to identify areas for improvement and ensure competitive positioning.
