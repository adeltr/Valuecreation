# 24 · Destination Marketing and Local Partnership Effectiveness

> **Part:** [[E - Marketing & Competitive Positioning]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine how local collaborations and tourism promotions contribute to bookings and revenue, guiding refinements to maximize mutual benefits.


## 📊 Data required
- Records of joint marketing campaigns, co‐branded promotions, and partnership agreements.
- Booking and revenue data attributed to destination marketing channels (tourism board referrals, local event collaborations).
- Visitor or guest surveys indicating the influence of local marketing on their decision to book.
- Marketing costs and ROI metrics for partnerships and co‐promotions (e.g., cost‐sharing with local businesses).
- Occupancy and ADR trends during local events or tourism board initiatives.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather partnership details. List all local alliances (tourism board, event organizers, cultural institutions) and the associated campaigns or promotions.
2. Collect booking data related to each partnership. Use unique booking codes or tracking links to determine how many room nights or packages were sold through specific marketing efforts.
3. Analyze revenue and ROI. For each collaboration or campaign, compare total bookings and revenue against the marketing cost or resource investment.
4. Review occupancy trends during partnered events. Correlate spikes (or lack thereof) in occupancy or ADR with promotional periods tied to destination marketing.
5. Assess guest feedback. Look for mentions of local attractions or events in guest surveys and reviews to see if the partnership influenced their stay experience.
6. Benchmark results. Compare metrics from different partnerships to identify the most and least effective collaborations.
7. Identify improvement areas. Determine which campaigns resonate with target demographics, and adjust partnership strategies accordingly.

## 📤 Output format
- A table listing each local partnership, associated campaigns, marketing costs, bookings generated, total revenue, and ROI.
- Charts showing occupancy or ADR changes during periods aligned with destination marketing efforts.
- Written summary highlighting the effectiveness of each collaboration and recommending next steps.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High ROI and increased booking volume from a partnership indicate that the collaboration effectively drives demand.
- If certain partnerships yield minimal returns or low guest engagement, they may require renegotiation or discontinuation.
- Positive guest feedback about local experiences can reinforce the value of destination marketing in enhancing overall satisfaction.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Negotiate co‐branding opportunities that align with the hotel’s target demographics (e.g., adventure tours for families, cultural events for older travelers).
2. Use data tracking methods (unique codes or digital links) to precisely measure the impact of each partnership on bookings.
3. Collaborate with local businesses to create exclusive packages (dining, tours, entertainment) that appeal to the hotel’s guest segments.
4. Allocate marketing budgets toward the most successful partnerships and phase out or refine underperforming collaborations.
5. Request and share post‐event feedback with partners to continuously improve offerings and promotional strategies.
