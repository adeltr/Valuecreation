# 11 · Channel Strategy Assessment

> **Part:** [[B - Sales]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Channel Strategy Assessment in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to evaluate the effectiveness of a company’s distribution channels in delivering products to consumers. This analysis helps CPG companies determine which channels (e.g., retail, e-commerce, wholesale, direct-to-consumer) are driving the most value and which may require optimization or investment. The objective is to align the channel strategy with consumer preferences, market trends, and company goals to maximize reach, sales, and profitability.


## 📊 Data required
1. Sales data by channel (e.g., retail, e-commerce, wholesale, direct-to-consumer)
2. Profitability metrics by channel (e.g., gross margin, cost-to-serve, channel-specific expenses)
3. Channel-specific customer demographics and preferences
4. Market share data by channel
5. Consumer behavior data (e.g., purchase frequency, product preferences by channel)
6. Distribution and logistics costs (e.g., warehousing, shipping, inventory management)
7. Competitor performance data in each channel
8. Marketing and promotional spend by channel
9. Retailer partnerships and contract terms (for traditional retail channels)
60
10. Customer feedback on purchasing experience by channel (e.g., online reviews, in-store feedback)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the scope and objectives:** Clearly outline the objectives for assessing the channel strategy. This could include determining which channels deliver the highest sales, optimizing profitability, identifying underperforming channels, or aligning with new consumer shopping habits (e.g., shift to e-commerce).
2. **Gather sales and profitability data by channel:** Collect detailed sales data from each channel (e.g., retail stores, e-commerce platforms, wholesale distributors, direct-to-consumer). Compare the total revenue and gross margins across channels to identify which ones are contributing the most to overall profitability. Calculate gross margin by channel using the formula:
`Gross Margin (%) = ((Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue) x 100`
For example, if the retail channel generates $500,000 in revenue and has a cost of goods sold (COGS) of $300,000:
`Gross Margin = (($500,000 - $300,000) / $500,000) x 100 = 40%`
3. **Analyze cost-to-serve for each channel:** Evaluate the operational and logistics costs associated with each channel, including warehousing, transportation, packaging, and handling. For channels like e-commerce, account for additional costs like shipping and returns management. The goal is to assess the cost-efficiency of each channel by comparing cost-to-serve with the channel's profitability.
4. **Evaluate channel-specific customer segments:** Segment your customer base by channel to understand which types of consumers are shopping through each channel. For example, younger consumers may prefer purchasing via e-commerce, while older consumers may prefer traditional retail. Analyze 61 demographic and psychographic data to determine if your channel strategy aligns with your target audience’s preferences.
5. **Assess market share by channel:** Compare your brand’s market share in each channel to that of key competitors. This can help identify whether the company is underrepresented in certain channels where competitors are strong or if it dominates specific channels. Calculate market share using the formula:
`Market Share (%) = (Company Sales in Channel / Total Sales in Channel) x 100`
For example, if the company has $1 million in sales in a channel, and the total sales in that channel are $10 million:
`Market Share = ($1 million / $10 million) x 100 = 10%`
6. **Review consumer behavior by channel:** Analyze how consumer behavior varies across channels. For example, consumers may make larger, less frequent purchases in wholesale channels but shop more regularly on e-commerce platforms. Key metrics to analyze include purchase frequency, basket size, product preferences, and average order value by channel.
7. **Assess channel-specific marketing effectiveness:** Review how marketing and promotional efforts perform in each channel. Evaluate return on marketing investment (ROMI) by channel using the formula:
`ROMI = (Revenue Attributable to Marketing - Marketing Spend) / Marketing`
Spend x 100 For example, if the e-commerce channel generated $400,000 in revenue with $100,000 in marketing spend:
`ROMI = (($400,000 - $100,000) / $100,000) x 100 = 300%`
Compare ROMI across channels to determine where marketing dollars are generating the most return.
8. **Identify underperforming channels:** Identify channels that are underperforming in terms of profitability, market share, or consumer engagement. If a channel generates low sales but high costs, consider scaling back or restructuring your approach. For example, if a certain retail partnership is not yielding the expected 62 return, it may be worth renegotiating contract terms or shifting focus to other retailers or channels.
9. **Evaluate the impact of omni-channel integration:** Consider how well the company integrates its various channels to create a seamless shopping experience for consumers. Omni-channel shoppers, who browse online but purchase in-store (or vice versa), are valuable, and assessing how well these channels work together can drive further consumer loyalty and sales. For example, track how often consumers use options like “buy online, pick up in-store” and how this affects sales.
10. **Review competitor performance by channel:** Analyze how competitors are performing in each channel, including any emerging channels where they have gained a competitive edge. This can help identify new opportunities or threats in your current channel strategy. For example, if competitors are growing significantly in direct-to-consumer or subscription models, this may signal an area where your company could expand.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Channel Strategy Assessment typically includes:
- A breakdown of sales, profitability, and cost-to-serve for each channel.
- Market share analysis by channel, comparing the company’s position against key competitors.
- A summary of channel-specific consumer segments, behavior patterns, and preferences.
- Marketing effectiveness metrics for each channel, including ROMI and other KPIs.
- Identification of underperforming or high-cost channels that may require optimization.
63
- Recommendations for channel expansion, consolidation, or investment based on performance and market trends.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High sales and profitability in specific channels:** Channels with high sales volume and strong profitability should be prioritized for further investment. Consider increasing marketing spend or expanding product offerings in these high-performing channels.
- **Low sales but high cost-to-serve:** Channels that generate low revenue but have high costs may need restructuring. Consider reducing marketing spend, renegotiating contracts, or exiting the channel if profitability cannot be improved.
- **Strong market share growth in emerging channels:** If your brand is gaining market share in emerging channels like e-commerce or direct-to-consumer, this is a sign of future growth potential. Consider shifting focus toward these channels, especially if they align with consumer behavior trends.
- **Discrepancies in consumer behavior:** If consumer preferences differ significantly between channels, tailor your product offerings, marketing, and promotions accordingly. For example, e-commerce shoppers may prefer premium product options, while retail shoppers may prioritize convenience and availability.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize underperforming channels:** Focus on reducing costs or improving sales in underperforming channels. This could involve renegotiating distribution contracts, consolidating partnerships, or offering channel-specific promotions to drive demand.
64
2. **Invest in high-potential channels:** Channels that show strong profitability and consumer engagement should receive additional investment. This may involve increasing marketing spend, expanding product lines, or introducing exclusive promotions to further capitalize on consumer interest.
3. **Align marketing with channel-specific behaviors:** Tailor marketing campaigns to fit the unique needs of each channel. For example, digital marketing should focus on personalization and convenience for e-commerce, while in-store promotions may focus on impulse purchases or discounts.
4. **Expand into new or emerging channels:** If the analysis reveals growth opportunities in new or emerging channels, such as subscription-based services or direct-to-consumer, consider exploring these areas to capture market share and meet evolving consumer preferences.
5. **Improve omni-channel integration:** Ensure a seamless experience for omni-channel shoppers by integrating online and offline channels. Implement features like buy-online-pick-up-in-store, synchronized loyalty programs, or cross-channel promotions to enhance consumer satisfaction and drive sales.
6. **Monitor competitive landscape:** Continuously track competitor performance across channels to identify opportunities or threats. If competitors are excelling in certain channels, consider adapting your strategy to maintain competitiveness.
