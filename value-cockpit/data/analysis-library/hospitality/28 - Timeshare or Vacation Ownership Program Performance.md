# 28 · Timeshare or Vacation Ownership Program Performance Analysis

> **Part:** [[F - Ownership & Financial Structures]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate sales, usage, and profitability of timeshare or vacation ownership offerings, helping you refine pricing and marketing or decide on future expansions.


## 📊 Data required
- Sales figures for timeshare or vacation ownership units (number of units sold, average sale price, financing terms if applicable).
- Marketing and lead generation costs tied specifically to timeshare/vacation ownership promotions.
- Occupancy or usage data for timeshare owners, exchange guests, and rental guests (if units are rented out when not used by owners).
- Maintenance fee collections, delinquency rates, and operating costs.
- Owner satisfaction scores or feedback, including resale and exit requests.
- Benchmark data from industry reports or comparable programs, if available.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Collect sales performance data. Gather information on the number of weeks/points sold, average price per point/week, and total sales revenue.
2. Determine marketing cost per sale. Divide total marketing expenses by the number of timeshare units/weeks sold to gauge cost‐effectiveness.
3. Analyze occupancy and usage patterns. Look at how frequently owners use their units versus exchanging or renting them, and compare to inventory levels.
4. Review maintenance fee compliance. Note delinquency rates and the total revenue generated from maintenance fees.
5. Calculate overall profitability. Subtract operational costs (including marketing, maintenance, financing, and administrative expenses) from total revenue (sales, fees, rentals).
6. Evaluate owner satisfaction and retention. Check feedback regarding booking availability, property condition, and any resale or exit inquiries.
7. Benchmark against industry or regional standards. Compare sales velocity, usage rates, and profit margins to similar timeshare or vacation ownership programs.
8. Identify areas for improvement. Pinpoint issues such as high marketing costs, low usage rates, or high owner dissatisfaction that could be limiting program success.

## 📤 Output format
1. **A table summarizing:** ○ Timeshare Sales (units/weeks/points) ○ Average Sale Price ○ Marketing Cost per Sale ○ Occupancy/Usage Rate ○ Maintenance Fee Collection Rate ○ Program Profit Margin (%)
2. Graphs depicting timeshare sales trends over time, usage rates, and maintenance fee trends.
3. A narrative report highlighting key findings and recommended changes to improve program effectiveness.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A high marketing cost per sale may signal inefficient lead generation or a need to refine the sales process.
- Frequent owner usage suggests strong program appeal, but if owners rarely use their units, it may indicate misalignment with their needs or scheduling frustrations.
- High delinquency rates in maintenance fees may point to owner dissatisfaction or financial stress among owners, affecting program cash flow.
- Positive owner feedback and strong resale demand typically indicate a healthy, appealing timeshare/vacation ownership product.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Refine marketing and sales strategies by targeting prospects more likely to become satisfied owners and reducing costly lead sources.
2. Enhance owner experiences with flexible usage options, streamlined booking, and additional property perks.
3. Review pricing and financing models to ensure they are competitive and attractive to potential buyers.
4. Strengthen communication and engagement with owners through personalized touchpoints, clear explanations of rules/fees, and regular updates on improvements.
5. Monitor delinquency and resale trends to intervene early, offer solutions, and maintain a healthy community of owners.
