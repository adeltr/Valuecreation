# KPI — Waste Management

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Waste Management & Sustainability]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Waste Management Company - Analyse]]

68 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Asset Turnover Rate | While common in many industries, this KPI can be particularly relevant in waste management given the industry's capital intensity. It measures how effectively a company uses its assets to generate revenue. |
| Average Age of Fleet | This KPI measures the average age of the company's fleet of vehicles. An older fleet may require more maintenance and be less fuel-efficient, impacting both costs and environmental performance. |
| BioGas Production Volume | For waste management companies that have anaerobic digestion facilities, the volume of biogas produced is a key measure of operational efficiency and potential revenue. |
| Carbon Footprint | With growing emphasis on sustainability, measuring the company's carbon footprint (for example, per ton of waste collected or processed) can provide insights into its environmental performance and potential regulatory and reputational risks. |
| Collection Efficiency | This KPI measures how effectively a company collects waste. It can be calculated by dividing the volume of waste collected by the number of collection rounds. Higher efficiency often means lower operating costs. |
| Collection Route Efficiency | This KPI measures how effectively a company designs its collection routes, which can be quantified in terms of miles driven per ton of waste collected or similar metrics. More efficient routes can reduce fuel costs and increase productivity. |
| Collection Vehicle Downtime | This measures the amount of time collection vehicles are not operational due to maintenance or repairs. High downtime can indicate inefficient operations and potential additional costs. |
| Compliance with Waste Hierarchy | The waste hierarchy prioritizes waste prevention, reduction, recycling, and recovery over disposal methods such as landfill and incineration. Compliance with this hierarchy can be a measure of the company's commitment to sustainability. |
| Construction and Demolition (C&D) Waste Recovery Rate | For companies involved in C&D waste management, this KPI measures the percentage of C&D waste that is recovered and recycled or repurposed. |
| Contamination Rate | For companies involved in recycling, this metric quantifies the proportion of non-recyclable materials in the recycling stream. A lower contamination rate often means lower processing costs and higher revenues from selling recyclable materials. |
| Cost Per Pickup | This KPI measures the cost associated with each waste pickup. It provides insight into the efficiency of the company's collection operations. |
| Cost Per Ton of Waste | This KPI measures the cost involved in collecting and disposing of one ton of waste. It's a crucial measure of operational efficiency and cost management. |
| Cost per Ton of Waste Transported | This KPI measures the costs associated with transporting a ton of waste, whether to a landfill, recycling facility, or other destination. This metric can reflect operational efficiency and the impact of fuel prices. |
| Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) | While not unique to the waste management industry, this metric is particularly relevant due to the contractual nature of the business. It measures the cost to acquire a new customer and can indicate the efficiency of the company's sales and marketing efforts. |
| Customer Churn Rate | Although used across many industries, in the waste management industry this KPI measures the rate at which customers terminate their contracts. High churn rates can indicate customer dissatisfaction or competitive challenges. |
| Customer Retention Rate | The waste management industry typically operates on contract-based relationships. Hence, the ability to retain customers is a key metric. A high retention rate indicates customer satisfaction and can lead to more predictable revenue streams. |
| Decommissioning and Post-Closure Care Costs | For landfill operators, this KPI estimates the future costs associated with closing a landfill and caring for it after closure. Higher costs can indicate significant future liabilities. |
| E-Waste Recovery Rate | For companies that handle electronic waste, this KPI indicates the proportion of e-waste that is successfully recovered for recycling. |
| Energy Production per Ton of Waste | For waste-to-energy companies, this metric measures the amount of energy (in kWh or MWh, for example) produced per ton of waste processed. It's an indicator of operational efficiency and the quality of waste feedstock. |
| Greenhouse Gas Emission Reduction Rate | Some waste management companies aim to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, for instance by capturing methane at landfills or improving their fleet's fuel efficiency. Tracking the rate of reduction in emissions can be a key measure of progress towards these goals. |
| Hazardous Waste Percentage | For companies dealing with hazardous waste, this metric quantifies the proportion of hazardous waste in the total waste handled. Management of hazardous waste often involves additional regulatory requirements and potential liabilities. |
| Hazardous Waste Treatment Efficiency | For companies dealing with hazardous waste, this KPI measures the effectiveness of their treatment process, often in terms of how much waste is neutralized or made safe per unit of input. |
| Incineration Efficiency | For companies that use incineration as a waste disposal method, the efficiency of the incineration process (measured in terms of how much waste volume is reduced, for example) is a key metric. |
| Landfill Gas Utilization Rate | For landfill operators, this KPI measures the proportion of landfill gas (often methane) that is captured and utilized for energy production, rather than being released into the atmosphere. |
| Landfill Lifespan | This is an estimate of how long a company's existing landfills can continue to accept waste before reaching capacity. A shorter lifespan could mean the company needs to secure additional landfill space or enhance its recycling capabilities. |
| Landfill Utilization Rate | This KPI indicates the percentage of landfill capacity used versus total capacity. A high rate could mean the company needs to find or create additional landfill space soon, which could entail significant costs. |
| Leachate Production Rate | For landfill operators, this metric quantifies the amount of leachate (liquid that has percolated through the waste) produced. Managing leachate is a significant operational and environmental challenge, so a lower rate can be advantageous. |
| Litter Control Compliance Rate | For landfill operators, this KPI measures compliance with regulations designed to prevent litter from escaping the landfill, which can affect local environments and communities. |
| Maintenance Cost per Vehicle | Given the importance of fleet management in waste collection, tracking the cost of maintaining each vehicle can provide insights into operational efficiency and the potential need for fleet renewal. |
| Material Recovery Facility (MRF) Sorting Accuracy | This KPI tracks the effectiveness of sorting at a Material Recovery Facility. Accurate sorting improves the quality and value of recovered materials. |
| Material Recovery Rate | This KPI, relevant for recycling companies, measures the percentage of incoming material that is successfully recovered and prepared for reuse. A higher rate indicates more efficient operations and greater potential revenue from selling recovered materials. |
| Medical Waste Disposal Compliance Rate | For companies handling medical waste, this KPI indicates the proportion of operations meeting the stringent regulations that govern medical waste disposal. High compliance can reduce legal and reputational risk. |
| Methane Capture Rate | For landfill operators, this KPI quantifies the percentage of methane (a potent greenhouse gas) that is captured and potentially used for energy production, instead of being released into the atmosphere. A higher rate can indicate better environmental performance and potentially additional revenue from the sale of captured methane. |
| Noise Pollution Level | Waste management operations can generate significant noise. This KPI tracks the noise pollution level, which if high, could lead to community complaints and regulatory challenges. |
| Number of Service Disruptions | This KPI measures the number of times a company's services are disrupted due to issues such as equipment failures or labor strikes. Frequent disruptions can be a sign of operational issues and may impact customer satisfaction and retention. |
| Occupational Illness Rate | Given the potential health hazards associated with waste handling, this KPI measures the incidence of occupational illnesses within the company's workforce. |
| Odor Control Efficiency | This KPI is especially important for organic waste and wastewater treatment facilities. It measures the effectiveness of odor control measures, which can impact community relations and regulatory compliance. |
| Organic Waste Percentage | Relevant to companies involved in composting, this KPI represents the percentage of total waste that is organic. Higher percentages could translate into higher revenues from the sale of compost. |
| Percent of Fleet Using Alternative Fuels | This measures the proportion of a company's fleet that uses alternative fuels like natural gas or electricity. High percentages can reduce fuel costs and emissions, aligning with environmental regulations and sustainability goals. |
| Percent of Fleet with Real-time Tracking | Real-time tracking technology can improve route efficiency and customer service. This KPI measures the percentage of a company's fleet equipped with such technology. |
| Percent of Waste Treated On-Site | For industrial waste management companies, this measures the percentage of waste treated on the customer's site as opposed to transported to a different location for treatment. On-site treatment can reduce transportation costs and associated environmental impacts. |
| Percentage of End-of-Life Products Recycled | For companies involved in producer responsibility schemes, this KPI measures the percentage of end-of-life products (like electronics or batteries) that are successfully collected and recycled. |
| Percentage of Waste Repurposed | This KPI represents the amount of waste that is repurposed into other products or uses, beyond just recycling. It provides insight into a company's innovation and its ability to create additional revenue streams. |
| Permit Violation Rate | Given the strict regulatory environment in waste management, this KPI tracks the frequency of violations of environmental permits, which can lead to fines and reputational damage. |
| Plastic Waste Percentage | This measures the percentage of waste collected that is plastic. With increasing focus on plastic pollution and the potential value in recycled plastic, this metric can offer valuable insights for companies involved in plastic waste management. |
| Processing Time per Ton of Waste | This KPI gauges the amount of time it takes to process a ton of waste, indicating the operational efficiency of a waste processing facility. |
| Recycling Contamination Costs | For companies that manage recycling programs, this KPI measures the costs associated with removing non-recyclable materials from the recycling stream. |
| Recycling Rate | This is the percentage of total waste that is recycled. A higher recycling rate can mean greater revenue from selling recyclables and potentially less reliance on landfilling, which can be costly and environmentally damaging. |
| Regulatory Compliance Rate | This is the percentage of operations that meet local, state, and federal environmental and safety regulations. A high compliance rate can reduce the risk of fines, legal issues, and reputational damage, which can all have significant financial impacts. |
| Residential vs Commercial Customer Ratio | This KPI measures the proportion of a waste management company's business that comes from residential versus commercial customers. This can influence the type and volume of waste handled, as well as the stability of revenues. |
| Return on Investment (ROI) for Capital Expenditures | This KPI measures the return on investment for capital expenditures such as new collection vehicles, recycling equipment, or landfill expansions. It provides insight into the efficiency and effectiveness of the company's capital allocation decisions. |
| Revenue Per Pickup | This measures the revenue generated from each pickup, providing insight into pricing and overall profitability at the transactional level. |
| Revenue Per Ton | This measures the revenue generated from the collection, disposal, or recycling of one ton of waste. It provides insight into a company's pricing power and revenue diversification. |
| Safety Incidents Rate | This KPI tracks the number of safety incidents per unit of operation (such as per employee or per ton of waste handled). A lower rate indicates a safer work environment, which can lead to lower insurance costs and improved employee morale and retention. |
| Single Stream Contamination Rate | For recycling companies using single-stream recycling, this measures the percentage of non-recyclable materials in the single-stream collection. A high contamination rate can reduce the value of recycled commodities and increase processing costs. |
| Sludge Processing Efficiency | For companies involved in wastewater treatment, this KPI measures the effectiveness of sludge processing, in terms of volume reduction, energy recovery, or other relevant measures. |
| Tonnage Collected | This is the volume of waste collected and managed. The greater the volume, the larger the company's operational scale. It can be further broken down into categories such as residential, commercial, and industrial waste. |
| Vehicle Fuel Efficiency | Given the high usage of trucks and other vehicles in waste management, fuel efficiency is an important metric. It can influence both operational costs and the company's environmental footprint. |
| Volume of Waste Rerouted from Landfill | This KPI measures the amount of waste a company is able to divert from landfills, either through recycling, composting, waste-to-energy conversion, or other methods. |
| Waste Collection Coverage | This indicates the geographic area or population covered by a company's waste collection services. A larger coverage can mean a larger potential customer base. |
| Waste Diversion Rate | This is the percentage of waste that is diverted from landfill to other processes such as recycling, composting, or waste-to-energy facilities. A higher rate can indicate a more sustainable and potentially more profitable business model. |
| Waste Generation Rate of Contracted Customers | This is the average amount of waste generated by the company's contracted customers. A higher rate may lead to more waste for the company to manage, potentially increasing revenues if the company can handle the additional volume efficiently. |
| Waste Heat Recovery Rate | For waste-to-energy companies, this KPI tracks how much waste heat from their processes is recovered and put to productive use. Higher rates can indicate more efficient operations and potentially additional revenue streams. |
| Waste Segregation Accuracy | For companies with advanced waste processing facilities, the accuracy of waste segregation (i.e., how effectively different types of waste are separated for disposal or recycling) is a key operational metric. |
| Waste Sorting Efficiency | This KPI measures the accuracy and effectiveness of waste sorting operations, which are critical for recycling and recovery activities. |
| Waste-to-Energy Conversion Cost | This KPI measures the cost to convert a unit of waste into energy. It is relevant for waste-to-energy companies and provides insights into their cost structure and efficiency. |
| Waste-to-Energy Efficiency | This KPI is relevant to waste management companies that operate waste-to-energy facilities. It measures the ratio of energy produced to the waste used. A higher ratio indicates better efficiency in converting waste to energy. |
| Water Usage Efficiency | This KPI measures how effectively a company uses water in its operations, which can be significant in activities like landfill operations and wastewater treatment. |
