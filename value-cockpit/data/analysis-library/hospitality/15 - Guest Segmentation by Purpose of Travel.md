# 15 · Guest Segmentation by Purpose of Travel

> **Part:** [[B - Guest Experience]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Classify guests by reason for travel to tailor pricing, marketing, and services that meet each segment’s unique needs and behaviors.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Reservation data indicating the stated purpose of travel (if collected during booking).
- ​ Ancillary spending records (e.g., F&B, spa, activities) tied to guest profiles.
- ​ Demographic or firmographic data (company name for business travelers, home city for leisure travelers).
- ​ Guest feedback or satisfaction scores, if segmented by travel purpose.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Gather all relevant guest data. Pull reservation information, ancillary spend, and any traveler profile details (e.g., reason for stay) from your PMS or CRM. 2.​ Segment guests according to their travel purpose. Classify each guest record into categories such as business, leisure, group/conference, or other specialized segments. 3.​ Calculate key performance metrics for each segment. Look at occupancy contribution, ADR, total revenue, length of stay, and ancillary spend to see how each segment performs. 4.​ Analyze booking channels and stay patterns by segment. Determine how far in advance each segment typically books, which channels they use, and typical length of stay. 5.​ Review guest satisfaction or feedback. Identify differences in satisfaction levels across segments to understand specific needs or pain points.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ A table or spreadsheet with columns for segment name, number of guests, occupancy share, ADR, total revenue, and satisfaction scores.
- ​ Charts or graphs (pie or bar) illustrating the proportion of guests in each segment and their revenue contribution.
- ​ Written summary highlighting major differences in behavior among the segments.

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ A large share of revenue from business travelers might signal the importance of corporate partnerships and loyalty programs.
- ​ Leisure guests might exhibit more price sensitivity but could yield higher ancillary spending on activities and dining.
- ​ Group/conference travelers can significantly impact occupancy during events but may have lower ADRs if negotiated group rates apply.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Develop targeted marketing campaigns for each segment, focusing on their distinct preferences (e.g., business amenities vs. family‐friendly packages). 2.​ Adjust distribution channels and rate strategies based on the booking patterns of different segments. 3.​ Enhance service offerings that are most valued by each group (e.g., faster check‐in for business travelers, activity planning for leisure guests). 4.​ Monitor shifts in segmentation over time to stay ahead of changing travel trends and refine strategies accordingly.
