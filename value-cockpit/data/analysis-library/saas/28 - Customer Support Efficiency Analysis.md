# 28 · Customer Support Efficiency Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Customer Retention & Success]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Customer Support Efficiency analysis is to assess how effectively a SaaS company’s customer support team resolves customer issues. Key metrics such as tickets per customer and resolution time help evaluate the efficiency of the support process and overall customer satisfaction. This analysis enables companies to identify bottlenecks in support operations and ﬁnd opportunities to improve the customer experience.


## 📊 Data required
1. Total number of support tickets opened during a speciﬁc period
2. Number of unique customers served during the same period
3. Average time taken to resolve support tickets (resolution time)
4. Number of support agents (optional, for staffing analysis)
5. Customer satisfaction data (optional, e.g., CSAT or NPS scores post-resolution)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Tickets per Customer Calculation:
1. **Determine the period for analysis:** Select a time period (e.g., monthly or quarterly) to analyze customer support efficiency.
2. **Collect the total number of support tickets opened:** Retrieve the total number of customer support tickets opened during the selected period from your customer support software or CRM system.
3. **Identify the number of unique customers served:** Count how many unique customers submitted support tickets during the same period.
4. **Calculate Tickets per Customer:** Use the following formula to calculate the average number of tickets per customer:
`Tickets per Customer = Total Number of Tickets / Total Number of Unique Customers`
For example, if 500 support tickets were opened by 200 customers during the month, the tickets per customer would be:
`Tickets per Customer = 500 / 200 = 2.5 tickets per customer`
Resolution Time Calculation:

1. **Collect the resolution time for each ticket:** Retrieve the resolution times for all tickets closed during the period (i.e., the time between when a ticket was opened and when it was resolved).
2. **Calculate the average resolution time:** Use the following formula to calculate the average time taken to resolve a support ticket:
`Average Resolution Time = Total Time to Resolve All Tickets / Total Number of Tickets Resolved`
For example, if the total time spent resolving 100 tickets is 3,000 hours, the average resolution time would be:
`Average Resolution Time = 3,000 hours / 100 tickets = 30 hours per ticket`


## 📤 Output format
The output typically includes two key metrics: the average number of tickets per customer and the average resolution time. For example:
•   Tickets per Customer: 2.5 •   Average Resolution Time: 30 hours


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   High Tickets per Customer: A high number of tickets per customer may indicate that customers are encountering frequent issues or are confused by the product. This could point to areas where the user experience needs improvement or where additional training or onboarding is required.
•   Low Tickets per Customer: A low number of tickets per customer suggests that users are generally self-sufficient or that the product is easy to use, resulting in fewer support requests. While this is generally positive, it’s important to ensure that customers aren’t avoiding support due to poor service experiences.
•   Long Resolution Time: Long resolution times could indicate inefficiencies in the support process, such as understaffing, complex issues, or lack of proper resources for support agents. This can lead to lower customer satisfaction and higher churn if left unaddressed.
•   Short Resolution Time: Short resolution times suggest that the support team is effectively resolving customer issues, which likely contributes to higher customer satisfaction and retention.


## 🚀 Steps to improve

1. **Invest in self-service options:** Provide customers with resources such as an FAQ section, help center, or knowledge base. This can reduce the number of tickets submitted, improving tickets per customer and allowing support agents to focus on more complex cases.
2. **Optimize support processes:** Streamline the support workﬂow to improve resolution time. Implement ticket prioritization systems, automated responses for common issues, or routing mechanisms to ensure that the right agent handles the ticket.
3. **OKer customer education:** Enhance customer onboarding with detailed product tutorials and guides to help users resolve issues themselves or avoid common mistakes that lead to support requests. This can reduce the number of tickets per customer.
4. **Improve staKing and training:** Ensure that the support team is adequately staffed to handle the volume of tickets, especially during peak periods. Additionally, provide ongoing training to support agents to improve their product knowledge and problem-solving skills, which can reduce resolution time.
5. **Analyze recurring issues:** Identify the most common problems reported by customers and take steps to address the root cause. This could involve ﬁxing product bugs, improving documentation, or updating the user interface to prevent confusion.
6. **Monitor customer satisfaction:** Collect feedback from customers after support interactions, such as CSAT (Customer Satisfaction Score) or NPS (Net Promoter Score), to ensure that the support team is providing a positive experience. Use this data to further reﬁne your support processes.
7. **Automate repetitive tasks:** Use automation tools like chatbots or AI-powered ticket routing to handle routine queries and free up support agents to focus on more complex issues, thereby improving resolution time.
