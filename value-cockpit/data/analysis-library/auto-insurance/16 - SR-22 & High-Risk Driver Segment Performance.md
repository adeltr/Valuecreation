# 16 · SR-22 & High-Risk Driver Segment Performance

> **Part:** [[B - Underwriting & Risk Segmentation]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate the profitability and claims experience of SR-22 and other high-risk driver segments, and determine if underwriting and pricing strategies appropriately reflect their elevated risk profiles.


## 📊 Data required
- List of SR-22 and high-risk driver policies (written premium, earned premium, in-force policies)
- Claims data (frequency, severity, incurred losses) segmented by SR-22/high-risk vs. standard drivers
- Underwriting guidelines for SR-22 and high-risk designations
- Loss ratio, renewal, and retention data for high-risk segments
- Benchmark data on typical SR-22 driver performance from industry sources


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify policies designated as SR-22 or high-risk, compiling written premium, earned premium, claims, and renewal details for these segments. Separate SR-22 drivers from other high-risk categories for more granular analysis.
2. Calculate claim frequency and severity for both SR-22 drivers and other high-risk insureds, using Claim Frequency = (Number of Claims / Number of Policies) x 100. Compare these metrics to standard driver segments to quantify the elevated risk levels.
3. Determine segment-level loss ratios using Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + LAE) / Earned Premium. Assess whether SR-22/high-risk premiums align with actual claims experience and profitability goals.
4. Review underwriting guidelines specific to SR-22 and high-risk drivers, noting eligibility criteria, surcharges, or coverage limitations. Identify possible gaps or inefficiencies in capturing the true risk.
5. Analyze retention and renewal patterns among SR-22/high-risk drivers to see if premium surcharges significantly impact loyalty. Look for trends in policy cancellations or lapses that may indicate price sensitivity.
6. Benchmark performance against industry data on SR-22 or high-risk segments, comparing average claim costs and loss ratios. Identify any significant deviations that might suggest underpricing or overpricing.

7. Identify ways to segment high-risk drivers further based on behavioral, demographic, or telematics data that may predict claim severity. Consider refining tiers within the SR-22 category to improve underwriting accuracy.
8. Summarize key findings and recommend changes such as adjusting surcharges, refining underwriting guidelines, or introducing risk mitigation programs. Present these recommendations to product and pricing teams for implementation.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables illustrating SR-22/high-risk claim frequency, severity, and loss ratios vs. standard segments
- Charts tracking retention and renewal rates for these segments
- Written summary highlighting pricing adequacy, risk drivers, and suggested underwriting/policy changes


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A higher-than-average claim frequency and severity for SR-22/high-risk drivers is expected and must be offset by appropriate premium surcharges.
- Low retention in this segment can indicate that pricing or underwriting measures may be driving away marginally risky drivers who could become profitable if given risk improvement options.
- Benchmark deviations suggest potential misalignment in pricing, indicating the need for adjustments to maintain profitability and competitive positioning.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Refine underwriting models and surcharges for SR-22/high-risk drivers based on deeper analysis of driving behavior or telematics data.
2. Offer driver rehabilitation courses or coaching programs to encourage safer driving and reduce future claim costs.
3. Monitor competitor rates to remain competitive while maintaining actuarially sound pricing for high-risk segments.
4. Implement tiered pricing or phased renewal approaches to reward proven risk improvement among SR-22/high-risk customers.
