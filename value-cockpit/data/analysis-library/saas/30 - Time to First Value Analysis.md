# 30 · Time to First Value Analysis

> **Part:** [[D - Product & Engagement]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Time to First Value (TTFV) analysis is to measure how long it takes for a new customer to realize their ﬁrst meaningful success with a SaaS product. TTFV is a critical metric that helps SaaS companies understand the efficiency of their onboarding process and how quickly customers start deriving value from the product. A shorter TTFV leads to improved customer satisfaction, higher engagement, and better retention rates.


## 📊 Data required
1. Customer sign-up date or start date
2. Date when the customer ﬁrst achieves a key success metric or milestone (e.g., completing a setup, launching their ﬁrst campaign, or reaching a usage goal)
3. Total number of customers during the analysis period
4. Deﬁnition of "ﬁrst value" (based on product-speciﬁc metrics or goals)
5. **Optional:** Segmentation by customer size, industry, or product usage


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Deﬁne the “First Value” milestone:** First, determine what constitutes "ﬁrst value" for your SaaS product. This is typically the ﬁrst time a customer achieves a signiﬁcant outcome that demonstrates the value of the product. Examples include:
o    Completing a product setup or integration o    Launching a campaign or project within the product o    Using a key feature for the ﬁrst time (e.g., generating a report, adding team members)
2. **Collect sign-up and ﬁrst value dates:** For each customer, record their sign-up date and the date when they reach the predeﬁned ﬁrst value milestone. This data is often available through product analytics, CRM, or customer onboarding platforms.
3. **Calculate the Time to First Value (TTFV) for each customer:** For each customer, calculate the time it takes from the sign-up date to when they achieve the ﬁrst value milestone. Use the following formula:
`Time to First Value (TTFV) = Date of First Value - Customer Sign-up Date`
96 For example, if a customer signs up on January 1 and achieves ﬁrst value on January 10, the TTFV would be:
`TTFV = January 10 - January 1 = 9 days`
4. **Calculate the average TTFV:** To get an overall view, calculate the average TTFV across all customers during the analysis period using this formula:
`Average TTFV = Total TTFV for All Customers / Total Number of Customers`
For example, if the total TTFV across 100 customers is 900 days, the average TTFV would be:
`Average TTFV = 900 days / 100 customers = 9 days`
5. **Segment customers by characteristics (optional):** If needed, break down TTFV by customer segments (e.g., small business vs. enterprise, different industries) to identify differences in how quickly various groups achieve ﬁrst value.


## 📤 Output format
The output is typically presented as the average Time to First Value in days, along with segmented results if applicable. For example:
Average Time to First Value: 9 days Small Business Customers: 7 days Enterprise Customers: 12 days


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
•   Shorter TTFV: A shorter TTFV indicates that customers are quickly realizing value from the product. This is generally a sign of an efficient onboarding process and strong product- market ﬁt. Customers who see value sooner are more likely to engage with the product and continue using it.
•   Longer TTFV: A longer TTFV suggests that customers are taking more time to understand or achieve success with the product. This could indicate onboarding issues, product complexity, or unclear user guidance. A longer TTFV may lead to frustration and higher churn rates if not addressed.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize onboarding processes:** Streamline the onboarding process to reduce friction and help customers reach key milestones faster. This could involve improving product tutorials, offering guided 97 walkthroughs, or using onboarding checklists to ensure customers take the necessary steps to reach ﬁrst value.
2. **Provide personalized customer success support:** Engage with customers proactively, particularly during their initial days or weeks. Offer personalized assistance, such as dedicated customer success managers, live chat, or onboarding webinars to accelerate their time to ﬁrst value.
3. **Enhance product training and education:** Ensure that new users have access to clear and comprehensive training materials, such as video tutorials, how-to guides, and documentation. Helping customers understand the core features and beneﬁts of the product early on can reduce TTFV.
4. **Segment customers for tailored onboarding:** Different customer segments may have different needs and paths to achieving ﬁrst value. Tailor the onboarding experience based on customer size, industry, or use case to provide a more relevant and efficient journey to ﬁrst value.
5. **Track product usage and identify friction points:** Use product analytics to monitor customer behavior during the onboarding process. Identify where customers are getting stuck or dropping oW, and take action to remove barriers or provide more guidance at critical points in the journey.
6. **Automate onboarding with triggered events:** Implement automation to guide users through key steps in the onboarding process. For example, trigger automated emails or in-app messages when customers complete certain milestones or when they appear to be struggling with speciﬁc tasks.
7. **OKer onboarding incentives:** To encourage faster onboarding and product adoption, offer time-limited incentives such as discounts, free product features, or exclusive access to advanced resources for customers who reach ﬁrst value within a certain time frame.
