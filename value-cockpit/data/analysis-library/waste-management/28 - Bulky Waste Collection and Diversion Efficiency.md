# 28 · Bulky Waste Collection and Diversion Efficiency

> **Part:** [[E - Fleet & Collection Optimization]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Waste Management Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate how effectively bulky waste (e.g., furniture, appliances, mattresses) is being collected and diverted from landfill, identify inefficiencies or gaps in the current system, and develop strategies to improve diversion rates and service levels.


## 📊 Data required
- Volume and types of bulky items collected (e.g., furniture, white goods, electronics)
- Current collection methods (scheduled pickups, drop-off centers, on-demand pickups)
- Transportation and handling costs (fuel, labor, maintenance)
- Disposal or processing fees (landfill, recycling, refurbishment costs)
- Existing diversion outlets (reuse organizations, recycling facilities, refurbishers)
- Customer service metrics (collection frequency, response time, complaints)
- Any relevant local regulations or guidance on bulky waste handling


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather data on bulky item volumes and collection patterns Record the total quantity and types of bulky waste items collected over a specific period. Note seasonal or event-based fluctuations (e.g., large furniture disposal after student move-outs).
2. Evaluate current collection methods and costs Analyze scheduled vs. on-demand collection models, including labor and vehicle costs. Identify any routes that incur excessive mileage or time.
3. Assess diversion and disposal pathways Break down how bulky items are processed after collection. Determine if items are landfilled, recycled, refurbished, or donated, and record associated costs or revenues (e.g., scrap metal sales).
4. Calculate diversion rate for bulky waste Compare the weight or volume of bulky items successfully diverted (recycled, reused) to total bulky items collected.

`Bulky Waste Diversion Rate (%) = ((Diverted Bulky Waste) / (Total Bulky Waste Collected)) x 100`
5. Identify bottlenecks and inefficiencies Look for gaps such as insufficient sorting, limited refurbishment partnerships, or logistics challenges leading to missed diversion opportunities.
6. Benchmark against best practices Compare results with similar municipalities or industry standards to gauge performance and spot potential improvements.
7. Develop improvement plan Propose targeted measures to increase diversion, such as expanding partnership networks, optimizing route schedules, or implementing bulk drop-off events.


## 📤 Output format
- A summary report detailing collected volumes, diversion rates, and related costs
- Tables or charts illustrating breakdowns of bulky item categories and their respective diversion outcomes
- Geographic mapping of collection routes to identify inefficiencies or high-volume areas
- Recommendations for operational changes or new partnership opportunities (e.g., reuse centers, repair workshops)


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- A high bulky waste diversion rate indicates effective partnerships, logistics, and public engagement in reuse or recycling.
- Low diversion or high landfilling suggests missed opportunities for refurbishment, resale, or recycling, often tied to process or market limitations.
- Rising complaints or costly operations may signal the need for route optimization or improved service models (e.g., scheduled curbside pickups vs. on-demand).


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Strengthen collaborations with local refurbishers, donation centers, or recyclers to expand end markets for bulky items.
2. Implement dedicated bulky waste pickup days or seasonal collection events to streamline routes and reduce on-demand scheduling costs.

3. Provide public education on proper bulky waste disposal and available diversion options (e.g., donation or repair).
4. Consider establishing a central drop-off facility with sorting and triage for repair, recycling, or reuse channels.
5. Monitor key metrics (cost per pickup, diversion rate, customer satisfaction) regularly to refine the program and target the greatest impact.
