# 27 · Customer Success Team Efficiency

> **Part:** [[C - Customer Retention & Success]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a SaaS Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Customer Success Team Efficiency analysis is to measure the workload and effectiveness of the customer success team, typically by analyzing the ratio of customers to each Customer Success Manager (CSM). This metric helps SaaS companies understand how well the customer success team is managing its customer base, ensuring that customers receive adequate support to achieve their goals and that CSMs aren’t over- or under-utilized. Proper workload management can improve customer satisfaction, reduce churn, and optimize team performance.


## 📊 Data required
1. **Total number of customers:** The number of active customers that the customer success team is currently managing.
2. **Number of Customer Success Managers (CSMs):** The total number of CSMs on the team.
3. **Customer segmentation (optional):** Customer data by segment (e.g., small business, enterprise) to calculate efficiency for different customer types, as workload may vary by customer segment.
4. **Customer satisfaction (optional):** Data on customer satisfaction or Net Promoter Score (NPS) to evaluate the quality of support provided by CSMs.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify the total number of active customers:
o    Gather the total number of active customers that the customer success team is responsible for managing. This could be measured by the number of accounts or subscriptions the team is supporting.
2. Determine the total number of Customer Success Managers (CSMs):
o    Count the number of full-time CSMs currently working in the team, including those managing speciﬁc customer segments (e.g., small business vs. enterprise).
3. Calculate the ratio of customers per CSM:
o    The ratio of customers per CSM provides insight into the workload distribution. A high ratio suggests that each CSM is managing a large number of customers, potentially affecting the quality of support, while a low ratio may indicate underutilization.
`o    Use the formula: Customers per CSM = Total Number of Customers / Number of CSMs`
86 o    For example, if the total number of customers is 500 and there are 10 CSMs:
`Customers per CSM = 500 / 10 = 50 customers per CSM`
4. Segment customer base (optional):
o    If necessary, segment customers by size or value (e.g., small business, mid-market, enterprise) to analyze whether different CSMs have varied workloads based on customer complexity. Larger or more strategic customers may require more attention, so the ratio may differ across segments.
`o    Use the formula: Customers per CSM by Segment = Total Number of Customers in Segment / Number of CSMs Assigned to that Segment`
o    For example, if there are 100 enterprise customers and 5 CSMs managing them:
`Customers per CSM (Enterprise) = 100 / 5 = 20 customers per CSM for enterprise`

5. Analyze customer satisfaction and retention (optional):
o    If available, correlate the customers per CSM ratio with customer satisfaction scores (e.g., NPS) or retention metrics (e.g., churn rate) to understand whether the current workload is affecting service quality. For example, a high customer-to-CSM ratio might lead to lower customer satisfaction or higher churn rates.


## 📤 Output format
The output is typically presented as the ratio of Customers per CSM, along with supporting metrics like customer segmentation or satisfaction. Example format:
•   Total Customers: 500 •   Number of CSMs: 10 •   Customers per CSM: 50 •   Customers per CSM by Segment:
o    Small Business: 100 customers per CSM o    Mid-market: 50 customers per CSM o    Enterprise: 20 customers per CSM •   Customer Satisfaction (NPS): 8.5 This output helps assess how efficiently the customer success team is managing the customer base.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
87 •   High customers per CSM ratio: A high ratio indicates that each CSM is managing a large number of customers, which may strain their capacity to provide personalized support. This could result in lower customer satisfaction, increased response times, and potentially higher churn. It’s important to monitor quality metrics like NPS or churn rate when the ratio is high.
•   Low customers per CSM ratio: A low ratio suggests that each CSM has fewer customers to manage, which may allow for more personalized attention and proactive support. However, it could also indicate underutilization of resources if the team is not handling enough accounts to justify its size.
•   Segmented analysis: If some customer segments (e.g., enterprise clients) have a much lower ratio, it indicates that those customers are receiving more dedicated attention, which may be appropriate given their higher value. In contrast, high-volume, low-value customers may have a higher ratio, reﬂecting a more scalable support model.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize the customer-to-CSM ratio:** Balance workloads by adjusting the number of CSMs per customer segment. For high-value or complex customers, maintain a low customer-to-CSM ratio to ensure personalized support. For lower-value segments, increase the ratio to ensure resources are efficiently utilized.
2. **Leverage technology for low-touch customers:** Implement automation, self-service tools, or customer success platforms to support a higher customer-to-CSM ratio for lower-tier customers. This allows CSMs to focus their time on high-value accounts while maintaining support quality for other customers.
3. **Improve customer segmentation:** Segment the customer base more effectively to tailor the level of support based on customer needs and value. For example, provide more high-touch support to enterprise customers while using automated solutions for smaller customers.
4. **Monitor customer satisfaction and churn:** Regularly track customer satisfaction scores (e.g., NPS) and churn rates by customer segment to ensure that the current CSM workload is not negatively impacting customer outcomes. If satisfaction or retention declines, consider adjusting the workload.
5. **Invest in CSM training and resources:** Ensure that CSMs are well-equipped to handle their customer base by providing them with the necessary training, tools, and resources to manage customer success efficiently. Better training and efficient processes can help each CSM manage more customers without compromising quality.
6. **Expand the team as the customer base grows:** As the customer base expands, regularly reassess the customer-to-CSM ratio to ensure that 88 the team is not overburdened. Hiring additional CSMs or adjusting the workload can help maintain high service levels.
