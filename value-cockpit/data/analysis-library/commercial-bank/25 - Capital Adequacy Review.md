# 25 · Capital Adequacy Review

> **Part:** [[D - Capital, Liquidity, and Funding]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Commercial Bank]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine whether the bank’s capital base is sufficient to absorb unexpected losses and maintain regulatory compliance under normal and stressed conditions.


## 📊 Data required
- Current capital structure (e.g., Common Equity Tier 1, Tier 2 capital)
- Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) calculations by asset class
- Regulatory capital ratios and minimum requirements (e.g., Basel III, local regulations)
- Internal capital adequacy assessment documents (ICAAP)
- Stress testing scenarios and results
- Peer or industry benchmark data


## 🪜 Step-by-step
Step 1: Collect comprehensive data on the bank’s capital components (such as Common Equity Tier 1, Additional Tier 1, and Tier 2) and verify each component’s eligibility under regulatory standards.
Step 2: Review Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) calculations for accuracy, ensuring each asset class is assigned the correct risk weighting according to the relevant regulatory framework.
Step 3: Calculate key capital ratios (for example, CET1 ratio, Tier 1 ratio, Total Capital ratio) and compare them against both regulatory minimums and the bank’s internal capital targets or buffers.
Step 4: Perform stress testing under various adverse scenarios (e.g., economic downturn, market shock) and evaluate potential impacts on capital ratios to determine if additional capital buffers might be needed.
Step 5: Benchmark the bank’s capital ratios against peer banks or industry averages to identify whether the bank’s positioning is competitive and aligned with best practices.
Step 6: Summarize findings, including any observed capital shortfalls, and recommend appropriate strategies for strengthening the capital base—such as raising equity, retaining earnings, or optimizing asset portfolios.

## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing capital components, RWAs, and calculated ratios
- Charts illustrating capital ratio trends over time and under stress scenarios
- Narrative report detailing compliance with regulatory thresholds, peer comparisons, and recommended actions


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- Capital ratios above regulatory minima and adequate buffers indicate a strong capital position, protecting the bank against unexpected losses.
- Results from stress tests that significantly reduce capital ratios may signal potential vulnerabilities requiring proactive capital planning.
- Peer comparisons reveal whether the bank’s capital structure aligns with industry norms or if it faces competitive disadvantages in funding or growth capacity.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Rebalance the capital structure through equity raises or debt refinancing to meet desired capital ratios.
2. Reduce Risk-Weighted Assets by optimizing the loan portfolio and divesting high- risk exposures.
3. Strengthen internal capital generation via improved profitability and dividend policy adjustments.
4. Conduct more frequent or enhanced stress testing to anticipate emerging risks and calibrate capital buffers.
5. Maintain ongoing dialogue with regulators to confirm alignment with evolving capital standards and guidelines.
