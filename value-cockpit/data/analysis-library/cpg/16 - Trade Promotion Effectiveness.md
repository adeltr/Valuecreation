# 16 · Trade Promotion Effectiveness

> **Part:** [[B - Sales]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Trade Promotion Effectiveness analysis in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to evaluate how well trade promotions (e.g., discounts, displays, in-store promotions) drive sales, increase market share, and improve profitability. This analysis helps CPG companies understand whether their trade promotions are delivering a strong return on investment (ROI), enhancing relationships with retail partners, and influencing consumer purchasing behavior. Optimizing trade promotion strategies can lead to better product visibility, more efficient use of resources, and stronger partnerships with retailers.


## 📊 Data required
1. Sales data before, during, and after the promotion period
2. Promotional spending data (e.g., discounts, trade allowances, in-store displays, co-op marketing funds)
3. Product-level profitability and margin data
4. Market share data for the promotion period
5. Redemption rates for promotions (e.g., coupons, discounts, buy-one-get-one-free offers)
6. Competitor promotional activity during the same period
7. Retailer-specific promotional support and execution (e.g., shelf placement, in-store displays, endcaps)
8. Consumer purchase behavior (e.g., purchase frequency, basket size, new vs. returning customers)
9. Incremental sales and volume lift from promotions
10. Trade spend by retailer and region 90


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define the objectives of the promotion:** Identify the goals of the trade promotion, such as increasing short-term sales, boosting market share, clearing out excess inventory, or launching a new product. The objectives will help guide the analysis and determine success metrics.
2. **Gather sales and spending data for the promotional period:** Collect data on sales, volume, and promotional spend for the period before, during, and after the promotion. This includes all forms of trade promotion investment, such as trade allowances, discounts, in-store displays, and promotional funds shared with retailers.
3. **Calculate incremental sales and volume lift:** Determine how much additional sales or volume was generated by the promotion compared to a baseline period without the promotion. The formula for incremental sales is:
`Incremental Sales = Sales During Promotion - Baseline Sales`
For example, if baseline sales (before the promotion) were $100,000 and sales during the promotion were $150,000:
`Incremental Sales = $150,000 - $100,000 = $50,000`
4. **Assess the impact on market share:** Analyze whether the promotion increased your brand’s market share in the category. Track market share before and after the promotion, and calculate the percentage change in market share using the formula:
`Market Share Change (%) = ((Market Share After Promotion - Market Share Before Promotion) / Market Share Before Promotion) x 100`
For example, if your brand’s market share increased from 12% to 14%:
`Market Share Change = ((14 - 12) / 12) x 100 = 16.67%`
5. **Analyze promotional effectiveness by retailer and region:** Break down the effectiveness of the promotion by retailer and geographic region. Some retailers may execute promotions better, leading to stronger sales lift. Compare 91 promotional spending, sales lift, and market share gain across retailers to identify which partners delivered the best results.
6. **Evaluate return on investment (ROI) for the promotion:** Calculate the return on investment (ROI) for each trade promotion to assess whether the promotional spending generated enough additional sales to justify the cost. The formula for promotional ROI is:
`ROI (%) = ((Incremental Sales - Trade Promotion Spend) / Trade Promotion Spend) x 100`
For example, if the incremental sales were $50,000 and the trade promotion spend was $20,000:
`ROI = (($50,000 - $20,000) / $20,000) x 100 = 150%`
A positive ROI indicates that the promotion was profitable, while a negative ROI suggests that the promotion cost more than it generated in additional sales.
7. **Track consumer behavior changes:** Analyze how the promotion influenced consumer behavior, such as whether the promotion attracted new customers or increased purchase frequency for existing customers. Key metrics include: ○ Basket size: Did the promotion encourage larger purchases? ○ Repeat purchase behavior: Did the promotion lead to increased loyalty, or was the sales lift primarily driven by short-term bargain hunters? ○ New vs. returning customers: Did the promotion attract new customers, or did it only appeal to existing ones?
8. **Review execution and compliance by retailers:** Assess whether retailers executed the promotion as planned. Check for compliance with agreed-upon display placements, shelf space, and promotional timing. Poor execution by retailers (e.g., failure to stock products or set up displays) can limit the effectiveness of the promotion.
9. **Benchmark against competitor promotions:** Compare the results of your promotion with competitor activity during the same period. If competitors offered deeper discounts or more prominent displays, assess whether this affected your promotional performance and consider adjustments for future campaigns.
92
10. **Evaluate post-promotion impact:** Analyze sales trends after the promotion to determine if there was a "post-promotion slump" where sales declined once the promotion ended. A significant drop-off may indicate that the promotion only attracted price-sensitive customers, who are unlikely to remain loyal without discounts. Consider strategies to retain customers beyond the promotion.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Trade Promotion Effectiveness analysis typically includes:
- A breakdown of sales, volume, and incremental sales for the promotion period.
- ROI analysis of the trade promotion, including comparisons across retailers and regions.
- Insights into how the promotion impacted market share.
- An assessment of promotional execution and retailer compliance.
- Analysis of consumer behavior changes, such as new vs. returning customers and basket size increases.
- Benchmarking of promotional performance against competitors.
- Recommendations for optimizing future trade promotions based on the results.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High incremental sales with a strong ROI:** Indicates that the promotion was effective in driving additional sales and was profitable. These types of promotions can be repeated or scaled up to other products or regions.
- **High sales lift with low or negative ROI:** While the promotion may have increased sales volume, it might not have been profitable due to high costs. This suggests that the discount or promotional spend may have been too deep or 93 poorly targeted, and future promotions should focus on balancing sales growth with profitability.
- **Minimal sales lift despite significant promotional spend:** This indicates that the promotion was ineffective. It could be due to poor execution, incorrect targeting, or weak consumer interest. Consider reviewing the promotional mechanics, product selection, or retail partner execution.
- **Post-promotion slump:** A sharp decline in sales after the promotion suggests that the sales boost was short-lived, likely driven by price-sensitive shoppers. To mitigate this, consider coupling promotions with loyalty programs or post-promotion engagement strategies to encourage long-term customer retention.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize promotional spend:** Focus promotional dollars on retailers and regions that deliver the highest ROI. Scale back spending on promotions that underperform or fail to drive profitability.
2. **Target promotions to specific customer segments:** Tailor promotions to the most responsive customer segments to maximize sales lift. For example, use data to identify which products appeal to frequent shoppers and target promotions accordingly.
3. **Enhance retailer execution:** Work closely with retail partners to ensure promotions are executed as planned, including proper shelf placement, displays, and stock levels. Poor execution can undermine even the most well-designed promotions.
4. **Refine discount levels:** Use historical data to determine the optimal discount depth that balances sales lift with profitability. Avoid deep discounts that erode margins without delivering sufficient incremental sales.
94
5. **Monitor competitor promotions:** Keep an eye on competitor activity and adjust your promotions accordingly. If competitors are offering more aggressive deals, ensure your promotions are competitive but profitable.
6. **Develop post-promotion retention strategies:** To avoid post-promotion slumps, implement strategies to retain customers after the promotion ends. Consider loyalty programs, personalized offers, or follow-up communications to encourage repeat purchases.
