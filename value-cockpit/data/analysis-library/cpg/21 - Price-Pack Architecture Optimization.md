# 21 · Price-Pack Architecture Optimization

> **Part:** [[B - Sales]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Price-Pack Architecture Optimization in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to assess and refine the range of product sizes (packs) and their corresponding price points to maximize sales, profitability, and consumer appeal. Optimizing the price-pack architecture ensures that the product portfolio meets diverse consumer needs, enhances affordability perceptions, and aligns with retail strategies. This analysis helps CPG companies strike the right balance between price sensitivity, product value, and profitability across different channels and consumer segments.


## 📊 Data required
1. Sales data by SKU, pack size, and price point
2. Profit margin data for each pack size
3. Competitor price-pack architecture (e.g., pack sizes and pricing strategies)
4. Consumer purchase behavior data (e.g., purchase frequency, basket size, price sensitivity)
5. Retailer-specific pricing strategies and margin requirements
6. Category growth trends and consumer demand patterns (e.g., preference for larger or smaller packs)
7. Promotional and discounting impact on price-pack performance
8. Channel-specific pricing strategies (e.g., e-commerce vs. traditional retail)
9. Shopper demographics and income segmentation
10. Feedback from retail partners on price-pack performance


## 🪜 Step-by-step
120
1. **Define the objectives of the analysis:** Set clear goals for optimizing the price-pack architecture, such as increasing sales volume, improving profit margins, expanding into new consumer segments, or gaining market share. For example, a goal could be to identify the most profitable pack sizes for specific consumer groups or to adjust pricing to better compete in a crowded category.
2. **Analyze sales and profitability by pack size:** Collect sales data for each SKU, including different pack sizes, and assess how each pack size contributes to overall sales and profitability. The formula for profit margin is:
`Profit Margin (%) = ((Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue) x 100`
For example, if a pack generates $200,000 in revenue with a cost of goods sold (COGS) of $120,000:
`Profit Margin = (($200,000 - $120,000) / $200,000) x 100 = 40%`
Identify the most and least profitable pack sizes to determine which packs should be prioritized or re-evaluated.
3. **Assess consumer purchase behavior and price sensitivity:** Review consumer behavior data to understand how different pack sizes and price points influence purchase decisions. Key metrics to consider include: ○ Purchase frequency: How often do consumers buy each pack size? ○ Basket size: How do pack sizes impact overall basket value? ○ Price elasticity: How sensitive are consumers to changes in price? Use historical data to assess whether price changes lead to a significant drop or increase in demand.
4. Price elasticity can be calculated using the formula:
`Price Elasticity of Demand = (% Change in Quantity Demanded / % Change in Price)`
For example, if a 10% price increase leads to a 5% decrease in quantity demanded:
`Price Elasticity = -5% / 10% = -0.5 (indicating inelastic demand).`
5. **Benchmark against competitor price-pack architectures:** Analyze competitor strategies to understand how they structure their price-pack offerings. Compare 121 their pack sizes, price points, and promotional strategies. If competitors are offering smaller or larger packs at more competitive prices, adjust your architecture accordingly to avoid losing market share.
6. **Segment consumer groups based on pack size preferences:** Group consumers based on their purchasing behavior and preferences for different pack sizes. For example, price-sensitive consumers may prefer smaller, lower-cost packs, while value-seeking consumers may opt for larger packs with lower per-unit costs. Understanding these segments can help tailor the price-pack architecture to different shopper needs.
7. **Align price-pack architecture with channel strategies:** Evaluate how price-pack performance varies by sales channel (e.g., e-commerce, traditional retail, wholesale). Some channels may benefit from larger pack sizes or multi-packs (e.g., club stores or bulk-buying consumers), while others may require smaller, convenience-oriented packs (e.g., drugstores or impulse-buy locations). Adjust pack sizes and price points to match the preferences of shoppers in each channel.
8. **Analyze the impact of promotions on price-pack performance:** Track how promotions and discounts affect the sales of different pack sizes. For example, promotional discounts may drive higher sales of larger packs, but at the cost of margin erosion. Calculate promotional sales uplift and assess whether promotional pricing leads to long-term gains in market share or is merely a short-term boost. Sales Uplift (%) = ((Sales During Promotion - Baseline Sales) / Baseline Sales) x 100 For example, if sales during a promotion increase from $50,000 to $70,000:
`Sales Uplift = (($70,000 - $50,000) / $50,000) x 100 = 40%`
9. **Align price-pack architecture with retailer goals:** Collaborate with retail partners to ensure that your price-pack architecture aligns with their sales and margin goals. Retailers may have specific requirements for margins, pack sizes, 122 and promotional strategies. Working closely with retail partners can improve shelf placement, promotional support, and overall category performance.
10. **Incorporate category growth trends and consumer demand patterns:** Analyze category trends to identify shifts in consumer demand for certain pack sizes. For example, larger pack sizes may be growing in popularity as consumers seek greater value, while convenience-driven shoppers may prefer smaller, single-serve packs. Align your price-pack architecture with these trends to capture emerging opportunities.
11. **Test and iterate price-pack architecture:** Use data-driven experimentation to test different pack sizes and price points. For example, launch new pack sizes in select markets or channels and measure their performance. Adjust the price-pack architecture based on consumer feedback, sales data, and margin performance to continuously optimize the portfolio.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Price-Pack Architecture Optimization analysis typically includes:
- A breakdown of sales, profit margins, and market share by pack size and price point.
- Consumer behavior insights, including purchase frequency, basket size, and price sensitivity.
- Competitor benchmarking data, showing differences in pack sizes and pricing strategies.
- Recommendations for adjusting pack sizes and price points to optimize performance.
- Analysis of channel-specific pricing strategies and the performance of pack sizes across different sales channels.

- Promotional impact data, highlighting the effect of discounts and promotions on pack size performance.
- Retailer alignment insights, including opportunities to improve margin and shelf placement through price-pack adjustments.
- Testing results from new pack sizes and price points, with recommendations for scaling successful offerings.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High sales volume with strong margins:** Indicates that the current pack size and pricing strategy is effective. Continue to prioritize these pack sizes and maintain or incrementally adjust prices to ensure ongoing profitability.
- **Low sales volume with high margins:** If a pack size generates strong margins but low sales, consider promotional strategies or price reductions to increase volume while maintaining profitability.
- **High sales volume but low margins:** If a pack size drives high sales but erodes margins, assess whether the price is too low relative to costs. Consider raising the price or adjusting the pack size to improve profitability without sacrificing demand.
- **Competitors outperforming in certain pack sizes:** If competitors offer more competitive price-pack options (e.g., larger packs at better value), adjust your strategy to meet consumer expectations for value and price. Ensure that your offerings are competitive in the market.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Adjust pricing for price-sensitive segments:** Based on consumer segmentation, adjust the pricing of smaller, convenience-oriented packs to 124 appeal to price-sensitive shoppers. Offer value packs for consumers who are willing to spend more upfront for a lower per-unit price.
2. **Expand pack sizes to capture different segments:** Introduce new pack sizes that align with emerging consumer trends, such as bulk packs for cost-conscious households or smaller packs for on-the-go convenience shoppers.
3. **Optimize promotional strategies by pack size:** Use promotions strategically to boost sales of underperforming pack sizes or introduce new ones. Ensure that promotional discounts do not significantly erode margins but still incentivize trial and repeat purchases.
4. **Align with retailer pricing strategies:** Work closely with retailers to ensure that the price-pack architecture aligns with their sales and profitability goals. Negotiate better shelf space or promotional support in exchange for offering pack sizes that meet retailer preferences.
5. **Test and iterate price-pack offerings:** Continuously test new price-pack configurations in select markets or channels to gauge consumer response. Use these learnings to refine the architecture and scale successful offerings across broader markets.
6. **Incorporate feedback into product offerings:** Gather consumer feedback on pack sizes, price points, and perceived value. Use this data to fine-tune your offerings and create a price-pack architecture that resonates with your target market.
