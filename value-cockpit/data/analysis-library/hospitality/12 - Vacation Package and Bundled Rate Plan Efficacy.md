# 12 · Vacation Package and Bundled Rate Plan Efficacy

> **Part:** [[A - Revenue & Pricing Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Hospitality Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Determine how bundled packages impact occupancy, revenue, and guest satisfaction, so you can optimize combined offerings to drive bookings and profitability.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Number of packages sold versus standard room‐only bookings within a specific time frame.
- ​ Breakdown of package components (e.g., room rate, F&B inclusions, activities).
- ​ Revenue and cost data for each package component.
- ​ Guest feedback or satisfaction scores related to bundled offerings.
- ​ Comparative data on ancillary revenue from guests not purchasing packages.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Identify the time frame and scope for the analysis. Select a period (e.g., quarterly or monthly) during which vacation packages were actively promoted. 2.​ Collect sales and revenue data. Gather information on total packages sold, package rates, and revenue generated from add‐ons. 3.​ Break down costs per package. Calculate the cost of each component (accommodation, dining, activities) included in the bundle. 4.​ Compare packaged bookings to room‐only bookings. Assess differences in average daily rate, length of stay, and ancillary spending behaviors. 5.​ Analyze guest satisfaction scores and feedback. Determine if package purchasers report higher satisfaction, leading to potential repeat business. 6.​ Evaluate profitability of packaged plans. Use a formula such as:​ Package Profit = Package Revenue – (Cost of Inclusions + Marketing/Distribution Costs) 7.​ Benchmark results with previous promotions or competitive offerings (if available). Identify whether the current bundles outperform past or competitor packages.


## 📤 Output format
- **​ A table comparing the following metrics for package vs. standard bookings:** total bookings, ADR, total revenue, net profit margin, and average length of stay.
- ​ Charts or graphs illustrating trends in package adoption rate and revenue over time.
- ​ Written insights on guest sentiment regarding packaged offerings.

## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ Higher package sales combined with increased net profit indicate successful bundling strategies.
- ​ If bundles boost occupancy but reduce overall ADR, carefully assess whether additional ancillary spending offsets the discount.
- ​ Positive guest feedback tied to package value may suggest increased loyalty and potential word‐of‐mouth marketing benefits.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Refine package components based on guest feedback and profitability analysis (e.g., swap low‐value inclusions for more desirable perks). 2.​ Test segmented offers (e.g., couples’ getaways, family packages) to appeal to specific customer groups. 3.​ Continuously adjust pricing to balance perceived value with profitability. 4.​ Use targeted digital marketing to promote packages to relevant segments (e.g., repeat guests, loyalty members, holiday travelers).
