# 8 · Grade Quality and Metallurgical Recovery Analysis

> **Part:** [[A - Resource and Reserve Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Metals & Mining Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to evaluate the quality of the ore grade and the efficiency of metallurgical recovery processes. Understanding these factors helps optimize operational decisions, forecast production outputs, and improve profitability by maximizing recoverable metals or minerals.


## 📊 Data required
- Assay results showing average ore grade (e.g., grams per ton for gold, percent concentration for other minerals).
- Mineralogical composition of the ore (e.g., sulfides, oxides).
- Metallurgical test results indicating recovery rates for various processing methods.
- Historical production data (grades and recoveries achieved).
- Processing plant performance metrics.
- Commodity price forecasts.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Gather Ore Grade Data:** ○ Collect assay data from drilling and sampling reports to determine the average grade of ore across the deposit. ○ Segment data by zones or ore types if grades vary significantly within the deposit.
2. **Evaluate Mineralogical Composition:** ○ Identify the dominant minerals and their impact on metallurgical recovery (e.g., refractory minerals may reduce recovery rates).
3. **Analyze Historical Recovery Rates:** ○ Review historical recovery data from the processing plant to establish benchmarks.
○ Identify any deviations or inefficiencies in current recovery processes.
4. **Calculate Recovery Rate:** ○ Use the formula:
`Recovery Rate (%) = (Recovered Metal / Contained Metal in Ore) x 100 ○ Calculate this rate for individual processing steps and overall plant performance.`
5. Perform Grade-Recovery Relationship Analysis:
25 ○ Plot ore grade against recovery rates to identify patterns, such as diminishing returns for lower grades. ○ Use regression analysis to determine the optimal grade range for economic recovery.
6. **Assess Metallurgical Test Results:** ○ Review laboratory tests to evaluate the effectiveness of alternative processing methods, such as flotation, leaching, or gravity separation.
7. **Conduct Economic Analysis:** ○ Calculate the recoverable value per ton of ore using the formula:
`Recoverable Value = (Grade x Recovery Rate x Commodity Price). ○ Compare recoverable value to processing costs to assess economic viability.`
8. **Benchmark Performance:** ○ Compare ore grades and recovery rates with industry averages or peer companies to evaluate competitiveness.
9. **Identify Improvement Opportunities:** ○ Highlight areas for improvement, such as optimizing process parameters or upgrading equipment.


## 📤 Output format
- Grade and Recovery Summary Table showing average grade, recovery rates, and recoverable value.
- Grade-Recovery Relationship Chart illustrating patterns or thresholds.
- Process Optimization Recommendations Report with insights on inefficiencies and suggested improvements.
- Benchmarking Graph comparing performance to industry standards.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Grades with High Recovery Rates:** Indicates strong operational and economic performance.
- **Low Grades with Low Recovery Rates:** Suggests the need for enhanced processing techniques or selective mining.
- **Significant Variance in Recovery Rates:** Points to inefficiencies in processing or challenges with ore variability.
- **Improving Recovery Rates Over Time:** Reflects successful process optimization efforts.

2. Upgrade processing equipment or adopt innovative technologies to improve recovery rates.

4. Optimize reagent use in processing circuits to improve mineral separation efficiency.
5. Train plant operators in advanced recovery techniques to minimize losses.
6. Evaluate alternative processing methods for challenging ore types, such as refractory ores or fine-grained materials.
7. Collaborate with research institutions or industry experts to explore cutting-edge recovery solutions.
