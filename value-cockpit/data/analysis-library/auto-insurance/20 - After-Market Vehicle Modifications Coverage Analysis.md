# 20 · After-Market Vehicle Modifications Coverage Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Underwriting & Risk Segmentation]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Assess how after-market modifications (e.g., performance enhancements, appearance upgrades) affect a vehicle’s risk profile, and determine whether current coverage terms, underwriting guidelines, and pricing accurately reflect the associated exposure.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Vehicle details showing type and extent of after-market modifications
- ​ Claims data (frequency, severity, total incurred losses) for modified vs. stock vehicles
- ​ Premium and exposure data (written premium, earned premium) broken down by modification category
- ​ Underwriting guidelines outlining acceptable vs. restricted modifications
- ​ Benchmark or industry data on claims and pricing for modified vehicles


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Identify all policies listing after-market modifications by extracting modification details, vehicle make/model, and associated coverage endorsements. Gather claims information, premium data, and any underwriting notes in a unified dataset. 2.​ Compare claim frequency and severity between modified and stock vehicles, using Claim Frequency = (Number of Claims / Total Policies) x 100 and Claim Severity = (Total Losses / Number of Claims). Look for significant differences tied to specific modification types or degrees of alteration. 3.​ Calculate the loss ratio for the modified vehicle segment using Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + LAE) / Earned Premium. Benchmark these results against the standard vehicle portfolio to measure profitability gaps. 4.​ Review underwriting guidelines to determine how modifications are documented and rated. Check if certain high-risk modifications (e.g., engine performance upgrades) are adequately surcharged or excluded. 5.​ Segment modified vehicles by categories such as cosmetic, performance, or off-road enhancements. Note claim trends within each category to see which modifications correlate with higher claim costs. 6.​ Benchmark your findings against industry or competitor data if available. Identify significant deviations that suggest potential mispricing or underwriting gaps.
45 7.​ Analyze potential fraud or misrepresentation by comparing declared modifications with claim photos or repair shop data. Recognize if undisclosed modifications appear frequently in claims, leading to unanticipated severity. 8.​ Document recommendations like adjusting premiums for specific modification types, tightening underwriting rules, or partnering with specialty repair shops. Present these to underwriting, product, and pricing teams for final review.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ Tables or charts showing modification categories, claim frequency, severity, and loss ratios
- ​ Segmented summaries of performance vs. cosmetic modifications
- ​ Written report highlighting key findings, pricing/underwriting recommendations, and potential financial impact


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ Higher-than-average loss ratios in certain modification categories may indicate insufficient surcharges or lax underwriting.
- ​ Consistent trends across multiple years suggest modification-driven risk is not random but systematically underpriced.
- ​ Unusually high severity for certain modifications (e.g., extreme engine boosts) signals the need for stricter coverage terms or exclusions.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Refine underwriting questionnaires and inspection protocols to capture all modifications accurately. 2.​ Implement tiered pricing or surcharges based on modification categories and demonstrated claim risk. 3.​ Use third-party data sources or partnerships with specialty garages to verify modifications and reduce fraud. 4.​ Review and update guidelines regularly to keep pace with emerging modification trends and technologies.
