# 12 · Accident Forgiveness Program Profitability Assessment

> **Part:** [[A - Pricing & Product Strategy]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Evaluate whether accident forgiveness offerings enhance customer retention and loyalty without compromising overall profitability, and determine if forgiven claims are adequately offset by premiums or other cost-saving measures.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Policies indicating accident forgiveness enrollment
- ​ Claims data (frequency, severity, total incurred losses) for forgiven vs. non-forgiven accidents
- ​ Premium and exposure data (earned premium, number of policies) split by accident forgiveness status
- ​ Renewal and retention data categorized by accident history and forgiveness usage
- ​ Competitor or market benchmarking data on accident forgiveness trends (if available)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Identify policies with accident forgiveness features. Gather data on premium, claim details, and retention for these policies, plus a comparable control group without accident forgiveness. 2.​ Calculate claim frequency and severity for forgiven accidents and compare them to standard accidents. Check for any differences in claim costs that might indicate a change in driver behavior after forgiveness. 3.​ Assess overall loss ratio by using Loss Ratio = (Incurred Losses + LAE) / Earned Premium for accident forgiveness policies. Compare these results against non-forgiveness policies to see if profitability is affected. 4.​ Review retention and renewal rates among policyholders who have had a forgiven accident. Determine if forgiveness significantly increases customer loyalty or if it merely attracts higher-risk drivers. 5.​ Analyze subsequent claims made by drivers with forgiven accidents to see if they exhibit heightened loss frequency or severity. Look for patterns suggesting moral hazard (i.e., riskier driving due to forgiveness). 6.​ Benchmark your accident forgiveness outcomes against competitor or industry data where possible. Identify major deviations that could indicate overpricing or underpricing of the program.
28 7.​ Evaluate the net financial impact by factoring in premium gains from higher retention against the cost of forgiven claims. Consider whether the program leads to cross-selling or bundling opportunities. 8.​ Document conclusions and recommendations such as adjusting premium surcharges, altering forgiveness eligibility criteria, or refining communication strategies. Present findings to product, pricing, and underwriting teams for potential modifications.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ Tables or charts comparing claim frequency, severity, and loss ratios between forgiven and non-forgiven accidents
- ​ Retention and renewal metrics for accident forgiveness vs. control group
- ​ Written summary outlining key findings, financial impact, and any suggested program changes


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ An increase in retention without a disproportionate rise in forgiven claim costs indicates the program may be successful and profitable.
- ​ Higher-than-expected claim frequency or severity in the forgiveness cohort may suggest moral hazard or insufficient eligibility thresholds.
- ​ Close alignment with competitor benchmarks can mean the program is competitively structured, while significant deviation calls for deeper investigation.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Refine eligibility criteria for accident forgiveness to ensure it targets safer drivers and prevents abuse. 2.​ Adjust premium contributions or surcharges to offset the financial impact of forgiven claims. 3.​ Incorporate driver education or safe driving incentives alongside accident forgiveness to reduce moral hazard. 4.​ Regularly track and benchmark program metrics against market norms to stay competitive and profitable.
