# 8 · Shopper Marketing and Path-to-Purchase Insights

> **Part:** [[A - Marketing]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Consumer Packaged Goods Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of a Shopper Marketing and Path-to-Purchase Insights analysis in the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry is to understand how consumers move through the buying process and what marketing tactics or touchpoints influence their purchasing decisions. This analysis helps CPG companies identify key moments in the shopper's journey where targeted marketing efforts can drive conversions and influence behavior, both in-store and online. By mapping the shopper journey, companies can optimize their strategies to enhance product visibility, engage consumers at the right time, and increase sales.


## 📊 Data required
1. Shopper demographics and psychographics (e.g., age, income, shopping habits, preferences)
2. Purchase behavior data (e.g., time of purchase, frequency, shopping channel)
3. **Path-to-purchase data (e.g., customer touchpoints:** ads, websites, social media, in-store displays)
4. In-store data (e.g., foot traffic patterns, shelf placement, planogram compliance)
5. Online shopping behavior (e.g., website traffic, product views, cart abandonment rates)
6. Marketing campaign performance data (e.g., promotional lift, coupon redemption, digital ad click-through rates)
7. Customer surveys and feedback (e.g., motivations for purchase, barriers to purchase)
8. Competitor marketing and shopper behavior data (e.g., promotions, in-store activation tactics)
9. Retailer-specific data (e.g., retailer promotions, in-store marketing tactics)

## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Map the shopper journey:** Identify the different stages in the path-to-purchase, from awareness to decision-making. Typically, the stages include: ○ Awareness: The consumer becomes aware of a product through advertisements, promotions, or word-of-mouth. ○ Consideration: The consumer starts researching and evaluating options, comparing brands and prices. ○ Purchase decision: The consumer makes a final decision and buys the product, either in-store or online. ○ Post-purchase behavior: The consumer’s experience with the product, which may influence repeat purchases or brand advocacy.
2. **Collect shopper data across channels:** Use both online and offline data to capture insights about shopper behavior. This includes tracking in-store movements (e.g., how shoppers navigate aisles) and online interactions (e.g., time spent on product pages, click-through rates from digital ads). Data sources can include loyalty programs, point-of-sale data, and digital analytics tools.
3. **Analyze in-store shopper behavior:** Evaluate how shoppers interact with products in the physical store. Key metrics include: ○ Foot traffic patterns: Determine the high-traffic areas of the store and how product placement affects sales. ○ Shelf placement and planogram compliance: Evaluate whether product visibility on the shelf is optimal. Products at eye level or near checkout tend to get more attention. ○ In-store marketing effectiveness: Assess the impact of in-store displays, endcaps, and signage on purchase decisions.
4. **Analyze online shopper behavior:** For e-commerce and digital channels, analyze how shoppers engage with the brand and products online. Key metrics include:
43 ○ Product page views and engagement: Track how often product pages are viewed and how much time shoppers spend on them. ○ Cart abandonment rates: Identify how many shoppers add items to their cart but do not complete the purchase. This can indicate barriers in the purchasing process. ○ Click-through rates (CTR) on digital ads: Assess how well online ads drive traffic to product pages or e-commerce platforms.
5. **Examine path-to-purchase touchpoints:** Identify the marketing touchpoints that influence the shopper's decision to purchase. These can include: ○ Advertising campaigns (TV, digital, social media): Evaluate how different types of advertising impact awareness and consideration. ○ In-store displays and promotions: Measure the effectiveness of in-store tactics, such as discounts, coupons, or product sampling. ○ Influencer marketing: Assess the role that social media influencers play in guiding shopper behavior and driving conversions.
6. **Assess promotional effectiveness:** Analyze how different promotional strategies (e.g., discounts, coupons, BOGO offers) affect shopper behavior. Key metrics include: ○ Promotional lift: Measure the increase in sales during a promotional period compared to a non-promotional period. ○ Coupon redemption rates: Track how often shoppers use physical or digital coupons to make a purchase.
7. **Segment shoppers by behavior and preferences:** Group shoppers based on their purchasing behavior, preferences, and demographics. Common shopper segments might include: ○ Bargain hunters: Shoppers primarily motivated by discounts and promotions. ○ Brand loyalists: Shoppers who consistently buy the same brand regardless of promotions.
44 ○ Impulse buyers: Shoppers who make spontaneous purchases based on in-store or online marketing cues. ○ Omnichannel shoppers: Consumers who research products online but make purchases in-store, or vice versa.
8. **Identify friction points and optimize the shopper experience:** Analyze areas in the shopper journey where friction may be occurring, such as poor product visibility, confusing navigation, or long checkout processes. Recommendations for improvement could include better in-store signage, optimized shelf placement, or simplified checkout processes online.
9. **Evaluate the role of retailers in the shopper journey:** Collaborate with retail partners to understand how their in-store and online platforms influence the shopper journey. Retailer-specific promotions, loyalty programs, or product placements can all have a significant impact on the path-to-purchase.


## 📤 Output format
The output of a Shopper Marketing and Path-to-Purchase Insights analysis typically includes:
- A visual map of the shopper journey, highlighting key touchpoints and interactions.
- Data on shopper behavior (both online and in-store), including foot traffic, product engagement, and conversion rates.
- An assessment of promotional effectiveness, including the impact of discounts, in-store marketing, and digital campaigns.
- Segmentation of shoppers based on behavior and preferences, with key insights into each group.
- Identification of friction points in the shopper journey and recommendations for optimizing the shopping experience.
45
- A summary of retailer-specific insights, focusing on how their marketing and promotions influence the shopper’s path to purchase.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High conversion rates with low engagement:** This indicates that while fewer shoppers are interacting with marketing touchpoints, those who do are more likely to make a purchase. Consider ways to expand engagement to reach a larger audience.
- **High foot traffic but low conversions:** High foot traffic in-store or on a website but low purchase rates could indicate barriers to purchase, such as poor product visibility, inadequate information, or inconvenient checkout processes.
- **Strong promotional lift but low long-term sales:** A high sales spike during promotional periods followed by a drop in sales may indicate that the brand is too reliant on discounts, with shoppers only purchasing when promotions are available. Consider focusing on value propositions that extend beyond price promotions.
- **Low cart abandonment rates but high drop-offs earlier in the journey:** If shoppers are not even reaching the cart, there may be issues with product discovery, product information, or navigation. Improving the shopper’s path to the cart could lead to higher conversion rates.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Enhance in-store visibility:** Improve product placement and signage within stores, ensuring key products are easy to find and attract attention. Use eye-level shelving and endcaps for featured products.
46
2. **Streamline the online purchase process:** Reduce cart abandonment by simplifying the online checkout process. Ensure that payment options are clear, shipping costs are transparent, and there are minimal steps to complete the purchase.
3. **Tailor promotions to shopper segments:** Offer targeted promotions based on shopper behavior. For example, offer discounts to price-sensitive shoppers or loyalty rewards to brand advocates.
4. **Improve cross-channel consistency:** Ensure that branding, product availability, and messaging are consistent across both physical and digital touchpoints, providing a seamless omnichannel experience.
5. **Use data to personalize marketing:** Leverage data to personalize marketing efforts at key touchpoints in the shopper journey. Use targeted ads, personalized email campaigns, or loyalty offers to engage shoppers at different stages of the journey.
6. **Collaborate with retailers:** Work closely with retailers to optimize product placement, in-store displays, and joint promotions that align with the brand’s marketing strategy and enhance the shopper experience.
