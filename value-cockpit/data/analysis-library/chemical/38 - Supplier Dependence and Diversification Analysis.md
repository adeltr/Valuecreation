# 38 · Supplier Dependence and Diversification Analysis

> **Part:** [[G - Supply Chain]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Chemical Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of this analysis is to evaluate the company’s dependence on key suppliers and assess diversification levels within the supply chain. By understanding supplier dependency, the company can identify risks associated with over-reliance, plan for alternative sources, and develop a resilient sourcing strategy that reduces supply chain disruptions and enhances bargaining power.


## 📊 Data required
1. List of key suppliers by product or material, including primary feedstocks and critical components
2. Spend allocation per supplier (percentage of total procurement spend)
3. Supplier performance metrics (e.g., on-time delivery rate, quality issues)
4. Geographic location of suppliers and associated geopolitical or environmental risks
5. Contract terms, including exclusivity clauses and lead times
6. Potential alternative suppliers and their capacity, quality, and costs
7. Industry benchmarks for supplier diversification and risk management


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify Key Suppliers and Assess Dependence Levels Create a list of key suppliers, including their geographic location, materials supplied, and the company’s reliance on each one (measured by spend allocation or volume). High dependence on a single supplier for critical materials increases supply chain vulnerability.
2. Analyze Spend Allocation and Supplier Concentration Calculate the percentage of total procurement spend allocated to each supplier. High spend concentration with a few suppliers suggests dependency, indicating potential supply risks and the need for diversification.
3. Evaluate Supplier Performance and Reliability Review performance metrics, such as on-time delivery rates, quality consistency, and responsiveness. Dependence on suppliers with frequent delays or quality issues poses additional risks, suggesting the need for alternative sourcing options.
4. Assess Geopolitical and Environmental Risks for Each Supplier Identify suppliers located in regions with potential geopolitical instability, regulatory issues, or environmental risks. These factors can lead to supply disruptions, making it important to diversify sources geographically to reduce exposure to regional risks.
5. Review Contract Terms and Flexibility Analyze the contract terms with each supplier, focusing on exclusivity clauses, minimum purchase requirements, and lead times. Rigid terms limit flexibility, making it difficult to switch suppliers or adapt to changes in demand.

6. Identify and Evaluate Alternative Suppliers Research potential alternative suppliers for high-dependency materials. Assess each alternative based on cost, quality, capacity, and lead times. Having reliable alternatives enables the company to mitigate risks associated with supplier dependency.
7. Benchmark Against Industry Standards for Supplier Diversification Compare the company’s supplier dependency and diversification levels with industry benchmarks. This benchmarking highlights if the company is overly reliant on a small number of suppliers compared to competitors and provides insights into best practices for supplier risk management.


## 📤 Output format
1.   Supplier dependency summary, showing spend allocation and dependency levels
2.   Supplier performance and reliability assessment
3.   Geographic risk analysis for each supplier location
4.   Contract flexibility and terms review
5.   List of alternative suppliers with a summary of their capacities and quality
6.   Benchmark comparison for supplier diversification and dependency


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High dependency on few suppliers with poor flexibility:** Indicates a supply chain vulnerability; consider diversifying suppliers or renegotiating terms to reduce dependency.
- **High supplier performance and reliability:** Suggests that current suppliers are strong partners, reducing the need for immediate diversification but still warranting contingency planning.
- **High geographic or geopolitical risk:** Points to the need for alternative suppliers in low-risk regions to avoid disruptions and enhance supply chain resilience.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Diversify Supplier Base for Critical Materials:** Identify and qualify secondary suppliers for high-dependency materials to reduce reliance on any single source and improve supply chain resilience.
2. **Negotiate More Flexible Contract Terms:** Work with suppliers to introduce non-exclusive clauses, flexible order volumes, and shorter lead times to improve adaptability and reduce risk.
3. **Implement a Supplier Performance Monitoring Program:** Regularly monitor supplier performance metrics to proactively identify and address reliability issues, ensuring dependable supply channels.
4. **Build Regional and Local Sourcing Options:** Explore sourcing options closer to manufacturing sites or in politically stable regions to minimize risks associated with long-distance or high-risk regions.
