# 10 · Embedded Derivatives Valuation and Sensitivity Testing

> **Part:** [[B - Actuarial and Reserve Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the fair value of embedded derivatives within life insurance products (e.g., guaranteed minimum benefits, interest rate guarantees) and assess their sensitivity to changes in market variables. This analysis ensures proper financial valuation, risk management, and compliance with accounting standards (e.g., IFRS 9, IFRS 17).


## 📊 Data required
1. **Policy-level data:** ○ Policies containing embedded derivatives (e.g., variable annuities, universal life policies). ○ Details of the embedded derivative terms (e.g., guaranteed minimum withdrawal, guaranteed returns).
2. **Financial data:** ○ Discount rates, risk-free rates, and market volatility assumptions. ○ Historical claims or redemptions linked to embedded derivatives.
3. **Actuarial assumptions:** ○ Mortality and lapse rates. ○ Expected policyholder behavior.
4. **Market data:** ○ Yield curves, interest rate trends, and equity market performance. ○ Economic scenarios for stress testing.
5. Industry benchmarks for valuation and risk practices.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify Embedded Derivatives a. Catalog all policies containing features classified as embedded derivatives (e.g., guaranteed minimum benefits or equity-linked returns). b. Confirm their classification under financial reporting standards requiring fair valuation.
2. Select a Valuation Methodology a. Use financial models appropriate for the derivative type, such as: ■ Black-Scholes Model for simple options. ■ Monte Carlo Simulation for complex guarantees with path dependencies.
3. Calculate the Fair Value a. Use the chosen model to determine the present value of the embedded derivative.
29 b. Formula for simple guarantees:
`Fair Value = Expected Payout x Discount Factor`
c. For complex guarantees, simulate multiple paths to project possible outcomes and calculate the expected payout.
4. Assess Sensitivity to Key Variables a. Perform sensitivity testing by varying input parameters, such as: ■ Interest rates (e.g., +/- 100 basis points). ■ Market volatility (e.g., +/- 10%). ■ Lapse and mortality rates. b. Record changes in fair value for each parameter shift.
5. Stress Testing Under Extreme Scenarios a. Model scenarios such as prolonged low-interest-rate environments or sharp equity market declines. b. Quantify the impact on derivative valuation and reserve requirements.
6. Benchmark Against Industry Standards a. Compare valuation methods, fair values, and sensitivities with industry benchmarks to identify gaps or alignment with best practices.
7. Generate Visualizations a. Create tables summarizing derivative valuations by policy type and scenario. b. Use sensitivity charts (e.g., tornado diagrams) to illustrate the impact of variable changes on fair value.


## 📤 Output format
- Tables with fair value estimates segmented by policy type, derivative feature, and scenario.
- Sensitivity testing results showing the effect of key variables (e.g., interest rates, volatility) on valuation.
- Stress test results highlighting financial impacts under extreme market conditions.
- Key findings report identifying high-risk policies and valuation priorities.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Higher sensitivity to certain market variables (e.g., interest rates or volatility) indicates higher exposure to risk, requiring additional hedging or reserve adjustments.
2. Fair values that deviate significantly from industry benchmarks may indicate the need to refine valuation models or assumptions.
30
3. Stress test results reveal potential financial vulnerabilities under extreme market conditions, helping prioritize risk mitigation efforts.
4. Negative valuation trends may signal increasing liabilities, requiring adjustments to pricing or product design.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Implement robust valuation models that account for the complexities of embedded derivatives, such as stochastic simulations for path-dependent guarantees.
2. Regularly update market and actuarial assumptions to reflect current trends in interest rates, equity markets, and policyholder behavior.
3. Strengthen hedging strategies to offset risks associated with high-sensitivity variables (e.g., interest rate swaps, equity options).
4. Enhance reinsurance agreements to transfer risk associated with embedded guarantees to third parties.
5. Conduct frequent scenario and stress testing to ensure adequate reserves and financial preparedness for extreme market conditions.
6. Ensure compliance with financial reporting standards (e.g., IFRS 9, IFRS 17) by aligning valuation practices with regulatory requirements.
7. Monitor competitor practices and benchmarks to identify gaps in current risk and valuation strategies.
