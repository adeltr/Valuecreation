# 42 · Retention vs. Lapse Rate Analysis by Customer Segment

> **Part:** [[F - Sales & Marketing]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Property & Casualty Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of Retention vs. Lapse Rate Analysis by Customer Segment is to compare the retention and lapse rates across different customer segments. This analysis helps the company understand which segments have the highest loyalty and which are at risk of leaving, informing targeted retention strategies and customer engagement efforts.


## 📊 Data required
1. Policy renewal and lapse data segmented by customer demographics (e.g., age, region, income level, policy type)
2. Total policies up for renewal in each segment
3. Renewal and lapse rates for each segment over time
4. Historical data on customer engagement (optional)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Define Customer Segments:** Identify customer segments based on relevant demographics, such as age, income, location, and policy type. This segmentation will form the basis for comparing retention and lapse rates.
2. **Calculate Retention Rate for Each Segment:** For each customer segment, calculate the retention rate:
`Retention Rate (%) = (Number of Policies Renewed in Segment / Total Policies up for Renewal in Segment) x 100`
This shows the percentage of customers who chose to renew within each segment.
3. **Calculate Lapse Rate for Each Segment:** Similarly, calculate the lapse rate for each segment:
`Lapse Rate (%) = (Number of Policies Lapsed in Segment / Total Policies up for Renewal in Segment) x 100`
This shows the percentage of customers who did not renew, indicating potential dissatisfaction or disengagement.
4. **Analyze Retention and Lapse Rates Together:** Compare retention and lapse rates within each segment to identify patterns. High retention with low lapse rates indicates loyalty, while low retention and high lapse rates may indicate potential attrition risks.
5. **Identify High-Risk Segments with High Lapse Rates:** Highlight customer segments with notably high lapse rates, as these may require targeted retention efforts. For example, younger or lower-income segments might exhibit higher lapse rates if affordability is an issue.
6. **Track Changes Over Time:** Analyze how retention and lapse rates change over multiple periods (e.g., quarterly, annually) to detect any emerging trends in customer behavior. This can reveal shifts in loyalty or retention risks for specific segments.
7. **Compile Results:** Summarize findings in a table showing retention and lapse rates for each customer segment, along with any significant trends or changes over time.


## 📤 Output format
The output should be a table with columns for:
- Customer Segment (e.g., age group, income level, policy type)
- Total Policies up for Renewal
- Retention Rate (%)
- Lapse Rate (%)
- Notable Trends (if applicable)
A bar chart comparing retention and lapse rates across customer segments or a line chart showing rate changes over time can provide a visual overview.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Retention and Low Lapse Rates:** Indicates strong customer loyalty in the segment, suggesting satisfaction with the product and engagement with the company.
- **Low Retention and High Lapse Rates:** Suggests potential disengagement or dissatisfaction within the segment, warranting targeted retention strategies.
- **High Lapse Rates with High-Risk Demographics:** May indicate that certain customer needs (e.g., affordability, coverage options) are not being met, signaling areas for product adjustments or enhanced customer outreach.
Benchmarking results against historical data or industry averages can provide context for identifying high-risk segments and prioritizing retention efforts.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Develop Targeted Retention Campaigns for High-Lapse Segments Implement retention efforts such as special renewal offers, personalized communications, or loyalty rewards in segments with low retention and high lapse rates.
2. Enhance Engagement and Communication for Low-Retention Segments Increase engagement efforts with segments exhibiting high lapse rates, providing educational content on policy benefits or regular check-ins to build loyalty.
3. Offer Flexible Payment and Coverage Options Address affordability concerns in high-lapse segments by introducing flexible payment plans or tailored coverage options that meet the specific needs of these customers.
4. Implement Customer Feedback Mechanisms Use surveys or feedback loops to understand why certain segments are not renewing, incorporating insights into product and service improvements.
5. Refine Product Offerings for High-Risk Demographics Tailor products and services to better match the preferences and needs of segments with high lapse rates, potentially improving retention by aligning with customer expectations.
6. Use Data Analytics to Predict Lapse Risks Leverage predictive analytics to identify customers at risk of lapsing, allowing for proactive retention actions targeted at high-risk individuals.
7. Benchmark Retention and Lapse Performance Against Competitors Compare retention and lapse rates with industry benchmarks to identify competitive gaps, enabling strategies to align with or surpass market standards.
