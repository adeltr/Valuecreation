# 11 · Plastic Credits and Recycled Plastic Feedstock Procurement Analysis

> **Part:** [[B - Recycling & Materials Recovery]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Waste Management Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate the financial and operational feasibility of purchasing plastic credits and procuring recycled plastic feedstock, ensuring that sustainability targets are met while maintaining cost-effectiveness and supply chain reliability.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Current and projected plastic usage (by resin type and volume)
- ​ Market prices for recycled plastic feedstock (e.g., rPET, rHDPE)
- ​ Availability of plastic credits, including their price and validation standards (e.g., Verra, Gold Standard)
- ​ Sustainability or ESG commitments related to plastic usage and recycling targets
- ​ Regulatory requirements (e.g., minimum recycled content mandates)
- ​ Supplier qualifications and production capacities for recycled plastics


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Identify sustainability goals and regulatory drivers​ Determine the specific recycled content or plastic reduction targets, along with any local or national regulations that require recycled content in plastic products.
2. Map out current plastic consumption​ Analyze the company’s current and forecasted plastic usage by resin type, volume, and application (e.g., packaging, products).
3. Evaluate recycled plastic feedstock supply​ Research available suppliers and their certifications, including capacity, pricing, and quality standards. Consider geographic location and logistics costs.
4. Assess plastic credit options​ Compare different plastic credit programs or standards, examining credit validity, cost, and reputation. Verify how credits align with corporate sustainability reporting frameworks.
5. Perform a cost-benefit analysis​ Compare the direct procurement of recycled plastics (and potential production modifications) against purchasing plastic credits to meet sustainability goals. Factor in any applicable taxes or incentives.

6. Develop a sourcing strategy​ Based on cost, quality, and sustainability impact, decide on an optimal mix of physical feedstock procurement and plastic credits. Address contingency plans for supply chain disruptions.


## 📤 Output format
1.​ A summary report or spreadsheet detailing: ○​ Projected plastic usage vs. recycled content targets ○​ Cost estimates for recycled feedstock and plastic credits ○​ Supplier evaluation metrics and risk assessments 2.​ Charts or tables comparing different sourcing scenarios (cost vs. sustainability impact) 3.​ Recommendations on the optimal blend of physical recycled content and credit purchases


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ High recycled plastic prices relative to virgin plastic may initially deter adoption but could be justified by sustainability commitments or consumer expectations.
- ​ Plastic credit purchases may be useful in the short term to fulfill targets but should ideally be paired with efforts to transition toward actual recycled feedstock for more tangible impact.
- ​ Supplier reliability and quality are critical; inconsistent or poor-quality feedstock can increase operational costs and disrupt production.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Develop long-term contracts or partnerships with reputable recyclers to secure stable pricing and supply for recycled resin. 2.​ Invest in R&D or product redesign to accommodate higher recycled content without compromising functionality or quality. 3.​ Explore blending recycled and virgin plastic to gradually increase recycled content while managing cost and quality constraints. 4.​ Engage in industry coalitions or circular economy initiatives to expand the overall market for high-quality recycled plastics. 5.​ Implement robust tracking and reporting tools to document plastic credit purchases and recycled content usage, ensuring transparency and credibility.
