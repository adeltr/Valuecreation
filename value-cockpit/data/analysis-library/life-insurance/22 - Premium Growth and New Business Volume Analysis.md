# 22 · Premium Growth and New Business Volume Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Profitability and Product Performance]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Life Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To evaluate premium growth trends and measure the performance of new business acquisition. This analysis identifies key drivers of premium growth, assesses the effectiveness of sales channels, and uncovers opportunities to improve new business volume and profitability.


## 📊 Data required
- **Policy-level data:** premium amounts for existing and new policies, policy type, policyholder demographics, and start dates for identifying new business
- **Financial data:** total premium revenue by period, commissions, and acquisition costs
- **New business data:** number of new policies written and premium amounts during the reporting period
- **Distribution channel data:** agents, brokers, bancassurance, direct sales
- Historical premium revenue trends over multiple years
- **Benchmark data:** industry growth rates and competitor performance


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Calculate total premium growth using the formula:
`Premium Growth (%) = [(Current Period Premium - Prior Period Premium) / Prior Period Premium] x 100`
2. Segment premium revenue into renewal premiums (from in-force policies) and new business premiums (from policies written during the reporting period).
3. Measure new business growth using:
`New Business Growth (%) = [(New Premium in Current Period - New Premium in Prior Period) / New Premium in Prior Period] x 100`
4. Break down premium growth and new business volume by product type, distribution channel, and demographics (e.g., age groups, geographic regions).
5. Measure the contribution of each distribution channel to total new business premiums and identify high-performing and underperforming channels.
6. Analyze historical trends to identify periods of growth or decline and evaluate the impact of external factors such as economic changes or marketing campaigns.
7. **Compare premium growth rates and new business volumes to industry:** benchmarks and competitor performance to assess market positioning.
8. Visualize the results using tables for premium growth, new business volume, and channel contributions, along with line and bar charts to display trends and segment performance.

## 📤 Output format
- Tables summarizing premium growth, new business volume, and contributions by product, channel, and demographics
- Line charts illustrating premium growth trends over time
- Bar charts showing new business premiums by product and channel


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
1. Strong premium growth indicates effective new business acquisition and retention strategies.
2. Stagnating or declining premium growth suggests issues with product demand, pricing, or distribution effectiveness.
3. High dependence on new business premiums without strong renewal revenue can signal a short-term growth strategy lacking long-term sustainability.
4. Underperforming distribution channels highlight inefficiencies or low penetration requiring realignment.
5. Benchmark comparisons provide insight into the company’s growth relative to competitors and industry standards.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Strengthen marketing campaigns and outreach efforts to increase acquisition of new business.
2. Improve training and support for underperforming distribution channels to boost effectiveness.
3. Introduce innovative products or features tailored to customer needs to attract new business.
4. Enhance retention efforts to ensure sustainable premium growth through renewal premiums.
5. Expand partnerships (e.g., bancassurance, digital platforms) to diversify distribution and capture new markets.
6. Analyze geographic and demographic segments to focus on areas with high growth potential.
7. Regularly benchmark performance against competitors and adjust pricing or sales strategies to align with market opportunities.
