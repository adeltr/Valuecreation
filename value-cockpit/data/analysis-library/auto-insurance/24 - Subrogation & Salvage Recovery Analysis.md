# 24 · Subrogation & Salvage Recovery Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Claims & Loss Management]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze an Auto Insurance Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Identify opportunities to maximize financial recoveries through effective subrogation and salvage processes, thereby reducing net claim costs and improving overall profitability.


## 📊 Data required
- ​ Claims data (paid amounts, liability determinations, settlement details)
- ​ Subrogation files (open, closed, and pending) including recovery amounts and timelines
- ​ Salvage records detailing the sale of damaged vehicles or parts
- ​ Vendor or auction house data on salvage sales and associated fees
- ​ Internal process metrics (average recovery timeline, success rate, staff workload)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1.​ Collect claims data where subrogation was pursued or salvage opportunities existed. Compile information on recoveries made, the parties pursued, and the final amounts or auction proceeds. 2.​ Calculate subrogation success rates by using Subrogation Success Rate = (Number of Successful Recoveries / Total Subrogation Attempts) x 100. Compare across different lines of business or claim types to see which areas yield higher or lower success. 3.​ Assess average recovery timelines by measuring the time from the claim’s settlement to receipt of subrogation proceeds. Identify process bottlenecks and evaluate if delayed recoveries reduce net present value. 4.​ Evaluate salvage proceeds by comparing actual sales prices to expected market values. Check if salvage partners or auction houses are meeting performance benchmarks or if alternative channels might improve returns. 5.​ Segment the data by claim type or cause of loss (e.g., total loss, theft, not-at-fault collision) to see where subrogation or salvage has the highest potential. Use these insights to prioritize resources or refine workflows. 6.​ Review internal procedures for subrogation and salvage, noting staff responsibilities and vendor contracts. Determine if specialized teams, automation, or revised guidelines could enhance recovery outcomes. 7.​ Benchmark against industry or competitor recovery rates if available. Identify significant gaps that might indicate inefficiencies or missed opportunities in your current program.
53 8.​ Document findings and propose improvements such as better training for subrogation teams, technology upgrades for faster lien checks, or renegotiated contracts with salvage vendors. Present this report to claims leadership for implementation.


## 📤 Output format
- ​ Tables or charts showing subrogation success rates, recovery timelines, and salvage proceeds by claim segment
- ​ Written summary highlighting key findings and potential enhancements
- ​ Financial impact projections illustrating the value of improved subrogation and salvage efforts


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- ​ Low success rates or protracted recovery timelines signal inefficiencies that may require process reengineering.
- ​ Underperforming salvage recoveries indicate the need to re-examine vendor partnerships, auction strategies, or pricing analyses.
- ​ High subrogation and salvage recoveries reduce net claim costs, directly improving loss ratios and financial performance.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1.​ Implement automated workflows that flag subrogation opportunities earlier and track progress in real time. 2.​ Negotiate better contracts or performance metrics with salvage vendors to optimize returns. 3.​ Provide dedicated training for claims adjusters and subrogation specialists to sharpen negotiation and legal knowledge. 4.​ Regularly review and refine subrogation and salvage policies to align with emerging market trends and regulatory environments.
