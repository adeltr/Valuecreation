# 36 · Product Shelf Life and Inventory Turnover Analysis

> **Part:** [[D - Operations and Supply Chain]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Cosmetics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
The goal of the Product Shelf Life and Inventory Turnover Analysis is to evaluate the efficiency of the brand’s inventory management in relation to product shelf life. This analysis focuses on understanding how long products can remain viable for sale, the speed at which inventory is sold, and how inventory turnover impacts waste, storage costs, and profitability. By optimizing inventory turnover and aligning it with product shelf life, the company can minimize waste, reduce excess inventory, and improve overall profitability.


## 📊 Data required
- Shelf life data for each product (e.g., expiration dates, best-before dates).
- Sales velocity data (e.g., how quickly each product is sold, by category and region).
- Inventory turnover rates by product category and SKU.
- Inventory holding costs (e.g., storage costs, handling, insurance).
- Inventory aging reports (e.g., how long products have been stored before being sold or discarded).
- Product return or disposal rates due to expired products.
- Supplier lead times for product restocking.
- Inventory forecast accuracy (e.g., differences between forecasted and actual demand).
- Competitor benchmarks for inventory turnover and shelf life management.
- Customer demand patterns and seasonality data.


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Gather product shelf life data:
a. Collect shelf life information for each product, including expiration dates or best-before dates, and categorize products based on their shelf stability (e.g., short-term vs. long-term shelf life).
b. Track how product shelf life affects inventory management, particularly for products that degrade quickly or require special storage conditions.
2. Measure inventory turnover rates:
a. Calculate inventory turnover rates for each product category using the formula:
`Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) / Average Inventory`
b. Compare turnover rates across product categories, identifying which products sell faster and which have slower turnover.
3. Assess sales velocity and demand patterns:
133 a. Analyze sales velocity data to determine how quickly products are sold after they are added to inventory.
b. Identify any seasonality or demand fluctuations that impact how fast or slow products move off the shelves.
4. Review inventory aging reports:
a. Track how long products remain in inventory before being sold or disposed of due to expiration.
b. Identify products that are nearing or have passed their expiration dates, and assess the financial impact of expired or unsold goods.
5. Evaluate inventory holding costs:
a. Calculate the costs associated with holding inventory, including storage, handling, insurance, and potential disposal of expired products.
b. Analyze whether slow-moving products increase holding costs and reduce profitability.
6. Analyze product return or disposal rates:
a. Review data on product returns or disposals due to expired products or quality degradation.
b. Identify which product categories are most susceptible to expiration issues and assess the financial impact of lost inventory.
7. Examine supplier lead times and restocking processes:
a. Review supplier lead times for restocking key products, focusing on how quickly inventory can be replenished without causing stock outs or overstocking.
b. Analyze how lead times affect inventory turnover and whether shorter lead times can improve inventory management.
8. Assess inventory forecast accuracy:
a. Compare forecasted demand with actual sales to assess how accurate inventory forecasts are for each product.
b. Identify any discrepancies between forecasted and actual sales that may lead to overstocking or understocking.
9. Benchmark against competitor practices:
134 a. Conduct a competitor analysis to benchmark the brand’s inventory turnover rates, shelf life management, and inventory costs against industry standards.
b. Identify best practices that competitors use to manage shelf life and inventory more efficiently.
10. Review customer demand patterns and seasonality:
a. Analyze customer demand patterns and seasonality to determine how these factors impact inventory turnover and shelf life management.
b. Adjust inventory levels and restocking strategies based on peak demand periods or slower sales seasons to avoid excess inventory and waste.


## 📤 Output format
- A product shelf life report summarizing the shelf life of each product category and highlighting products with shorter or more critical expiration windows.
- An inventory turnover analysis showing the turnover rates for each product category and identifying which products move quickly and which experience slower turnover.
- A sales velocity report analyzing how fast each product sells and identifying seasonal trends or demand fluctuations.
- An inventory aging report tracking how long products remain in inventory and highlighting any risks of expiration or overstock.
- A cost analysis of inventory holding costs, including storage, handling, and disposal expenses associated with slow-moving or expired products.
- A return and disposal analysis summarizing the financial impact of product returns and disposals due to expiration or quality issues.
- A supplier lead time and restocking efficiency report showing how supplier lead times impact inventory turnover and product availability.
- An inventory forecast accuracy report comparing forecasted demand with actual sales to identify discrepancies and opportunities for improved forecasting.
- A competitor benchmarking report comparing the brand’s inventory turnover and shelf life management practices to key competitors.
- Recommendations for improving inventory turnover, reducing waste, and optimizing product shelf life management.


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High inventory turnover and short shelf life alignment:** If products with shorter shelf lives have high inventory turnover, the company is efficiently managing these products. The brand should continue to optimize inventory levels and align replenishment with demand to avoid waste.
- **Low turnover for products with short shelf lives:** If products with short shelf lives have slow turnover, there is a risk of expiration and financial losses. The brand may need to adjust pricing, run promotions, or reduce order quantities to avoid overstock and waste.
135
- **Excessive inventory holding costs:** If holding costs are high, especially for slow-moving or perishable products, the brand should explore strategies to reduce these costs by improving inventory forecasting, reducing stock levels, or negotiating better terms with suppliers.
- **High product disposal rates:** If a significant number of products are being disposed of due to expiration, the company needs to address inefficiencies in supply chain management, such as adjusting reorder points, lead times, or restocking processes.
- **Inaccurate demand forecasting:** If there are discrepancies between forecasted and actual sales, the brand should refine its forecasting methods, especially for seasonal products or those with fluctuating demand.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Optimize inventory levels:** Adjust inventory levels to align with product shelf life and sales velocity. For products with shorter shelf lives, maintain lower inventory levels and restock more frequently to minimize waste.
2. **Improve demand forecasting:** Use advanced forecasting tools and sales data analysis to improve accuracy in predicting demand. Incorporate seasonality and customer behavior patterns to ensure the right amount of inventory is available at the right time.
3. **Enhance supplier relationships:** Work with suppliers to shorten lead times, allowing for more responsive restocking and reducing the need to hold large amounts of perishable inventory.
4. **Run promotions for slow-moving inventory:** Use promotions, discounts, or bundling to move slow-moving products before they expire. This can help reduce overstock and prevent product waste.
5. **Reduce inventory holding costs:** Explore cost-saving measures in storage, such as negotiating lower fees with warehouses, consolidating shipments, or using just-in-time (JIT) inventory practices to reduce storage needs.
6. **Monitor and reduce product disposal rates:** Track the reasons behind product disposals and address the root causes, such as overstocking, quality degradation, or slow turnover. Implement systems to move inventory faster before it expires.
7. **Benchmark and implement best practices:** Learn from competitors or industry leaders in inventory management and shelf life optimization, adopting best practices that can help reduce waste and improve profitability.
