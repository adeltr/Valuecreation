# KPI — Nonprofit


51 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| Advocacy Impact | For nonprofits engaged in policy work, this could involve measures such as the number of policies influenced or changed. This is a crucial KPI to understand the societal impact of such organizations. |
| Artistic Impact | For cultural and arts nonprofits, this KPI might track the number of exhibitions held, the number of new works commissioned, or the number of individuals reached through educational programs. |
| Average Gift Size | This metric averages the size of monetary donations. A larger average might signal the presence of major donors or successful fundraising strategies. |
| Beneficiary Satisfaction | This metric evaluates the satisfaction level of the people or causes the organization serves. High beneficiary satisfaction can signify effective service delivery and mission achievement. |
| Charitable Commitment Ratio | This KPI calculates the percentage of total expenses that is spent on the organization's charitable activities. A higher ratio can be a positive signal to donors and funders. |
| Cost Per Outcome | This is a measure of the organization's effectiveness in delivering its core services. It's calculated by dividing the total cost of a program by the number of outcomes or results produced. Lower costs per outcome indicate more efficient program delivery. |
| Crowdfunding Success Rate | This measures the percentage of crowdfunding campaigns that reach their target. Crowdfunding is becoming an increasingly popular tool for nonprofits, making this KPI relevant for many organizations. |
| Current Ratio | This financial metric compares an organization's current assets to its current liabilities, providing insight into its short-term financial health. |
| Days Cash on Hand | This metric calculates the number of days the organization could operate using only its available cash. It's a measure of the organization's short-term liquidity. |
| Digital Inclusion Measures | For nonprofits working on digital inclusion, KPIs might measure the number of individuals gaining internet access, improving digital literacy skills, or securing employment in tech-related fields. |
| Direct Mail Response Rate | This KPI measures the response rate to direct mail fundraising campaigns. It provides insights into the effectiveness of these campaigns. |
| Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) Metrics | These KPIs track the organization's progress toward DEI goals. They could include the demographic makeup of staff, leadership, and board members, or measures of program accessibility and equity. |
| Donor Retention Rate | This is the percentage of donors who continue to donate to the organization from one year to the next. A higher retention rate indicates a strong relationship between the organization and its donors, which contributes to financial stability. |
| Endowment Spending Rate | For organizations with endowments, this KPI measures the percentage of the endowment spent each year. This balance is key to maintaining the endowment's value while also supporting the organization's operations. |
| Environmental Impact Metrics | For environmental nonprofits, this could include measures such as the amount of land preserved, number of trees planted, amount of emissions reduced, etc. |
| Event ROI | For nonprofits that host fundraising events, this KPI measures the return on investment for each event. It's calculated by subtracting the event costs from the funds raised, then dividing by the event costs. |
| Food Security Measures | For food banks or other food security organizations, KPIs could include the number of meals provided, the number of families served, or rates of food insecurity in their service area. |
| Full Time Equivalent (FTE) Volunteers to FTE Staff Ratio | This calculates the ratio of full-time equivalent volunteers to full-time equivalent staff. This can give insight into the organization's dependence on volunteer labor. |
| Fundraising Efficiency Ratio | This shows the organization’s effectiveness in raising funds. It is calculated by dividing fundraising expenses by the total contributions received. A lower ratio implies that the organization spends less to raise each dollar, which is a positive signal to donors and investors. |
| Gift Aid Claims | For nonprofits in countries like the UK where gift aid is available, this KPI measures the percentage of eligible donations for which gift aid has been successfully claimed. |
| Gift Dependency Ratio | This ratio, also known as the public support ratio, measures the proportion of the organization's total revenue that comes from charitable contributions. Organizations with a higher ratio could be more vulnerable to fluctuations in donor behavior. |
| Government Funding Ratio | This KPI measures the portion of the organization's total revenue that comes from government grants or contracts. A high ratio may indicate dependency on government funding and potential vulnerability to policy or budget changes. |
| Grant Acquisition Rate | This measures the success rate of grant applications. A higher rate can indicate an effective grants team and a compelling mission or program proposal. |
| Health Outcome Measures | For health-based nonprofits, specific health outcome measures could be important KPIs. For example, a mental health nonprofit might track reduction in symptom scores or an improvement in quality of life indicators. |
| Housing Stability Rate | For nonprofits addressing homelessness, this KPI could track the number of individuals or families who maintain stable housing over a specific period after intervention. |
| Improvement in Educational Outcomes | Nonprofits focused on education might measure improvements in students' grades, graduation rates, or other academic outcomes. |
| In-Kind Contributions as a Percentage of Total Contributions | This measures the percentage of total contributions received in the form of goods and services rather than cash. This can be relevant for organizations that heavily rely on these types of contributions, such as food banks. |
| Leverage Ratio | This ratio measures the percentage of the nonprofit's capital that is obtained through debt. Even though nonprofits aim not to make a profit, they sometimes use debt to finance their activities. |
| Lobbying Success Rate | For advocacy organizations, this KPI measures the success of lobbying activities, such as the number of bills or policies influenced or passed due to their efforts. |
| Mission Impact Measurement | This varies significantly by organization but includes any metrics that directly measure the impact of the organization's mission. For example, a nonprofit focused on reducing poverty might track income levels or employment rates of its program participants. |
| Number of Policy Changes Achieved | For advocacy nonprofits, this KPI can be used to measure the number of concrete policy changes achieved as a result of their advocacy efforts. |
| Outcome Measures | These are specific metrics tied to the organization's mission. For example, a nonprofit dedicated to homelessness might measure the number of people housed, while a literacy program might measure the number of people who improve their reading skills. |
| Overhead Ratio | This calculates the percentage of an organization's budget spent on administrative and fundraising expenses. A lower overhead ratio is generally more appealing to donors, as it suggests more funds go directly towards the organization's mission. |
| Patient Satisfaction Rate | For health nonprofits, patient satisfaction is a critical measure of the quality of care and services provided. |
| Peer Comparison Metrics | These KPIs involve comparing an organization's metrics (such as fundraising efficiency or program outcomes) to similar organizations in the same sector. This can provide valuable context for performance. |
| Percentage of Funding from Diversified Sources | This KPI measures the extent to which a nonprofit's funding is diversified across different sources (e.g., individual donors, corporate sponsors, grants). Diversified funding can reduce financial risk. |
| Percentage of Recurring Donors | This measures the portion of donors who are committed to regular contributions. A higher percentage means more predictable revenue streams. |
| Program Efficiency Ratio | This measures the percentage of total expenses that a nonprofit organization spends on its core services. It's calculated by dividing the program expenses by the total expenses. A higher ratio suggests that the organization is effectively utilizing its resources for its mission. |
| Program Growth Rate | This measures the year-over-year percentage growth in the organization's programs or services. Rapid growth could signal a rising demand for the organization's services or successful expansion strategies. |
| Recovery Rate | For nonprofits involved in resource recovery or recycling, this measures the percentage of materials successfully recovered or recycled compared to the total received. |
| Reduction in Poverty Level | For nonprofits focused on poverty reduction, this KPI could measure changes in the poverty level in the communities they serve. |
| Research Grant Success Rate | For research nonprofits, this KPI measures the proportion of research grant applications that are successful. It's a key indicator of the quality and relevance of the organization's research. |
| Reserve Adequacy | This measures the number of months an organization can cover its operating expenses with current net assets. It's a crucial indicator of financial stability. |
| Return on Fundraising Expenses | This measures the return on investment from fundraising activities. It's calculated by dividing the total contributions received by the total fundraising expenses. |
| Service Accessibility Metrics | These KPIs measure how accessible an organization's services are to its target population. This could include geographic coverage, the number of languages services are offered in, or measures of physical accessibility. |
| Service Delivery Efficiency | This KPI measures how efficiently a nonprofit delivers its services. For example, a food bank might measure the number of meals provided per volunteer hour. |
| Social Media Engagement Rate | For nonprofits that leverage social media for outreach, this KPI measures the amount of interaction (likes, shares, comments) on their social media posts. Higher engagement can indicate effective communication and community building. |
| Sustainability Ratio | This ratio measures how long an organization could function using its unrestricted reserves without additional net assets. This ratio provides a measure of financial resilience. |
| Time to Adoption | For animal shelters or similar nonprofits, this KPI tracks the average amount of time from when an animal is received to when it's adopted. |
| Volunteer Turnover Rate | Similar to employee turnover in for-profit entities, this measures the rate at which volunteers discontinue their service. High turnover can indicate potential issues with volunteer engagement or management. |
| Website Traffic and Conversion Rates | This measures the amount of traffic on the organization's website and the percentage of visitors who take a desired action such as signing up for a newsletter, making a donation, or registering for an event. |
