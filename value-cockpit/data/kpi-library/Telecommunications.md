# KPI — Telecommunications

**Secteur (comprendre) :** [[Technology & Telecom]]
**Analyses d'entreprise liées :** [[Telecommunications Company - Analyse]]

51 indicateurs clés pour ce secteur (source : Umbrex Library of Industry-specific KPIs).

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| 5G Penetration Rate | For companies involved in providing 5G services, the percentage of their user base that is using 5G can be a key KPI, indicating adoption of the new technology. |
| Average Call Setup Time | This measures the time taken to establish a call on the network. Shorter times usually result in a better user experience. |
| Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) | This KPI measures the average revenue generated by each subscriber or user. It is a fundamental metric for understanding the value that individual customers bring to the business and how successfully the company monetizes its services. |
| Broadband Subscriber Growth | Specifically for telecommunication companies that offer internet services, the growth of broadband subscribers can be a key indicator of performance. It provides an idea of how well the company is doing in the expanding field of internet service provision. |
| Broadcast Signal Penetration | For telecoms involved in broadcast services, this KPI measures the extent of their signal's coverage area. |
| Churn Rate | The churn rate represents the percentage of subscribers who discontinue their subscriptions within a given time period. It’s a critical metric to track as it directly influences revenue, and high churn rates can indicate customer dissatisfaction. |
| Cloud Revenue | For telecom companies offering cloud services, tracking the revenue from this segment can give insight into the success of this increasingly important line of business. |
| Content Revenue | For telecom companies that have diversified into content creation or ownership (like owning TV channels or streaming platforms), tracking the revenue from this segment can give insight into the success of these endeavors. |
| Data ARPU | Given the growing significance of data services, Data ARPU, or the average revenue per user for data services, can be a crucial metric, particularly for mobile operators. |
| Data Breach Frequency | Given the importance of cybersecurity in the telecommunications sector, the frequency of data breaches can be a key performance indicator. Lower frequencies are generally better as they indicate more robust cybersecurity measures. |
| Data Center Capacity Utilization | For telecoms that operate data centers, the level of capacity utilization can indicate the efficiency of resource usage and potential for future expansion. |
| Data Traffic | With the rise of smartphones and other data-consuming devices, the amount of data traffic a network can handle has become an important KPI. This can influence the user experience and, ultimately, customer satisfaction and loyalty. |
| Dedicated Internet Access (DIA) Revenue | This measures the revenue generated from providing dedicated, high-speed internet connections to businesses. |
| Edge Computing Capabilities | For telecoms that are developing edge computing services, tracking the number of edge data centers or the amount of data processed at the edge can be important KPIs. |
| Enterprise Service Level Agreement (SLA) Fulfillment Rate | For telecoms providing services to enterprise clients, tracking the percentage of service level agreements that are met is crucial. |
| Fiber Miles | For companies involved in the broadband sector, the total length of fiber-optic cable laid can be a critical KPI. It can reflect the company's reach and investment in its network infrastructure. |
| First Call Resolution (FCR) | This is a metric used in call centers operated by telecom companies. FCR measures the percentage of calls where the customer's query or issue is resolved during the first interaction, which is an important determinant of customer satisfaction. |
| Fixed-line Vs. Mobile Subscribers | In some markets, fixed-line services remain important. This KPI is the ratio of fixed-line to mobile subscribers and can provide insights into market dynamics and the company's customer base. |
| Fleet Management Subscriptions | For those telecom companies offering fleet management solutions (like GPS tracking), the number of subscriptions can indicate the health of this business line. |
| HD Voice Subscribers | For telecom operators offering High Definition (HD) voice services, tracking the number of HD voice subscribers can be a useful metric. |
| Hosted PBX Seats | For telecom companies providing hosted PBX (Private Branch Exchange) services, the number of 'seats' or users is an important metric. |
| Infrastructure Downtime Rate | This measures the percentage of time the company's network infrastructure is unavailable due to planned or unplanned maintenance. Lower downtime rates generally indicate better service reliability and efficiency. |
| Infrastructure Sharing Revenues | Some telecom companies share their infrastructure (like cell towers) with other telecom operators for a fee. The income generated from such arrangements can be an important KPI. |
| Interconnection Usage Charges (IUC) | This KPI measures the charges that telecom operators pay each other for using each other's networks. Lower IUC can indicate a more self-reliant network. |
| Internet Service Uptime | For companies that provide internet services, the percentage of time that the service is available (uptime) is a crucial measure of reliability and quality of service. |
| IoT (Internet of Things) Revenue | For telecom companies involved in providing IoT services, the revenue from this segment can be a crucial KPI. |
| Latency | This KPI refers to the delay before a transfer of data begins following an instruction for its transfer. Lower latency is usually better as it provides a better experience for users, particularly in applications like online gaming and video streaming. |
| Leased Line Revenues | In telecom companies providing dedicated lines to businesses, the revenues from these leased lines is an important metric. |
| Minutes of Usage (MOU) | MOU shows the total number of minutes that customers use the company's services. Higher MOU suggests greater customer engagement. |
| Network Error Rate | The rate at which errors occur in the network transmission of data. A lower rate indicates a more reliable network. |
| Network Expansion Rate | This KPI refers to the rate at which a telecommunications company is expanding its network, such as adding new cell towers or laying new fiber-optic cables. |
| Network Utilization Rate | This KPI measures the extent to which the telecommunications company's network capacity is being used. A high rate may suggest that the company is effectively utilizing its assets, but it may also indicate the need for more network infrastructure if the company is frequently operating at full capacity. |
| OTT Service Revenues | Over-the-top (OTT) service revenues measure income derived from the delivery of video and voice content over the internet. This is particularly relevant for telecoms that have expanded into the digital content space. |
| Peak Data Throughput | This measures the maximum speed of data transfer that a telecom network can achieve under ideal conditions. A higher peak data throughput means the network can handle more data, improving overall performance. |
| Percentage of Population Covered | This KPI measures the percentage of the total population covered by the company's network. Greater coverage can imply a larger potential customer base. |
| Percentage of Revenue from Business Customers | This KPI indicates the share of a telecom company's revenue that comes from business or enterprise customers. A higher percentage can be a positive indicator if the company is strategically targeting higher-value business customers. |
| Percentage of Traffic Offloaded | This KPI measures how much data traffic is offloaded from cellular networks to Wi-Fi networks, which can help manage network load and provide a better customer experience. |
| Quality of Service (QoS) Metrics | These are technically defined performance metrics like call setup success rate, dropped call rate, blocked call rate, etc. These measures are indicators of the quality of service being provided to customers. |
| Revenue from Roaming Charges | This applies to companies with significant international mobile services. It measures the income earned from charges applied to customers for using mobile services while abroad. |
| Revenue from Telematics | For telecoms offering telematics services, tracking the revenue from this line of business can give an insight into its performance. |
| Roaming Revenues | For operators with a significant number of customers who travel internationally, the revenue generated from roaming charges can be an important metric to track. |
| Rural vs. Urban Subscriber Ratio | This KPI indicates the ratio of rural to urban subscribers, which can provide insights into a telecom company's market distribution and potential for growth in underserved areas. |
| Satellite Uplink Success Rate | For satellite telecom operators, the successful transmission of data to satellites is a critical KPI, reflecting the reliability and efficiency of the service. |
| SD-WAN (Software-Defined Wide Area Network) Customers | For telecom companies offering SD-WAN services, the number of customers using this service can indicate its market acceptance and growth potential. |
| Smart Home Subscriptions | For telecoms offering smart home services (like home automation or security), tracking the number of these subscriptions can be a valuable KPI. |
| Spectrum Efficiency | This KPI assesses the capacity of a telecom company to provide services to its customers within its allocated radio frequency spectrum. Higher efficiency usually means the company can support more users or provide more services within its allocated bandwidth. |
| Subscriber Growth Rate | This KPI measures the rate at which new subscribers are being added. A consistently high growth rate may indicate that a company's services are in high demand and the company is growing. |
| Tower Tenancy Ratio | For telecom companies with a significant presence in owning and operating telecom towers, this ratio measures the average number of operators (tenants) per tower. A higher ratio indicates efficient use of assets and can lead to higher profitability. |
| Traffic from Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) | For telecoms involved in internet content delivery, measuring the volume of data traffic handled by their CDNs can be an important KPI. |
| Video Traffic | As video streaming becomes more popular, the proportion of network traffic devoted to video can indicate the demand for this type of content and the strain on the network. |
| VoLTE (Voice over Long-Term Evolution) Penetration | The VoLTE penetration rate shows the adoption of VoLTE services among the telecom operator's customer base, reflecting how well the company is transitioning to this newer technology. |
