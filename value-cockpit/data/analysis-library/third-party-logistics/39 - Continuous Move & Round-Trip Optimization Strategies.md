# 39 · Continuous Move & Round-Trip Optimization Strategies

> **Part:** [[H - Cost Optimization & Efficiency]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Third Party Logistics Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
Maximize asset utilization and reduce empty miles by planning continuous moves or round-trip routes for trucks and trailers, thereby lowering transportation costs and improving driver productivity.


## 📊 Data required
- Historical load data (origin, destination, frequency, capacity utilization)
- Current driver schedules, shift lengths, and hours-of-service rules
- Fuel costs, tolls, and other operational expenses
- Network or lane overlaps (inbound, outbound, and potential backhauls)
- Seasonal volume fluctuations or demand surges
- Real-time visibility of available loads or digital freight matching options


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. **Aggregate Lane & Volume Data:** Compile shipment information by route, noting which lanes are frequently used, which have consistent backhaul potential, and any seasonality trends affecting load availability.
2. **Identify Potential Continuous Move Sequences:** Look for route patterns where one truck can finish a delivery and pick up another load nearby. Match origins and destinations that align well to form a continuous cycle.
3. **Evaluate Driver & Hours-of-Service Constraints:** Ensure route planning respects driver shift limits and rest requirements. Check if proposed continuous moves fit within legal driving windows or if additional staging is required.
4. **Analyze Cost Savings & Asset Utilization:** Estimate reduced empty miles and associated cost reductions when trucks transition directly to a new load without returning empty. Example formula:
`Empty Miles Reduction = (Baseline Empty Miles - Optimized Empty Miles)`
5. **Consider Equipment Compatibility:** Verify that trailer type, load requirements (temperature control, flatbed, dry van), and regulatory constraints match across potential continuous move routes.
6. **Pilot & Measure Outcomes:** Implement continuous move scenarios for a selection of lanes. Track changes in 99 average load factor, fuel usage, and on-time performance to validate assumptions.
7. **Scale & Integrate into Daily Operations:** If pilot results are favorable, gradually expand continuous move strategies across multiple regions or customers. Integrate with TMS or digital freight matching tools for real-time route adjustments.


## 📤 Output format
- A route map showing potential continuous move or round-trip loops
- A before-and-after comparison table for total miles, empty miles, and cost per mile
- A performance dashboard tracking driver utilization, on-time delivery, and cost savings
- A recommendations document outlining optimal lanes for continuous move programs and steps to scale implementation


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- **High Reduction in Empty Miles:** Indicates a successful matching of backhauls or continuous moves, lowering costs significantly.
- **Improved Driver Productivity:** Suggests better scheduling alignment and less downtime, potentially enhancing driver retention.
- **Limited Opportunities for Certain Lanes:** May reflect a low volume of outbound or inbound shipments that match continuously, requiring broader network collaboration.
- **Potential Equipment or Compatibility Issues:** Could restrict full implementation if specialized loads or trailers cannot be easily transitioned from one route to another.


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. **Leverage TMS & Data Analytics:** Use real-time data to identify feasible continuous move or round-trip matches, automating load assignment where possible.
2. **Engage in Collaborative Networks:** Partner with customers or other shippers in shared regions to combine volumes and expand backhaul opportunities.
3. **Optimize Driver Schedules:** Coordinate dispatch planning with hours-of-service regulations to keep trucks moving within legal limits.
