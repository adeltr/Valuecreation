# 17 · Mechanical Biological Treatment (MBT) Facility Performance and Throughput Analysis

> **Part:** [[C - Organics & Composting]]  ·  **Index:** [[How to Analyze a Waste Management Company]]

## 🎯 Goal
To measure and optimize the operational performance of a Mechanical Biological Treatment (MBT) facility by assessing material throughput, recovery rates, and the efficiency of the biological treatment process, thereby maximizing resource recovery and minimizing residual waste.


## 📊 Data required
- Incoming waste quantities (tons per day or week) and composition (organics, recyclables, residuals)
- Facility design capacity for both mechanical (sorting) and biological (composting or anaerobic digestion) stages
- Operational parameters (retention time, temperature, aeration for biological process)
- Equipment performance data (downtime, throughput rates of sorting lines)
- Output quantities and quality (e.g., recovered recyclables, compost-like output, refuse-derived fuel)
- Residual disposal volumes and costs
- Energy consumption and generation data (if anaerobic digestion is used)
- Regulatory requirements related to process outputs (e.g., compost standards, emissions)


## 🪜 Step-by-step
1. Characterize incoming waste stream Determine the composition and volume of waste processed. Identify the proportion of organics, recyclables, and non-recoverable material.
2. Evaluate mechanical pre-treatment performance Review the efficiency of sorting, shredding, or screening equipment, measuring both throughput rates and contaminant removal from recyclable fractions.
3. Assess biological treatment stage Analyze retention times, temperature profiles, and oxygen supply (or biogas production in anaerobic digestion). Track reductions in volatile solids or moisture content.
48
4. Calculate recovery and diversion rates Measure how much material is recovered or stabilized, compared to total incoming waste.
Equation:
`Diversion Rate (%) = ((Amount of Recovered/Processed Material) / (Total Incoming Waste)) x 100`
5. Determine residual disposal requirements Quantify the fraction of the MBT output that still requires landfilling or incineration, noting associated costs.
6. Monitor energy usage or production If anaerobic digestion is used, track biogas output and any energy generated. Compare energy production with facility consumption.
7. Identify bottlenecks and improvement opportunities Pinpoint process bottlenecks, such as insufficient capacity in the mechanical stage or suboptimal biological treatment conditions. Develop proposals for equipment upgrades, process modifications, or staff training.


## 📤 Output format
- A performance report summarizing throughput rates, recovery rates, and energy production (if applicable)
- Tables or charts illustrating waste composition, process outputs, and residual disposal volumes
- Cost-benefit analysis linking recovery efficiencies and operational expenses to potential revenue (recyclables, energy)
- Recommendations for facility design improvements, process optimization, or technology upgrades


## 🔍 How to interpret the results
- High recovery and diversion rates indicate that mechanical separation and biological treatment are effectively reducing landfill-bound material.
- Excessive residual fractions may reflect inadequate sorting or incomplete biological stabilization.
- Energy production exceeding on-site consumption can signal both operational efficiency and revenue opportunities (e.g., selling surplus electricity).
49


## 🚀 Steps to improve
1. Upgrade or regularly maintain mechanical sorting equipment to improve separation efficiency and throughput.
2. Optimize biological treatment parameters (e.g., aeration, temperature, or retention time) to achieve higher stabilization rates.
3. Conduct regular waste audits to adapt processes to changes in the incoming waste composition.
4. Explore additional end markets or refine outputs (e.g., high-quality compost, RDF) to increase revenue streams.
5. Implement continuous monitoring and advanced control systems to proactively address process variations and reduce downtime.
